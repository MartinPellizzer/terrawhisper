Rheum spiciforme

Rheum spiciforme, commonly known as the Spiked Rhubarb, is a perennial herb native to the high-altitude regions of the Himalayas. Valued in traditional medicine, its roots possess significant therapeutic properties, including anti-inflammatory and purgative effects. This resilient plant plays a vital role in both ecological and medicinal ecosystems.

  • Scientific name: Rheum spiciforme
  • Family: Polygonaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Under the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls into the order Caryophyllales and the family Polygonaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Rheum as the species Rheum spiciforme.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions of Asia. Within Central Asia, it can be found growing in the territories of Kazakhstan, Kirgizstan, and Tadzhikistan. Its presence extends into Western Asia, specifically within the borders of Afghanistan. Additionally, the species is documented in the eastern part of the continent in China, specifically in the Qinghai province. Together, these locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in diverse mountainous landscapes.
