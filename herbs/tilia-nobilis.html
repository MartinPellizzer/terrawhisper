Tilia nobilis

What's the taxonomical classification of Tilia nobilis?

Tilia nobilis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Moving through its hierarchical structure, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant is further organized under the order Malvales and the family Malvaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Tilia and the species nobilis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Tilia nobilis has heart-shaped leaves with serrated margins, a woody trunk reaching moderate heights, and small yellowish flowers enclosed by a specialized leaf-like bract. The foliage is typically dark green and possesses a smooth texture on the upper surface while being slightly pubescent underneath. Its flowers produce a sweet fragrance and are clustered in drooping cymes that emerge from the axils. The distinctive bract serves a functional role by assisting in wind dispersal of the seeds once they mature. This species exhibits a spreading canopy structure that provides significant shade in its natural habitat.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the mountainous regions of central and southern Europe, specifically spanning from the Alps and the Carpathians to the Balkan Peninsula. It primarily flourishes in temperate climates within montane forests at elevations ranging from several hundred to over one thousand meters. Its distribution is often restricted to well-drained, limestone-rich soils found along mountain slopes and valley sides. While its natural range is concentrated in the European continent, it is occasionally found in cultivated botanical gardens elsewhere. The species tends to favor shaded or semi-shaded forest edges where moisture levels remain consistent.
