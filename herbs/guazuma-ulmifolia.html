West Indian-elm

Guazuma ulmifolia · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (336 studies) · ★★★★★

West Indian-elm, scientifically known as Guazuma ulmifolia, is a versatile tropical shrub celebrated in traditional medicine. Native to the Caribbean and Central America, it possesses potent anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties. Its leaves and bark are frequently utilized in herbal remedies to treat skin ailments, digestive issues, and respiratory inflammation.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malvales, it is positioned in the family Malvaceae. Specifically, it is categorized under the genus Guazuma.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse distribution across several key regions in the Indian Ocean and the Asian continent. Within the Western Indian Ocean, its presence is noted in both Mauritius and Réunion. Moving toward the Indian Subcontinent, the species is widely established in India. It also maintains a significant presence across the landscape of Pakistan. Finally, the plant can be found growing within the borders of Sri Lanka.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Guácimo, possesses various components that are utilized for their therapeutic properties, particularly the stem bark. In many traditional medicinal practices, the bark of the stem is harvested to prepare infusions or decoctions used to treat inflammatory conditions. The medicinal potency is often concentrated within the stem bark, making the extraction of the bark a vital step in processing the plant. Ultimately, whether referred to as the bark or the stem bark, these structural parts are essential for accessing the bioactive compounds required for healing.
