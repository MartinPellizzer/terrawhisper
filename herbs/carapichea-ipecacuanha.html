Ipecac, scientifically known as Carapichea ipecacuanha, is a medicinal plant native to the Brazilian rainforest. Traditionally used to induce vomiting, its roots contain emetine and cephaeline. While valued for treating certain parasitic infections and respiratory ailments, its potent alkaloids require careful dosage to avoid serious toxicity and side effects.

Physiology

The physiology of Carapichea ipecacuanha is characterized by complex metabolic pathways centered on the biosynthesis of psychoactive and emetic alkaloids, most notably emetine and cephaeline, which are sequestered within the plant's root tissues. These secondary metabolites are produced as a chemical defense mechanism against soil-borne pathogens and herbivores. The plant's nutrient acquisition processes are strictly dependent on the availability of nitrogen within the soil substrate, as it lacks the physiological capacity for symbiotic nitrogen fixation. Consequently, it is not a nitrogen fixer, meaning it cannot convert atmospheric nitrogen into organic forms and must rely on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium from its environment to support its growth and protein synthesis. Its physiological efficiency is also closely tied to the moisture levels and organic matter content of the tropical forest floor, which dictates the rate of mineral absorption and the metabolic energy allocated to alkaloid production.
