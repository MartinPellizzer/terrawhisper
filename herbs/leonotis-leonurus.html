Lion's ear, scientifically known as Leonotis leonurus, is a striking perennial shrub native to South Africa. Characterized by its vibrant, velvety orange flower spikes, this medicinal plant has long been utilized in traditional healing to treat ailments ranging from inflammation to respiratory issues, making it a staple in ethnobotany.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide-ranging natural distribution across several distinct African regions. In West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence is specifically noted in Burundi. The species is also extensively found throughout Southern Africa, including the Cape Provinces. Furthermore, it thrives in the KwaZulu-Natal region of the south. Finally, its geographical reach extends to the Free State and the Northern Provinces within Southern Africa.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Wild Dagga, possesses several parts that are utilized in traditional medicine. The vibrant, orange tubular flowers are perhaps its most striking feature, often growing in dense whorls along the upright shoots. The lush green leaves and general plant leaves are rich in bioactive compounds, which are frequently harvested for therapeutic use. Depending on the specific preparation required, practitioners may use the fresh foliage or rely on dried leaves to create infusions and herbal remedies.
