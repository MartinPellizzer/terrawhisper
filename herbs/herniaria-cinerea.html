Herniaria cinerea

Herniaria cinerea, commonly known as Ashy Herniaria, is a small, creeping perennial herb found in various tropical regions. Renowned in traditional medicine, it is frequently utilized for its diuretic and anti-inflammatory properties. This resilient plant plays a significant role in treating urinary ailments and supporting overall renal health.

  • Scientific name: Herniaria cinerea
  • Family: Caryophyllaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further organized into the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Caryophyllales, falling under the family Caryophyllaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Herniaria.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Herniaria cinerea, is referred to by various common names depending on the language and region. In Czech, it is called průtržník popelavý, while in Arabic, it is known as غبرة. In Spanish, it is commonly referred to as Hierbamenoa, and it is also identified by its taxonomic subspecies names, specifically Herniaria hirsuta subsp. cinerea and Herniaria hirsuta subsp cinerea.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions of Europe. In Middle Europe, its presence is noted within the territories of Germany as well as the Czechia-Slovakia border region. Moving toward the Mediterranean, the species is also found throughout various parts of Southwestern Europe. Specifically, it inhabits the islands of the Baleares and the island of Corse. Finally, its range extends across the mainland of France within the Southwestern European zone.
