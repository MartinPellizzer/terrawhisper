European Olive

Olea europaea subsp. europaea · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (9 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

European Olive, scientifically known as Olea europaea subsp. europaea, is a cornerstone of Mediterranean culture and medicine. Renowned for its nutrient-dense fruit, it produces olive oil, a powerhouse of polyphenols and antioxidants. Used traditionally to support cardiovascular health and reduce inflammation, this resilient evergreen remains a vital therapeutic staple.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Lamiales, it is classified under the family Oleaceae. Within this family, it is specifically identified by the genus Olea as the subspecies Olea europaea subsp. europaea.

Distribution

This plant, Olea europaea subsp. europaea, is widely distributed across several regions within Southwestern Europe. It can be found growing in the coastal and inland areas of France and Portugal. The species also thrives within the Mediterranean island territories of Corse and Sardegna. Furthermore, its presence is well-documented throughout the Baleares archipelago. Together, these locations highlight the plant's significant footprint across the southwestern Mediterranean landscape.
