Carex capillaris subsp. capillaris

Carex capillaris subsp. capillaris, commonly known as hair sedge, is a perennial graminoid native to various temperate regions. While primarily valued in horticulture for its fine, hair-like foliage, it holds ecological significance. In traditional contexts, certain sedge species are explored for their diverse phytochemical properties and medicinal potential.

  • Scientific name: Carex capillaris subsp. capillaris
  • Family: Cyperaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Under the subclass Magnoliidae and order Poales, it is further categorized into the family Cyperaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Carex.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across the northern reaches of Europe. Its presence is well-documented in the rugged landscapes of Iceland and the Faroe Islands. Additionally, it can be found growing throughout the various regions of Norway and Finland. The species also extends its range into the territories of Great Britain. Together, these locations highlight its preference for northern European environments.
