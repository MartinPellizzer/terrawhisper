Prunus domestica

Plum tree

Prunus domestica, commonly known as the European plum, is a versatile deciduous tree valued for its nutrient-dense fruit. Beyond culinary uses, it holds significant medicinal importance. Rich in dietary fiber, antioxidants, and vitamins, its fruit and bark are traditionally utilized to support digestive health and alleviate constipation effectively.

  • Scientific name: Prunus domestica
  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following its subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized into the order Rosales and the family Rosaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Prunus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Prunus domestica, is recognized by various common names across different languages and regions. In Albanian, it is referred to as Promm, while in Quechua, it is called Sirwila. In Bosnian, the name used is Šljiva, and in Malay, it is known as Pokok Plum. Finally, in Hungarian, this medicinal and fruit-bearing species is called nemes szilva.

Plant Parts

This plant, Prunus domestica, commonly known as the plum, is valued for its medicinal and nutritional properties, which are primarily derived from its fruits. The fruit is a fleshy drupe that contains a sweet pulp surrounding a single, hard stone or pit. In various traditional practices, the fruit is utilized for its high fiber content to aid digestion, while the dried fruit is often used to manage constipation and promote regular bowel movements.
