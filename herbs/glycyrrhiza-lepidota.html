Glycyrrhiza lepidota

American licorice

Glycyrrhiza lepidota, commonly known as American licorice, is a perennial herb native to North American prairies. Renowned for its sweet, aromatic root, it has long been utilized in traditional medicine to soothe digestive ailments, alleviate coughs, and reduce inflammation, offering a potent, indigenous alternative to Asian licorice varieties.

  • Scientific name: Glycyrrhiza lepidota
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fabales, it is classified under the family Fabaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Glycyrrhiza, specifically as the species Glycyrrhiza lepidota.

Common Names

This plant, known in English as American licorice, carries various names across different languages and cultures.
