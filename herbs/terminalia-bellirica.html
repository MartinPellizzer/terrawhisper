Bastard Myrobalan

Terminalia bellirica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (462 studies) · ★★★★★

Bastard Myrobalan, scientifically known as Terminalia bellirica, is a prominent medicinal tree found in tropical Asia. Highly valued in Ayurveda, it is a key component of Triphala. Renowned for its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties, this plant supports digestive health, respiratory wellness, and overall immune system strength.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Myrtales, it is classified under the family Combretaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus name, Terminalia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution spanning across several distinct regions. In the Western Indian Ocean, it can be found growing in both Mauritius and Rodrigues. Its presence extends to East Asia, specifically within the South-Central regions of China. Within the Indian Subcontinent, the species is naturally distributed in Assam. Additionally, the plant is documented to inhabit the territories of Bangladesh.

Plant Parts

This plant, Terminalia bellirica, is a versatile medicinal species where various components are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The leaves are often harvested for their specific chemical constituents, and even a single leaf can provide significant bioactive compounds when processed. The stem serves as a structural foundation and is frequently used in traditional decoctions. Most notably, the fruit is highly valued in Ayurvedic medicine, and the collection of its fruits is essential for preparing various herbal formulations.
