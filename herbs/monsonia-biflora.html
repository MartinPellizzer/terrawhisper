Monsonia biflora

Monsonia biflora, commonly known as the "Two-flowered Monsonia," is a medicinal shrub native to the arid regions of Africa. Renowned for its therapeutic properties, it is traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat various ailments, including skin conditions and digestive issues, offering significant potential for modern pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Monsonia biflora
  • Family: Geraniaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further organized into the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Geraniales, falling under the family Geraniaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Monsonia, specifically the species Monsonia biflora.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found within the borders of Burundi and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Its presence extends into South Tropical Africa, specifically inhabiting the territories of Angola and Zimbabwe. Furthermore, the species is also documented within the Southern African region of Botswana. Collectively, these locations highlight the diverse habitats where Monsonia biflora thrives.
