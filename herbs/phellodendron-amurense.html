Phellodendron amurense

Phelloendron amurense, commonly known as Amur pheasantwood, is a medicinal shrub native to East Asian forests. Renowned in traditional medicine, its bark and leaves possess potent bioactive compounds. It is frequently utilized for its anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties, offering significant therapeutic potential in treating various skin and systemic ailments.

  • Scientific name: Phellodendron amurense
  • Family: Rutaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Sapindales and the family Rutaceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Phelloodendron.

Common Names

This plant, Phellodendron amurense, is known by various regional names across different languages and cultures. Additionally, in German-speaking contexts, it is called the Amur-Korkbauum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution that spans across distinct regions of Eurasia. In Southeastern Europe, its presence is documented within the territories of Bulgaria and Romania. Moving toward the Asian continent, the species is found extensively throughout the Russian Far East. Specifically, it inhabits the Amur and Khabarovsk regions of this vast area. Additionally, the plant can be located as far east as the Kuril Islands.
