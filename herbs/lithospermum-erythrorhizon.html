Lithospermum erythrorhizon

Lithospermum erythrorhizon, commonly known as Shikon, is a perennial herb prized in traditional medicine. Its roots contain unique naphthoquinones, which impart a deep red color. Renowned for their potent anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and wound-healing properties, these bioactive compounds make it a vital ingredient in various dermatological and restorative treatments.

  • Scientific name: Lithospermum erythrorhizon
  • Family: Boraginaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae in the order Boraginales and the family Boraginaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Lithospermum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions in Northern and Eastern Asia. In the vast expanse of Siberia, it can be found specifically in the areas of Chita and Yakutiya. Moving toward the Russian Far East, the species is also established in the Amur and Primorye regions. Its range extends significantly further south into the territory of China, particularly within the China South-Central region. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive across diverse continental landscapes.
