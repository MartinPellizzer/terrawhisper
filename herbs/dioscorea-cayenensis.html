Physiology

The physiology of Dioscorea cayennensis is characterized by its specialized metabolic processes designed for survival in tropical environments, primarily centered on its robust tuberous root system which functions as a major sink for starch accumulation and nutrient storage. The plant exhibits complex carbohydrate metabolism, converting photosynthates produced in its leaves through photosynthesis into complex polysaccharides within the tubers to support dormancy and subsequent regrowth. Regarding its interaction with soil nutrients, the plant does not possess the physiological mechanisms required for symbiotic nitrogen fixation; therefore, it is not a nitrogen fixer, as it lacks the specialized root nodules and the biological partnership with diazotrophic bacteria necessary to convert atmospheric nitrogen into a usable form. Instead, its nitrogen uptake is entirely dependent on the absorption of nitrates and ammonium ions present in the soil solution through its root hair system. The plant's water relations and transpiration rates are regulated by stomatal movements that balance gas exchange for carbon fixation against the risk of desiccation, a critical physiological adaptation for maintaining turgor pressure in its sprawling vine structure.
