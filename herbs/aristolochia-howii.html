What's the taxonomical classification of Aristolochia howii?

Aristolochia howii belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this group, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant further follows the taxonomic hierarchy through the order Piperales and the family Aristolochiaceae. Finally, it is situated within the genus Aristolochia, specifically identifying as the species howii.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains aristolochic acids, which are the primary bioactive compounds found within its roots and stems. These nitrogen-containing compounds are known for their potent pharmacological properties but are also highly toxic to human cells. Researchers have identified that these acids can cause significant damage to the kidneys and are linked to the development of urothelial cancers. Beyond these toxic elements, the plant may also host various alkaloids and flavonoids that contribute to its traditional medicinal uses. Understanding the chemical makeup of these compounds is crucial for both botanical study and medical safety.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe nephrototoxicity, carcinogenicity, and uropathy, which lead to permanent kidney damage, cancer, and urinary tract destruction. Consumption of the plant is also frequently associated with acute gastrointestinal distress including nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and diarrhea. These toxic effects are primarily attributed to aristolochic acids present within the tissues. The damage to the renal system can be irreversible and life-threatening for individuals exposed to it. Consequently, the plant is recognized as a significant health hazard due to these systemic complications.

  • Uropathy (urinary tract damage)
