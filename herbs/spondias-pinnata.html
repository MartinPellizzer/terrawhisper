Common hog-plum, scientifically known as Spondias pinnata, is a versatile tropical tree revered in traditional medicine. Its fruit, bark, and leaves possess potent antioxidant, antimicrobial, and anti-inflammatory properties. Widely used in Ayurveda, this plant offers therapeutic benefits for digestive health, skin ailments, and managing various inflammatory conditions.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Spondias pinnata is characterized by the production of fleshy, indehiscent drupes that serve as the primary dispersal units. These fruits typically exhibit a mean length of 4.25 cm, ranging from a minimum of 3.5 cm to a maximum of 5 cm. As fleshy fruits, they are adapted for a zoochorous dispersal syndrome, relying on animals for movement and distribution. Within these drupes, the seeds are substantial, maintaining a consistent mass of 10 g (with both minimum and maximum values recorded at 10 g) and a uniform seed volume of 9900 mm³. Because the fruit is indehiscent, the seeds are contained within the fleshy tissue until the fruit is consumed or decays, facilitating the plant's reproductive cycle through biotic agents.
