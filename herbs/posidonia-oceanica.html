Posidonia oceanica

Neptune grass, mediterranean tapeweed

Posidonia oceanica, commonly known as Neptune grass, is a foundational seagrass species essential to Mediterranean ecosystems. While not a traditional medicinal herb, its role in stabilizing marine environments and providing oxygen supports coastal biodiversity. Researchers increasingly study its complex biochemical properties for potential pharmacological and environmental applications.

  • Scientific name: Posidonia oceanica
  • Family: Posidoniaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Alismatales. Finally, it is a member of the family Posidoniaceae, falling under the genus Posidonia.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found within the coastal waters of Southwestern Europe. Its distribution includes the scenic islands of the Baleares and Sardegna. It also extends to the Mediterranean shores of Corse and France. Furthermore, the species is widely recorded along the coastlines of Spain. These specific locations define its primary ecological range in the region.

Plant Parts

This plant, Posidonia oceanica, is a seagrass species that thrives in the Mediterranean Sea, and its structural foundation relies heavily on its underground rhizome. This creeping, horizontal stem acts as the primary anchoring system, extending through the seabed to create vast, stable meadows. Through this rhizome, the plant can spread vegetatively, allowing it to colonize large areas and store essential nutrients required for survival in marine environments.
