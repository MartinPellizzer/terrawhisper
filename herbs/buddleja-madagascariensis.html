Buddleja madagascariensis

Buddleja madagascariensis, commonly known as the Madagascar butterfly bush, is a versatile medicinal shrub native to Madagascar. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, this plant is traditionally utilized in various cultures to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to digestive issues, offering significant potential for modern pharmacological research and development.

  • Scientific name: Buddleja madagascariensis
  • Family: Scrophulariaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Buddleja madagascariensis, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, falling within the order Lamiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Scrophulariaceae and the genus Buddleja.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution that spans across multiple continents. In Southeastern Europe, its presence can be observed in Greece and Sicilia. Moving toward Northern Africa, the species is also found within the borders of Tunisia. Its range extends further into West-Central Tropical Africa, where it inhabits Burundi. Finally, the plant is documented to grow within the region of Rwanda.

Plant Parts

This plant, Buddleja madagascariensis, possesses distinct morphological features, most notably its large, lanceolate leaves which play a significant role in its biological functions. These leaves are typically arranged oppositely along the stems and are characterized by their soft, pubescent texture, often feeling velvety to the touch due to a fine covering of hairs. In traditional medicinal practices, the leaves are the primary part utilized, as they are frequently harvested and prepared to harness the plant's various therapeutic properties.
