Silver oak

Silver oak, scientifically known as Grevillea robusta, is a magnificent evergreen tree native to Australia. Renowned for its rapid growth and striking fern-like foliage, it serves both ornamental and medicinal purposes. Often used in agroforestry, its extracts show potential in traditional healing for their various therapeutic properties and benefits.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Proteales, it is classified under the family Proteaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Grevillea.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several distinct regions. In Southeastern Europe, its presence can be noted within the country of Italy. It also inhabits parts of Macaronesia, specifically the Canary Islands and Cape Verde. Moving toward the African continent, it is found in West Tropical Africa, notably in Gambia. Finally, its range extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, where it is located in Cameroon.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Silky Oak, utilizes its specialized foliage for various biological and medicinal purposes. The leaves are large, deeply lobed, and fern-like in appearance, providing a significant surface area for photosynthesis. Each individual leaf is characterized by its distinctive serrated edges and fine hairs, which contribute to the plant's overall texture and help it thrive in diverse environments.
