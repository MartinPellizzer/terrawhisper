What's the taxonomical classification of Salix tianschanica?

Salix tianschanica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Moving through its hierarchical lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, the plant is categorized under the family Salicaceae. Finally, it is defined by the genus Salix and is specifically identified as the species tianschanica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Salix tianschanica has lanceolate to narrowly elliptic leaves that measure approximately 3 to 10 centimeters in length, featuring finely serrated margins and a distinctively pale, glaucous underside. The stems are typically woody and can vary in color from greyish-brown to reddish tones depending on the age of the branch. Its catkins appear as cylindrical structures that emerge from short lateral shoots during the flowering season. The plant often displays a spreading or shrubby growth habit with flexible, elongated branches. Small, inconspicuous flowers are borne on these catkins, characteristic of the Salicaceae family.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

The species typically inhabits rocky slopes, stream banks, and moist mountain meadows at elevations ranging from moderate to high altitudes.
