Cornus wilsoniana

Ghost dogwood

Cornus wilsoniana, commonly known as Wilson's Dogwood, is a striking ornamental shrub prized for its exquisite, creamy-white bracts. While primarily grown for its aesthetic elegance in temperate gardens, its lush foliage and structural beauty provide significant value in landscape design, offering a serene, naturalistic atmosphere to any botanical collection.

  • Scientific name: Cornus wilsoniana
  • Family: Cornaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Cornus wilsoniana, is referred to by several different common names depending on the language and region. In English, it is most frequently called ghost dogwood or Wilson's dogwood. In Chinese, the names vary by script, being identified as 光皮梾木 in both general usage (zh) and in mainland China (zh-cn), while in Taiwan (zh-tw), it is specifically written as 光皮棶木.
