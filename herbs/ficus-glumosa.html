Ficus glumosa · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (11 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Hairy Rock Fig, scientifically known as Ficus glumosa, is a resilient, creeping shrub native to tropical Africa. Often found clinging to rocky outcrops, this versatile plant is highly valued in traditional medicine. It is frequently utilized to treat various ailments, including skin conditions, digestive issues, and inflammatory ailments.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the diverse landscapes of West Tropical Africa. Its presence is documented within the borders of Benin, where it plays a role in the local ecosystem. It can also be found growing throughout the territory of Burkina. Furthermore, the species extends its range into the coastal and inland regions of Gambia. Finally, its geographical footprint includes the nation of Ghana and the country of Guinea-Bissau.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Ficus glumosa is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, primarily mediated by insects. The plant produces fleshy fruits, which facilitate a zoochorous dispersal syndrome, relying on animals to aid in the movement of its seeds.
