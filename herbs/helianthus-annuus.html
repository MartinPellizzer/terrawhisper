Helianthus annuus

Common sunflower

Helianthus annuus, commonly known as the common sunflower, is a versatile annual plant celebrated for its vibrant yellow blooms. Beyond its ornamental beauty, it holds significant medicinal value. Its seeds and oil are rich in antioxidants, vitamins, and essential fatty acids, offering therapeutic benefits for skin and heart health.

  • Scientific name: Helianthus annuus
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales, it is further categorized into the family Asteraceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Helianthus, which includes the species Helianthus annuus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Helianthus annuus, is referred to by various names depending on the language and region. In English, it is widely known as the common sunflower, though in both Canada (en-ca) and the United Kingdom (en-gb), it is simply called the sunflower. In the Anglonese language (ang), it is called Sōlate, while in Arabic (ar), it is known as دوار الشمس.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the sunflower, offers various components that are studied for their biological properties. The leaves serve as the primary site for photosynthesis, while the young leaves are often noted for their tender texture during early growth stages. A single leaf can become quite large and expansive as the plant matures. The reproductive center is characterized by the flower disc, which contains numerous tiny individual florets arranged in a dense pattern.
