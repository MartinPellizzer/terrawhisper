Tamanu

Calophyllum inophyllum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (77 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Tamanu, scientifically known as Calophyllum inophyllum, is a versatile tropical tree revered for its therapeutic properties. Primarily valued for its nutrient-rich oil extracted from pressed seeds, it offers potent anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and skin-healing benefits. Traditionally used to treat burns and scars, this botanical treasure remains a cornerstone of natural skincare.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malpighiales, it is positioned in the family Calophyllaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Calophyllum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern primarily concentrated within the tropical regions of Africa. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in Gambia, Guinea, and Ivory Coast. The species also extends into the West-Central Tropical Africa zone. Within this central region, it can be found growing in Cameroon and Congo. Consequently, its geographical range spans across several diverse nations within the western and central tropical belts.

Plant Parts

This plant, Calophyllum inophyllum, possesses various components that are utilized for their medicinal and practical properties. The glossy, evergreen leaves are frequently used in traditional remedies, while the sturdy stems and smaller twigs serve as the structural foundation of the tree. The thick, protective bark is often harvested for its therapeutic extracts, and perhaps most notably, the seeds yield a valuable nut oil, which is often supplemented by the extraction of nutrient-rich resin for topical applications.
