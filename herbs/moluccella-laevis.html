Moluccella laevis

Molucella laevis

Moluccella laevis, commonly known as the Bladder Campion, is a distinctive herbaceous plant prized for its unique, translucent, bell-shaped calyxes. While primarily ornamental in modern gardens, it holds traditional significance in herbal lore. This resilient species thrives in well-drained soils, adding structural elegance to diverse botanical collections worldwide.

  • Scientific name: Moluccella laevis
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is situated in the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Moluccella.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Moluccella laeviis, is referred to by various names depending on the region and language. In Poland, it is commonly called dzwonki irlandzkie, while in Finland, it is known as kotilokukka. In Russia, the name used is Молюцелла гладкая, and in Arabic-speaking regions, it is referred to as ذبيبينة ناعمة. Additionally, in Sweden, the plant is known as musselsyska.
