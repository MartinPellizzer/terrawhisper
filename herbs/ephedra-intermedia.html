Ephedra intermedia

Ephedra intermedia, commonly known as Mormon tea, is a resilient shrub native to the arid regions of western North America. Renowned for its medicinal properties, it contains alkaloids like ephedrine. Historically utilized by Indigenous peoples, this plant serves as a significant botanical specimen in traditional herbalism and pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Ephedra intermedia
  • Family: Ephedraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Ephedra intermedia, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Gnetidae, it is classified under the order Ephedrales. Finally, it is a member of the family Ephedraceae and the genus Ephedra.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the vast landscapes of northern and central Asia. In the Russian region of Siberia, it can be found within the Altai mountains and throughout West Siberia. Its range extends significantly into Central Asia, where it inhabits various terrains. Specifically, the species is documented growing in the territories of Kazakhstan and Kirgizstan. Additionally, its geographical presence reaches as far south as Turkmenistan.
