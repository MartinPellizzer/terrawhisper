Laser archangelica

Laser archangelica, commonly known as laserwort, is a perennial herb valued for its aromatic rhizomes. Historically prized in traditional medicine, it possesses potent antimicrobial and digestive properties. Often used to treat gastrointestinal issues and inflammation, this botanical offers a unique blend of therapeutic potential and rich, spicy fragrance.

  • Scientific name: Laser archangelica
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Apiales, it is classified under the family Apiaceae. Its specific taxonomic designation is within the genus Laser.

Common Names

This plant, Laser archangelica, is known by various names across different languages and regions. In English, it is frequently referred to as garden lovage or alexanders. Moving into German, it is commonly called Liebstöckel, while in French, it is known as livèche. In many Romance languages, the names reflect its botanical roots, such as livèche in French or livèche de jardin. Additionally, in other European contexts, it may be called lovage or similar derivatives, highlighting its long history of medicinal and culinary use throughout the continent.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Laserarchangelica (often referred to as garden angelica), is highly valued in traditional herbalism, particularly for the therapeutic properties found within its seeds. These small, fragrant seeds contain concentrated essential oils and bioactive compounds that are frequently harvested and used to support digestive health and alleviate gastrointestinal discomfort. While the roots and stems are also used in various preparations, it is the potent chemical profile of the seeds that makes them a primary focus for medicinal applications.
