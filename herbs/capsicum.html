Capsicum

Pepper

Capsicum, commonly known as chili peppers, is a versatile genus celebrated for its pungent heat and nutritional richness. Beyond culinary uses, these plants offer significant medicinal value, containing capsaicin, which possesses potent anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antioxidant properties. They are widely utilized in topical treatments to relieve various types of pain.

  • Scientific name: Capsicum
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, specifically within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Solanales and the family Solanaceae. Ultimately, the medicinal plant is identified by the genus Capsicum.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Capsicum, is referred to by various common names across different languages and cultures. In English, it is often called Pimento (an), while in Arabic, it is known as فليفلة (ar). In Azerbaijani, the name used is Bibər (az), whereas in Catalan, it is called Pebrotera (ca), and in Czech, it is commonly known as paprika (cs).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse presence across several European regions. In Northern Europe, it can be found growing in Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward the center of the continent, its range extends into Middle Europe. Specifically, it is distributed throughout Austria and the region of Czechia-Slovakia. Finally, its geographical footprint includes the country of Germany.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Capsicum, is highly valued for its medicinal properties, which are primarily concentrated within its specialized structures. The most significant part used is the fruit, though various species produce different types of fruits ranging from sweet to intensely spicy. The characteristic heat of these pepper fruits is derived from specific pungent ingredients, most notably capsaicin, which serves as a powerful anti-inflammatory agent. Interestingly, the highest concentration of these active compounds is often found within the placenta tissues, which are the internal structures connected to the seeds that deliver the intense spicy sensation.
