Oxalis corniculata

What's the taxonomical classification of Oxalis corniculata?

Oxalis corniculata belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is situated in the subclass Magnoliidae and follows the order Oxalidales. This plant is a member of the family Oxalidaceae, falling under the genus Oxalis, with its specific designation being the species corniculata.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Oxalis corniculata has trifoliate leaves composed of three heart-shaped leaflets, small five-petaled flowers that range from white to pale pink, and slender, erect stems arising from underground rhizomes. The foliage often displays a delicate texture with leaflets that fold inward during the night or under stress. Its inflorescence typically consists of solitary flowers or small clusters situated atop long pedicels. The reproductive structures include a central ovary surrounded by five stamens and five petals that protect the internal nectar. These morphological features allow the plant to thrive in shaded, moist environments where it spreads via its creeping root systems.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant thrives in well-draining, humus-rich soil and requires consistent moisture alongside partial shade to prevent leaf scorch.

Gardeners should maintain a temperature range between 60 and 75 degrees Fahrenheit for optimal growth. Propagation is most effectively achieved by dividing the underground bulbils during the early spring or autumn months. Regular weeding and occasional fertilization will help maintain its dense, creeping groundcover habit.

Careful monitoring for moisture levels is essential to prevent the delicate foliage from wilting in intense heat.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Oxalis corniculata is utilized medicinally through the use of its aerial parts, which include the leaves and the whole plant. The entire above-ground structure is often prepared as a decoction or infusion to treat various ailments. Specifically, the leaves are frequently harvested to extract bioactive compounds for therapeutic purposes. These plant components are valued in traditional medicine for their potential healing properties. Utilizing the whole aerial portion ensures a complete profile of the plant's available nutrients and chemical constituents.

According to a study published by "Galen medical journal", the aerial parts of Oxalis corniculata were collected in Lahijan city, Iran, and extracted with ethanol for 24 h. The total phenolic content of this extract was 31.30±02 μg of gallic acid equivalents/mg of dry extract, while the total flavonoid content was 49.61±04 μg of quercetin as equivalents/mg of extract. Antioxidant activity was measured at the dose of 619.2 μg/μl, which decreases cancer cell viability and enhances apoptosis. In MCF-7 cells treated with the extract, real-time PCR revealed substantial increases in p53 (P<0.001), CD95 (P<0.05), and bcl-2 (P<0.05) expression. These findings suggest that Oxalis corniculata may cause apoptosis by oxidative stress in cancer cells.

    What side effects this plant can have?

    This plant can cause kidney stones, electrolyte imbalance, nausea, and abdominal pain due to its high concentration of oxalic acid. When consumed in large quantities, the acid can bind with calcium in the body to form calcium oxalate crystals. These crystals are a primary component of kidney stones and can lead to significant physical distress. The resulting disruption of mineral levels may also trigger a dangerous electrolyte imbalance. Individuals experiencing these issues often report acute symptoms such as nausea and sharp abdominal pain.
