Cassia nodosa

What's the taxonomical classification of Cassia nodosa?

Cassia nodosa belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant follows the order Fabales and is a member of the family Fabaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Cassia and the species nodosa.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Cassia nodosa has pinnately compound leaves with numerous small, oblong leaflets and bright yellow, five-petaled flowers that grow in large terminal racemes. The tree typically reaches heights of up to 15 meters, characterized by a spreading crown and a relatively short trunk. Its bark is generally smooth and light gray, often becoming slightly fissured as the specimen matures. The fruit consists of long, cylindrical, woody pods that hang from the branches and turn dark brown upon ripening. These pods eventually dehisce to release small, dark seeds into the surrounding environment.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical regions of Central and South America, spanning from southern Mexico through the Caribbean and down to Brazil. It thrives primarily in moist, lowland environments such as rainforests and gallery forests. Within these habitats, the species is often found along riverbanks and in areas with high humidity. Its distribution is closely tied to the presence of warm, frost-free climates typical of the Neotropical realm. Because it prefers subtropical and tropical moisture, it rarely extends into more arid or temperate latitudes.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant Cassia nodosa thrives in tropical and subtropical climates when grown in well-drained, sandy or loamy soils with full sun exposure.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Cassia nodosa provides medicinal benefits through the use of its leaves, bark, and seeds. The leaves are frequently utilized in traditional remedies to treat skin conditions and inflammatory ailments. Additionally, the bark contains compounds that can assist in digestive health and fever reduction. The seeds are also harvested for their specific therapeutic properties in various herbal preparations. These diverse components make the entire plant a valuable resource in ethnobotanical practices.

According to a study published by "Journal of Asian natural products research", a new chromone named as 5,4'-dihydroxy-7-methyl 3-benzyl chromone (1) was isolated from the leaves of Cassia nodosa. Three known flavonoid compounds, including unsubstituted flavone, kaempferol-3-o-rhamnoside, and quercetin-3-o-arabinoside, were also identified from these leaves. The structures of these components were established through chemical and spectral evidences such as IR, UV, 1H-NMR, 13C-NMR, and mass spectra.
