Life history

The life history of Calendula officinalis is characterized by its status as an annual plant, completing its entire reproductive cycle within a single growing season. Functioning as a therophyte, the species survives through seeds, avoiding the need to maintain perennial above-ground structures during unfavorable conditions. As a deciduous species, it sheds its foliage as it reaches the end of its seasonal cycle, focusing its energy on seed production before the plant dies back. This therophytic life form allows it to thrive in disturbed or open habitats where it can rapidly establish itself from seed, grow, bloom, and disperse its progeny before the onset of dormancy or seasonal changes.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Calendula officinalis is primarily a sexual process, characterized by bisexual flowers that facilitate genetic diversity. The flowering period is quite extensive, typically beginning in January and continuing through September, though it can be variable depending on environmental conditions. These vibrant orange flowers vary in size, with a length ranging from 1.5 to 2.5 cm and a width between 4 and 5.5 cm. The pollination syndrome is biotic, specifically relying on insects to facilitate the transfer of pollen, as self-fertilization is notably absent. Following successful pollination, the plant produces fruit in the form of an achene. These fruits are indehiscent and possess a mean length of approximately 0.6 cm. Each fruit is prolific, containing a high seed count ranging from 101 to 1000 seeds. The individual seeds exhibit a mean mass of approximately 0.0044 g, with values ranging from a minimum of 0.0022 g to a maximum of 0.01632 g. Dispersal of the seeds occurs through multiple mechanisms, including anemochorous (wind) and anthropochorous (human-mediated) syndromes. Asexual reproduction is absent in this species.
