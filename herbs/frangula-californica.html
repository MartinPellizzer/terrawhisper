In this C3 process, carbon dioxide is fixed directly from the atmosphere into a three-carbon compound via the enzyme RuBisCO during the Calvin cycle. This pathway involves the standard light-dependent reactions to generate ATP and NADPH, which subsequently drive the light-independent reactions within the mesophyll cells. Because it lacks the specialized adaptations of C4 or CAM pathways, such as spatial separation of CO2 fixation or nocturnal acid accumulation, the plant's gas exchange and water-use efficiency are closely linked to its stomatal conductance and the environmental availability of moisture.
