Mosla chinensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Mosla chinensis?

Mosla chinensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is further organized under the order Lamiales. It falls within the family Lamiaceae, which defines its closer botanical relatives. Ultimately, the plant is identified by its specific genus, Mosla, and its unique species designation, chinensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Mosla chinensis has a woody, perennial structure characterized by upright stems, lanceolate leaves with serrated margins, and small, inconspicuous white flowers arranged in axillary clusters. The root system is fibrous and well-developed to ensure stability in its native soil. These morphological traits collectively allow the plant to thrive in varied subtropical environments.

According to a study published by "Chinese journal of natural medicines", the aerial parts of Mosla chinensis Maxim. and Mosla chinensis cv. 'Jiangxiangru' (MCJ) are widely utilized in traditional Chinese medicine. These parts are known collectively as Xiang-ru. The study identified 203 compounds through detailed biosynthetic and chemometric analyses. Among these, 5 phenolic compounds were pinpointed as reliable chemical markers, which include salviaflaside, cynaroside, scutellarein-7-O-D-glucoside, rutin, and vicenin-2.

What are the pharmacological activities of Mosla chinensis?

This plant has antibacterial, antioxidant, and spleen strengthening pharmacological activities. The antibacterial properties allow it to combat various pathogenic microorganisms effectively. Its antioxidant capacity helps neutralize free radicals and reduce oxidative stress within the body. Furthermore, the plant is traditionally used for its ability to support spleen strengthening and improve digestive health. These combined medicinal effects make Mosla chinensis a valuable resource in natural therapeutics.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Mosla chinensis exhibited considerable antibacterial activity against both serotypes of Streptococcus mutans. The minimal inhibitory concentration for this plant was measured at 7.8-23.4 mg/ml. This activity was demonstrated against both serotypes of the cariogenic bacterium. The study included a total of 79 aqueous extracts in its preliminary antibacterial screening.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is Mosla chinensis, which is processed into medicinal herbal preparations such as essential oils and aqueous extracts to utilize its therapeutic properties. The essential oil is obtained through steam distillation to concentrate the plant's volatile aromatic compounds for topical or inhalation use. Aqueous extracts are created by soaking the plant material in water to pull out water-soluble bioactive constituents. These different preparation methods allow practitioners to target specific health concerns depending on the desired potency and delivery method. Both forms serve as foundational components in various traditional medicinal applications.

According to a study published by "Molecules (Basel, Switzerland)", the essential oils of Mosla chinensis contain major components such as carvacrol, thymol, o-cymene, and terpinene. To address low water solubility and volatility, an inclusion complex was formed using the ultrasonication method. Through a two-step optimization process involving a Plackett-Burman design and a central composite design, specific results were achieved. The optimal encapsulation efficiency for the Mosla chinensis essential oil in the inclusion complexes was 86.17%. Furthermore, the loading capacity of the essential oil was determined to be 8.92%. Molecular docking confirmed that the component carvacrol could be encapsulated via noncovalent interactions.
