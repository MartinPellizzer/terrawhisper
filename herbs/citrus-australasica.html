Australian finger lime, scientifically known as Citrus australasicia, is a unique citrus species native to subtropical rainforests. Renowned for its distinctive pearl-like juice vesicles, it offers an intense, acidic flavor profile. Beyond its culinary appeal, this "citrus caviar" is celebrated for its high antioxidant content and medicinal properties.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Citrus australasica is characterized by the development of a fleshy fruit classified as a berry, which serves as the primary vehicle for seed dispersal. This fruit type is notably indehiscent, meaning it does not split open at maturity to release its contents; instead, the seeds remain enclosed within the juice-filled pulp of the fruit until the outer layers decay or are consumed by animals. This reproductive strategy relies on the protection of the seeds within the moist, acidic interior of the berry, ensuring they are distributed through biotic vectors that consume the fruit.
