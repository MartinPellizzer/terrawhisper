Plectranthus ecklonii

Plectranthus ecklonii, commonly known as the "madoqua" or "scented bush," is a resilient medicinal herb native to Southern Africa. Renowned for its aromatic, succulent leaves, this plant is traditionally used to treat various ailments, including digestive issues and skin irritations, offering significant therapeutic potential in ethnomedicine.

  • Scientific name: Plectranthus ecklonii
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic designation is placed within the genus Plectranthus.

Distribution

This plant is primarily native to the various regions of Southern Africa. Within this continent, its natural range includes the Cape Provinces, KwaZulu-Natal, and the Northern Provinces. Beyond its African origins, it has established a presence in the Middle Atlantic Ocean on the island of St. Helena. Additionally, the species can be found growing in New Zealand North.
