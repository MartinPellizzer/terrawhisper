Momordica charantia subsp. charantia

Momordica charantia subsp. charantia, commonly known as bitter melon or bitter gourd, is a versatile medicinal plant celebrated in traditional healing. Renowned for its unique bitter flavor, this vine offers significant therapeutic potential, particularly in managing blood sugar levels, enhancing digestion, and providing potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties.

  • Scientific name: Momordica charantia subsp. charantia
  • Family: Cucurbitaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further organized into the order Cucurbitales and the family Cucurbitaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Momordica, specifically as the subspecies charantia.

Common Names

This plant, Momordica charantia subsp. charantia, is recognized globally through a variety of regional common names. In English, it is most frequently referred to as bitter melon or bitter gourd, reflecting its distinctively sharp flavor profile. In Chinese, it is known as ku gua, while in Hindi, it is commonly called karela. It is also widely identified by the name balsam pear in several English-speaking regions, and in many parts of Southeast Asia, it is referred to as pare or paxu.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution that spans several regions across the Atlantic and African continents. In the Macaronesian region, its presence is documented in the Cape Verde islands. Moving to the mainland, it is widely distributed throughout West Tropical Africa. Specific records confirm its occurrence in nations such as Benin, Burkina, and the Gambia. Furthermore, the species is also established within the borders of Ghana.
