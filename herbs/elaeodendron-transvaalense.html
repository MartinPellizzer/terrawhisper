Elaeodendron transvaalense

Ingwavuma

Elaeodenro transvaalense, commonly known as the Transvaal Alder, is a significant medicinal shrub native to Southern Africa. Renowned for its diverse pharmacological properties, various parts of the plant are traditionally utilized to treat ailments ranging from digestive issues to skin inflammations, offering valuable insights into ethnobotanical healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Elaeodendron transvaalense
  • Family: Celastraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further categorized under the order Celastrales and the family Celastraceae. Finally, it is placed in the genus Elaeodendroon.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Elaeodendroon transvaalense, is recognized by various common names depending on the language and region. In Afrikaans, it is referred to as the Bosveldsaffraan, while in Tsonga, it is called Ximapana. In English, it is commonly known as Bushveld Saffron, and it is also identified by the names Elaeodendroon transvaalensis and Ingwavuma in other contexts.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the diverse landscapes of South Tropical Africa. Its presence can be found within the borders of Angola and Mozambique. Additionally, the species thrives in the territories of Zambia and Zimbabwe. It also extends its range into the southern reaches of the continent within Botswana. Together, these regions form the core geographical footprint of the species.
