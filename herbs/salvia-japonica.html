Life history

The life history of Salvia japonica is characterized by its classification as a therophyte, meaning it completes its entire life cycle within a single growing season. As a therophyte, the plant functions primarily through the production of seeds that remain dormant during unfavorable environmental conditions, allowing the species to persist through periods of drought or cold. This life strategy is reflected in its specific botanical categorization as both a nanophanerophyte and a therophyte, indicating a small stature where the aerial parts are relatively short but the reproductive effort is concentrated on rapid growth and seed dispersal. By prioritizing a fast-paced developmental cycle, Salvia japonica effectively exploits seasonal resource availability to ensure the next generation is established before the onset of senescence.
