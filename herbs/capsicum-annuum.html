Pepper, scientifically known as Capsicum annuum, is a versatile plant renowned for its culinary heat and medicinal properties. Rich in vitamins A and C, it contains capsaicin, a powerful compound valued for its analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and metabolic-boosting effects. This essential species remains a cornerstone of global herbal medicine.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Capsicum annuum is primarily a sexual process, as asexual reproduction is notably absent. The flowering cycle typically commences in June and continues through September. The plant utilizes biotic pollination syndromes, specifically relying on insects to facilitate the transfer of pollen. While pollination is insect-mediated, self-fertilization is present, ensuring seed set. The resulting fruit is classified as a fleshy berry that is indehiscent. These berries reach an average length of 10 cm and an average width of 1.5 cm. Contained within the fruit are seeds with highly consistent dimensions, characterized by a mean length of 3 mm, a mean width of 3 mm, and a mean volume of 2.5 mm³ (with both minimum and maximum volume values also recorded at 2.5 mm³). The seed mass varies slightly, with a mean mass of 0.004335 g, ranging from a minimum of 0.00285 g to a maximum of 0.00787 g.
