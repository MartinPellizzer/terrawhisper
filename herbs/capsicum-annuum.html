Pepper, scientifically known as Capsicum annuum, is a versatile plant renowned for its culinary heat and medicinal properties. Rich in vitamins A and C, it contains capsaicin, a powerful compound valued for its analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and metabolic-boosting effects. This essential species remains a cornerstone of global herbal medicine.

Plant Parts

This plant, Capsicum annuum, is a versatile medicinal species characterized by several distinct anatomical structures. The most commonly utilized part is the fleshy fruit, which is often referred to in the plural as fruits when discussing various cultivars like peppers or chilies. Within each fruit, the tiny, teardrop-shaped seeds contain concentrated amounts of capsaicin, a compound known for its analgesic properties. To support the plant's growth and nutrient uptake, a sturdy system of roots anchors it into the soil, while the herbaceous stems provide the necessary framework to transport water and minerals throughout the organism.
