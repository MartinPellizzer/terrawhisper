The physiology of Pelargonium odoratissimum is characterized by a robust metabolic framework designed to support its aromatic and succulent growth patterns. As a perennial herb, its physiological processes are heavily centered on the biosynthesis of volatile essential oils, primarily within specialized glandular trichomes located on the leaf surfaces. These trichomes facilitate the production and storage of complex terpenoids, which serve as both chemical defenses against herbivores and metabolic byproducts of secondary pathways. The plant exhibits efficient photosynthetic regulation to manage water loss in varied environments, utilizing a specialized stomatal control mechanism to balance gas exchange with transpiration.
