Parkia biglobosa

Parkia biglobosa, commonly known as the African locust bean tree, is a vital multipurpose plant across tropical Africa. Renowned for its nutritious seeds used in fermentation, it also holds significant medicinal value. It is widely utilized in traditional medicine to treat hypertension, fever, and various inflammatory conditions.

  • Scientific name: Parkia biglobosa
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fabales, it is classified under the family Fabaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Parkia, specifically the species Parkia biglobosa.

Distribution

This plant, Parkia biglobosa, exhibits a specific distribution pattern within the West Tropical Africa region. It can be found growing in the diverse landscapes of Benin. The species is also widely distributed across the territory of Burkina. Furthermore, its presence is documented throughout the nation of Gambia. Finally, the plant is also established within the borders of Ghana and Guinea-Bissau.

Plant Parts

This plant, Parkia biglobosa, is a versatile medicinal resource where various components offer unique therapeutic benefits. The leaves are frequently utilized in traditional medicine for their cooling properties and are often prepared as decoctions to treat various ailments. The stem bark and the inner bark are also highly valued, often being processed to manage inflammation or used in topical applications. Furthermore, the seeds, which are central to the plant's nutritional and medicinal profile, are often fermented to create condiments that carry health-promoting properties. Even the hard shell surrounding the pods plays a role in the plant's ecosystem, though its primary medicinal utility is often linked to the processing and protection of the valuable seeds within.
