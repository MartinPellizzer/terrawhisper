Hibiscus mutabilis

Confederate rose

Hibiscus mutabilis, commonly known as the Confederate Rose, is a captivating shrub celebrated for its color-changing blooms. Beyond its ornamental beauty, this tropical plant holds significant value in traditional medicine. It is frequently utilized for its therapeutic properties, offering potential benefits for skin health, digestion, and various inflammatory conditions.

  • Scientific name: Hibiscus mutabilis
  • Family: Malvaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Malvales and the family Malvaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its membership in the genus Hibiscus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide range of distribution across several distinct regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence can be noted in Gambia. Moving into West-Central Tropical Africa, it is found in Cameroon as well as throughout the Gulf of Guinea Islands. Its reach extends further south to South Tropical Africa, specifically within Zimbabwe. Finally, the species is also documented in the Northern Provinces of Southern Africa.
