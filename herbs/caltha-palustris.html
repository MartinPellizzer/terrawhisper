Caltha palustris

Marsh marigold

Caltha palustris, commonly known as Marsh Marigold, is a perennial herb flourishing in damp, marshy habitats. Renowned for its vibrant, butter-yellow blooms, it serves as an early spring herald. Historically utilized in traditional medicine, this resilient plant holds significant botanical interest for its ecological role and unique medicinal properties.

  • Scientific name: Caltha palustris
  • Family: Ranunculaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Ranunculales. Finally, it is a member of the family Ranunculaceae within the genus Caltha.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across the northern regions of Europe. It can be found growing within the coastal and inland habitats of Denmark. Its range also extends to the northern territories of Finland and the islands of Iceland. Additionally, the species is present in the Faroe Islands, known locally as Føroyar. Finally, the plant is documented throughout the various landscapes of Great Britain.
