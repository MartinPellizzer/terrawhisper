Reproduction

The reproduction of Ziziphus lotus is characterized by the production of fleshy, indehiscent fruits classified as drupes. These fruits serve as the primary vessel for seed protection and distribution, typically measuring an average of 1.73 cm in length and 1.54 cm in width. The seeds contained within these drupes possess a mean mass of 0.352 g. Dispersal is facilitated through a zoochorous syndrome, meaning the plant relies on animals to aid in the movement of its seeds, likely due to the attractive nature of the fruit. Because the fruit is indehiscent, it does not split open at maturity to release its contents, instead requiring external biological agents to facilitate the release of the seeds during or after consumption.
