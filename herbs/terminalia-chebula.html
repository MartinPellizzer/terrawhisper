Terminalia chebula · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (864 studies) · ★★★★★

Chebulic myrobalan, scientifically known as Terminalia chebulla, is a cornerstone of Ayurvedic medicine. Renowned as the "king of medicines," this potent fruit offers diverse therapeutic benefits, including digestive support, antioxidant properties, and immune boosting. It is highly valued for its ability to balance bodily doshas and promote holistic wellness.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, specifically classified within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further categorized under the order Myrtales and the family Combretaceae. Within these groups, it is identified by the genus Terminalia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning several distinct regions across Asia and Africa. In the Indian Subcontinent, it is notably found within the area of Assam. Its presence extends into Eastern Asia, where it can be located in Taiwan. The species is also widely distributed throughout China, specifically across the South-Central and Southeast regions. Additionally, it grows in West-Central Tropical Africa, particularly within the DR Congo.

Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as Haritaki, utilizes various components for its extensive medicinal purposes. The most significant part is the fruit, which is often referred to in its dried form as the fruits used in traditional Ayurvedic formulations. Within this drupe, the seed is embedded, though it is typically discarded during processing. The medicinal properties are also found in the leaves, which are used for their various therapeutic qualities, as well as the stem, which can be utilized in specific decoctions.
