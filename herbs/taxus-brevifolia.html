Pacific yew, scientifically known as Taxus brevifolia, is a distinctive evergreen conifer native to the coastal regions of the Pacific Northwest. Renowned for its dense, dark foliage and small red arils, this resilient species holds significant ecological value and historical importance in traditional botanical studies and medicinal research.

Plant Parts

This plant, Taxus brevifolia, is characterized by several distinct morphological features used in botanical study. The most prominent feature is its foliage, which consists of dark green needles that grow in a spiral arrangement along the stems. Each individual needle is typically linear and relatively short compared to other yew species, functioning as the primary photosynthetic leaf structure. Beyond the greenery, the bark of the tree is generally smooth and greyish, providing a protective layer for the woody stem. Furthermore, while the fleshy red arils are often noted, the seed contained within is highly toxic and must be handled with extreme caution in medicinal or scientific contexts.
