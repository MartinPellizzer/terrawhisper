Taxus brevifolia

Pacific yew

Taxus brevifolia, commonly known as the shortleaf yew, is a resilient evergreen conifer native to the rugged landscapes of North America. Renowned for its dense, needle-like foliage and decorative potential, this species belongs to the Taxaceae family, carrying both botanical significance and complex phytochemical properties within its tissues.

  • Scientific name: Taxus brevifolia
  • Family: Taxaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Pinidae. Within the order Pinales, it is classified under the family Taxaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Taxus.

Moving south, it inhabits parts of Western Canada, specifically within the provinces of Alberta and British Columbia. Plant Parts

This plant, Taxus brevifolia, is characterized by several distinct morphological features used in botanical study. The most prominent feature is its foliage, which consists of dark green needles that grow in a spiral arrangement along the stems. Each individual needle is typically linear and relatively short compared to other yew species, functioning as the primary photosynthetic leaf structure. Beyond the greenery, the bark of the tree is generally smooth and greyish, providing a protective layer for the woody stem. Furthermore, while the fleshy red arils are often noted, the seed contained within is highly toxic and must be handled with extreme caution in medicinal or scientific contexts.
