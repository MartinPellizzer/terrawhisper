Sesamum indicum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (947 studies) · ★★★★★

Sesame plant, scientifically known as Sesamum indicum, is a versatile oilseed crop cherished for millennia. Renowned for its nutrient-dense seeds, it serves as a vital source of healthy fats, proteins, and antioxidants. Beyond nutrition, this medicinal powerhouse offers therapeutic benefits, supporting skin health and providing potent anti-inflammatory properties.

The reproduction of Sesamum indicum is characterized by a flowering period that typically begins in June and concludes in July. During this reproductive phase, the plant produces flowers capable of self-fertilization, which is present within the species. The resulting fruit is a dry capsule, measuring on average 2.5 cm in length, with a range spanning from a minimum of 2 cm to a maximum of 3 cm. These capsules are dehiscence-type fruits, meaning they split open to release their seeds. The seeds themselves are quite small, possessing a consistent volume of 2.2 mm³ (with both minimum and maximum values recorded at this level).
