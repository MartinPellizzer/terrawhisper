Chinese holly, scientifically known as Ilex cornuta, is a versatile evergreen shrub prized in traditional medicine and ornamental landscaping. Renowned for its glossy foliage and dark berries, it offers therapeutic potential in treating inflammation and various ailments, making it a significant botanical treasure in both cultural and medicinal practices.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several specific regions within East Asia. In China, its presence is noted in the South-Central and North-Central territories. It can also be found growing in the Southeast regions of China as well as on the island of Hainan. Beyond mainland China, the species extends its range into the neighboring country of Korea. Together, these locations define the primary geographical footprint of Ilex cornuta.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Ilex cornuta is characterized by the production of specialized fruit and seed structures designed for efficient propagation. The plant produces fruits classified as a drupe, which are indehiscent in nature, meaning they do not split open at maturity to release their contents. These fruits possess an average width of 0.98 cm. Within these fruits, the seeds exhibit a highly specific mass profile; the mean seed mass is recorded at 0.01846 g, with both the maximum and minimum recorded values reaching 0.02229 g, indicating a very tight range in seed size. The dispersal of these reproductive units follows a zoochorous syndrome, meaning the seeds and fruits are dispersed by animals, which facilitates the spread of the species across its habitat.
