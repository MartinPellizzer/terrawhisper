Myrcia tricona

Myrcia tricona, commonly known as a versatile medicinal shrub, holds significant value in traditional healing practices. Renowned for its bioactive compounds, this plant is frequently utilized for its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Exploring its botanical characteristics and therapeutic potential offers vital insights into natural pharmacology and ethnobotanical wellness.

  • Scientific name: Myrcia tricona
  • Family: Myrtaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Myrtales and the family Myrtaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Myrcia.

Common Names

This plant, Myrcia tricona, is known by various regional names depending on the linguistic context of its habitat. In English, it is commonly referred to as the tricona, while in Portuguese, it is known as tricona. In Spanish, the species is called tricona, and in the local vernacular of certain regions, it may also be identified as mirtáceas.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across parts of South America. In Brazil, it can be found occurring within the Southeast region. Additionally, its presence extends into the South of Brazil. Moving further south, the species is also located within the Northeast of Argentina. Therefore, its habitat is concentrated in these particular southern regions of the continent.
