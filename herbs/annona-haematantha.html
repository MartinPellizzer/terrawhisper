The ecology of Annona haematantha is specifically defined by its occurrence within the Floresta de Igapó, a specialized type of blackwater flooded forest ecosystem. In these environments, the species thrives under conditions of seasonal inundation, where the soil is frequently submerged by nutrient-poor, acidic waters. This particular habitat dictates a specialized adaptation to high moisture levels and low nutrient availability, shaping the plant's growth patterns and interaction with the surrounding aquatic-terrestrial transition zone. As a component of the Igapó flora, Annona haematantha plays a role in the structural complexity of these seasonally flooded forests, navigating the unique hydrological cycles that characterize these distinct tropical ecosystems.
