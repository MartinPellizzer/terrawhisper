Rubus lambertianus

What's the taxonomical classification of Rubus lambertianus?

Rubus lambertianus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this group, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving through its hierarchical classification, it falls under the order Rosales and the family Rosaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by its genus, Rubus, and its specific species designation, lambertianus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Rubus lambertianus has arching, woody stems known as canes that are densely covered in small, sharp prickles and fine hairs. These canes bear compound leaves that typically consist of three to five leaflets with serrated margins and deeply textured surfaces. The foliage often displays a slightly pubescent underside, adding to the plant's tactile complexity. During the flowering season, small white blossoms emerge from the leaf axils, eventually developing into aggregate fruits. These berries transition from green to a deep, glossy black when fully ripe and are edible.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, commonly known as the Lambert blackberry, requires well-drained soil and full sun to produce its characteristic dark, flavorful fruit.

Growers should plant these vigorous canes in areas where they have ample space to spread or can be trained along a sturdy trellis system. Regular pruning is necessary to remove old wood and encourage the growth of new, productive primocanes. What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant, Rubus lambertianus, is primarily utilized for its medicinal leaves which are harvested to prepare various healing remedies. These leaves contain specific phytochemicals that contribute to their therapeutic properties in traditional medicine. They are often dried and steeped into herbal teas to address digestive issues or reduce inflammation. Some practitioners also apply leaf extracts topically to soothe skin irritations. By utilizing these specific botanical components, people have long relied on the plant for its natural healing potential.

According to a study published by "Chemical & pharmaceuctical bulletin", chemical examination of the leaves of Rubus lambertianus Seringe has led to the isolation of four new ellagitannins. These isolated compounds include two dimers, known as lambertianins A (6) and B (7), one trimer identified as lambertianin C (8), and one tetramer identified as lambertianin D (10). All of these substances possess sanguisorbic acid ester group(s) serving as linking unit(s) between glucopyranose moieties. While HPLC analyses involved fifteen Rubus species from Japan and Taiwan, the specific characterization of these four compounds was derived from the leaves of Rubus lambertianus.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains high concentrations of ellagitannins, specifically sanguiin H-6, along with various phenolic acids and flavonoids like quercetin and kaempferol. What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause allergic skin reactions, gastrointestinal distress, and potential interference with blood clotting in sensitive individuals. Contact with the leaves or stems may trigger contact dermatitis, resulting in redness, itching, or a localized rash. If consumed in large quantities, some people report experiencing nausea, stomach cramps, or diarrhea. There is also limited evidence suggesting that the compounds within the plant might influence blood coagulation processes. It is important to exercise caution when handling or ingesting any part of this species to avoid these adverse reactions.
