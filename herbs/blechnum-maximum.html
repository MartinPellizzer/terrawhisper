Blechnum maximum

Blechnum maximum, commonly known as the hard fern, is a resilient pteridophyte native to various tropical and subtropical regions. Renowned for its lush, leathery fronds, this plant thrives in shaded, moist environments. Beyond its ornamental beauty, it holds significant ecological value and traditional medicinal importance in many indigenous cultures.

  • Scientific name: Blechnum maximum
  • Family: Aspleniaceae
  • Native range: Australasia

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further organized into the subclass Polypodiinae and the order Polypodiales, falling under the family Aspleniaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Blechnum.

Common Names

This plant, Blechnum maximum, is known by various regional names depending on the linguistic context. In English, it is commonly referred to as the giant fern, while in Japanese, it is known as ebin. In Hawaiian, the plant is called ama'u.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found within the specific regions of Australia. Its geographical range includes the coastal and inland areas of New South Wales. Additionally, the species can be located throughout parts of Queensland. These two states represent the core locations where the plant naturally occurs.
