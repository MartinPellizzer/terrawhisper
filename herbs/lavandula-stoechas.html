French lavender

Lavandula stoechas · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Moderate (124 studies) · ★★★☆☆

French lavender, scientifically known as Lavandula stoechas, is a distinctive Mediterranean shrub celebrated for its striking, rabbit-ear bracts. Renowned for its aromatic properties, this hardy perennial offers therapeutic benefits, including calming anxiety and soothing skin irritations, making it a cherished staple in traditional herbal medicine and aromatherapy.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the Mediterranean regions of Southwestern Europe. It can be found growing naturally in the Balearic Islands, known as Baleares. Its presence extends to the island of Corsica and the coastal areas of France. Additionally, the species is widely distributed throughout Portugal. Finally, it is commonly located on the island of Sardegna.

Plant Parts

This plant, Lavandula stoechas, is highly valued for its therapeutic properties, which are primarily concentrated in its aerial parts. The most visually striking and medicinally significant component is the flower part, which features distinctive bracts and fragrant blooms. Within these structures, the plant synthesizes concentrated essential oils (EOs) that provide its signature calming aroma. When harvested and processed, the essential oil extracted from the plant is used extensively in aromatherapy and topical applications, as the quality of these essential oils is a key indicator of the plant's overall medicinal potency.
