Bitter aloe

Aloe ferox · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (69 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Bitter aloe, scientifically known as Aloe ferox, is a powerful medicinal succulent native to South Africa. Renowned for its intense bitterness, the plant's sap is traditionally harvested for its potent laxative properties. Beyond digestion, it is widely utilized in herbal medicine for its antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory therapeutic benefits.

Physiology

The physiology of Aloe ferox is characterized by specialized metabolic adaptations designed for survival in arid environments, most notably its use of the Crassulacean Acid Metabolism (CAM) photosynthetic pathway. By utilizing this CAM mechanism, the plant can fix carbon dioxide at night when temperatures are lower and humidity is higher, storing it as organic acids to be utilized for photosynthesis during the day; this process significantly minimizes transpirational water loss through nocturnal stomatal opening. Furthermore, regarding its nutrient acquisition strategies, Aloe ferox is not a nitrogen fixer, meaning it lacks the symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms, relying instead on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium from the soil through its root system.
