Bitter aloe

Aloe ferox · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (69 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Bitter aloe, scientifically known as Aloe ferox, is a powerful medicinal succulent native to South Africa. Renowned for its intense bitterness, the plant's sap is traditionally harvested for its potent laxative properties. Beyond digestion, it is widely utilized in herbal medicine for its antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory therapeutic benefits.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Distribution

This plant is widely recognized for its presence within the diverse landscapes of Southern Africa. Its primary distribution can be found throughout the various Cape Provinces. Additionally, the species is known to inhabit the mountainous regions of Lesotho. It also grows naturally across the expansive plains of the Free State. Consequently, these specific areas define the core geographical range of Aloe ferox.

Plant Parts

This plant, Aloe ferox, is a succulent known for its medicinal potency, which is primarily concentrated in its large, fleshy leaves. While the whole-leaf can be processed for various applications, the bitter yellow sap found within the leaf is the most sought-after component for its purgative properties. Unlike some other species, the roots are not typically the primary focus of medicinal extraction, nor are the seeds commonly used in traditional remedies. Instead, the medicinal value is most effectively derived from the thick, juice-filled leaves that characterize the plant's structure.
