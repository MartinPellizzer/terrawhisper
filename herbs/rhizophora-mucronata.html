Mangrove, scientifically known as Rhizophora mucronata, is a vital estuarine species renowned for its ecological significance and medicinal potential. Often called the loop-root mangrove, it thrives in intertidal zones. Various cultures utilize its bark and leaves to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to gastrointestinal issues and inflammation.

Plant Parts

This plant, Rhizophora mucronata, possesses distinct morphological features across its various components that are of significant interest. The root system is characterized by prominent, arching stilt roots that provide stability in unstable, muddy mangrove environments and facilitate gas exchange. Its leaves are thick, leathery, and elliptical, designed to withstand high salinity and intense sunlight. Additionally, the fruit is a specialized viviparous propagule, which germinates while still attached to the parent tree, allowing it to take root quickly once it drops into the water.
