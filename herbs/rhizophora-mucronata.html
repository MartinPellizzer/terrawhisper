Rhizophora mucronata

Mangrove

Rhizophora mucronata, commonly known as the loop-root mangrove, is a vital estuarine species found in tropical coastal ecosystems. Renowned for its distinctive stilt roots, this plant plays a crucial role in shoreline protection and biodiversity. Beyond ecology, it holds significant medicinal potential, offering bioactive compounds for traditional healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Rhizophora mucronata
  • Family: Rhizophoraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, it is classified under the family Rhizophoraceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Rhizophora.

Plant Parts

This plant, Rhizophora mucronata, possesses distinct morphological features across its various components that are of significant interest. The root system is characterized by prominent, arching stilt roots that provide stability in unstable, muddy mangrove environments and facilitate gas exchange. Its leaves are thick, leathery, and elliptical, designed to withstand high salinity and intense sunlight. Additionally, the fruit is a specialized viviparous propagule, which germinates while still attached to the parent tree, allowing it to take root quickly once it drops into the water.
