Arisaema erubescens

Arisaema erubescens, commonly known as the Japanese Jack-in-the-pulpit, is a perennial herb native to East Asia. Valued in traditional medicine, its tuberous roots contain bioactive compounds used to treat inflammation and various ailments. However, extreme caution is required, as the plant contains calcium oxalate crystals, making it toxic.

  • Scientific name: Arisaema erubescens
  • Family: Araceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it resides in the order Alismatales and the family Araceae. Finally, it is specifically categorized under the genus Arisaema.

Plant Parts

This plant, Arisaema erubescens, possesses significant medicinal value derived primarily from its underground structures. The most important parts utilized are the tubers and the raw rhizome, which contain various bioactive compounds. While the tubers serve as the primary storage organs for the plant's nutrients, the raw rhizome is often the focus of traditional preparations, requiring careful handling due to the presence of irritating substances inherent in its natural state.
