The physiology of Alchornea cordifolia is characterized by its metabolic efficiency and nutrient acquisition strategies. The plant utilizes a C3 photosynthetic pathway, which involves the initial fixation of carbon dioxide into a three-carbon compound during the Calvin cycle, a process typical of most woody dicotyledonous shrubs found in tropical and subtropical environments. Regarding its interaction with soil nutrients, the plant is not a nitrogen fixer, meaning it does not possess specialized root nodules containing symbiotic bacteria to convert atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia; instead, it relies on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium present in the soil through its root system to satisfy its nitrogen requirements for protein synthesis and growth.
