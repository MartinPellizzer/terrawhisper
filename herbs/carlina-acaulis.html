Carlina acaulis

Stemless carline thistle

Carlina acaulis, commonly known as the stemless carline thistle, is a resilient perennial native to European mountain ranges. Distinguished by its lack of a visible stem and striking, thistle-like flower heads, this medicinal plant has historically been utilized in traditional herbalism for its various therapeutic and restorative properties.

  • Scientific name: Carlina acaulis
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Asterales and the family Asteraceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Carlina, with the species name acaulis.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as carline thistle, relies heavily on its specialized underground structures for survival and medicinal potency. The primary focus of its therapeutic application lies within the roots, which are characterized by a thick, sturdy, and often branched architecture. These roots serve as the plant's main storage organ, concentrating the bioactive compounds that are frequently harvested for traditional healing purposes. Because the plant is acaulescent, meaning it lacks a visible stem above ground, the development and strength of the root system are essential to its overall vitality and its ability to support the large, flowering heads that emerge directly from the base.
