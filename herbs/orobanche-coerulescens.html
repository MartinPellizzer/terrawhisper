Orobanche coerulescens

Orobanche coerulescens, commonly known as the blue broomrape, is a fascinating holoparasitic plant that lacks chlorophyll. Lacking the ability to photosynthesize, it survives by extracting nutrients directly from host roots. While not widely used in mainstream medicine, its unique parasitic biology offers significant interest for botanical and pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Orobanche coerulescens
  • Family: Orobanchaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is categorized within the order Lamiales and the family Orobanchaceaae. Ultimately, its taxonomic classification concludes within the genus Orobanche.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Orobanche coerulescens, is recognized by various common names depending on the language and region. In German, it is referred to as the Bläuliche Sommerwurzel, while in Polish, it is called Zaraza błękitnawa. In Upper Sorbian, the name used is Namódra hubinka. Furthermore, in Chinese, it is known as 紫花列当, a name that is also utilized in its traditional character form as 紫花列當 in Taiwan.
