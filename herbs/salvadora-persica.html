Salvadora persica

Salvadora persica, commonly known as the toothbrush tree, is a versatile medicinal shrub native to arid regions of Africa and Asia. Renowned for its antimicrobial properties, its twigs are traditionally used for dental hygiene, while various parts offer therapeutic benefits in treating inflammation, digestive issues, and skin conditions.

  • Scientific name: Salvadora persica
  • Family: Salvadoraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Brassicales, it is classified under the family Salvadoraceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Salvadora and the specific species Salvadora persica.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several regions of the African continent. In Northern Africa, it can be found growing in the territories of Algeria and Egypt. Its presence continues through the North African landscapes of Libya and Morocco. Furthermore, the species extends into West Tropical Africa, specifically within the borders of Burkina. This distribution highlights the plant's ability to thrive in diverse environments across the continent.
