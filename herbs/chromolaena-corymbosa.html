Chromolaena corymbosa

Chromolaena corymbosa, commonly known as a medicinal shrub, is a significant plant in traditional herbal medicine. Belonging to the Asteraceae family, it is valued for its diverse therapeutic properties, including anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial effects. This species plays a vital role in treating various ailments across several indigenous healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Chromolaena corymbosa
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and order Asterales, it is further categorized into the family Asteraceae. Specifically, it is identified by the genus Chromolaena.

Common Names

This plant, Chromolaena corymbosa, is known by various regional names across different linguistic landscapes. In English, it is frequently referred to as the Siam weed, while in Spanish, it is commonly called la escoba de monte. Moving to Portuguese, it is identified as erva-de-santa-maria, and in the local context of French-speaking regions, it is known as l'herbe de la Sainte-Marie.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across multiple regions in Asia and the Caribbean. Within the Indian Subcontinent, it is commonly found throughout India and specifically in the state of Assam. Its presence is also documented in the mountainous terrain of the West Himalaya. Moving across the ocean to the Caribbean, the species can be found growing in the Bahamas. Additionally, it is distributed within the islands of Cuba.
