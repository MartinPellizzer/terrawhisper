Salvia officinalis

Sage

Salvia officinalis, commonly known as culinary sage, is a perennial herb prized for its aromatic leaves and potent medicinal properties. Rich in antioxidants and essential oils, it has been used historically to support digestive health, reduce inflammation, and boost cognitive function, making it a cornerstone of traditional herbal medicine.

  • Scientific name: Salvia officinalis
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae, it is classified under the genus Salvia. Its specific scientific name is Salvia officinalis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Salvia officinalis, is referred to by various common names depending on the language and region. In Italian, it is simply known as Salvia, while in Chinese, it is called 鼠尾草 in Simplified (zh-hans), Traditional (zh-hant), and Cantonese (zh-hk) scripts. Additionally, in the Walloon language, it is referred to as Franke saedje.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across several regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, it can be found growing within the territories of Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward the center of the continent, the species is also established in Middle Europe. Specifically, it inhabits parts of Austria as well as the regions of Czechia and Slovakia. Finally, its presence is well-documented throughout the country of Germany.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as sage, is valued for its various components that contribute to its therapeutic properties. The most significant parts used in herbal medicine are the aerial parts, which encompass everything above the ground. Within these structures, the leaves serve as the primary source of bioactive compounds, particularly when they are processed into dried leaves for storage and consumption. Furthermore, the plant is rich in volatile essential oils, and the concentrated essential oil extracted from the foliage is often utilized for its potent aromatic and medicinal qualities.
