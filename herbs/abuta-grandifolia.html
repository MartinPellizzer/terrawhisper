Abuta grandifolia

Abuta grandifolia, commonly known as the large-leaved abuta, is a significant medicinal climber native to tropical regions. Renowned in traditional medicine, its leaves and roots are utilized for their diverse therapeutic properties, including anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial effects, making it a vital resource in herbal healing and ethnobotanical studies.

  • Scientific name: Abuta grandifolia
  • Family: Menispermaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Ranunculales. Finally, it is a member of the Menispermaceae family within the genus Abuta.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several regions of the South American continent. In Northern South America, it can be found growing in French Guiana, Guyana, and Suriname. Its range also extends throughout the territory of Venezuela. Moving toward the western part of the continent, the species is present in Bolivia. Together, these locations define its specific geographical footprint across the region.
