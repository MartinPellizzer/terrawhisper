Esfand

Peganum harmala · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (487 studies) · ★★★★★

Esfand, scientifically known as Peganum harmala, is a perennial herb widely recognized in traditional medicine across the Middle East and Central Asia. Renowned for its potent alkaloids, particularly harmine, it is frequently utilized for its antimicrobial, antioxidant, and sedative properties, often featured in ritualistic practices and folk remedies.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindales, it is further categorized into the family Nitrariaceae. Ultimately, it is identified by the genus Peganum, specifically known as Peganum harmala.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several European regions. In Middle Europe, it can be found growing in Hungary. Its presence extends into Southwestern Europe, where it inhabits the Baleares islands. Additionally, the species is located within the territories of France and Sardegna. Finally, it is also widely distributed throughout Spain.

Plant Parts

This plant, Peganum harmala, is a versatile medicinal herb where various components are utilized for their pharmacological properties. The most potent part is often considered to be the tiny, dark seeds, which contain high concentrations of harmala alkaloids. These seed units are contained within a distinctive, capsule-like fruit that develops as the plant matures. Beneath the surface, the roots can also be harvested for medicinal purposes, while the upright stems provide the structural support necessary to hold the flowers and developing fruit aloft.
