Esfand

Peganum harmala · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (487 studies) · ★★★★★

Esfand, scientifically known as Peganum harmala, is a perennial herb widely recognized in traditional medicine across the Middle East and Central Asia. Renowned for its potent alkaloids, particularly harmine, it is frequently utilized for its antimicrobial, antioxidant, and sedative properties, often featured in ritualistic practices and folk remedies.

The life history of Peganum harmala is characterized by its classification as a perennial plant, allowing it to persist through multiple growing seasons within its natural habitat. Structurally, it functions as a hemicryptophyte, a life form where the perennating buds are located at or near the soil surface, protected by the remains of previous seasons' foliage or the soil layer itself. This combination of a long-lived life cycle and a low-growing, bud-protected strategy enables the species to endure varying environmental conditions and establish a stable presence in arid or semi-arid landscapes.
