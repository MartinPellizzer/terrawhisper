Selaginella doederleinii

Selagineella doederleinii, commonly known as the Resurrection Plant, is a fascinating lycopod renowned for its remarkable ability to survive extreme desiccation. This resilient species undergoes anhydrobiosis, appearing dormant when dry and reviving instantly upon rehydration. Beyond its biological intrigue, it holds significant potential in ethnobotany and modern physiological research.

  • Scientific name: Selaginella doederleinii
  • Family: Selaginellaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Lycopodiidae, it is organized into the order Selaginellales and the family Selaginellaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement within the genus Selaginella.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Selaginella doederleinii, is recognized by various common names across different languages and regions. In Japan, it is referred to as オニクラマゴケ (ja), while in Chinese-speaking areas, it is known as 生根卷柏 (zh-tw) or 深绿卷柏 (zh). In Indonesia and among Javanese speakers, the plant is called Cakar ayam (tanaman) (id) or Cèkèr pitik (jv).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions in East Asia. In China, it can be found throughout the South-Central regions and across the Southeast. Additionally, its presence is noted on the island of Hainan. Moving further east, the species is distributed throughout Japan. Finally, its range extends to the Nansei-shoto islands within Eastern Asia.
