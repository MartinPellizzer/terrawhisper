This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is positioned in the order Lamiales and the family Verbenaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is established within the genus Lantana as Lantana trifolia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several regions of the African continent. Within West Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in Benin and Guinea. Its presence is also well-documented throughout Nigeria and Togo. Moving toward the central part of the continent, the species extends into West-Central Tropical Africa. Specifically, its range includes the nation of Burundi.

This plant, Lantana trifolia, utilizes its leaves as a primary component in traditional medicinal applications. These leaves are typically characterized by their textured surface and distinct aromatic properties, which are often harnessed to prepare various herbal remedies. In many folk medicine practices, the leaves are crushed or decocted to exploit their bioactive compounds for their therapeutic potential.
