Lantana trifolia

Lantana trifolia, commonly known as the three-leaved lantana, is a versatile medicinal shrub prized in traditional healing. Renowned for its aromatic foliage and vibrant blooms, this plant offers significant therapeutic potential, particularly for its antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties, making it a vital resource in ethnobotanical studies worldwide.

  • Scientific name: Lantana trifolia
  • Family: Verbenaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is positioned in the order Lamiales and the family Verbenaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is established within the genus Lantana as Lantana trifolia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Lantana trifolia, is referred to by various names across different languages and regions. In Arabic, it is called لانتانا ثلاثية الأوراق, while in Spanish, it is commonly known as cariaquito moraado. Its names in Chinese include both 三叶马缨丹 and 三叶臭金凤.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several regions of the African continent. Within West Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in Benin and Guinea. Its presence is also well-documented throughout Nigeria and Togo. Moving toward the central part of the continent, the species extends into West-Central Tropical Africa. Specifically, its range includes the nation of Burundi.

In many folk medicine practices, the leaves are crushed or decocted to exploit their bioactive compounds for their therapeutic potential.
