White mustard, scientifically known as Sinapis alba, is a versatile annual herb renowned for its pungent seeds and therapeutic properties. Widely cultivated for culinary and medicinal uses, it contains glucosinolates that offer anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial benefits. This resilient plant remains a cornerstone in traditional herbal medicine and flavorful gastronomy.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Sinapis alba is characterized by a sexual process involving bisexual flowers, with asexual reproduction being absent. The plant produces yellow flowers arranged in a raceme inflorescence, with individual flower lengths ranging from a minimum of 0.6 cm to a maximum of 1.1 cm. The flowering period typically begins in February and concludes in July. Pollination is a biotic process primarily driven by insects, specifically bees. Self-fertilization is noted as absent, emphasizing the importance of these pollinators. Following successful pollination, the plant develops a capsule-type fruit. These fruits vary in size, with a mean length of 2.6 cm (ranging from 2 cm to 4 cm) and a mean width of 0.325 cm (ranging from 0.3 cm to 0.4 cm). Each fruit is prolific, containing between 101 and 1000 seeds. The seeds themselves are quite small, with a mean mass of approximately 0.0040121488 g, though they can range from a minimum of 6.46e-05 g to a maximum of 0.00704 g. Dispersal of the seeds occurs through unspecialized methods as well as anthropochory, where human activity aids in their movement.
