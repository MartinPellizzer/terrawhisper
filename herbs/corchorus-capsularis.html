Corchorus capsularis

Jew's marrow

Corchorus capsularis, commonly known as jute, is a versatile plant valued for both its fibrous properties and medicinal potential. Traditionally used in various cultures, its leaves and extracts possess antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties. Research continues to explore its bioactive compounds for developing new therapeutic applications in modern medicine.

  • Scientific name: Corchorus capsularis
  • Family: Malvaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malvales, it is specifically situated in the family Malvaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Corchorus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Corchorus capsularis, is recognized by various common names depending on the region and language. In Polish, it is called Juta torebkowa, while in Upper Sorbian, it is referred to as Aziski konopjowc. Its names in Portuguese and Catalan are simply Juta and jute, respectively, and in Azerbaijani, it is known as Girdəmeyvə cut.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several key tropical regions. In West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence is noted around the Gulf of Guinea Islands. It can also be found in Northeast Tropical Africa within Somalia and throughout East Tropical Africa in Tanzania. Moving toward the ocean, the species is distributed across the Western Indian Ocean. Specifically, it inhabits the islands of Mauritius and Réunion.
