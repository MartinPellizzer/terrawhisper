Dodonaea angustifolia

What's the taxonomical classification of Dodonaea angustifolia?

Dodonaea angustifolia belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it falls under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Its taxonomic journey continues through the order Sapindales and the family Sapindaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Dodonaea, with its specific designation being the species angustifolia.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Dodonaea angustifolia has narrow, lanceolate leaves that are arranged oppositely along its woody, sprawling stems. The foliage typically exhibits a dull green color and can vary in size depending on the specific environmental conditions. Small, inconspicuous flowers are produced in clusters, often appearing in late summer or autumn. These plants frequently develop winged seeds that facilitate dispersal through the wind. The overall growth habit is characterized by a dense, shrubby structure that helps it adapt to various habitats.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical and subtropical regions of northern Australia, specifically spanning across the Northern Territory, Queensland, and Western Australia. It is commonly found inhabiting diverse environments such as coastal dunes, mangrove margins, and rocky outcrops. The species also extends its range into parts of New Guinea and several neighboring Pacific islands. Within these habitats, it tends to thrive in sandy or rocky soils where drainage is sufficient. Its distribution is closely tied to warm, seasonal climates that support its growth patterns.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant Dodonaea angustifolia thrives in well-drained, sandy, or loamy soils within full sun to partial shade environments.

It is a hardy, drought-tolerant shrub that requires minimal irrigation once its root system is firmly established. Cultivators should prioritize planting in areas with excellent drainage to prevent root rot during heavy rainfall. Propagation is most effectively achieved through stem cuttings taken during the growing season.

Regular pruning helps maintain a compact shape and encourages dense, bushy growth for ornamental purposes.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant uses its leaves for medicinal purposes to treat various ailments such as skin infections, respiratory issues, and digestive problems. The leaves are often prepared as a decoction or a poultice to apply directly to wounds. Some traditional practices involve crushing the foliage to extract juices for topical healing. These leaf extracts are believed to possess antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties. By utilizing the leaves, practitioners can address both internal and external health concerns.

According to a study published by "Heliyon", the leaves of Dodonaea angustifolia are used in folklore medicine for wound healing. These leaves were processed into 80% methanol extracts for evaluation using excision and incision wound models. On days 8, 10, 12, and 14, the 5% and 10% extracts of Dodonaea angustifolia increased the wound contraction rate with significant levels of P < 0.01 and P < 0.001. On the last day of treatment, the 10% extract showed a maximum wound contraction of 99.9% (P < 0.001), while the 5% extract reached 99.15% (P < 0.001). Additionally, both the 5% and 10% doses of Dodonaea angustifolia significantly increased tensile strength by 54.10% and 56.58% (P < 0.01) against the control.

What are the pharmacological activities of Dodonaea angustifolia?

This plant has diverse pharmacological activities including anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, antimicrobial, antioxidant, and antiviral properties. Research indicates that its bioactive compounds can effectively suppress inflammation and neutralize harmful free radicals within biological systems. The plant also demonstrates significant antimicrobial and antibacterial capabilities by inhibiting the growth of various pathogenic microorganisms. Furthermore, its antiviral potential suggests it may play a role in combating different viral infections. These multifaceted therapeutic effects make it a subject of intense interest in modern ethnopharmacology.

According to a study published by "Saudi pharmaceutical journal : SPJ : the official publication of the Saudi Pharmaceutical Society", Dodonaea angustifolia is a medium-sized shrub used in traditional medicine to treat various diseases. The study screened the ten most prevalent Dodonaea angustifolia components to identify their anti-inflammatory effects. Through this research, 175 overlapping genes were discovered as prospective Dodonaea angustifolia anti-inflammatory targets. Key compounds identified in the plant include 6-Methoxykaempferol and 5-Hydroxy-7,8 dimethoxyflavone, which interact with key targets such as AKT1, VEGFA, and EGFR. These findings demonstrate the plant's multi-target, multi-compound, and multi-pathway strategies against inflammation.

Its medicinal properties are frequently utilized to reduce swelling and promote the rapid recovery of skin tissues. Research into its bioactive compounds suggests a potential role in addressing complications associated with HIV. Specifically, studies have examined how extracts might impact the progression of both HIV-1 and HIV-2 infections. These various applications highlight the plant's importance in traditional and experimental medicine.

The study aims to survey medicinal plants and herbs used for the treatment of HIV. While research to date is limited, the study identifies Dodonaea angustifolia as a plant with potential for these specific medicinal uses.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause skin irritation, allergic reactions, and gastrointestinal distress if ingested improperly. Contact with the leaves or stems may trigger redness, itching, or a localized rash in sensitive individuals. When consumed, the plant might lead to stomach aches, nausea, or vomiting due to its chemical composition. There is also a lack of extensive clinical data regarding its long-term safety in humans. Therefore, cautious handling and avoiding ingestion are recommended to prevent adverse physiological responses.
