Pseudotsuga taxifolia, scientifically known as Pseudotsuga menziesii, is the impressive Douglas fir, a coniferous evergreen native to western North America. While primarily valued for timber, various parts of this resilient tree have historically been explored in traditional ethnobotany for their potential therapeutic properties and essential oils in medicinal applications.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Douglas fir, possesses various anatomical features, though its medicinal history is often linked to its structural components. The bark of Pseudotsuga menziezii is thick, corky, and deeply furrowed, providing essential protection for the tree's vascular system. In certain traditional applications, extracts derived from the bark have been studied for their potential properties, though the tree is most frequently valued for its timber and ecological significance rather than primary pharmaceutical use.
