Scilla luciliae

Chionodoxa luciliae

Scylla luciliae, commonly known as the Lilium Squill, is a rare medicinal plant valued for its unique biochemical properties. Traditionally used in specialized herbal remedies, it possesses potent bioactive compounds. Understanding its pharmacological potential and ecological significance is essential for advancing natural medicine and preserving this precious botanical species.

  • Scientific name: Scilla luciliae
  • Family: Asparagaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and order Asparagales, it is a member of the family Asparagaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Scylla.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Scylla luciliae, is referred to by various common names depending on the region and language. In German, it is called die Gewöhnliche Sternhyazinthe, while in Russian, it is known as Пролесka Люцилии. In the Netherlands, it is identified as the middelste sneeuwroeim, and in Finland, it is called Isokevättähti. Furthermore, in Sweden, the plant is commonly known as the Stor vårstjärna.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution that spans across several continents and regions. In Northern Europe, it can be found growing in Great Britain. Its presence extends into Middle Europe, specifically within the territories of Germany and the Czechia-Slovakia border region. Moving toward Western Asia, the species is also documented in Türkiye. Finally, its range reaches North-Central U.S.A., where it is located in Illinois.
