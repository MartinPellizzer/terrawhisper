Corymbia terminalis

Western bloodwood

Corymbia terminalis, commonly known as the Terminal Bloodwood, is a distinctive tree native to the tropical regions of Northern Australia. Characterized by its deeply fissured, persistent bark and vibrant floral displays, this species plays a vital role in its ecosystem, providing essential habitat and resources for diverse local wildlife.

  • Family: Myrtaceae
  • Native range: Australasia

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Myrtales and the family Myrtaceae, it is classified under the genus Corymbia.

Plant Parts

This plant, Corymbia terminalis, is known for producing a distinct medicinal substance known as kino. This kino is a dark, resinous exudate that seeps from the bark and stems of the tree, often appearing as droplets or streaks on the trunk. In traditional medicinal practices, this kino is harvested and utilized for its various properties, typically serving as a topical treatment for skin ailments or as an astringent to help manage internal issues.
