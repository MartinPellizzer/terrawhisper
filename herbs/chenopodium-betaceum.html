Chenopodium betaceum

Chenopodium betaeum, commonly known as Swiss chard, is a nutrient-dense leafy green valued for its medicinal and dietary properties. Rich in vitamins A, C, and K, it supports immune function and bone health. Traditionally used to combat inflammation and promote digestion, this versatile plant remains a nutritional powerhouse.

  • Scientific name: Chenopodium betaceum
  • Family: Amaranthaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, situated within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified under the order Caryophyllales and the family Amaranthaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Chenopodium.

Common Names

This plant, Chenopodium betaeum, is known by various common names depending on the region and language. In English, it is frequently referred to as groundsel or groundsel goosefoot, while in Spanish, it is known as quiebrapata. In French, the plant is called chique-pied, and in German, it is referred to as schiebebein.

Distribution

This plant, Chenopodium betaeum, exhibits a specific geographical presence across several European regions. In Northern Europe, it can be found growing in Great Britain and Ireland. Its distribution extends further north into the territory of Norway. Moving into Middle Europe, the species is also documented in Austria. Finally, its range includes the central regions of Czechia and Slovakia.
