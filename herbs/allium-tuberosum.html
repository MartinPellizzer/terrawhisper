Allium tuberosum

Kutsay

Allium tuberosum, commonly known as garlic chives, is a versatile perennial herb prized for its delicate, mild garlic aroma. Beyond its culinary uses, this medicinal plant offers significant therapeutic potential. Rich in bioactive compounds, it is traditionally utilized to support digestive health, boost immunity, and provide antioxidant benefits.

  • Scientific name: Allium tuberosum
  • Family: Amaryllidaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Asparagales and the family Amaryllidaceae. It is formally identified by the genus Allium.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Allium tuberosum, is recognized by various common names across different languages and cultures. In Taiwan, it is referred to as Kú-chhài (nan), while in the Philippines, it is commonly called Kuse (pam). In French-speaking regions, it is known as Ciboule de Chine (fr), and in Russia, it is identified as Лук клубневой (ru). Additionally, in Korea, the plant is called 부추 (ko).
