Reproduction

The reproduction of Avena fatua is characterized by a sexual process involving bisexual flowers, with asexual reproduction being entirely absent. The plant produces inflorescences in the form of a panicle, which can range in length from a minimum of 0.1 m to a maximum of 0.4 m. The individual flowers within these inflorescences measure between 1.5 cm and 2 cm in length. Flowering typically begins in June and concludes in August. The pollination syndrome is abiotic, specifically relying on the wind for the transfer of pollen, and self-fertilization is noted as absent. Following successful pollination, the plant produces dry fruits that contain a high volume of seeds, ranging from 101 to 1000 seeds per fruit. The resulting seeds have a mean length of 7 mm, a mean width of 2 mm, and a mean volume of 12 mm³. The seed mass varies, with a mean of approximately 0.02099 g, a minimum of 0.00415 g, and a maximum of 0.0222 g. Dispersal of the seeds occurs through both anemochorous (wind) and zoochorous (animal) syndromes.
