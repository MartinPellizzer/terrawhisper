Korean ginseng

Panax ginseng · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (4437 studies) · ★★★★★

Korean ginseng, scientifically known as Panax ginseng, is a revered medicinal root celebrated in traditional Eastern medicine. Renowned for its adaptogenic properties, it helps the body manage stress, enhances cognitive function, and boosts energy levels. This potent botanical is widely used to support immune health and promote overall vitality.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Apiales and the family Araliaceae. Its taxonomic identity is further defined by its membership in the genus Panax.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across several key regions within Eastern Asia. In the Russian Far East, it can be found growing in the Khabarovsk and Primorye territories. Its range extends into China, specifically throughout the Manchuria and China North-Central regions. Additionally, the species is widely recognized as being native to Korea. Together, these locations define the specific ecological niche where this medicinal species thrives.

Plant Parts

This plant, Panax ginseng, is a perennial herb characterized by a distinct morphological structure. The most pharmacologically significant part is the fleshy, perennial root, which often develops a bifurcated shape resembling a human figure. These primary roots serve as the main storage organ for active ginsenosides. Above the ground, the plant features several large, palmate leaves that grow in a whorled arrangement. Each individual leaf typically consists of multiple leaflets branching from a central point. Furthermore, the plant can develop adventitious roots that emerge from the stem or nodes, contributing to its complex underground network.
