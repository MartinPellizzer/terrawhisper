Physiology

The physiology of Aloe macrocarpa is characterized by specialized metabolic processes designed for survival in arid environments, primarily driven by Crassulacean Acid Metabolism (CAM). This photosynthetic pathway allows the plant to minimize transpirational water loss by keeping its stomata closed during the heat of the day and opening them at night to collect carbon dioxide, which is then stored as malic acid. The plant's succulent leaves function as highly efficient water reservoirs, utilizing large vacuoles to maintain turgor pressure and facilitate nutrient transport. Regarding its nutrient cycling capabilities, Aloe macrocarpa is not a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria, such as Rhizobium, required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms. Consequently, its physiological growth and nitrogen uptake are entirely dependent on the availability of nitrates and ammonium within the soil substrate.
