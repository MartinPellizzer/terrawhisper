Ceriops tagal

Ceriops tagal, commonly known as the mangrove or Indian mangrove, is a vital species found in tropical intertidal zones. Renowned for its medicinal properties, it is traditionally used to treat wounds, inflammation, and digestive issues. Its bark, rich in tannins, plays a significant role in traditional coastal healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Ceriops tagal
  • Family: Rhizophoraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Malpighiales and the family Rhizophoraceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Ceriops, as part of the species Ceriops tagal.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across several key regions of the African continent. In Northeast Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in Djibouti and Somalia. Its range extends further into East Tropical Africa, where it is located in Kenya and Tanzania. Additionally, the species reaches into South Tropical Africa, specifically inhabiting Mozambique. Collectively, these locations highlight its presence within various tropical African ecosystems.
