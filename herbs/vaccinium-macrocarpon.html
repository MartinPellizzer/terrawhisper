Vaccinium macrocarpon

Large cranberry

Vaccinium macrocarpum, commonly known as the large cranberry, is a perennial shrub native to North American wetlands. Renowned for its tart, antioxidant-rich berries, this plant is highly valued in both culinary and medicinal applications. It is frequently utilized to support urinary tract health and combat oxidative stress naturally.

  • Scientific name: Vaccinium macrocarpon
  • Family: Ericaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Ericales and the family Ericaceae, it is classified under the genus Vaccinium. As part of this taxonomic hierarchy, Vaccinium macrocarpum is identified by its specific botanical lineage.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the large cranberry, offers various components that are utilized for their medicinal properties. The nutrient-dense pulp is often consumed directly or juiced to provide antioxidants, while the thin skin and outer peels contain concentrated amounts of polyphenols and flavonoids. Within the fruit, the tiny seeds are also rich in essential fatty acids and bioactive compounds. Furthermore, various plant extracts derived from these different parts are frequently used in pharmacological studies and supplemental products to support urinary tract health and combat inflammation.
