Grindelia integrifolia

Puget sound gumweed

Grindelia integrifolia, commonly known as gumweed, is a perennial herb native to North American wetlands. Renowned for its sticky, resinous leaves, this plant has long been utilized in traditional medicine. It is primarily valued for its potent expectorant properties, helping to soothe respiratory ailments and support lung health.

  • Scientific name: Grindelia integrifolia
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales, it is a member of the Asteraceae family. It is specifically identified by the genus Grindelia.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several specific regions in North America. In Western Canada, its presence is noted within the province of British Columbia. Moving south into the Northwestern United States, it can be found growing in Oregon. Additionally, the species inhabits the state of Washington. Together, these areas define the primary geographical range of Grindelia integrifolia.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as gumweed, is primarily utilized in herbal medicine for its aerial parts. These above-ground components, which include the stems, leaves, and flowering heads, are highly valued for their resinous, sticky properties. When the aerial parts are harvested and processed, they provide the essential compounds used to create expectorant syrups and topical applications, making the entire upper structure of the plant a vital resource for therapeutic use.
