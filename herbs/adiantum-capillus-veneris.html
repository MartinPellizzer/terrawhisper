Adiantum capillus-veneris

Maidenhair fern

Adiantum capillus-venetum, commonly known as Maidenhair fern, is a delicate, moisture-loving plant cherished for its fine, dark stems and lush foliage. Beyond its ornamental beauty, this species holds significant ethnobotanical importance, traditionally utilized in various cultures for its perceived medicinal properties, particularly in treating respiratory ailments and skin conditions.

  • Scientific name: Adiantum capillus-veneris
  • Family: Pteridaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Adiantum capillus-veneris, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Polypodiinae under the order Polypodiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Pteridaceae and the genus Adiantum.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as maidenhair fern, is characterized by its delicate and distinctive leaves, which are technically known as fronds. These thin, fan-shaped leaflets possess a fine, lace-like texture and are supported by slender, dark, hair-like stems. In traditional medicinal practices, these vibrant green leaves are often the primary part utilized for their therapeutic properties.
