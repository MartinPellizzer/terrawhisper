Breynia fruticosa

What's the taxonomical classification of Breynia fruticosa?

Breynia fruticosa belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Following its taxonomic lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Its evolutionary placement continues through the order Malpighiales and the family Phyllanthaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by its specific genus, Breynia, and its unique species designation, fruticosa.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Breynia fruticosa has a woody, multi-stemmed shrubby habit characterized by small, elliptical leaves and tiny, inconspicuous flowers that lack petals. The foliage is typically dark green and arranged alternately along slender, branching stems. Small, fleshy white berries develop from the flowers and serve as a primary reproductive feature of the plant. The bark on older stems becomes somewhat rough and grayish as the plant matures. These morphological traits collectively define its compact and bushy growth form.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant has its aerial parts, including the leaves and stems, utilized for various medicinal purposes. The leaves are frequently prepared as a decoction or infusion to treat ailments such as skin inflammation and digestive issues. Some traditional practices also involve using the crushed foliage as a topical application for wounds. These aerial components contain bioactive compounds that contribute to the plant's therapeutic potential. Practitioners often harvest these specific sections during certain seasons to ensure maximum potency.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", researchers investigated the chemical constituents from the aerial parts of Breynia fruticosa. Through various chromatographic techniques, 7 compounds were obtained and identified by spectroscopic methods. These constituents included aviculin [(+)-isolariciresinol-9'-rhamno-pyranoside], friedelan-3beta-ol, friedelin, arborinone, isoarborinol, 5-hydroxy-7,8,4'-trimethoxy flavone, and 2,4-dihydroxy-6-methoxy-3-methyl-acetophenone. All of these compounds were first isolated from the B. genus.
