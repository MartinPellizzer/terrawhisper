Zanthoxylum dimorphophyllum

What's the taxonomical classification of Zanthoxylum dimorphophyllum?

Zanthoxylum dimorphophyllum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its hierarchical classification through the order Sapindales, it is placed within the family Rutaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by the genus Zanthoxylum and is specifically distinguished as the species dimorphophyllum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Zanthoxylum dimorphophyllum has leaves characterized by a distinct dimorphism where some leaflets are deeply lobed while others remain entire or only slightly serrated. The plant typically features pinnately compound leaves that vary in shape depending on their position on the stem. These leaves are arranged oppositely along the branches and possess a leathery texture. The leaflets often exhibit an acute apex and a finely serrated margin along the edges. Small, inconspicuous flowers eventually give rise to drupaceous fruits that contribute to the overall morphological profile of the species.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant has its root utilized medicinally for its various therapeutic properties. Traditional practitioners often process the root to create decoctions or powders intended to treat specific ailments. These preparations are frequently valued for their potential to alleviate pain or reduce inflammation within the body. Scientific interest in the root often focuses on its unique chemical compounds and bioactive constituents. Such studies aim to better understand the biological mechanisms behind its traditional uses.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", the chemical constituents of the dried roots of Zanthoxylum dimorphophyllum r. spinifoliuum were studied to find active constituents. Using modern chromatography, 4 compounds were isolated and identified from the plant for the first time. These identified compounds include isopimpinellin (I), xanthoxyletin (II), 6-(2', 3'-dihydroxy-3'-methyl-butyl)-7-hydroxy-2H-1-benzopyran-2-one (III), and 6-(2'-O-beta-D-glucopyranosyloxy, 3'-dihydroxy-3'-methybutyl)-7-hydroxy-2H-1-benzopyran-2-one (IV). The study focused on the structures of these 4 specific substances found within the roots.
