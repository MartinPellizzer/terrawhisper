Maize, scientifically known as Zea mays, is more than a dietary staple; it holds significant medicinal potential. Rich in bioactive compounds like antioxidants and lutein, it supports eye health and combats inflammation. Traditionally used in various cultures, this versatile cereal offers promising therapeutic benefits for modern nutritional and holistic wellness.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as maize or corn, possesses a distinct anatomical structure where each part serves a specific physiological role. The roots form a robust fibrous system that anchors the plant and absorbs essential nutrients from the soil, while the sturdy stems provide the necessary structural support for upward growth. Attached to these stems are long, parallel-veined leaves that facilitate photosynthesis to fuel the plant's development. As the plant matures, it develops a thick, central stalk that carries the weight of the developing ears. Finally, the reproductive process involves the female flowers, where the style acts as a crucial channel to receive pollen, ensuring the successful production of seeds.
