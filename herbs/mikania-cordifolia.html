Mikania cordifolia, scientifically known as Mikania cordifolia, is a vigorous climbing vine native to tropical regions. Commonly referred to as "doveweed," this medicinal plant is celebrated in traditional herbalism for its potent anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and wound-healing properties, offering significant therapeutic potential for treating various skin and respiratory ailments.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical range concentrated within the southern regions of the United States. In the South-Central U.S.A., its presence can be noted within the state of Texas. Moving toward the Southeastern U.S.A., it is also found growing in Alabama. The distribution continues through the Southeastern U.S.A. into the states of Georgia and Florida. Finally, its range extends through the Southeastern U.S.A. to include Louisiana.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Mexican mint leaf or Akarkara, is a climbing vine that utilizes various structures for its growth and medicinal properties. The root system serves as a vital anchor in the soil, absorbing necessary nutrients to support its vigorous climbing nature. Above the ground, the stem acts as a flexible, trailing axis that allows the plant to scramble over other vegetation, eventually developing the nodes from which its characteristic heart-shaped leaves emerge.
