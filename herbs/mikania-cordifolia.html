Mikania cordifolia, scientifically known as Mikania cordifolia, is a vigorous climbing vine native to tropical regions. Commonly referred to as "doveweed," this medicinal plant is celebrated in traditional herbalism for its potent anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and wound-healing properties, offering significant therapeutic potential for treating various skin and respiratory ailments.

Ecology

The ecology of Mikania cordifolia is characterized by a broad environmental plasticity, allowing it to colonize a diverse array of ecosystems ranging from anthropogenic areas and open grasslands (Campo Limpo) to highly varied forest types. It is commonly found within the Cerrado (latu sensu), riparian or gallery forests (Floresta Ciliar ou Galeria), and both seasonal deciduous (Floresta Estacional Decidual) and semi-deciduous (Floresta Estacional Semidecidual) forests. Additionally, this species thrives in humid environments such as ombrophilous forests (Floresta Ombrófila, including both pluvial and mixed types) and coastal Restinga habitats. This adaptability extends to its altitudinal distribution, where it can be found at any elevation from sea level (0 m AMSL) up to a maximum of 3000 m AMSL.

Morphology

The morphology of Mikania cordifolia is characterized by its growth as a woody liana, functioning as an obligatory climber that utilizes other vegetation for structural support. Although it exhibits characteristics of a woody herb or forb, its primary growth habit is that of a climbing vine rather than a self-supporting plant. It is a terrestrial species, growing in soil rather than being aquatic, semiaquatic, or an epiphyte (such as a holoepiphyte or hemiepiphyte). Furthermore, the plant is an independent organism, meaning it does not function as a parasite, and it maintains a woody structure as it matures.
