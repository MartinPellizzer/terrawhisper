Lavandula angustifolia

What's the taxonomical classification of Lavandula angustifolia?

Lavandula angustifolia belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its descent through the order Lamiales, it is placed in the family Lamiaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by its membership in the genus Lavandula and its unique species designation, angustifolia.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Lavandula angustifolia has narrow, linear leaves that measure between 1 and 4 centimeters in length and a terminal spike of purple flowers arranged on a square stem. The foliage is typically grayish-green due to a fine layer of fine hairs that cover the surface. This perennial shrub grows in a mounding habit, often reaching heights of 30 to 60 centimeters. Each flowering spike is supported by a long, slender peduncle that rises above the dense canopy of leaves. The woody base of the plant provides structural stability as it matures over several years.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the Mediterranean region, specifically spanning across southern Europe, North Africa, and parts of Western Asia. It thrives naturally in rocky, well-drained soils and areas with high sunlight exposure characteristic of its original habitat. While its primary range is concentrated around the Mediterranean basin, it has been widely introduced to temperate climates across the globe. Cultivators in North America and other parts of Europe successfully grow it in various gardening landscapes. Its ability to adapt to different Mediterranean-style climates allows it to persist in many non-native regions.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, Lavandula angustifolia, requires well-draining, sandy or loamy soil with a neutral to slightly alkaline pH and full sun exposure for at least six to eight hours daily.

Growers should avoid overly moist conditions to prevent root rot, which is a common issue for this species. Planting should occur in early spring once the danger of frost has passed and the soil has warmed. Regular pruning after the flowering period helps maintain a compact shape and encourages vigorous new growth.

Once established, the lavender is relatively drought-tolerant but benefits from occasional deep watering during extreme heat.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Lavandula angustifolia is utilized medicinally through its fragrant flowers, aromatic leaves, concentrated essential oil, woody stems, and underground roots. The flowers and leaves are often dried to create calming herbal teas or topical infusions for skin irritation. Extracting the essential oil from these botanical components provides a potent remedy for anxiety, insomnia, and digestive issues. While the stems are primarily used to support the structure of the plant, they can be integrated into herbal preparations. The roots are sometimes employed in traditional practices to address various systemic ailments.

According to a study published by "Avicenna journal of phytomedicine", the lavender flower of Lavandula angustifolia L. was used as part of a combination of medicinal plants. This study was conducted on 150 COVID-19 patients to evaluate the effect of these plants. The intervention group, which received the herbal medicine, had a median oxygen saturation percentage (SpO2) of 88.00 compared to a median of 86.00 in the control group. Additionally, the intervention group had a median C-reactive protein (CRP) of 20.00, which was significantly lower than the control group median of 28.00 at the time of hospital discharge (p<0.05). These results suggest the combination could significantly reduce the oxygen requirement and oxygen therapy.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, Lavandula angustifolia, has been utilized across various global cultures for centuries as a therapeutic agent to treat nervous disorders, skin inflammations, and respiratory ailments. In ancient Roman culture, it was frequently used in communal baths to provide antiseptic properties and a calming scent. Danish folk medicine traditionally incorporated the herb into local remedies to soothe digestive issues and ease symptoms of anxiety. Throughout medieval Europe, the dried flowers were often placed in linen sachets to ward off insects and improve sleep quality. These diverse historical practices highlight the plant's long-standing reputation for its calming and medicinal benefits.

Additionally, the plant functions as an antispasmodic agent to relieve muscle contractions and smooth muscle spasms. What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds linalool, linalyl acetate, rosmarinic acid, and lavanduly acetate. These chemical constituents contribute significantly to the therapeutic properties of the species. Linalool and linalyl acetate are major monoterpenoids responsible for much of the plant's calming scent and sedative effects. The presence of rosmarinic acid provides essential antioxidant and anti-inflammatory benefits to the organism. Additionally, lavanduly acetate works alongside these other molecules to enhance the overall chemical profile of the essential oil.

According to a study published by "PloS one", Lavandula angustifolia contains the major active compound Linalool. Both methanolic and essential oil extracts of this plant inhibit Cav3.2 current in a concentration-dependent manner. These products induce a negative shift of the steady-state inactivation of CaV3.2 current while causing no change in activation properties. The findings suggest that the inhibition of T-type calcium channels (TTCCs) by these compounds could contribute to the anxiolytic and neuroprotective effects of the plant.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is Lavandula angustifolia, which is used to create medicinal preparations such as essential oil, herbal tea, tincture, cream, and aqueous extract. The essential oil provides a highly concentrated aromatic essence often used in aromatherapy to promote relaxation. Drinking herbal tea made from the dried flowers serves as a gentle way to soothe the nervous system. Tinctures offer a potent alcohol-based solution for long-term storage and efficient absorption. Topical creams utilize the plant's soothing properties to calm skin irritations, while aqueous extracts use water to pull out water-soluble therapeutic compounds.

According to a study published by "Molecules (Basel, Switzerland)", Lavandula angustifolia is a medicinal plant source of bioactive essential oil with antiseptic, analgesic, and anticancer effects. The composition of the oil varies, with a commercial oil from France containing 40.2% linalool and 44% linalyl acetate, compared to a self-manufactured oil with 23.2% linalool and 40.6% linalyl acetate. The self-manufactured preparation also contained a significantly higher proportion of lavandulyl acetate at 23.2% versus 5.5%. These lavender oils demonstrate a concentration-dependent effect on the production of IL-6, IL-8, and VEGF in HaCaT cells. Furthermore, the in-house preparation performed significantly better in in vitro cell models regarding the increase of VEGF production.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause allergic reactions, contact dermatitis, headache, insomnia, and skin inflammation in sensitive individuals. Topical application of the essential oil may trigger redness or skin inflammation through contact dermatitis. Some people might also experience systemic allergic reactions such as hives or breathing difficulties. When ingested or inhaled in large amounts, it has been known to induce a headache or cause insomnia. Therefore, cautious use is necessary to avoid these various adverse health side effects.

According to a study published by "Foods (Basel, Switzerland)", the essential oil compounds of Lavandula angustifolia were identified using GC-MS and quantified through GC-FID, representing more than 95% of the total essential oils. While the oil showed high phenolic content, the amounts of certain compounds such as linalool and citral were at high levels. These high levels of linalool and citral can be a danger for some sensitive population groups due to allergic reactions. Therefore, the chemical composition of the plant presents potential risks related to these specific substances.
