English lavender

Lavandula angustifolia · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (774 studies) · ★★★★★

English lavender, scientifically known as Lavandula angustifolia, is a beloved perennial herb prized for its fragrant purple blooms. Renowned in herbal medicine, it offers potent calming properties, helping to reduce anxiety and improve sleep quality. Its essential oils are widely used in aromatherapy to promote relaxation and soothe skin.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further organized into the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, Lavandula angustifolia is identified under the genus Lavandula.

Distribution

This plant, known as Lavandula angustifolia, is widely distributed across several key regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence can be found within the landscapes of both Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward the center of the continent, it is commonly established in Middle Europe, specifically in Austria and Germany. Furthermore, its natural range extends into Southwestern Europe, where it thrives in the climate of France. Together, these locations define the primary geographical footprint of this medicinal species.

Plant Parts

This plant, Lavandula angustifolia, is highly valued for its various components, particularly the aerial parts which encompass the stems and leaves. The most therapeutically significant elements are the fragrant flowers, from which the highly concentrated essential oils are extracted. This precious essential oil is widely used in aromatherapy and skincare due to its calming properties. While the seed can be produced for reproduction, it is the aromatic essence found in the upper structures that defines the plant's medicinal utility.
