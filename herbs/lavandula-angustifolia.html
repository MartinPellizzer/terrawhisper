Lavandula angustifolia

Lavandula angustifolia, commonly known as English lavender, is a cherished perennial herb prized for its aromatic purple blooms. Renowned for its calming properties, this medicinal plant is widely used in aromatherapy to reduce anxiety, improve sleep, and soothe skin irritations, making it a cornerstone of holistic wellness traditions.

  • Scientific name: Lavandula angustifolia
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further organized into the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, Lavandula angustifolia is identified under the genus Lavandula.

Distribution

This plant, known as Lavandula angustifolia, is widely distributed across several key regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence can be found within the landscapes of both Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward the center of the continent, it is commonly established in Middle Europe, specifically in Austria and Germany. Furthermore, its natural range extends into Southwestern Europe, where it thrives in the climate of France.
