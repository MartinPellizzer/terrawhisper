Trigonella spruneriana

Trigonella spruneriana, commonly known as spruner's fenugreek, is a delicate, creeping medicinal herb belonging to the Fabaceae family. Renowned for its nutritional density, this plant offers various therapeutic benefits, including antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, making it a valuable subject in ethnobotany and traditional herbal medicine studies worldwide.

  • Scientific name: Trigonella spruneriana
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Fabales and the family Fabaceae. Finally, its taxonomic designation is completed within the genus Trigonella.

Common Names

This plant, Trigonella spruneriana, is known by various regional names depending on the linguistic context. In English, it is frequently referred to as fenugreek, while in French, it is commonly called fenugrec. Moving to other languages, it is known as fenogreco in Italian, fenogreco in Spanish, and fenogreco in Portuguese. In German, the plant is identified as Bockshornklee, and in Dutch, it is called fenukriek.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning across several key regions of Eurasia. In Southeastern Europe, its presence is documented in both Greece and the European portion of Türkiye. The species also extends into the Caucasus region, specifically within the Transcaucasus area. Moving toward Central Asia, its distribution reaches as far as Turkmenistan. Finally, the plant can be found in Western Asia, specifically on the island of Cyprus.
