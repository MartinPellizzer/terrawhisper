Cryptocarya chinensis

Cryptocarya chinensis, commonly known as the Chinese Cryptocarya, is a significant medicinal plant valued in traditional practices. Native to subtropical regions, it contains diverse bioactive compounds. Researchers study its phytochemical profile for potential anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and antimicrobial properties, highlighting its importance in developing future natural therapeutic treatments.

  • Scientific name: Cryptocarya chinensis
  • Family: Lauraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Laurales and the family Lauraceae, it is classified under the genus Cryptocarya. Specifically, the medicinal species is known as Cryptocarya chinensis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Cryptocarya chinensis, is recognized by various common names across different linguistic contexts. In Mandarin Chinese, it is referred to as 厚壳桂 (hòukéguì) or 香果 (xiāngguǒ). Specifically, in Simplified Chinese used in mainland China, it is written as 厚壳桂, while in Traditional Chinese used in Taiwan, it is documented as 厚殼桂. Additionally, in Cantonese (Yue), it is commonly known as 厚殼桂.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across several key regions of East Asia. Within China, it can be found growing in the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan areas. Its presence also extends to the island nation of Japan. Furthermore, the species is documented within the Nansei-shotō archipelago. Collectively, these locations define the natural geographical range of Cryptocarya chinensis.
