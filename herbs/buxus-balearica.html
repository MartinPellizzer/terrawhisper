Buxus balearica

Buxus balearicica, commonly known as the Balearic boxwood, is a resilient evergreen shrub endemic to the Mediterranean. Valued for its dense, slow-growing wood, this plant holds significance in traditional herbalism. Its unique botanical properties make it a subject of interest for studying natural compounds within specialized medicinal ecosystems.

  • Scientific name: Buxus balearica
  • Family: Buxaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Buxales, it is classified under the family Buxaceae. It is further identified by its genus, Buxus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Buxus balearica, is referred to by several different common names depending on the language. In German, it is called the Balearen-Buchsbaum, while in French, it is known as the Buis des Baléares. The Catalan name for this species is boix baleàric, and in Lower Sorbian, it is called the Balearski buksowc. Additionally, in Norwegian Bokmål, the plant is commonly known as the Balearbuksbom.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a distinct geographical distribution concentrated around the Mediterranean basin. Within Southwestern Europe, it can be found in the Balearic Islands, Sardinia, and mainland Spain. Its range also extends significantly into Northern Africa. Specifically, the species inhabits parts of Algeria and Morocco. Consequently, its habitat is characterized by these specific coastal and Mediterranean regions.
