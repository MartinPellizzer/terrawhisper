Common Grape Vine

Vitis vinifera · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1756 studies) · ★★★★★

Common Grape Vine, scientifically known as Vitis vinifera, is a versatile medicinal and nutritional powerhouse. Renowned for its antioxidant-rich skins and seeds, it supports cardiovascular health, combats inflammation, and promotes skin vitality. Beyond its culinary fame, this ancient vine offers significant therapeutic benefits through its potent polyphenolic compounds.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Vitis vinifera is characterized by a biotic pollination process primarily driven by insects. The flowering period typically commences in April and extends through June, featuring small green flowers that range in length from 0.5 cm to 0.55 cm. These flowers are arranged in paniculate inflorescences, which vary in length from a minimum of 0.1 m to a maximum of 0.2 m. While self-fertilization is present, the plant relies on insect-mediated pollination to facilitate its reproductive cycle. Following successful fertilization, the plant develops fleshy, indehiscent fruit categorized as a berry, which exhibits a blue coloration. These berries typically possess a mean length of 1.38 cm (ranging from 0.6 cm to 2.5 cm) and a mean width of approximately 1.14 cm (ranging from 1.5 cm to 2 cm). Each fruit contains between 1 and 10 seeds, with an average seed mass of 0.0255 g (ranging from 0.018 g to 0.043 g) and a consistent seed volume of 24 mm³. The dispersal of the species is achieved through a zoochorous syndrome, specifically endozoochory, where seeds are dispersed via the digestive tracts of animals. Asexual reproduction is absent in this species.
