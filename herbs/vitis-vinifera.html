Vitis vinifera

Common grape vine

Vitis vinifera, commonly known as the European grapevine, is a versatile medicinal and nutritional powerhouse. Beyond its iconic role in viticulture, it offers potent antioxidant properties through its leaves and seeds. Rich in resveratrol, this plant supports cardiovascular health, combats oxidative stress, and possesses significant anti-inflammatory therapeutic benefits.

  • Scientific name: Vitis vinifera
  • Family: Vitaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Vitales and family Vitaceae, it is classified under the genus Vitis. Its specific scientific name is Vitis vinifera.

Common Names

This plant, scientifically known as Vitis vinifera, is recognized by various common names across different languages and cultures. In Polish, it is referred to as winorośl właściwa, while in Korean, it is known as 포도. In Chinese, the name used is 釀酒葡萄, and in the Wa language, it is called Vegne. Additionally, in the language of Luxembourg, it is identified as Wäirief.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific presence across various regions within Middle Europe. In the western part of this area, its distribution includes Belgium. Moving toward the central territories, it can be found growing in Germany. The plant also extends its reach into Austria and Hungary. Finally, its geographical range covers the combined regions of Czechia and Slovakia.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the grapevine (Vitis vinifera), offers various components that are utilized for their diverse medicinal and nutritional properties. The leaves are often used in traditional remedies to help manage blood sugar levels and reduce inflammation. The fruits, which are the grapes themselves, are rich in antioxidants that support cardiovascular health. Within these grapes, the skin serves as a concentrated source of polyphenols and resveratrol, which are vital for fighting oxidative stress. Furthermore, the seeds (or seeds extracted as oil) contain potent bioactive compounds that contribute to skin health and cellular protection.
