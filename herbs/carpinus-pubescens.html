Carpinus pubescens

What's the taxonomical classification of Carpinus pubescens?

Carpinus pube sceus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Its taxonomic progression continues through the order Fagales and the family Betulaceae, eventually reaching its specific classification within the genus Carpinus. Ultimately, the plant is identified by its unique specific epithet, pubeceus, forming the complete scientific name Carpinus pubeceus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Carpinus pubescens has simple, ovate to elliptical leaves with serrated margins and a distinctively pubescent underside, alongside woody stems and small, inconspicuous flowers. The foliage is characterized by prominent, parallel veins that extend from the central midrib to the leaf edges. Its bark typically develops a fine texture that smooths out as the tree matures. The fruit is a small nutlet enclosed in a leafy, three-lobed bract that facilitates wind dispersal. These morphological features collectively allow the species to thrive in specific forest understory environments.
