Reproduction

The reproduction of Persicaria maculosa is primarily sexual, characterized by bisexual flowers that facilitate a reproductive cycle through biotic pollination. The flowering period typically commences in June and continues through October, featuring pink-colored flowers arranged in paniculate inflorescences that range in length from 0.02 m to 0.06 m. Individual flowers are relatively small, measuring between 0.2 cm and 0.3 cm in length. The pollination syndrome is insect-mediated, and the plant is capable of self-fertilization. Following successful fertilization, the plant produces dry, indehiscent fruits known as achenes, which are black in color and measure between 0.2 cm and 0.25 cm in length, with a mean length of 0.2125 cm. Each fruit can be highly prolific, containing between 101 and 1000 seeds. The resulting seeds are uniform in length at approximately 2.3 mm, with a mean volume of 2.25 mm³ (ranging from 2 to 2.5 mm³) and a mean mass of approximately 0.00184 g (ranging from 0.00105 g to 0.0033 g). Dispersal of these seeds is achieved through both hydrochorous (water) and epizoochorous (attachment to animals) syndromes, ensuring the spread of the species across its habitat. No asexual reproduction was noted for this species.
