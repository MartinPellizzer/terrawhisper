Trigonella caerulea

What's the taxonomical classification of Trigonella caerulea?

Trigonella caerulea belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant is further categorized under the order Fabales and is a member of the family Fabaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Trigonella, and its unique species designation, caerulea.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Trigonella caerulea has an erect, herbaceous growth habit characterized by compound, pinnately divided leaves and small, bluish-white flowers arranged in racemes. The leaves consist of several pairs of oblong to lanceolate leaflets that emerge from a central petiole. Its stems are typically slender and can reach moderate heights depending on environmental conditions. The inflorescence features delicate petals that provide a subtle contrast to the green foliage. Small, cylindrical pods develop from the flowers and eventually contain the plant's seeds.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

Additionally, the leaves are often utilized in traditional practices for their nutritional and therapeutic properties. Together, these plant parts serve as important components in various herbal remedies.

According to a study published by "Analytical and bioanalytical chemistry", the seeds of Trigonella caerulea were examined through a metabolome-based approach. Using UPLC-MS, researchers were able to identify 93 metabolites within the seeds. This group of identified metabolites included 5 peptides, 2 phenolic acids, 22 C/O-flavonoid conjugates, 26 saponins, and 9 fatty acids. The study utilized GC-MS analysis on Trigonella seed extracts to identify 91 metabolites. These identified metabolites consisted mostly of fatty acyl esters, free fatty acids, organic acids, sugars, and amino acids.
