Cornus macrophylla

Cornus macrophylla, commonly known as Pacific dogwood, is a resilient deciduous shrub native to western North America. Renowned for its striking white bracts and lush foliage, this medicinal plant has historically been used in traditional remedies to treat various ailments, offering significant botanical interest and ecological value to woodland ecosystems.

  • Scientific name: Cornus macrophylla
  • Family: Cornaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Cornales. Finally, it is a member of the family Cornaceae within the genus Cornus.

This plant exhibits a wide-ranging geographical distribution across several distinct regions. In the Russian Far East, its presence can be documented on the island of Sakhalin. Its range extends significantly into Western Asia, specifically within the borders of Afghanistan.
