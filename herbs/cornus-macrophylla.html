Physiology

The physiology of Cornus macrophylla is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, a mechanism typical of most temperate woody perennials where carbon fixation occurs via the Calvin cycle within the mesophyll cells. This metabolic process involves the initial carboxylation of ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP) by the enzyme RuBisCO to form 3-phosphoglycerate. Because it utilizes the C3 pathway, the plant is particularly adapted to environments with moderate temperatures and sufficient moisture, where the energetic costs of carbon fixation are balanced against the risk of photorespiration. Its physiological efficiency is heavily dependent on stomatal conductance to manage gas exchange, ensuring that CO2 uptake is optimized for glucose production while minimizing transpirational water loss.
