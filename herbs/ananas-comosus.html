Ananas comosus

Pineapple

Ananas comosus, commonly known as pineapple, is a tropical perennial plant celebrated for its succulent, nutrient-dense fruit. Beyond its culinary appeal, it holds significant medicinal value. Rich in bromelain, an enzyme with potent anti-inflammatory and digestive properties, this plant serves as a natural remedy for inflammation and healing.

  • Scientific name: Ananas comosus
  • Family: Bromeliaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Ananas comosus, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, falling under the order Poaales. Finally, it is a member of the Bromeliaceae family within the genus Ananas.

Plant Parts

This plant, Ananas comosus, commonly known as pineapple, possesses unique morphological features that contribute to its medicinal and nutritional value. The stem serves as the central structural axis of the plant, though it is relatively short and stout, supporting the rosette of leaves and the developing fruit. Within the plant's tissues, particularly in the stem and the fruit, lies bromelain, a powerful proteolytic enzyme widely used for its anti-inflammatory and digestive properties. Additionally, the plant develops a fibrous root system that anchors it firmly in the soil and facilitates the absorption of water and essential nutrients necessary for its growth.
