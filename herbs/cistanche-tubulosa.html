Desert hyacinth

Cistanche tubulosa · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (88 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Desert hyacinth, scientifically known as Cistanche tubulosa, is a fascinating parasitic plant native to arid regions. Known for its tubular, yellowish flowers, it thrives by extracting nutrients from host plant roots. Traditionally valued in various folk medicines, this resilient species plays a unique role in desert ecosystem dynamics and pharmacology.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several distinct regions of the African continent and its surrounding islands. In Northern Africa, its presence is documented in both Egypt and Libya. The species also extends into the Macaronesian archipelago, specifically found in Cape Verde. The ecology of Cistanche tubulosa is characterized by its specialized parasitic lifestyle, as it relies entirely on host plants within the genus Tamarix L. for its nutritional requirements. This species is primarily found inhabiting river and lake valleys, where the presence of its host species is most abundant. Its environmental preference is strictly tied to specific edaphic conditions, specifically thriving in sandy and pebbly soils that provide the necessary substrate for its development.
