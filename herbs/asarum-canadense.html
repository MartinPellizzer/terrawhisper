Asarum canadense

Asarum canadense, commonly known as Canadian mayapple, is a perennial herb native to North American woodlands. Characterized by its distinctive heart-shaped leaves and ground-level flowers, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Historically used in traditional medicine, it contains bioactive compounds that warrant careful study regarding their therapeutic potential.

  • Scientific name: Asarum canadense
  • Family: Aristolochiaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known as Asarum canadense, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae within the order Piperales. Finally, it is a member of the family Aristolochiaceae and is categorized under the genus Asarum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide distribution across various regions of North America. In Western Canada, its presence can be noted within the province of Manitoba. Moving toward the east, it is found throughout Ontario, Québec, and New Brunswick. The species also extends into the North-Central United States, specifically in Illinois. Consequently, its habitat spans from the Canadian prairies to the eastern forests and central American plains.
