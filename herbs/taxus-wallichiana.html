Himalayan yew

Taxus wallichiana · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (228 studies) · ★★★★★

Himalayan yew, scientifically known as Taxus wallichiana, is a majestic evergreen conifer native to the high-altitude regions of the Himalayas. Renowned for its medicinal potency, this species contains potent alkaloids used in advanced pharmacology. It serves as a vital botanical resource, bridging ancient traditional knowledge with modern life-saving medicine.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several key regions in Asia. Within China, it can be found specifically in the South-Central areas and throughout Tibet. Its range extends significantly into the Indian Subcontinent, where it inhabits the East Himalaya and Nepal. Additionally, the species is present in the Indo-China region, specifically within Laos. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's preference for diverse mountainous and subtropical habitats.

Plant Parts

This plant, Taxus wallichiana, is a coniferous evergreen characterized by its distinct morphological features used in various medicinal applications. The most recognizable parts are the soft, dark green needles, which are arranged spirally on the branches. These leaves, which are synonymous with the singular needle structure, contain various bioactive compounds. Beyond the foliage, the bark of the tree is often harvested for its chemical properties, while the dense wood provides the structural integrity of the species. Together, these components—the needles, leaves, bark, and wood—constitute the essential parts of this significant medicinal plant.
