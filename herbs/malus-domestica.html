Milton

Malus domestica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1446 studies) · ★★★★★

Milton, scientifically known as Malus domestica, is the botanical name for the common apple tree. Renowned for its nutritional density, this plant provides essential vitamins, antioxidants, and dietary fiber. Beyond its culinary value, Malus domestica has been utilized in traditional medicine to support digestive health and boost immunity.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Malus domestica is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects for successful fertilization. The flowering period typically commences in April and concludes in May, with individual flowers measuring between 3 cm and 3.5 cm in length. Notably, self-fertilization is absent, necessitating cross-pollination to facilitate the development of its fruit. The resulting fruit is classified as a fleshy pome, which exhibits a mean length of 2.875 cm (ranging from 2 cm to 4.5 cm) and a consistent mean width of 2 cm. For the purpose of seed dispersal, the plant utilizes a zoochorous strategy, specifically endozoochory, where seeds are dispersed via the digestive tracts of animals. The seeds themselves are relatively small, with a mean mass of approximately 0.02586 g (ranging from 0.0222 g to 0.0329 g) and a uniform volume of 23 mm³.
