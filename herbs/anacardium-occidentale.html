Anacardium occidentale

Cashew

Anacardium occidentale, commonly known as the cashew tree, is a versatile tropical plant valued for both its nutritional and medicinal properties. Beyond providing edible nuts, various parts of the tree—including the bark, leaves, and shell oil—are utilized in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to inflammation.

  • Scientific name: Anacardium occidentale
  • Family: Anacardiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindales, it is classified under the family Anacardiaceae. Specifically, it is identified by the genus Anacardium.

Distribution

This plant, known scientifically as Anacardium occidentale, is distributed across several regions within West Tropical Africa. Specifically, its presence is documented in the nation of Benin. It can also be found growing within the borders of Burkina Faso. Furthermore, the species is located in Gambia and Guinea-Bissau. Finally, its geographical range extends to include the country of Guinea.

Plant Parts

This plant, Anacardium occidentale, commonly known as the cashew tree, utilizes various components for both ecological function and medicinal potential. The sturdy stem serves as the primary structural support, while the protective bark covers the trunk and branches. The lush green leaves are responsible for photosynthesis, driving the plant's energy production. Most notably, the plant produces specialized reproductive structures, including the fleshy cashew apple and the true fruit, which is a hard nutshell containing a single kernel. Within this protective shell lie the seeds, which are the most valued part of the plant for both nutritional and therapeutic applications.
