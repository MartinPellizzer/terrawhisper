Bulbous Nettle

Laportea bulbifera · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (47 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Bulbous Nettle, scientifically known as Laportea bulbifera, is a stinging herbaceous plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its unique bladder-like structures, it holds significant ethnobotanical importance. Traditionally, various parts of this plant are utilized in folk medicine to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and various digestive disorders.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range spanning several distinct regions across East Asia. Within the Russian Far East, its presence is noted in both the Kuril Islands and the Primorye region. Its distribution extends significantly into China, where it can be found in the Manchuria area. Furthermore, the species is widespread throughout China South-Central and China North-Central. Collectively, these locations highlight the diverse environmental settings where the plant thrives.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the False Nettle, utilizes its various structures for survival, but the root serves as a critical component in its traditional medicinal applications. The root of Laportea bulbifera is often collected and processed to prepare decoctions or poultices, as it is believed to possess specific therapeutic properties in folk medicine. While the plant is also known for its stinging hairs on the leaves and stems, the subterranean root remains a vital part of the organism's biological makeup and its ethnobotanical utility.
