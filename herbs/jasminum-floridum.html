Jasminum floridum

What's the taxonomical classification of Jasminum floridum?

Jasminum floridum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this group, it falls under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic hierarchy further, it is placed in the order Lamiales and the family Oleaceae. Finally, the plant is categorized within the genus Jasminum, specifically identified as the species floridum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Jasminum floridum has woody, scrambling stems that produce opposite, ovate to elliptic leaves with entire margins and pinnate venation. The foliage typically reaches lengths of two to four inches and maintains a glossy, deep green appearance throughout the growing season. Small, tubular white flowers emerge in clusters from the leaf axils, characterized by five spreading lobes. These blossoms are accompanied by small, fleshy drupes that transition from green to dark purple or black upon maturity. The overall structure of the plant allows it to function as a climbing vine or a dense, spreading shrub.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the subtropical and tropical regions of southern China, specifically within provinces such as Guangdong, Guangxi, Hainan, and Fujian. It thrives in warm, humid environments characterized by mild winters and significant rainfall. Due to its ornamental value, it has been widely introduced to other subtropical climates around the world.
