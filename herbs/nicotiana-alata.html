Reproduction

The reproduction of Nicotiana alata is characterized by a biotic pollination process, primarily driven by insects, with a specific emphasis on butterflies. The flowering period typically begins in July and extends through September. The flowers are green in color and exhibit significant morphological variation, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 4 cm to a maximum of 11.5 cm, and a maximum width of 2 cm. Following successful pollination, the plant produces a dehiscence-type fruit classified as a capsule. These capsules vary in size, with a mean length of 1.475 cm (ranging from 1.2 cm to 1.7 cm) and a width between 0.65 cm and 0.7 cm. The resulting seeds are quite small, maintaining a consistent length of approximately 1 mm. The seed mass is notably minute, with a mean of 9.25e-05 g, ranging from a minimum of 7e-05 g to a maximum of 1e-04 g. Dispersal of these seeds follows an unspecialized pattern, though it also exhibits anthropochorous tendencies. Notably, asexual reproduction is absent in this species, indicating a reliance on sexual reproductive cycles.
