Humulus scandens

Humulus scandens, commonly known as the climbing hop, is a vigorous perennial vine native to various tropical regions. While closely related to the commercial hop, this medicinal plant is valued in traditional practices for its unique bioactive properties, often utilized for its soothing effects and various therapeutic applications in herbalism.

  • Scientific name: Humulus scandens
  • Family: Cannabaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Rosales and the family Cannabaceae. Finally, it is classified under the genus Humulus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across several regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence is documented within the borders of Denmark and Sweden. Moving toward Middle Europe, the species can also be found growing in Belgium. Its range extends further into Germany, which serves as another key location for this medicinal plant. Finally, its geographical reach includes Hungary, completing its documented distribution in Middle Europe.

Plant Parts

This plant, Humulus scandens, is characterized by its climbing growth habit, which is facilitated by its long, slender stem that utilizes specialized structures to ascend surrounding vegetation. The foliage consists of a distinct leaf structure, typically featuring serrated margins and a characteristic shape that distinguishes it from other members of the Cannabaceae family. Together, the climbing stem and the patterned leaves allow the plant to maximize its exposure to sunlight within its natural habitat.
