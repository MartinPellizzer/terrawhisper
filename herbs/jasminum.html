Jasminum

Jasmine

Jasminum, commonly known as jasmine, is a diverse genus of shrubs and vines celebrated globally for its intoxicating fragrance. Beyond its aesthetic beauty in perfumery, jasmine holds significant medicinal value. It is frequently utilized in traditional practices to alleviate anxiety, soothe skin irritations, and promote overall emotional well-being.

  • Scientific name: Jasminum
  • Family: Oleaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales, it is classified under the family Oleaceae. Its specific taxonomic designation is the genus Jasminum.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Jasminum, is recognized by various names across different cultures and languages. In Chinese, it is referred to as 素馨属 (sù xīn shǔ), while in Javanese, it is called Mlathi. The Tibetan name for the plant is བ་སྤུའི་མེ་ཏིག (ba spu'i me tig), and in Polish, it is commonly known as Jaśmin. Additionally, in Bosnian, the name used is Jasmin.
