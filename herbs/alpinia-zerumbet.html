Pink porcelain lily, scientifically known as Alpinia zerumbet, is a versatile tropical perennial prized in traditional medicine. Belonging to the ginger family, its aromatic rhizomes and leaves possess potent anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant properties. Often used to treat digestive ailments and skin irritations, it remains a significant botanical treasure.

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several oceanic regions and continental areas. Its presence extends into the Middle Atlantic Ocean, specifically on the islands of Ascension and Saint Helena. Reproduction

The reproduction of Alpinia zerumbet is characterized by bisexual flowers arranged in a paniculate inflorescence, which can reach a maximum length of 0.4 meters. These flowers, which exhibit a red color, typically measure up to 5 cm in length. The flowering period is quite extensive and variable, beginning around April and continuing through October. The plant follows a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects for the transfer of pollen, and self-fertilization is noted as being absent. Following successful pollination, the plant produces fleshy fruits that contain a significant number of seeds, ranging from 11 to 100 seeds per fruit, with an average seed mass of 0.015 g. The dispersal of these seeds is categorized under an unspecialized syndrome, though it also exhibits anthropochorous traits, indicating that human activity plays a role in its movement and spread.
