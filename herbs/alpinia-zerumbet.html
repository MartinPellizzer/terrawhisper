Alpinia zerumbet

Pink porcelain lily

Alpinia zerumbet, commonly known as bitter ginger or shell ginger, is a perennial herb celebrated in traditional medicine. Native to tropical regions, this aromatic plant offers potent anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. Its versatile rhizomes and leaves are utilized to treat various ailments, making it a valuable botanical resource.

  • Scientific name: Alpinia zerumbet
  • Family: Zingiberaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Alpinia zerumbet, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Zingiberales and the family Zingiberaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is completed within the genus Alpinia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several oceanic regions and continental areas. Within the Macaronesia archipelago, it can be found growing in both the Azores and the Canary Islands. Its presence extends into the Middle Atlantic Ocean, specifically on the islands of Ascension and Saint Helena. Additionally, the species is documented in the mainland regions of China, particularly within China South-Central. These varied locations highlight its ability to inhabit both isolated island ecosystems and larger landmasses.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Alpinia zerumbet, possesses various components that are utilized in traditional medicine. The large, lanceolate leaves are a primary part used for their aromatic and therapeutic properties, with the individual leaf often being harvested for decoctions. The reproductive structures include small fruits that develop from the flowering spikes, and within these, the seeds play a crucial role in the plant's life cycle. In certain medicinal contexts, the dried botanical specimen is referred to as Fructus, signifying the importance of the fruit in various herbal formulations.
