Ipomoea capillacea

Ipomoea capillacea, commonly known as "manto santo," is a versatile medicinal plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, it is traditionally used to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and digestive issues. This botanical specimen holds significant value in ethnobotany, offering promising potential for modern pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Ipomoea capillacea
  • Family: Convolvulaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Following its descent through the subclass Magnoliidae and order Solanales, it is a member of the family Convolvulaceae. Finally, it is taxonomically identified under the genus Ipomoea.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ipomoea capillacea, is recognized by various common names depending on the region and language. In English, it is frequently referred to as bindweed or morning glory, reflecting its climbing growth habit and floral characteristics. In Spanish, it is commonly called "bejuco" or "corre-corre," terms that highlight its tendency to scramble over other vegetation. Furthermore, in Portuguese, it is known as "corda-de-viola," a name that captures its long, vine-like structure.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning across North America. In the Southwestern United States, its presence is noted within the state of Arizona. Moving into the South-Central United States, it can be found throughout New Mexico and Texas. Its distribution extends significantly into Mexico, covering the central regions of the country. Furthermore, the species is also documented across Northeast Mexico.
