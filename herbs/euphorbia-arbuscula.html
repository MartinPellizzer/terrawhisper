Euphorbia arbuscula

Euphorbia arbuscula, commonly known as the African Spurge, is a resilient succulent prized for its unique architectural form. Beyond its ornamental beauty, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditionally used in various cultures, its specialized latex contains bioactive compounds that are currently being studied for diverse medicinal applications.

  • Scientific name: Euphorbia arbuscula
  • Family: Euphorbiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Malpighiales and the family Euphorbiaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Euphorbia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Euphorbia arbuscula, is referred to by various common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In Arabic, it is frequently called فربيون شجيري, حلبلوب شجيري, or رقيب الشمس الشجيري, while in the Arabic Sinai dialect, it is known as فربيون شجيرى. Additionally, in Spanish, it is identified as Tirucailla arboscua.

Distribution

This plant, Euphorbia arborea, possesses a highly restricted and specialized geographical distribution. Its presence is uniquely tied to the specific ecological conditions found in Northeast Tropical Africa. Specifically, this species is endemic to the island of Socotra. This isolated archipelago provides the distinct environment necessary for the plant to thrive. Consequently, its natural range is limited to this singular, fascinating location.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Euphorbia arbuscula, is characterized by its distinct physiological features, most notably the presence of a specialized substance within its tissues. The most significant medicinal component is derived from the stem latex, a milky, sap-like fluid that flows from the plant when the stem is injured or broken. This latex contains a complex mixture of diterpene esters and other bioactive compounds that serve as the primary source of its pharmacological properties.
