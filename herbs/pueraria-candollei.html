Morphology

The morphology of Pueraria candollei is characterized by its growth habit as an obligatory climber, utilizing specialized structures to ascend through surrounding vegetation. The plant features slender, twining stems that exhibit a vigorous climbing nature, relying entirely on external support to reach sunlight. Its foliage typically consists of trifoliate leaves, where each leaf is composed of three distinct leaflets that are often ovate to elliptical in shape with acute or obtuse apices. The venation is prominent, providing structural integrity to the leaf blades. The inflorescence is generally arranged in axillary racemes, bearing small, papilionaceous flowers that are characteristic of the Fabaceae family. The root system is robust, often developing thickened structures that can serve as storage organs, while the reproductive organs consist of standard legume-type pods that dehisce to release seeds, ensuring the continuation of its climbing life cycle.
