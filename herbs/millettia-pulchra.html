Millettia pulchra

What's the taxonomical classification of Millettia pulchra?

Millettia pulchra belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the evolutionary lineage of the order Fabales. It is further categorized under the family Fabaceae, which places it within the legume group. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Millettia and the species epithet pulchrra.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant uses its roots and stems for various medicinal purposes in traditional healing practices. The roots are often processed into decoctions to treat internal ailments and inflammatory conditions. Meanwhile, the stem is utilized to prepare topical applications or infusions for specific ailments. These distinct parts contain bioactive compounds that contribute to the overall therapeutic value of the species. Practitioners select specific segments based on the required strength and type of treatment.

According to a study published by "Carbohydrate polymers", the root of Millettia pulchrha (YLS) has been traditionally used as a folk medicine for the treatment of depression and insomnia in the Zhuang nationality of China. A novel homogeneous polysaccharide named YLP-1 was purified from the crude polysaccharides of YLS. This YLP-1 is mainly composed of glucose, arabinose and mannose with a molar ratio of 87.25%, 10.77%, and 1.98%, respectively. YLP-1 is a novel α-glucan with a backbone of 1,4-Glcp and is branched at C6 of 1,4,6-Glcp to combine 1,4-Manp and 1,5-Araf. This polysaccharide effectively ameliorated depression-like ethological behaviors and restored decreased catecholamine levels in chronic variable stress (CVS)-induced depression rats.

What are the pharmacological activities of Millettia pulchra?

This plant has analgesic, antiinflammatory, cardiovascular remodeling remedy, and hypertension remedy pharmacological activities. Its bioactive compounds work to alleviate pain and reduce systemic inflammation in various biological models. These properties extend to the heart, where it acts as a remedy for cardiovascular remodeling by preventing adverse structural changes in cardiac tissue. Additionally, the plant serves as an effective hypertension remedy by helping to regulate and lower blood pressure levels. Such diverse medicinal effects highlight its significant therapeutic potential in managing multiple chronic conditions.

According to a study published by "Biological & pharmaceutical bulletin", the ethanol extract of the stem of Millettia pulchrha (EMP) was found to reduce xylene-induced ear edema and relieve both acetic acid-induced pain and pain in the hot plate test. The study also observed a significant decrease in nitric oxide (NO) production in cells treated with isolated compounds from the plant. Among the tested compounds, Lanceolatin B showed the greatest inhibition of NO synthesis. This specific compound also reduced levels of interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β), IL-6, tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS), nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB), and phosphorylation inhibitory kappa B alpha (p-IκBα) in a dose-dependent manner. These findings provide evidence that EMP and individual isolated compounds possess significant antiinflammatory and analgesic activities.

According to a study published by "Carbohydrate polymers", the root of Millettia pulchrha contains polysaccharides with potential antidepressant effects. A novel homogeneous polysaccharide named YLP-1 was purified from the crude polysaccharides of the root and is mainly composed of glucose, arabinose, and mannose with a molar ratio of 87.25%, 10.77%, and 1.98%, respectively. This YLP-1 is an alpha-glucan featuring a backbone of 1,4-Glcp and branches at C6 of 1,4,6-Glcp to combine 1,4-Manp and 1,5-Araf. The compound effectively ameliorated depression-like behaviors and restored decreased catecholamine levels in chronic variable stress-induced depression rats. Furthermore, YLP-1 mitigated the disruption of tryptophan metabolites in urine and serum and promoted short-chain fatty acids production by Lactobacillus spp.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Millettia pulchrha Kurz var-laxior (Dunn) Z. belongs to the Fabaceae family and possesses multifarious medicinal properties. Yulangsan polysaccharide (YLSPS) is a chief ingredient of its root used in Chinese traditional medicine for the remedy of acute or chronic hepatitis and jaundice. In a study involving mice, YLSPS was orally treated daily 1h after the injection of diclofenac for 2 weeks. This substance effectively reduced elevated levels of serum alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, and alkaline phosphatase. It also reduced the content of malondialdehyde and inhibited proinflammatory mediators such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha and interleukin 1 beta. These findings suggest that YLSPS protects against acute hepatic injury by reducing oxidative stress and suppressing inflammatory responses.
