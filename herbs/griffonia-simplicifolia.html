Griffonia simplicifolia

Griffonia simplicifolia, commonly known as African grape, is a medicinal shrub native to West Africa. It is highly valued for its seeds, which are a potent natural source of 5-HTP. This precursor to serotonin is widely used to support mood regulation, sleep quality, and healthy appetite management naturally.

  • Scientific name: Griffonia simplicifolia
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fabales, it is classified under the family Fabaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic designation is within the genus Griffonia.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the West Tropical Africa region. Specifically, its natural habitat includes the country of Benin. It can also be found growing within the borders of Ghana. Furthermore, the species is documented in Ivory Coast and Liberia. Finally, the geographical range extends into Nigeria, completing its presence in these tropical areas.

Plant Parts

This plant, Griffonia simplicifolia, is a woody shrub native to West Africa, and its primary medicinal value is concentrated within its seeds. These small, dark seeds are harvested from the plant's pods and are a rich natural source of 5-HTP (5-hydroxytryptophan), a chemical precursor to the neurotransmitter serotonin. Because the seeds contain this essential compound, they are widely utilized in dietary supplements to support mood regulation, sleep quality, and appetite control.
