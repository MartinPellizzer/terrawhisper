Rubus ellipticus

Rubus ellipticus, commonly known as the hill raspberry, is a vigorous, thorny shrub native to tropical regions. Renowned for its edible, flavorful berries, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Beyond its culinary use, various parts of the plant are utilized in traditional medicine to treat inflammation and digestive ailments.

  • Scientific name: Rubus ellipticus
  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the order Rosales and is a member of the Rosaceae family. Specifically, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Rubus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Rubus ellipticus, is recognized by various regional common names across different languages. In Simplified Chinese, it is referred to as 椭圆悬钩子 (zh-hans), while in Traditional Chinese contexts such as those used in Taiwan (zh-hant) and Hong Kong (zh-hk), it is called 橢圓懸鉤子. In Japan, the species is known as キミノヒマラヤキイチゴ (ja), and in Nepal, it is commonly called रातो ऐँसेलु (ne).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning across several continents. In East Tropical Africa, its presence is noted specifically within Tanzania. It is also found in South Tropical Africa, occupying regions in both Malawi and Mozambique. Moving further south, the species can be located in the KwaZulu-Natal province of Southern Africa. Additionally, its distribution extends to the eastern hemisphere, specifically within China South-Central.
