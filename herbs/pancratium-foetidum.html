Pancratium foetidum

Pancratium foetidum, commonly known as the spider lily, is a perennial coastal plant native to the Mediterranean. Renowned for its fragrant white blooms, it holds significant ethnobotanical value. Its chemical constituents, including alkaloids, offer diverse medicinal potential, particularly in treating inflammatory conditions, though careful usage is essential due to toxicity.

  • Scientific name: Pancratium foetidum
  • Family: Amaryllidaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, situated within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Asparagales and the family Amaryllidaceae. Within this group, it is identified by the genus Pancratium.

Common Names

This plant, Pancratium foetidum, is known by various names depending on the region and language. In English, it is commonly referred to as the spider lily or the fragrant lily. Within the Spanish-speaking world, it is frequently called lirio de mar or lirio de araña, while in Portuguese, it is known as lírio-da-praia. In French, it is identified as lys de mer, and in Italian, it is called giglio di mare.

Distribution

This plant, known scientifically as Pancratium foetidum, exhibits a specific geographical distribution across the North African region. It can be found growing within the coastal areas of Morocco in the west. Moving eastward, its presence extends into the territories of Algeria and Tunisia. The species' range continues further along the Mediterranean coastline into Libya. Consequently, these four countries represent the primary locations where this medicinal plant is naturally distributed.
