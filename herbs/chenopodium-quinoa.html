Physiology

The physiology of Chenopodium quinoa is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, which dictates its carbon fixation processes and metabolic efficiency under varying environmental conditions. Unlike plants that utilize C4 or CAM mechanisms to minimize photorespiration, Chenopodium quinoa relies on the standard Calvin cycle, making it particularly sensitive to temperature and water availability during the photosynthetic process. Furthermore, the plant does not function as a nitrogen fixer, meaning it lacks the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into a usable form; instead, it relies entirely on the uptake of inorganic nitrogen, such as nitrates and ammonium, from the soil to support its protein synthesis and overall growth.
