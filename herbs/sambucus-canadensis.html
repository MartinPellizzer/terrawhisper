Sambucus canadensis

American black elderberry

Sambucus canadensis, commonly known as American elderberry, is a versatile deciduous shrub native to North America. Renowned for its dark, nutrient-dense berries and fragrant white flowers, it is widely utilized in traditional medicine for its potent antiviral, immune-boosting, and antioxidant properties, making it a staple in natural wellness.

  • Scientific name: Sambucus canadensis
  • Family: Viburnaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Dipsacales, it is classified under the family Viburnaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Sambucus, specifically the species Sambucus canadensis.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as American elderberry (Sambucus canadensis), possesses various components that are utilized in traditional medicine. While the whole plant is occasionally referenced in holistic studies, most therapeutic applications focus on specific tissues. The leaves and the bark contain cyanogenic glycosides and are generally considered toxic if ingested, requiring careful handling. Conversely, the roots are often used in decoctions for their specific medicinal properties, though they must be prepared with caution. The seeds, found within the dark purple berries, are typically consumed alongside the fruit but should be processed correctly to avoid discomfort.
