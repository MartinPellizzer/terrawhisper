Viola yunnanensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Viola yunnanensis?

Viola yunnanensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is positioned in the subclass Magnoliidae and falls under the order Malpighiales. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, it is a member of the family Violaceae and the genus Viola, ultimately reaching its specific classification as the species yunnanensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Viola yunnanensis has a perennial herbaceous habit characterized by basal leaves that are typically cordate or reniform with crenate margins and a single, slender, erect flowering stem. The leaves are arranged in a rosette pattern, featuring long petioles and a distinctively textured surface. Its flowers are zygomorphic and emerge from the leaf axils, displaying five petals that often feature a yellow throat and purple or violet hues. The reproductive structures include a superior ovary that develops into a capsule containing numerous small seeds. This species is distinguished from related violets by the specific shape of its leaf bases and the arrangement of its floral bracts.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude regions of southwestern China, specifically within the provinces of Yunnan and Sichuan. It typically thrives in the mountainous terrain of the Hengduan Mountains where the climate is cool and moist. The species is often found growing in forest understories or along rocky slopes at elevations ranging from 2,000 to 3,500 meters. Its distribution is closely tied to the specific microclimates created by the complex topography of this subtropical highland area.
