Myrica rubra

Myrica rubra, commonly known as red bayberry, is a versatile evergreen shrub prized for its nutrient-dense, crimson fruits. Beyond its culinary appeal, this plant holds significant medicinal value in traditional practices, offering potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties that support digestive health and overall wellness in various cultures.

  • Scientific name: Myrica rubra
  • Family: Myricaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, placing it within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Fagales and the family Myrricaceae. Ultimately, the species is identified within the genus Myrica.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Myrica rubra, is referred to by several common names depending on the language and region. In Southern Min (nan), it is called Chhiū-m̂, while in Chinese, its names are consistent across various scripts and regions: it is written as 杨梅 in Simplified Chinese (zh-hans and zh-cn), 楊梅 in Traditional Chinese (zh-hant), and 楊梅 in the Cantonese usage of Hong Kong (zh-hk).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical presence across several key regions of East Asia. Within China, it can be found growing in the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan areas. Its distribution extends beyond the Chinese mainland into neighboring territories. Specifically, it is documented as part of the natural flora in Japan. Finally, the species is also located within the Korean Peninsula.

Plant Parts

This plant, Myrica rubra, possesses various components that are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The medicinal value is often derived from the bark, which can be harvested from different sections of the tree. Specifically, both the stem bark and the ground bark are frequently collected to prepare traditional remedies. In addition to the woody parts, the leaf serves as a vital part of the plant's medicinal profile, especially when processed into dried leaves for storage and subsequent use in decoctions or infusions.
