Aconitum hemsleyanum var. hemsleyanum

Aconitum hemsleyanum var. hemsleyanum, commonly known as Hemsley's Monkshood, is a potent medicinal perennial native to East Asian montane regions. Highly valued in traditional pharmacopoeia, its alkaloids possess profound physiological effects. However, due to extreme toxicity, precise processing is essential to harness its therapeutic potential safely and effectively.

  • Scientific name: Aconitum hemsleyanum var. hemsleyanum
  • Family: Ranunculaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Following the order Ranunculales, it is a member of the family Ranunculaceae. Specifically, it is categorized under the genus Aconitum as the variety hemsleyanum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of habitats across several distinct regions. In China, its presence is widely recorded throughout the South-Central and North-Central provinces. Additionally, the species can be found growing in the Southeast regions of China. Its geographical reach extends further west into the high-altitude areas of Tibet. Beyond China, the distribution also encompasses the Indo-China region, specifically within Myanmar.

Plant Parts

This plant, Aconitum hemsleyanum var. hemsleyanum, is a member of the Ranunculaceae family, and its primary medicinal value is concentrated within its specialized underground structures. The most significant part of the plant used in traditional practices is the roots, which typically develop into tuberous, fleshy organs that store essential alkaloids. These roots are carefully harvested and processed, as they contain the potent chemical compounds responsible for the plant's therapeutic and toxicological properties.
