Black Nightshade

Solanum nigrum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (555 studies) · ★★★★★

Black Nightshade, scientifically known as Solanum nigrum, is a versatile herbaceous plant widely used in traditional medicine across Asia and Africa. Renowned for its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, its berries and leaves are carefully prepared to treat ailments ranging from digestive issues to skin inflammations and various systemic infections.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Solanum nigrum is primarily sexual, characterized by bisexual flowers that facilitate efficient reproductive cycles. The flowering period typically begins in June and extends through October. These white-colored flowers utilize a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects for successful pollen transfer. Within the floral structure, self-fertilization is present, ensuring reproductive success even in varying conditions. Following fertilization, the plant produces fleshy, indehiscent fruits classified as berries, which transition to a black color upon maturity. These berries are relatively small, with a mean length of approximately 0.757 cm and a mean width of 0.773 cm, though they can reach a maximum dimension of 0.8 cm in both length and width and a minimum width of 0.5 cm. The dispersal of the plant is zoochorous, specifically employing an endozoochorous strategy where seeds are dispersed through the digestive tracts of animals. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing more than 1,000 seeds. The seeds themselves are minute, with a mean mass of approximately 0.000788 g (ranging from 0.00023 g to 0.00122 g), a mean length of 1 mm, and a mean width of 2 mm. The seed volume varies, with a mean of 0.59725 mm³, a minimum of 0.014 mm³, and a maximum of 1.9 mm³. Asexual reproduction is notably absent in this species.
