Arisaema amurense

Arisaema amurense, commonly known as the Amur jack-in-the-pulpit, is a perennial herbaceous plant native to East Asia. Valued in traditional medicine, its tubers contain bioactive compounds used to treat various ailments. However, due to its toxicity, careful handling is essential when exploring its complex pharmacological potential.

  • Scientific name: Arisaema amurense
  • Family: Araceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Arisaema amurense, belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Alismatales. Finally, it is a member of the family Araceae and falls under the genus Arisaema.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed throughout the temperate regions of East Asia. In the Russian Far East, its presence is documented in Khabarovsk, Primorye, and on the island of Sakhalin. The species also extends into northern China, where it can be found within Inner Mongolia. Furthermore, its range encompasses the broader Manchuria region.
