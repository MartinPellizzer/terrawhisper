What's the taxonomical classification of Arisaema amurense?

Arisaema amurense belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant is further organized under the order Alismatales and falls within the family Araceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Arisaema, with its specific designation being the species amurense.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Arisaema amurense has a fleshy, tuberous rhizome that produces upright, single-leaved stems reaching heights of up to 40 centimeters. Each stem is topped by a solitary, broadly ovate leaf that features a distinctively cordate base and a pointed apex. The reproductive structure consists of a spadix partially enclosed by a spathe, which typically transitions from a pale green to a purplish hue. During flowering, the spathe serves to protect the spadix and attracts pollinators through its unique shape and color. As the plant matures, the leaf tissue provides the necessary energy for the development of the underground storage organ.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant Arisaema amurense requires well-draining, humus-rich soil and a shaded, moist environment to thrive during its growing season.

Because it is a woodland species, it performs best under a canopy of deciduous trees that provide dappled light. It is critical to maintain consistent moisture in the substrate, though the soil must not remain waterlogged to prevent tuber rot. During the winter months, the plant enters a period of dormancy and should be protected from extreme desiccation.

Proper cultivation often involves planting the tubers in late autumn or early spring before new growth emerges.

According to a study published by "Genetics and molecular research : GMR", the plant Arisaema amurense is used as part of the herbal medicine Arisaematis Rhizoma. The study involved five authentic species including Arisaema amurense, A. amurense var. serratum, A. erubescens, and A. heterophyllum. Researchers analyzed matK and rbcL genes to identify these species and obtained 45 species-specific nucleotides sufficient to identify each species, with the exception of A. erubescens using matK. Additionally, 28 marker nucleotides were identified for each species, excluding P. pedatisecta when using rbcL. These genetic markers help distinguish Arisaematis Rhizoma from various adulterants through a phylogenetic tree showing nine distinct groups.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains calcium oxalate crystals, saponins, and various alkaloids that serve as its primary medicinal and chemical constituents. The presence of calcium oxalate in needle-like structures can cause significant irritation to the mouth and throat if the plant is consumed raw. However, specific saponins within the roots are often studied for their potential pharmacological properties in traditional medicine. These bioactive compounds must be carefully processed to neutralize toxicity before they can be used for therapeutic purposes. Researchers continue to analyze the complex chemical profile of Arisaema amurense to better understand its biological activity.

According to a study published by "The Journal of emergency medicine", the toxicity of Arisaema amurense is due to its content of calcium oxalate. This substance causes symptoms such as painful oropharyngeal edema, hypersalivation, aphonia, oral ulceration, esophageal erosion, and hypocalcemia. In one reported case, a 60-year-old man ingested a rhizome of the Arisaema amurensese plant as a medicinal herb. After the ingestion, the patient required endotracheal intubation and treatment with antihistamines and corticosteroids. Following these interventions, the patient had improved and was extubated 3 days after treatment.

Methanol extracts were separated on a Diamonsil ODS column at 35 degrees C using a methanol-water-glacial acetic acid mixture of 75:25:0.01 and a flow-rate of 1.0 mL x min(-1). Hyodeoxycholic acid in the methanol extract showed a linear relationship between the logarithms of injection amount and peak area (r = 0.9998) over the range of 2.4-24 microg. The average recovery for Bile Arisaema was 97.1% with an RSD of 2.9% for n=5.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe oral irritation, swelling of the mouth and throat, a burning sensation, dysphagia, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and skin irritation. Ingesting the plant often leads to an intense burning sensation in the mouth and throat due to its calcium oxalate crystals. This irritation can progress to significant swelling of the mouth and throat, which may result in dysphagia or difficulty swallowing. If consumed, the digestive system may react with nausea, vomiting, and intense abdominal pain. Furthermore, direct contact with the plant's tissues can lead to painful skin irritation.
