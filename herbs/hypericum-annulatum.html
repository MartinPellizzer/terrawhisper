Hypericum annulatum, scientifically known as Hypericum annulatum, is a perennial herb native to the Mediterranean region. Often referred to as annual St. John's wort, this resilient plant features vibrant yellow flowers. It is valued in traditional medicine for its therapeutic properties, particularly in treating skin ailments and inflammatory conditions.

Life history

The life history of Hypericum annulatum is characterized by its classification as a hemicryptophyte, a growth form where the perennating buds are located at or just below the soil surface. This strategy allows the plant to endure seasonal environmental stresses by protecting its vital reproductive structures beneath the substrate while allowing the aerial parts to emerge during favorable growing periods. As a hemicryptophyte, its life cycle involves a perennial structure that facilitates regrowth after dormant phases, distinguishing it from purely ephemeral therophytes or more permanent phanerophytes. This specific life form enables the plant to navigate fluctuating climatic conditions, ensuring its survival through cycles of vegetative growth and seasonal senescence.
