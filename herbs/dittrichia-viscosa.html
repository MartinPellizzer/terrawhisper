Woody Fleabane

Dittrichia viscosa · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (96 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Woody Fleabane, scientifically known as Dittrichia viscosa, is a hardy, perennial shrub native to the Mediterranean region. Often found in disturbed soils, this resinous plant is valued in traditional medicine for its potent antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties, making it a significant subject for modern pharmacological research and development.

This plant exhibits a varied presence across several distinct regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its range extends to include both Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward Middle Europe, the species can be found growing in Belgium and Germany. Its distribution further reaches the Mediterranean as part of Southwestern Europe. Specifically, it is known to inhabit the Baleares islands within that southern region.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Tansy hemp, possesses various parts that are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The aromatic leaves are a primary source of its medicinal value, especially when used as fresh leaves in topical applications to soothe skin irritations. The bright yellow flowers also contribute to the plant's chemical complexity, often being processed to create potent extracts used in traditional remedies. Furthermore, the plant is rich in bioactive compounds that can be isolated as essential oils, which are highly valued for their antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory characteristics.
