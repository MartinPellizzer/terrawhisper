Woody Fleabane, scientifically known as Dittrichia viscosa, is a hardy, perennial shrub native to the Mediterranean region. Often found in disturbed soils, this resinous plant is valued in traditional medicine for its potent antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties, making it a significant subject for modern pharmacological research and development.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Dittrichia viscosa is characterized by a sexual process involving bisexual flowers, which ensures genetic diversity through biotic pollination. The flowering period typically commences in August and extends through November, featuring vibrant orange flowers that range in length from 0.65 cm to 1.2 cm and in width up to 1.5 cm. These flowers rely on an insect-driven pollination syndrome to facilitate fertilization, as self-fertilization is noted to be absent. Following successful pollination, the plant produces dry, indehiscent fruits categorized as achenes. These fruits are relatively small, measuring between 0.2 cm and 0.25 cm in length. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing more than 1,000 seeds. The seeds themselves are minute, with a mean mass of approximately 0.0003417 g, ranging from a minimum of 0.0003 g to a maximum of 0.00042 g. Dispersal is achieved via an anemochorous syndrome, meaning the seeds are primarily dispersed by the wind, allowing the species to spread effectively across its habitat.
