Digitalis lanata subsp. trojana

Digitalis lanata subsp. trojana, commonly known as a subspecies of Grecian foxglove, is a rare medicinal plant endemic to specific regions of Turkey. Renowned for its high concentration of digoxin, it serves as a critical botanical source for potent cardiac glycosides used in treating various heart conditions.

  • Scientific name: Digitalis lanata subsp. trojana
  • Family: Plantaginaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Lamiales and the family Plantaginaea. Finally, it is identified as a member of the genus Digitalis, specifically the subspecies Digitalis lanata subsp. trojana.

Common Names

This plant, Digitalis lanata subsp. trojana, is known by various regional names depending on the language. In English, it is commonly referred to as trojan woolly foxglove, while in Turkish, it is known as troya yünlü digitalis.

Distribution

This plant is a specific subspecies characterized by its unique regional presence. Its geographical distribution is notably restricted to a very specific part of the world. Specifically, this medicinal species can be found within the region of Western Asia. Within this broad area, it is endemic to the country of Türkiye. Consequently, its natural habitat is confined to the distinct landscapes found in this single nation.
