Fineleaf water dropwort, scientifically known as Oenanthe aquatica, is a perennial herb native to wetland habitats. While historically used in folk medicine, it is highly toxic if ingested. This plant is primarily distinguished by its delicate, finely divided leaves and its preference for damp, marshy environments across Europe.

Ecology

The ecology of Oenanthe aquatica is defined by its strict requirement for high-moisture environments, allowing it to thrive across a diverse range of hydrological landscapes. It is commonly found colonizing the margins and shallow waters of lakes, as well as the flowing currents of rivers and the stagnant, nutrient-rich conditions of swamps. Beyond lowland aquatic systems, its adaptability extends to varied topographical elevations, enabling it to establish itself in mountain regions as well as broad, open plains, provided that the soil remains saturated. This ability to inhabit such a wide spectrum of wetlands—from high-altitude water bodies to lowland alluvial plains—underscores its role as a versatile component of freshwater and semi-aquatic ecosystems.
