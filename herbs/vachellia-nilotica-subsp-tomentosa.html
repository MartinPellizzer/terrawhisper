Vachellia nilotica subsp. tomentosa

Vachellia nilotica subsp. tomentosa, commonly known as a significant medicinal shrub in various African regions, possesses remarkable therapeutic properties. Renowned for its bioactive compounds, this subspecies is traditionally utilized to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to digestive issues, offering a rich natural resource for ethnobotanical research and pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Vachellia nilotica subsp. tomentosa
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales, it falls under the family Fabaceae. Specifically, it is identified by the genus Vachellia as the subspecies tomentosa.

Common Names

This plant, Vachellia nilotica subsp. tomentosa, is known by various regional names depending on the linguistic context. In English, it is commonly referred to as the hairy acacia, while in Arabic, it is known as al-samur (السمر). In Swahili, the plant is identified as mshindano.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across several regions of the African continent. In Northern Africa, it can be found growing within the borders of Algeria and Egypt. Its presence also extends to the North African nations of Libya and Morocco. Moving toward the West Tropical Africa region, the species is documented in Burkina. Consequently, its habitat spans from the Mediterranean coast to the tropical zones of the west.
