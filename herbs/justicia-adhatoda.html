What's the taxonomical classification of Justicia adhatoda?

Justicia adhatoda belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant is further organized into the order Lamiales and belongs to the family Acanthaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Justicia and the species name adhatoda.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Justicia adhatoda has opposite, lanceolate to ovate leaves with entire margins and a smooth surface, upright square stems, and tubular, bilabiate flowers that are typically white with purple markings. The foliage is characterized by prominent venation and a slightly glossy texture that distinguishes its vegetative structure. Its inflorescence consists of dense terminal spikes where the flowers emerge from specialized bracts. The plant exhibits a characteristic herbaceous habit with a branching pattern that supports its floral display. These morphological features collectively define its appearance within the Acanthaceae family.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Justicia adhatoda is utilized medicinally through the application of its leaves and roots to treat various respiratory and inflammatory conditions. The leaves are most commonly employed in traditional medicine to extract alkaloids like vasicine, which act as effective bronchodilators and expectorants. These leafy extracts are often prepared as decoctions or syrups to alleviate symptoms of asthma, bronchitis, and coughing. Additionally, the roots are sometimes incorporated into specific herbal formulations to address different ailments. This dual utility of the plant's parts makes it a vital resource in various ethnobotanical practices.

According to a study published by "Phytomedicine : international journal of phytotherapy and phytopharmacology", the leaf of Justicia adhatoda L. is used as part of the Kan Jang oral solution. This aqueous ethanolic extract is administered at a dosage of 30 ml/day, which contains 762 mg of genuine extracts. The extract includes standardized contents of 0.2 mg/ml vasicine and 0.8 mg/ml chicoric acid. In a clinical trial involving 177 patients aged 18-65 years, the treatment was studied over a 5 day period. The study observed that the treatment group showed faster improvement on the third and fourth days of treatment compared to the 100% of patients in the bromhexine or placebo groups.

Traditionally, it is used to treat cough, asthma, phlegm, and bleeding hemorrhoids for both adults and youth. The plant possesses antiarthritis, antiseptic, antimicrobial, anti-tuberculosis, anti-inflammatory, and abortifacient properties. What are the pharmacological activities of Justicia adhatoda?

This plant has diverse pharmacological activities including analgesic, anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, antifungal, and antiviral properties. The analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects allow it to effectively manage pain and reduce swelling in various biological models. Its antimicrobial profile includes the ability to inhibit the growth of various bacteria and fungi that cause infection. Furthermore, the plant demonstrates antiviral capabilities that can help combat certain viral pathogens. These multifaceted therapeutic actions make it a significant subject of medicinal research.

According to a study published by "Frontiers in pharmacology", a gas chromatography-mass spectrometry analysis of the ethyl acetate fraction of Justicia adhatoda identified 21 compounds, 14 of which are recognized for their pharmacological potential. Among the four evaluated fractions, the chloroform fraction exhibited 46.51% anti-inflammatory activity in carrageenan-induced inflammation. The ethyl acetate fraction showed analgesic potential at 300 mg/kg in the acetic acid-induced test and achieved maximum inhibition in the formalin test during both early (74.35%) and late (88.38%) phases. This same ethyl acetate fraction at 300 mg/kg recorded a maximum inhibition of pyrexia at 77.98%. In the DPPH assay, the ethyl acetate fraction demonstrated scavenging potential where 50 μg/ml resulted in 50.40% and 100 μg/ml resulted in 66.74% scavenging.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal quinazoline alkaloids vasicine, vasicinone, vasicoline, vasicolinone, and adhatodine. These specific chemical compounds are primarily responsible for the plant's potent bronchodilator and expectorant properties. Vasicine and its derivatives work effectively to relax smooth muscles in the respiratory tract. Scientific studies often focus on these alkaloids due to their potential applications in treating asthma and other pulmonary conditions. The presence of these bioactive substances makes the species a significant subject in pharmacological research.

The study observed these components in patients aged 18-65 years old during a 5 day period.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is Justicia adhatoda, a medicinal herb used to create various pharmacological preparations including acetone extract, ethanol extracts, CHCl3 fraction, and herbal drinks. Researchers often utilize ethanol extracts and acetone extracts to isolate specific bioactive alkaloids like vasicine for therapeutic use. The CHCl3 fraction is frequently studied in laboratory settings to identify concentrated lipophilic compounds within the plant matter. Additionally, traditional medicine practitioners commonly prepare herbal drinks from the leaves to treat respiratory ailments. These diverse preparation methods allow for the extraction of different chemical profiles suited for specific medical applications.

According to a study published by "Current drug safety", the petroleum ether extract of Justicia adhatoda exhibited maximum activity against Aspergillus fumigatus, Aspergillus flavus and Aspergillus niger. The in vitro minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) for these activities were found to be 156.0-312.0 µg/ml by microbroth dilution and spore germination inhibition assays. Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GCMS) studies were performed for petroleum ether, chloroform and acetone extracts of Justicia adhatoda to identify several bioactive compounds. Extracts isolated from Justicia adhatoda were found to be non-toxic during in vitro and in vivo toxicity evaluations. The antifungal activity along with acute toxicity, cytoto-toxicity and genotoxicity of extract fractions from Justicia adhatoda justifies the use of such screening for new drugs.
