Gentianeella amarella amarella, scientifically known as Gentianeella amarella subsp. amarella, is a perennial herb native to Europe. Renowned for its intensely bitter roots, it has long been utilized in traditional herbalism. This subspecies is frequently employed in digestive formulations to stimulate appetite and support overall gastrointestinal health and function.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Gentianales, it falls under the family Gentianaceae. Specifically, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Gentianella, specifically the subspecies amarella.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across the northern regions of Europe. It can be found growing naturally in the coastal and inland landscapes of Denmark. Its presence is also well-documented throughout the vast territories of Sweden and Norway. Furthermore, the species extends its range into the northern reaches of Finland. Finally, this medicinal plant is also a documented part of the flora in Great Britain.

Plant Parts

This plant, Gentianella amarella subsp. amarella, is a perennial herb that derives its primary medicinal value from its underground structures. The most significant part used in traditional herbalism is the roots, which are characterized by their intense bitterness. These roots are typically harvested and dried to be used in various tinctures and infusions, serving as a potent digestive tonic and bitter agent.
