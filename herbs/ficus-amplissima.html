Ficus amplissima

Ficus ampliissma, commonly known as the Embay Laurel, is a striking tropical evergreen celebrated for its large, glossy leaves. While primarily cherished as an ornamental houseplant for its lush foliage, it holds significance in traditional herbal practices, offering potential therapeutic properties within the diverse and potent Ficus genus.

  • Scientific name: Ficus amplissima
  • Family: Moraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it is placed in the order Rosales and the family Moraceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is established within the genus Ficus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution concentrated within parts of Asia. Within the Indian Subcontinent, it can be found growing in India. Its presence extends across the waters to the islands of the Maldives. Furthermore, the species is documented throughout the nation of Sri Lanka. Finally, its range reaches into the Indo-China region, specifically in Vietnam.

Plant Parts

This plant, Ficus ampliissma, is characterized by its large, glossy, and vibrant green leaves that serve as its most striking feature. When the leaves are damaged or broken, the plant exudes a milky, white substance known as latex. This sticky latex, which can also be collected as a viscous juice, contains various chemical compounds that are often studied for their potential medicinal properties in traditional practices.
