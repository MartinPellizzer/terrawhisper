Heracleum sphondylium subsp. verticillatum

Heracleum sphondylium subsp. verticillatum, commonly known as whorled hogweed, is a distinctive perennial herb found in damp habitats. Renowned for its large, umbelliferous white flower clusters, this subspecies possesses traditional medicinal properties. Historically used to treat digestive ailments and skin inflammations, it remains a significant botanical subject.

  • Scientific name: Heracleum sphondylium subsp. verticillatum
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Apiales, it is classified under the family Apiaceae. It is further identified by the genus Heracleum as the subspecies verticillatum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical range focused within the regions of Southeastern Europe. It can be found growing in the country of Albania, contributing to the local biodiversity. Its presence also extends into Bulgaria, where it occupies suitable habitats. Additionally, the species is distributed throughout Greece, marking a significant part of its southern range. Finally, the plant is documented across the Northwestern Balkan Peninsula, completing its regional distribution pattern.
