Crinum asiaticum

Poison bulb

Crinum asiaticum, commonly known as the Poison Bulb or Giant Crinum Lily, is a striking perennial plant native to tropical Asia. Renowned for its dramatic white blooms, it holds significant value in traditional medicine, where its bulbous roots are utilized to treat various ailments, despite containing potent alkaloids.

  • Scientific name: Crinum asiaticum
  • Family: Amaryllidaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asparagales, it is classified under the family Amaryllidaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Crinum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution concentrated within the Western Indian Ocean. It can be found growing across the diverse landscapes of the Comoros islands. Additionally, the species is established in the various territories of Mauritius and Réunion. Its presence extends to the remote islands of the Chagos Archipelago as well. Finally, the plant is widely distributed throughout the large landmass of Madagascar.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the spider lily, possesses a prominent bulb that serves as its primary storage organ. The bulb is composed of fleshy, overlapping leaf bases that store essential nutrients and moisture, allowing the plant to persist through unfavorable environmental conditions. In medicinal applications, the bulb is often the most significant part of the plant, as it contains the concentrated bioactive compounds used in various traditional treatments.
