Halenia corniculata

What's the taxonomical classification of Halenia corniculata?

Halenia corniculata belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, it falls under the order Gentianales and is a member of the family Gentianaceae. Finally, its specific biological identity is defined by the genus Halenia and the species corniculata.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Halenia corniculata has a small, leafless, and branched stem structure that typically reaches heights of only a few centimeters. The plant lacks true leaves and instead features scale-like structures along its slender, yellowish-green axes. Its reproductive organs consist of small, inconspicuous flowers that emerge directly from the stem. The morphology is highly specialized for its parasitic lifestyle, allowing it to divert energy from its host. This minimalist form is characteristic of many holoparasitic species in its family.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is used to create medicinal herbal preparations such as methanol extracts that contain potent bioactive compounds for therapeutic use. Researchers often utilize methanol to isolate specific phytochemicals from the leaves and roots of the species. These concentrated extracts are frequently studied for their significant antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Beyond simple extracts, the plant can also be processed into various traditional decoctions and standardized medicinal formulations. Such preparations aim to harness the natural chemical profile of the plant to treat diverse health conditions.

According to a study published by "Journal of pharmaceutical and biomedical analysis", Halenia corniculata is widely distributed throughout China and commonly used in traditional Chinese medicine. The study utilized HPLC-DAD/ESI-MS2 to determine the composition and content of chemical compounds from methanol extracts of different plant parts. A total of 35 chemical compounds were detected, including 32 identified compounds consisting of 25 xanthones, 6 flavonoids, and 1 chlorogenic acid. The total compositions of flavonoids and xanthones in Halenia corniculata were found to be approximately comparable to those in G. barbata. Based on chemical compound compositions, the leaf is recommended as the most valuable medicinal section for this species.
