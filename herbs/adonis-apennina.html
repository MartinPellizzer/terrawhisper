Adonis apennina

Adonis apennina, commonly known as Italian Adonis, is a striking perennial herb native to the Mediterranean. Renowned for its vibrant yellow blooms, this medicinal plant contains glycosides used in traditional pharmacology to treat cardiovascular conditions. Its potent bioactive compounds make it a significant subject in modern botanical research.

  • Scientific name: Adonis apennina
  • Family: Ranunculaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Ranunculales, falling into the family Ranunculaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Adonis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Adonis apennina, is referred to by various common names depending on the language and region. In Chinese, it is called 北侧金盏花, with the simplified form 北侧金盏花 used in mainland China and the traditional form 北側金盞花 used in Taiwan. Additionally, in Azerbaijani, the plant is known as Apennin xoruzgülü.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range that stretches across several key regions of Northern Eurasia. In Eastern Europe, its presence is documented within both East European Russia and North European Russia. Moving eastward, the species is widely distributed throughout the vast territory of Siberia. Specifically, it can be found in the Altay region, as well as in Buryatiya. Finally, its distribution extends to the area of Chita, completing its spread across these diverse landscapes.
