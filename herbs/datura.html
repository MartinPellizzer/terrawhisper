Datura

Datura, commonly known as Jimsonweed or Thorn Apple, is a genus of highly toxic perennial herbs in the Solanaceae family. Renowned for its trumpet-shaped flowers, it contains potent tropane alkaloids. While historically used in traditional medicine and rituals, its extreme toxicity makes it dangerous and potentially fatal if misused.

  • Scientific name: Datura
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Solanales and the family Solanaceae, it is classified under the genus Datura.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Datura, possesses various parts that contain potent alkaloids. The large, green leaves are often used in traditional medicine, though they are highly toxic if misused. The sturdy, upright stems support the plant's growth and transport nutrients throughout its structure. One of its most distinctive features is the spiny fruit, which develops into a protective capsule as it matures. Inside this capsule, numerous small, dark seeds are housed; each individual seed contains concentrated medicinal compounds that must be handled with extreme caution due to their intense physiological effects.
