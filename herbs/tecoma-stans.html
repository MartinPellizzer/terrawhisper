Tecoma stans

Arizona yellow bells

Tecoma stans, commonly known as Yellow Bells or Yellow Elder, is a vibrant flowering shrub celebrated for its brilliant golden blooms. Beyond its ornamental appeal, this medicinal plant holds significant ethnobotanical value, traditionally utilized in various cultures to treat inflammatory conditions, skin ailments, and fever due to its bioactive compounds.

  • Scientific name: Tecoma stans
  • Family: Bignoniaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is situated in the order Lamiales and the family Bignoniaceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Tecoma.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several continents and regions. In Southwestern Europe, its presence is specifically noted in Spain. The species also extends into Northern Africa, where it can be found in Tunisia. Moving toward the Atlantic, it inhabits the Macaronesian region, particularly within the Canary Islands. Finally, its range reaches West Tropical Africa, specifically occurring in Gambia and Guinea.

Plant Parts

This plant, Tecoma stans, possesses various parts that are utilized for their medicinal properties. The vibrant green leaves are often harvested to prepare traditional remedies, while the leaf extract serves as a concentrated source of bioactive compounds used in topical or oral applications. The sturdy stems and the textured barks are also significant, frequently decocted to treat inflammation or infections. Additionally, the roots are valued in many folk medicine practices for their specific therapeutic potencies, making the entire structure of the plant a vital resource in herbal healing.
