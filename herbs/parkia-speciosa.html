Bitter bean, scientifically known as Parkia speciosa, is a pungent tropical legume widely used in Southeast Asian cuisine. Often called "stink beans" due to its distinct aroma, this nutritious plant offers significant health benefits, including antioxidant properties and high fiber content, making it a functional staple in traditional medicine.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions in Southeast Asia. Within the Indo-China region, its presence is specifically noted in Thailand. Moving into the Malesia zone, it can be found throughout the island of Borneo. Its habitat also extends to Jawa and the Malay Peninsula. Finally, the species is distributed across the Philippine archipelago.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as bitter bean or petai, utilizes various components for its medicinal and nutritional value. The most widely recognized part is the seeds, which are harvested from mature fruit and are known for their distinct pungent aroma and therapeutic properties. These seeds are contained within long, green, flattened pods that serve as the primary vessel for the edible kernels. Additionally, the roots of the Parkia speciosa are often utilized in traditional medicine, where they are processed to extract compounds believed to assist in treating various ailments.
