Life history

The life history of Salvia chinensis is characterized by its classification as a therophyte, a life form that completes its entire developmental cycle within a single growing season. As a therophyte, the plant survives unfavorable environmental periods, such as extreme cold or drought, by persisting in the form of resistant seeds, which remain dormant in the soil until conditions become favorable for germination. This annual strategy allows the species to rapidly exploit seasonal resources, progressing through germination, vegetative growth, flowering, and seed production before the onset of seasonal stress. In the context of its growth habit and vertical structure, it is categorized as a therophyte, reflecting its reliance on ephemeral growth patterns rather than perennial structures like woody stems or underground rhizomes.

Morphology

The morphology of Salvia chinensis is characterized by its non-woody, herbaceous structure, which lacks significant lignification. As a terrestrial plant, it grows directly in the soil rather than functioning as an epiphyte. While it possesses a growth habit that can lean or spread, it is classified as a self-supporting plant rather than an obligatory or facultative climber. Furthermore, it is an independent organism that does not exhibit any parasitic behavior, maintaining its own nutritional processes without reliance on a host.
