Abrus precatorius

Crab's eye

Abrus precatorius, commonly known as Rosary Pea or Jequirity, is a creeping vine native to tropical regions. Renowned for its striking red seeds with black spots, this plant is chemically potent. While used in traditional medicine, it contains abrin, a highly toxic protein requiring careful handling and expert application.

  • Scientific name: Abrus precatorius
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales. As a member of the Fabaceae family, it is specifically identified by the genus Abrus.

Common Names

This plant, Abrus precatorius, is known by various regional names across different Chinese linguistic contexts. In Simplified Chinese, as used in mainland China, it is referred to as 鸡母珠 (zh-hans and zh-cn), a name that is also the standard designation in Singapore (zh-sg). In Traditional Chinese contexts, such as in Taiwan, it is written as 雞母珠 (zh-hanth), and it carries the same name in Hong Kong (zh-hk).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning multiple regions across the Atlantic and Africa. In the Macaronesia region, it can be found specifically in Cape Verde. Its presence extends significantly into West Tropical Africa, where it is documented in Benin and Burkina. Additionally, the species is located within the territories of Gambia and Guinea-Bissau. Together, these locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in various tropical environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as Rosary Pea, possesses various components that are utilized in traditional medicine. The most distinctive feature is the seeds, which are bright red with a black spot and contain potent toxins. The leaf structure consists of trifoliate arrangements, where each individual leaves unit contributes to the plant's overall photosynthetic capacity. In many pharmacological studies, the seed is specifically isolated for its chemical properties, while the aerial parts are often harvested in their entirety for decoctions and topical applications.
