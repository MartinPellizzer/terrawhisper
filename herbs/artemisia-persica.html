Artemisia persica

Artemisia persica, commonly known as Persian wormwood, is a perennial medicinal herb native to Western Asia. Renowned for its aromatic, silvery foliage, this plant has been utilized in traditional medicine for centuries. It possesses potent antimicrobial, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory properties, making it a valuable subject in modern ethnopharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Artemisia persica
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Under the subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified in the order Asterales and the family Asteraceae. Ultimately, it is identified by its genus, Artemisia, and its specific species name, Artemisia persica.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the vast landscapes of Central Asia. It can be found growing within the borders of Kazakhstan, where it occupies various terrains. The species also thrives in the diverse environments of Kirgizstan. Furthermore, its presence extends to the territories of Turkmenistan and Tadzhikistan. Finally, the geographical range of this medicinal herb includes the country of Uzbekistan.
