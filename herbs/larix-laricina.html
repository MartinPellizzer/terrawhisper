Larix laricina

Hackmatack

Larix laricina, commonly known as the tamarack or American larch, is a unique deciduous conifer native to northern much of North America. While it produces needles like most conifers, it sheds them annually. Historically, various parts of this resilient tree have been utilized in traditional medicinal practices for healing.

  • Scientific name: Larix laricina
  • Family: Pinaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known as Larix laricina, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Pinidae, falling within the order Pinales and family Pinaceae. Ultimately, it is placed within the genus Larix.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as the tamarack or American larch, possesses various components that have historically been utilized for their medicinal properties, most notably the bark. The bark of Larix laricina is characterized by its reddish-brown color and can be harvested to create decoctions or extracts used in traditional folk medicine. While the needles and twigs are also frequently studied for their chemical constituents, the bark remains a significant part of the plant's traditional medicinal profile.
