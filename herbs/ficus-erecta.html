Ficus erecta

Japanese fig

Ficus erecta, commonly known as the upright fig, is a distinctive medicinal plant valued in traditional healing practices. Renowned for its unique botanical structure, this species offers diverse therapeutic potential. Research focuses on its bioactive compounds, which may provide significant antioxidant and anti-inflammatory benefits for various health conditions.

  • Scientific name: Ficus erecta
  • Family: Moraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Rosales and the family Moraceae, it is classified under the genus Ficus. Specifically, this medicinal species is identified as Ficus erecta.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ficus erecta, is referred to by various common names depending on the language and region. In Korean, it is called 천선과나무, while in Japanese, it is commonly known as イヌビワ. In Chinese, the names vary slightly by region and simplified/traditional script, being called 矮小天仙果 in both mainland China (zh-cn) and general Chinese (zh), whereas in Taiwan, it is known as 假枇杷 (zh-tw).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions in East Asia. Within China, it is primarily found in the South-Central and Southeast territories. Its range extends further into Eastern Asia, where it is documented in Japan and Korea. Additionally, the species can be located within the Nansei-shoto islands. These specific locations define the natural habitat and spread of Ficus erecta.
