Syringa pubescens subsp. patula

Syringa pubescens subsp. patula, commonly known as the spreading lilac, is a resilient shrub valued for its fragrant blooms and medicinal potential. Traditionally used in various cultures, it possesses antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. This subspecies is increasingly studied for its bioactive compounds and therapeutic applications in holistic wellness.

  • Scientific name: Syringa pubescens subsp. patula
  • Family: Oleaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales, it is classified under the family Oleaceae and the genus Syringa. Specifically, it is identified as the subspecies patula of the species Syringa pubescens.

Common Names

This plant, Syringa pubescens subsp. patula, is known by several different common names depending on the language and region. In Chinese, it is referred to as 关东巧玲花 or 关东丁香, while in German, it is commonly called Koreanischer Flieder.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution concentrated within East Asia. It is native to the Manchuria region of China, where it can be found growing in its natural habitat. Furthermore, its range extends into the neighboring territory of Korea. These locations represent the primary areas where the subspecies is naturally documented. Consequently, its presence is limited to these specific parts of the northeastern Asian continent.
