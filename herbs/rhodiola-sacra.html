Rhodiola sacra

What's the taxonomical classification of Rhodiola sacra?

Rhodiola sacra belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the taxonomic hierarchy further, it resides in the order Saxifrages and the family Crassulaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by its specific genus, Rhodiola, and its unique species name, sacra.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude regions of the Himalayas, specifically found within parts of Tibet, Nepal, and northern India. It typically thrives in alpine or subalpine zones where rocky terrain and cold temperatures are prevalent. These plants are often located at elevations ranging from 3,000 to 5,000 meters above sea level. The specific microclimates they inhabit usually feature well-drained soil and significant exposure to sunlight. Due to its specialized habitat requirements, the distribution of this species remains restricted to these particular mountain ecosystems.

What are the pharmacological activities of Rhodiola sacra?

This plant has pharmacological activities including active-oxygen scavenging activity, alleviating altitude sickness, anti-hypoxia properties, prolyl endopeptidase inhibitory activity, and reducing fatigue. Its potent antioxidant capabilities allow it to effectively neutralize free radicals through its active-oxygen scavenging activity. The plant also functions as an anti-hypoxia agent, which helps in alleviating altitude sickness by improving cellular oxygen utilization. Furthermore, its prolyl endopeptidase inhibitory activity contributes to its complex biochemical influence on the body. According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Rhodiola sacra water and methanol extracts especially showed strong scavenging activity against superoxide anion radical (O2-). Additionally, 4-hydroxybenzoic acid (2), 3, 4-hydroxycinnamic acid (4), 6-8 and 19 inhibited hydroxyl radical (OH). These active-oxygen scavengers may contribute to anti-aging action.

According to a study published by "Journal of chromatography. B, Analytical technologies in the biomedical and life sciences", Rhodiola sacra is a renowned medicinal Tibetan herb. This plant is widely used in Asia and Europe for alleviating altitude sickness and reducing fatigue. The study identified a total of 42 metabolites, which included 13 phenolic acids, 13 flavonoids, 7 tannins, 2 phenylethanoid glycosides, and 7 others. Six discriminatory chemical markers were selected from these components, such as epicatechin gallate, beta-glucogaullin, citric acid, epigallocatechin, gallic acid, and salidroside. These markers were found to be enriched in the roots with concentrations tens to hundreds of times higher than in the stems and leaves.
