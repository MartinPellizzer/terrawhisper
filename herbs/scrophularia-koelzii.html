Scrophularia koelzii

Scrophularia koelzii, commonly known as Koehl's figwort, is a distinctive medicinal herb native to specific East Asian regions. Characterized by its unique flowering structure, this plant has long been studied for its bioactive compounds. It holds significant interest in traditional pharmacology for its potential therapeutic and healing properties.

  • Scientific name: Scrophularia koelzii
  • Family: Scrophulariaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Lamiales, falling into the family Scrophulariaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Scrophularia, specifically as the species Scrophularia koelzii.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Scrophularia koelzii, is referred to by several different common names depending on the language and region. In English, it is often called the Japanese figwort, while in Japanese, it is known as kousou. Additionally, it is referred to as kousou in Chinese.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution across several regions in Asia. Within Western Asia, its presence is documented in Afghanistan. Moving into the Indian Subcontinent, it can be found growing in Nepal. The species is also widely distributed throughout Pakistan. Finally, its range extends across the mountainous terrain of the West Himalaya.

Plant Parts

This plant, Scrophularia koelzii, is primarily utilized in traditional medicine through its aerial parts. These above-ground structures, which encompass the stems, leaves, and flowers, contain the concentrated bioactive compounds necessary for its therapeutic effects. By harvesting the aerial parts, practitioners can access the plant's medicinal properties while leaving the root system intact within the soil.
