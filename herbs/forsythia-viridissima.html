Forsythia viridissima

Forsythia viridissima, commonly known as Forsythia, is a versatile medicinal shrub valued in traditional practices. Renowned for its antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties, its bark and flowers are frequently utilized to treat respiratory ailments, skin infections, and fevers. This resilient plant remains a cornerstone in holistic herbal pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Forsythia viridissima
  • Family: Oleaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further organized into the order Lamiales and the family Oleaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is established under the genus Forsythia.

Common Names

This plant, Forsythia viridissima, is known by various common names depending on the region and language. In German, it is referred to as Grüne Forsythie, while in Chinese, it is known as 金钟花 (used in both simplified and traditional contexts, though specifically written as 金钟花 in zh-cn and 金鐘花 in zh-tw). In Japanese, the species is called シナレンギョウ.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning several key regions across Eurasia. In Southwestern Europe, its presence can be identified specifically within Spain. Moving toward Southeastern Europe, it is also documented to grow in Bulgaria. Within the vast territory of China, the species is widely distributed across both the South-Central and Southeast regions. Finally, its distribution extends into Eastern Asia, where it is native to Japan.

Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as Forsythia, utilizes its twigs as a primary source for medicinal applications. In traditional herbal practices, the dried twigs are harvested and processed to extract therapeutic compounds believed to help alleviate symptoms such as fever, inflammation, and various types of swelling. These woody stems contain the active principles necessary for their pharmacological use, making the collection and preparation of the twigs a vital component of the plant's medicinal profile.
