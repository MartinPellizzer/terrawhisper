Cinchona officinalis

Cinchona officinalis, commonly known as the quinine tree, is a historic medicinal plant native to the Andean forests. Renowned for its alkaloid-rich bark, it serves as the primary natural source of quinine. This essential compound revolutionized medicine as a powerful antimalarial agent, playing a crucial role in global healthcare.

  • Scientific name: Cinchona officinalis
  • Family: Rubiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Cinchona officinalis, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Gentianales and the family Rubiaceae. Finally, it is situated within the genus Cinchona.

Plant Parts

This plant, Cinchona officinalis, is highly valued in pharmacology primarily for its medicinal properties derived from its specialized tissues. The most significant parts utilized for the extraction of quinine are the bark and the stem bark. These woody layers contain high concentrations of alkaloids, which are harvested from the mature stem bark to treat conditions such as malaria. By carefully stripping the bark from the stems, practitioners can access the chemical compounds essential for therapeutic applications.
