Rafflesia kerrii

Rafflesia kerrii, commonly known as the giant corpse flower, is a remarkable parasitic plant native to Southeast Asia. Lacking leaves, stems, or true roots, it produces massive, spectacular blooms that emit a pungent odor to attract pollinators. This rare species is a biological marvel, symbolizing the jungle's extraordinary biodiversity.

  • Scientific name: Rafflesia kerrii
  • Family: Rafflesiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, it is uniquely classified under the family Rafflesiaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Rafflesia.

Distribution

This plant possesses a specific and limited range across Southeast Asia. Its presence can be traced within the Indo-China region, specifically in Thailand. Additionally, it is found within the Malesia zone, occurring in Malaya. These distinct locations represent the primary areas where the species can be identified. Consequently, its geographical distribution is tied to these particular tropical territories.

Plant Parts

This plant is a unique parasitic species known for its massive and unusual morphology, as it lacks stems, leaves, or true roots. The most prominent and significant part of Rafflesia kerrii is its enormous flower, which can grow to an impressive size and is characterized by its fleshy, reddish-brown petals marked with white spots. Because the plant lives entirely within the tissue of its host vine, the development of this spectacular bloom is the only visible sign of its presence above ground.
