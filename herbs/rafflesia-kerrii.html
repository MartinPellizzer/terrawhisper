Rafflesia kerrii, scientifically known as Rafflesia kerrii, is a remarkable parasitic plant native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for producing some of the world's largest flowers, it lacks true leaves, stems, or roots. This extraordinary species relies entirely on host vines, offering unique insights into evolutionary biology and rainforest biodiversity.

Plant Parts

This plant is a unique parasitic species known for its massive and unusual morphology, as it lacks stems, leaves, or true roots. The most prominent and significant part of Rafflesia kerrii is its enormous flower, which can grow to an impressive size and is characterized by its fleshy, reddish-brown petals marked with white spots. Because the plant lives entirely within the tissue of its host vine, the development of this spectacular bloom is the only visible sign of its presence above ground.
