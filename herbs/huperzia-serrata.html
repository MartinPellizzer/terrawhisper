Scientific literature: Extensive (174 studies) · ★★★★☆

Toothed club-moss, scientifically known as Huperzia serrata, is a perennial herbaceous plant prized in traditional medicine. It contains huperzine A, a potent acetylcholinesterase inhibitor. This alkaloid is widely studied for its neuroprotective properties, showing significant potential in enhancing cognitive function and managing neurodegenerative conditions like Alzheimer's disease.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, specifically falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Lycopodiidae. Within the order Lycopodiales and the family Lycopodiaceae, it is classified under the genus Huperzia. This taxonomic lineage identifies Huperzia serrata as a distinct member of the clubmoss group.

Plant Parts

This plant, Huperzia serrata, is a lycophyte where the medicinal properties are often derived from the whole plant, though specific components are frequently utilized in pharmacological studies. The structure is characterized by upright, dichotomously branching stems that support the foliage. The leaves are small, scale-like, and arranged densely along these stems, while the young leaves at the growing tips are particularly significant due to their high metabolic activity. Below the ground, the plant develops a rhizomatous system that functions as the roots, anchoring the species within its forest habitat.
