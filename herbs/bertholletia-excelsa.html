Bertholletia excelsa

Brazil-nut tree

Bertholletia excelsa, commonly known as the Brazil nut tree, is a majestic rainforest giant prized for its nutrient-dense seeds. Beyond its culinary importance, this medicinal powerhouse offers potent antioxidant properties, essential fatty acids, and minerals, playing a vital role in supporting cardiovascular health and fighting oxidative stress in humans.

  • Scientific name: Bertholletia excelsa
  • Family: Lecythidaceae
  • Native range: Pacific

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized into the order Ericales and the family Lecythidaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Bertholletia.

Distribution

This plant, known as Bertholletia excelsa, exhibits a specific geographical range across several tropical regions. In the Northwestern Pacific, its distribution includes the Caroline Islands. Moving to the South American continent, it is widely found throughout the northern territories. Specifically, it thrives within the borders of French Guiana, Guyana, and Suriname. Finally, its presence extends into the northernmost parts of Venezuela.

Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as the Brazil nut tree, is highly valued for its nutrient-dense seeds, which are the primary source of its commercially significant oil. This oil is extracted from the kernels of the seeds and is prized in both culinary and cosmetic industries due to its high concentration of essential fatty acids and antioxidant properties. While the tree itself is a massive component of the Amazonian ecosystem, it is the specific oil derived from its fruit that defines its medicinal and economic importance.
