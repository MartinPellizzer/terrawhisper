Ocimum lamiiifolium, scientifically known as Ocimum lamiiifolium, is a potent medicinal herb belonging to the Lamiaceae family. Commonly referred to as "False Basil," this aromatic plant is celebrated in traditional medicine for its diverse therapeutic properties, offering significant anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant benefits to support holistic wellness.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several regions of the African continent. Within West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in Cameroon and Gabon. Its presence also extends to the Democratic Republic of the Congo in the same central region. Additionally, the species is located in Rwanda, further establishing its footprint in West-Central Tropical Africa. Life history

The life history of Ocimum lamiifolium is characterized by its status as a perennial plant, allowing it to persist through multiple growing seasons rather than completing its life cycle in a single year. In terms of its growth habit and structural architecture, it is classified as a chamaephyte, meaning its perennable buds are located close to or just above the soil surface, which influences how it manages environmental stresses and regenerative growth. This combination of a perennial lifecycle and a chamaephyte life form enables the species to maintain a stable presence within its ecological niche, utilizing its low-growing, bud-protected structure to endure seasonal variations while continuing its reproductive cycles over time.
