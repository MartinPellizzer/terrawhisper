Commiphora

Commiphora, commonly known as myrrh, is a genus of small trees and shrubs prized for their aromatic resin. Extracted from the bark, this medicinal substance has been used for millennia in traditional medicine to treat inflammation, promote wound healing, and provide antiseptic properties, making it a cornerstone of aromatherapy.

  • Scientific name: Commiphora
  • Family: Burseraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindales. Finally, it is a member of the family Burseraceae and is identified by the genus Commiphora.

Plant Parts

This plant, belonging to the genus Commiphora, is highly valued for its various medicinal components, most notably the aromatic oleo-gum-resins harvested from its bark. The most famous product derived from this genus is myrrh, a highly prized resinous substance used globally in traditional medicine and perfumery. These oleogum resins are extracted through incisions in the stem, providing a potent source of therapeutic compounds. In addition to the resin, the leaf of the plant is also studied for its specific phytochemical properties and potential biological activities.
