Corkwoods, scientifically known as Commiphora, is a diverse genus of small trees and shrubs renowned for producing aromatic resins. Widely distributed across Africa and Asia, these plants offer significant medicinal value. Their resins, often used in traditional healing, possess potent antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and wound-healing properties essential to pharmacology.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several distinct regions of the African continent. In Northern Africa, its presence is documented within the borders of Egypt. Moving toward the West Tropical Africa region, the species can be found in Benin and Burkina. Additionally, the plant is known to inhabit the territories of Gambia and Ghana.
