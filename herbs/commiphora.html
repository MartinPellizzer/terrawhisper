Corkwoods, scientifically known as Commiphora, is a diverse genus of small trees and shrubs renowned for producing aromatic resins. Widely distributed across Africa and Asia, these plants offer significant medicinal value. Their resins, often used in traditional healing, possess potent antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and wound-healing properties essential to pharmacology.

Plant Parts

This plant, belonging to the genus Commiphora, is highly valued for its various medicinal components, most notably the aromatic oleo-gum-resins harvested from its bark. The most famous product derived from this genus is myrrh, a highly prized resinous substance used globally in traditional medicine and perfumery. These oleogum resins are extracted through incisions in the stem, providing a potent source of therapeutic compounds. In addition to the resin, the leaf of the plant is also studied for its specific phytochemical properties and potential biological activities.
