Reproduction

The reproduction of Leucojum aestivum is characterized by a primarily sexual process, as asexual reproduction is absent. The flowering period typically begins in April and extends through June, though the timing can be variable. This plant relies on biotic pollination syndromes, specifically utilizing insects, with bees being a noted pollinator. Interestingly, self-fertilization is absent in this species, necessitating cross-pollination. Following successful fertilization, the plant produces fruit in the form of a capsule. These capsules are dehiscent, meaning they split open at maturity to release the seeds. The seeds vary in mass, with a minimum weight of approximately 0.02258 g, a mean mass of 0.045320926 g, and a maximum mass of 0.05338 g. Seed dispersal is categorized as unspecialized, though it also exhibits endozoochorous characteristics, where seeds are dispersed via the digestive tracts of animals.
