Caryocar coriaceum

Caryocar coriaceum, commonly known as pequi-do-mato, is a remarkable medicinal plant native to the Brazilian Cerrado. Renowned for its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, it offers significant therapeutic potential. This resilient species is increasingly studied for its bioactive compounds, providing valuable natural resources for modern pharmacology and traditional wellness.

  • Scientific name: Caryocar coriaceum
  • Family: Caryocaraceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, it is classified under the family Caryocaraceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Caryocar.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Caryocar coriaceum, possesses various parts that are utilized for their medicinal and nutritional properties. The large fruits serve as the primary source of interest, particularly the nutrient-dense fruit pulp which is rich in vitamins and essential fatty acids. Extracted from these components is a highly valued fixed oil, which is frequently used in traditional applications for its emollient and healing qualities. Additionally, the bark of the tree is often employed in decoctions, contributing to the plant's diverse range of therapeutic uses in folk medicine.
