Sea hibiscus, scientifically known as Hibiscus tiliaceus, is a perennial shrub renowned for its large, striking flowers and significant therapeutic potential. Widely used in traditional medicine, it possesses antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and wound-healing properties. This versatile plant serves as a vital resource in herbal pharmacology and nutritional wellness.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Hibiscus tiliaceus is facilitated through a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically involving birds as key pollinators. The plant produces characteristic red flowers to attract these agents. Following successful pollination, the plant develops a capsule-type fruit that is dehiscent in nature. These fruits vary in size, with a mean length of 2 cm, ranging from a minimum of 1.3 cm to a maximum of 2.8 cm. Within these capsules, the seeds are produced with a mean length of 4 mm (ranging from 4 mm to 4.5 mm) and a mean mass of approximately 0.0174775 g, with individual seed masses spanning from a minimum of 0.0156 g to a maximum of 0.02052 g. The dispersal of the species follows a hydrochorous syndrome, meaning the seeds are primarily dispersed by water.
