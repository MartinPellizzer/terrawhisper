Hibiscus tiliaceus

Sea hibiscus

Hibiscus tiliaceus, commonly known as the Confederate Rose, is a striking ornamental shrub valued for its large, trumpet-shaped flowers. Beyond its beauty, this medicinal plant is prized in traditional practices for its potential anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and wound-healing properties, making it a fascinating subject for ethnobotanical study and wellness.

  • Scientific name: Hibiscus tiliaceus
  • Family: Malvaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae, the order Malvales, and the family Malvaceae. Ultimately, it is identified by its genus, Hibiscus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Hibiscus tiliacus, is recognized by various common names across different cultures and languages. In Vietnam, it is called Tra làm chiếu, while in Bulgaria, it is referred to as морски хибискус. In Chinese-speaking regions, it is known as 黃槿 in both Traditional Chinese (zh-tw) and Simplified Chinese (zh) scripts. Additionally, in Arabic, the plant is called خطمي ساحلي.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found within the diverse landscapes of West Tropical Africa. Its presence is specifically documented in the nation of Gambia, where it thrives in local ecosystems. It also grows across various regions of Guinea, including Guinea-Bissau. Furthermore, the species is distributed throughout Ghana and the Ivory Coast. These specific locations highlight the plant's importance within this particular African climatic zone.
