Cuscuta japonica

Cuscuta japonica, commonly known as Japanese dodder, is a parasitic plant characterized by its leafless, yellow, thread-like stems. Lacking chlorophyll, it survives by attaching to host plants through specialized structures called haustoria. In traditional medicine, various parts are utilized for their unique bioactive properties and therapeutic potential in various cultures.

  • Scientific name: Cuscuta japonica
  • Family: Convolvulaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Solanales and the family Convolvulaceae. Its taxonomic identity is completed by its designation within the genus Cuscuta.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across the northernmost reaches of Asia. Within the vast territory of Siberia, it can be found in the Chitá region as well as in Yakutiya. Its presence extends significantly into the Russian Far East, where it inhabits the Amur area. Additionally, the species is documented throughout the Khabarovsk territory. Finally, its geographical range includes the Primorye region of the Russian Far East.

Plant Parts

This plant, Cuscuta japonica, is a parasitic climber that lacks functional leaves and roots, relying instead on its specialized structure to thrive. The lifecycle begins with the seed, which is capable of remaining dormant in the soil until it detects a suitable host plant nearby. Once germinated, the plant develops a twining stem that wraps tightly around its host, using specialized organs called haustoria to penetrate the host's tissues and extract essential nutrients and water.
