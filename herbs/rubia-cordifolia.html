Rubia cordifolia

Indian madder

Rubia cordifolia, commonly known as Manjistha in Ayurveda, is a perennial climbing shrub highly valued in traditional medicine. Renowned for its potent blood-purifying and anti-inflammatory properties, its roots contain vibrant red pigments. It is widely utilized to support skin health, detoxify the body, and promote overall lymphatic circulation.

  • Scientific name: Rubia cordifolia
  • Family: Rubiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Gentianales, it is situated in the Rubiaceae family. Finally, it is identified by the genus Rubia.

Its presence extends significantly into West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically within Burundi, Rwanda, and the DR Congo. Additionally, the species is located in the Northeast Tropical Africa region, specifically in Ethiopia. Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as Manjistha, is primarily valued for its medicinal properties derived from its underground structures. While botanical descriptions focus on the therapeutic potency of the roots, specifically the dried root mass which contains the active coloring agents, the plant's growth habit involves a thickened, creeping rhizome that helps it spread through the soil. These underground parts are harvested and processed to create various traditional remedies used in Ayurvedic medicine.
