Indian madder

Rubia cordifolia · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (388 studies) · ★★★★★

Indian madder, scientifically known as Rubia cordifolia, is a perennial climbing shrub native to the Indian subcontinent. Renowned in Ayurvedic medicine, its roots contain valuable rubiadin, used for treating skin disorders, inflammation, and blood purification. This versatile plant remains a cornerstone in traditional healing and natural dye production.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Gentianales, it is situated in the Rubiaceae family. Finally, it is identified by the genus Rubia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution that spans across multiple continents and diverse climates. In Southeastern Europe, it can be found growing in the country of Greece. Its presence extends significantly into West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically within Burundi, Rwanda, and the DR Congo. Additionally, the species is located in the Northeast Tropical Africa region, specifically in Ethiopia. Through these varied locations, the plant demonstrates a wide range of ecological adaptability.
