Viburnum opulus · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (693 studies) · ★★★★★

Viburnum opulus, scientifically known as Viburnum opulus, is a deciduous shrub native to Europe and Asia. Commonly called Guelder-rose, it is prized for its striking red berries and ornamental foliage. In traditional medicine, its bark and roots are utilized for their anti-inflammatory and antispasmodic properties to support reproductive health.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Dipsacales, it is classified under the family Viburnaceae. Finally, it is placed in the genus Viburnum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across the northern regions of Europe. It can be found growing naturally throughout the various landscapes of Denmark. Its presence is also well-documented in the northern territories of Finland and Norway. Furthermore, the species is widely distributed across the islands of Great Britain and Ireland. These locations collectively highlight the plant's preference for Northern European climates.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Guelder-rose, possesses distinct morphological features that are utilized in various traditional applications. The leaves are characterized by their opposite arrangement and deeply lobed, maple-like shape, which provides a lush green canopy during the growing season. Most notably, the plant produces bright red, translucent fruits that appear in clusters; while these berries are often visually striking, they are typically consumed only after being processed to mitigate their natural toxicity.
