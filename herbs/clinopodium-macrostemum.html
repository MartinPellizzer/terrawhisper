Clinopodium macrostemum

Clinopodium macrosystemum, commonly known as a species of mint, is a versatile medicinal plant valued in traditional healing. Characterized by its distinct foliage and aromatic properties, it offers significant therapeutic potential. Research suggests its bioactive compounds possess antimicrobial and antioxidant qualities, making it a subject of growing pharmacological interest.

  • Scientific name: Clinopodium macrostemum
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae, it is classified under the genus Clinopodium.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Clinopodium macrosstemum, is recognized by various common names depending on the region and language. In English, it is often referred to as large-stemmed clary or large-stemmed knotgrass. Within Spanish-speaking communities, it is known as menta de monte or menta de campo, while in Portuguese, it is called menta-do-campo. Additionally, in French, the plant is commonly called menthe des champs.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad presence across various regions throughout the country of Mexico. Its distribution begins in the central parts of the nation, where it is found in Mexico Central. It also extends into the coastal and interior areas of Mexico Northeast and Mexico Gulf. Furthermore, the species can be identified within the diverse landscapes of Mexico Northwest. Finally, its geographical reach is completed by its occurrence in Mexico Southwest.
