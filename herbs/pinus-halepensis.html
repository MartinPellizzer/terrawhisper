Aleppo Pine

Pinus halepensis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (47 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Aleppo Pine, scientifically known as Pinus halepensis, is a resilient coniferous tree native to the Mediterranean region. Renowned for its aromatic resin and essential oils, it holds significant medicinal value. Traditionally, its extracts are utilized for their antiseptic, anti-inflammatory, and expectorant properties, supporting respiratory and skin health.

Distribution

This plant, known as Pinus halepensis, is widely distributed across several regions in Southwestern Europe. Its presence is notably documented within the Balearic Islands, which form part of its Mediterranean range. The species also thrives in the coastal and mountainous landscapes of Corsica. Furthermore, it can be found growing throughout various territories in mainland France. Finally, the plant's geographical reach extends to the island of Sardinia and the coastal areas of Portugal.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as the Monterey pine, utilizes various components for its biological functions and traditional medicinal interests. Its long, needle-like leaves are arranged in bundles, serving as the primary site for photosynthesis and transpiration. The protective bark is thick and deeply furrowed, providing essential defense for the tree's internal vascular system. While botanically distinct from flowering plants, the green cones emerge as immature reproductive structures that eventually mature into woody seed-bearing units. Although the seeds contained within these structures are often studied for their nutritional properties, they are technically the result of the gymnosperm's reproductive cycle rather than true botanical fruits.
