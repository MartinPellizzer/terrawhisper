Aleppo Pine

Pinus halepensis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (47 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Aleppo Pine, scientifically known as Pinus halepensis, is a resilient coniferous tree native to the Mediterranean region. Renowned for its aromatic resin and essential oils, it holds significant medicinal value. Traditionally, its extracts are utilized for their antiseptic, anti-inflammatory, and expectorant properties, supporting respiratory and skin health.

The reproduction of Pinus halepensis is characterized by a monoecious sexual reproductive system, as asexual reproduction is absent. The flowering process typically commences in February and concludes in May, featuring brown-colored flowers. As an abiotic pollinator, the plant relies on a wind-based pollination syndrome to facilitate fertilization. Following successful pollination, the plant develops a cone-type fruit. These cones exhibit significant morphological variation, with fruit lengths ranging from a minimum of 4 cm to a maximum of 10 cm (averaging 8.7 cm), and widths ranging from 2.5 cm to 4.2 cm (averaging 4 cm). The seeds produced within these cones possess a mean mass of approximately 0.0228 g, with individual seed masses fluctuating between a minimum of 0.01619 g and a maximum of 0.0447 g. Finally, the dispersal of the species is achieved through an anemochorous syndrome, utilizing the wind to distribute seeds away from the parent plant.
