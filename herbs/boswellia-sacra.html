Frankincense tree, scientifically known as Boswellia sacra, is a revered species native to the arid regions of the Arabian Peninsula. Renowned for its aromatic resin, this tree has been vital to spiritual rituals and traditional medicine for millennia, offering potent anti-inflammatory properties and therapeutic benefits in holistic healing.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution spanning across Northeast Tropical Africa and the Arabian Peninsula. In the African region, its presence is notably documented within Somalia. Moving into the Arabian Peninsula, it can be found throughout the various Gulf States. The species also maintains a significant range within the country of Oman. Finally, its geographical reach extends into the territory of Yemen.

Plant Parts

This plant, Boswellia sacra, is highly valued for the various medicinal components derived from its structure, particularly the exudates produced from its trunk. The most significant part utilized is the oleo-gum resin, which is harvested after making incisions in the bark. This substance is often categorized more broadly as a gum resin or simply as resins, depending on its specific chemical composition and consistency. Within these resins, various bioactive compounds are stored that provide therapeutic benefits. Additionally, certain traditional applications may involve the use of the underbark, which contains concentrated botanical properties, as well as the purified resin extracted directly from the tree's wounded tissue.
