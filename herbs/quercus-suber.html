Quercus suber

Cork oak

Quercus suber, commonly known as the cork oak, is a resilient evergreen tree native to the Mediterranean. Renowned for its thick, insulating bark, it holds significant medicinal potential. Traditionally, its extracts have been studied for anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and antimicrobial properties, offering promising therapeutic applications in modern natural medicine.

  • Scientific name: Quercus suber
  • Family: Fagaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fagales and the family Fagaceae, it is classified under the genus Quercus.

Distribution

This plant, known scientifically as Quercus suber, is primarily distributed across the Mediterranean region of Southwestern Europe. Its habitat includes the island of Corsica, which provides an ideal climate for its growth. The species is also widely found throughout the coastal and inland areas of France. Additionally, it is a prominent feature of the landscapes in Portugal. Finally, its geographical range extends to the islands of the Balearic Islands and Sardegna.
