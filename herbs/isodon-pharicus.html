Isodon pharicus

Isodon pharicus, commonly known as the Eastern Hopbush, is a versatile medicinal shrub native to North America. Traditionally utilized in various herbal practices, its leaves and stems possess unique bioactive properties. This resilient plant offers significant potential for pharmacological research, particularly in exploring its diverse therapeutic and antioxidant capabilities.

  • Scientific name: Isodon pharicus
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Isodon chaenopus (often referred to as Isodon pharicus), belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. It is ultimately classified under the genus Isodon.

Distribution

This plant is native to several distinct regions across Asia. In China, its presence is noted within the South-Central provinces and the high-altitude areas of Tibet. Its distribution extends into the Indian Subcontinent, specifically within the East Himalaya range. Additionally, the species can be found growing throughout the country of Nepal. These specific locations define the natural geographical range of Isodon pharicus.
