Citrus assamensis

Citrus assamenis, commonly known as the Assamese lemon, is a unique citrus species native to the Brahmaputra Valley. Renowned for its intense aroma and distinct flavor profile, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. It is widely utilized in traditional medicine and culinary practices for its nutritional and therapeutic properties.

  • Scientific name: Citrus assamensis
  • Family: Rutaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindales. Finally, it is a member of the family Rutaceae and falls under the genus Citrus.

Common Names

This plant, Citrus assamensis, is known by various names depending on the region and language. In English, it is commonly referred to as the Assamese orange, while in Bengali, it is known as the Assamese kumra. In Assamese, the plant is called the Asomiya kumra.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found within the vast landscapes of the Indian Subcontinent. Its natural habitat is specifically centered in the lush regions of Assam. Beyond the borders of India, it also maintains a significant presence in Bangladesh. These areas provide the ideal environmental conditions necessary for its growth and survival. Consequently, its geographical range is concentrated within these specific subtropical territories.
