Aegiphila integrifolia

Aegiphila integrifolia, commonly known as the White Aegiphila, is a versatile tropical shrub prized for its medicinal properties. Found across Southeast Asia, its leaves and bark are traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and fever, offering a natural resource for holistic healing and wellness.

  • Scientific name: Aegiphila integrifolia
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Aegiphila.

Common Names

This plant, Aegiphila integrifolia, is known by various names across different linguistic and regional contexts. In English, it is frequently referred to as the Siamese nettle, while in Spanish, it is commonly called muerde-pata. In Portuguese, the species is known as muerde-pata, and in Thai, it is called ka-pau.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several tropical regions in the Americas. In Central America, its presence is specifically noted within the borders of Panamá. Moving toward the Caribbean, it can be found inhabiting the islands of Trinidad and Tobago. The species also extends into Northern South America, covering several distinct territories. Specifically, its range includes the nations of Guyana, Suriname, and French Guiana.
