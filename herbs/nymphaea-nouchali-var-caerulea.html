This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Nymphaeales, it is a member of the family Nymphaeaceae. Its specific taxonomic placement concludes within the genus Nymphaea as the variety caerulea.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across multiple regions of the African continent. In Northern Africa, its presence is specifically noted within the country of Egypt. Moving towards the central regions, it is widely found throughout West-Central Tropical Africa. This includes its occurrence in Burundi, Rwanda, and the Congo. Furthermore, the species is also documented to grow within the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the blue lotus, features distinct morphological structures that contribute to its medicinal significance. The leaves are large, circular, and float upon the water's surface, providing a broad expanse of green that helps the plant photosynthesize effectively in aquatic environments. Most notably, the flowers are striking, star-shaped blooms that emerge from the water, displaying a beautiful blue hue and possessing various bioactive compounds that are often the focus of traditional medicinal uses.
