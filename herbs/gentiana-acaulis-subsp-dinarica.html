Gentiana acaulis subsp. dinarica

Gentiana acaulis subsp. dinarica, commonly known as the Dinaric gentian, is a rare medicinal perennial endemic to the Balkan Peninsula. Renowned for its striking blue flowers, this subspecies contains bitter glycosides used traditionally to stimulate digestion, support liver function, and treat various gastrointestinal ailments through its potent phytochemical properties.

  • Scientific name: Gentiana acaulis subsp. dinarica
  • Family: Gentianaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Gentianales, it is classified under the family Gentianaceae and the genus Gentiana. Specifically, this medicinal variety is identified as Gentiana acaulis subsp. dinarica.

Common Names

This plant, Gentiana acaulis subsp. dinarica, is known by various regional names depending on the local language. In English, it is commonly referred to as the Dinaric stemless gentian, while in Croatian, it is known as dinarska bezstelnica. In Serbian, the plant is called dinarska bezstelnica gentiana, and in Bosnian, it is also identified as dinarska bezstelnica gentiana.
