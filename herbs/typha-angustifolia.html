Lesser Bulrush

Typha angustifolia · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (34 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Lesser Bulrush, scientifically known as Typha angustifolia, is a resilient aquatic perennial found in wetlands globally. Renowned for its medicinal versatility, this plant offers therapeutic benefits through its rhizomes and pollen. Traditionally used to treat inflammation and digestive issues, it remains a significant subject in ethnobotanical and pharmacological research.

Plant Parts

This plant, Typha angustifolia, possesses a unique reproductive structure where the flowering spike is divided into distinct sections. The female flowers are located on the lower part of the spike, providing the foundation for seed development, while the upper section contains the male flowers that produce abundant pollen for reproduction. Supporting these delicate reproductive organs are the filaments, which hold the anthers in place to facilitate the dispersal of genetic material. Beyond its reproductive components, the long, slender leaves are a prominent feature, playing a crucial role in photosynthesis and supporting the plant's overall vitality in its wetland habitat.
