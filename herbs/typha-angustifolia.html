Scientific literature: Very Sparse (34 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Lesser Bulrush, scientifically known as Typha angustifolia, is a resilient aquatic perennial found in wetlands globally. Renowned for its medicinal versatility, this plant offers therapeutic benefits through its rhizomes and pollen. Traditionally used to treat inflammation and digestive issues, it remains a significant subject in ethnobotanical and pharmacological research.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Typha angustifolia is characterized by a monoeciously sexual reproductive strategy, with asexual reproduction being notably absent. The flowering period typically commences in June and concludes in August. The pollination process is driven by abiotic factors, specifically utilizing a wind-based pollination syndrome. Following successful pollination, the plant produces fruit in the form of an achene. These fruits are indehiscent and maintain a consistent length of 0.15 cm (with a minimum, mean, and maximum all measuring 0.15 cm). The resulting seeds are exceptionally lightweight, with a mean mass of 0.0001326 g, ranging from a minimum of 3.9e-05 g to a maximum of 0.00031 g. Dispersal is achieved through an anemochorous syndrome, meaning the seeds are primarily dispersed by the wind. Self-fertilization in this species is absent, ensuring genetic diversity through its reproductive mechanisms.
