Bauhinia coulteri

Bauhinia coulteri, commonly known as Coulter's orchid tree, is a resilient desert shrub prized for its striking, butterfly-shaped blossoms. Beyond its ornamental beauty, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditional practitioners utilize its various parts to treat diverse ailments, highlighting its important role in regional medicinal landscapes and biodiversity.

  • Scientific name: Bauhinia coulteri
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Fabales and is a member of the family Fabaceae. Specifically, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Bauhinia, where it is known as Bauhinia coulteri.

Common Names

This plant, Bauhinia coulteri, is known by several different common names depending on the region and language. In English, it is most frequently referred to as Coulter's orchid tree or the desert orchid tree. When translated into Spanish, it is commonly called palo arbol de orquídea de Coulter.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Bauhinia coulteri, is characterized by its distinctively shaped leaves, which are a primary focus for botanical study and potential medicinal application. These leaves feature a unique bilobed structure, often resembling the shape of a butterfly or a camel's foot, with a deep cleft at the apex that divides the leaf into two symmetrical halves. Beyond their striking appearance, the leaves serve as vital photosynthetic organs that support the plant's overall health and contain various phytochemical compounds that contribute to its medicinal properties.
