Allium sativum

Garlic plant

Allium sativum, commonly known as garlic, is a versatile medicinal plant celebrated for its potent therapeutic properties. Rich in bioactive compounds like allicin, it offers significant antibacterial, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory benefits. Used globally in traditional medicine, garlic supports cardiovascular health, strengthens immunity, and promotes overall physiological well-being.

  • Scientific name: Allium sativum
  • Family: Amaryllidaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Allium sativum, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Asparagales and the family Amaryllidaceae. It is further categorized into the genus Allium.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Allium sativum, is recognized by various common names across different languages and cultures. In Chinese, it is referred to as 蒜 (suàn), while in Kyrgyz, it is called Сарымсак. In Poland, the common name is czosnek pospoly, and in the Basque language, it is known as baratxuri. Additionally, in Bosnia and Herzegovina, it is frequently called bijeli luk.

Distribution

This plant, Allium sativum, exhibits a widespread presence across various regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its distribution includes the territories of Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward the center of the continent, the species is also commonly found throughout Middle Europe. Specifically, it is established within the borders of Austria and the Czechia-Slovakia region. Furthermore, the plant maintains a significant presence within Germany, completing its regional range.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Allium sativum, is a perennial herb characterized by its distinct underground structure. The most significant part used for medicinal and culinary purposes is the bulb, which is composed of several individual segments known as cloves. These bulbs serve as the primary storage organ for the plant's nutrients and active compounds. Above the ground, the plant develops long, green, hollow leaves that are essential for photosynthesis. While the plant can produce seeds for reproduction, the therapeutic potency is most concentrated within the fleshy layers of the bulbous base.
