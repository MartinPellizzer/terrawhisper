Macadamia integrifolia

Macadamia nut species

Macadamia integrifolia, commonly known as the macadamia nut, is a versatile evergreen tree native to Australia's subtropical rainforests. Renowned for its rich, buttery kernels, this plant also holds significant medicinal value. Its oils and extracts are prized for their antioxidant properties, supporting skin health and reducing systemic inflammation.

  • Scientific name: Macadamia integrifolia
  • Family: Proteaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Proteales, it falls within the family Proteaceae. Specifically, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Macadamia, with the species being Macadamia integrifolia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across two major continents. In Africa, its range extends through South Tropical Africa, specifically within Mozambique. It is also well-established in Southern Africa, where it can be found in the Northern Provinces and KwaZulu-Natal. Moving to the Australian continent, the species is widely distributed across the state of New South Wales. Furthermore, it maintains a significant presence throughout the subtropical regions of Queensland.
