Indian kudzu

Pueraria tuberosa · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (68 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Indian kudzu, scientifically known as Pueraria tuberosa, is a versatile climbing legume native to India. Highly valued in traditional Ayurvedic medicine, this perennial herb is renowned for its potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective properties. It serves as a vital natural remedy for managing various ailments and promoting overall wellness.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fabales, it is a member of the Fabaceae family. Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across the diverse landscapes of the Indian Subcontinent. It is widely recognized as being native to various regions within India, including the specific area of Assam. Beyond these borders, the species can also be found flourishing in the neighboring country of Bangladesh. Its presence extends into the rugged terrains of the East Himalaya, showcasing its adaptability to different altitudes. Furthermore, the plant is well-established within the mountainous landscapes of Nepal.
