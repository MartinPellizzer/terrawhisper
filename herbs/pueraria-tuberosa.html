Indian kudzu, scientifically known as Pueraria tuberosa, is a versatile climbing legume native to India. Highly valued in traditional Ayurvedic medicine, this perennial herb is renowned for its potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective properties. It serves as a vital natural remedy for managing various ailments and promoting overall wellness.

Physiology

The physiology of Pueraria tuberosa is characterized by a highly efficient metabolic synergy with soil microorganisms, most notably through its capacity as a nitrogen fixer. This process is facilitated by a symbiotic relationship with rhizobia bacteria, which colonize the plant's root nodules to convert atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia, a form readily assimilable by the plant. This biological nitrogen fixation provides a sustainable internal source of nutrients, reducing the plant's dependence on soil nitrogen levels and enhancing its growth vigor in nutrient-poor environments. Beyond this nitrogenous metabolism, the plant exhibits specialized physiological adaptations for storage and survival, utilizing its tuberous roots to accumulate significant starch reserves and secondary metabolites. These physiological mechanisms support energy mobilization during vegetative growth cycles and contribute to the complex biochemical profile used in various medicinal applications.
