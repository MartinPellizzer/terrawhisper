What's the taxonomical classification of Ophioglossum vulgatum?

Ophioglossum vulgatum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida, it is further categorized under the subclass Ophioglossidae and the order Ophioglossales. This plant is a representative of the family Ophioglossaceae, falling under the genus Ophioglossum, with its specific designation being the species vulgatum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Ophioglossum vulgatum has a single, fleshy, unbranched subterranean stem that produces a simple, linear, grass-like sterile leaf and a separate, upright, fertile spike. The sterile portion consists of a long, narrow, dark green blade that lacks a distinct midrib. This blade emerges directly from the ground to provide photosynthetic energy for the plant. The fertile spike grows centrally from the base and carries numerous small, brownish spores in dense clusters. Once the spores are released, the entire structure typically withers away.

According to a study published by "Journal of natural medicines", the species Ophioglossum vulgatum L. is traditionally used as an ointment for wounds and burns. A galactosyldiacylglycerol mixture was isolated from the frond dichloromethane extract. This mixture, composed mainly of 1,2-di-O-linolenoyl-3-O-β-D-galactopyranosyl-glycerol, demonstrated wound healing properties in keratinocyte monolayers. The strongest effect was obtained at a dose of 5 μg/mL, which approached the results of a platelet lysate used in clinical settings. This action suggests a Ca(2+)-dependent, nongenoic mechanism involving the activation of ERK1/2 MAP kinase.

According to a study published by "FEBS open bio", Ophioglossum vulgatum L. is a representative species of the fern genus Ophioglossum that possesses anti-inflammatory properties recognized in folk medicine. The plant contains the characteristic homoflavonoid ophioglonin (OPN), which was identified as a bioactive component through a nitric oxide (NO) assay-guided screening in RAW264.7 cells. The antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities of OPN were validated using lipopolysaccharide-stimulated RAW264.7 cells, mouse bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs), and peritoneal exudate macrophages (PEMs). In vivo experiments using a carrageenan-induced mouse paw edema model further confirmed these anti-inflammatory effects. Additionally, both OPN and Ophioglossum vulgatum extracts inhibit the activation of signal transducers including NF-κB p65, IκBα, ERK, p38, and JNK.
