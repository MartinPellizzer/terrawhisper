Pachysandra procumbens

Pachysandra procumbens, commonly known as the creeping Pachysandra, is a resilient medicinal plant valued for its diverse therapeutic properties. Often found in shaded forest environments, this evergreen species is traditionally utilized in various cultures to treat ailments ranging from skin irritations to digestive issues, showcasing its significant ethnobotanical importance.

  • Scientific name: Pachysandra procumbens
  • Family: Buxaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, situated within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Buxales and the family Buxaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Pachysandra.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Pachysandra procumbens, is referred to by various common names depending on the linguistic context. In Hsbin, it is called Ameriska pachysandra, while in Ethiopian Amharic, it is known as laiuv puksrohi, and in Chinese, it is identified as 平铺富贵草.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution across several regions of the United States. In the North-Central region, its presence has been documented in Missouri. Moving toward the Northeast, the species can be found in Indiana. Its range further extends through Pennsylvania and West Virginia within the same Northeastern area. Finally, the plant is also recorded in the state of Connecticut.
