Ammocharis coranica

Ammocharis coranica, commonly known as the Cornica Amaryllis, is a striking bulbous perennial native to South Africa. While celebrated for its elegant, white star-shaped blooms, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditional healers utilize its chemical constituents for various medicinal purposes, highlighting its unique importance in botanical studies.

  • Scientific name: Ammocharis coranica
  • Family: Amaryllidaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asparagales. Finally, it is a member of the family Amaryllidaceae and falls under the genus Ammocharis.

Distribution

This plant is native to several distinct regions across the southern portion of the African continent. Its distribution extends into South Tropical Africa, where it can be found in both Angola and Zimbabwe. Moving into the Southern Africa region, the species is also established within the borders of Botswana and Lesotho. Furthermore, it is widely recognized for its presence throughout the Cape Provinces. Collectively, these locations define the specific geographical range of Ammocharis coranica.
