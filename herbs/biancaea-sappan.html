Biancaea sappan

Sappanwood

Biancaea sappan, commonly known as sappanwood, is a versatile medicinal plant native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its vibrant red heartwood, it is widely used in traditional medicine for its potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties. This botanical treasure offers significant therapeutic potential for supporting overall holistic wellness.

  • Scientific name: Biancaea sappan
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fabales, it is classified under the family Fabaceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Biancaea, with its species name being sappan.

Distribution

This plant, known as Biancaea sappan, exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found within the borders of Nigeria. Its presence extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically located in the DR Congo. Moving towards the eastern part of the continent, the species is distributed throughout Tanzania and Uganda. Finally, its range reaches into South Tropical Africa, where it is documented in Mozambique.

Plant Parts

This plant, Biancaea sappan, is highly valued in traditional medicine primarily for its distinct botanical components. The most significant part used for producing its characteristic red pigment and medicinal extracts is the heartwood, which is often referred to as sappanwood. This dense heartwood is prized for its antioxidant properties, and whether referred to as heartwoods in a collective sense or as the concentrated lignum within the stem, it serves as the core therapeutic element. While the seeds may be present, it is the deep, crimson-colored heartwood that remains the most essential part for various medicinal applications.
