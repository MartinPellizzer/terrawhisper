Crotalaria yunnanensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Crotalaria yunnanensis?

Crotalaria yunnanensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Fabales. Its taxonomic hierarchy continues through the family Fabaceae, placing it within the genus Crotalaria, and it is ultimately identified by its specific species name, yunnanensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Crotalaria yunnanensis has an erect herbaceous habit characterized by branched stems that typically reach heights of thirty to sixty centimeters. The leaves are alternate and pinnately compound, featuring several pairs of oblong to lanceolate leaflets with entire margins. Its inflorescence consists of small, yellow, papilionaceous flowers that emerge from the leaf axils in clusters. The plant produces distinct legume pods that are compressed and contain several small, hard seeds. These structural features are adaptations suited for its specific ecological niche in montane regions.

This plant is native to the subtropical and temperate regions of Southwest China, specifically within the provinces of Yunnan and Sichuan. Within these specific habitats, it colonizes areas characterized by moderate moisture and seasonal temperature fluctuations. How is this plant cultivated?

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe toxicity due to pyrrolizidine alkaloids which lead to significant physiological harm. Consuming the plant often results in acute gastrointestinal distress such as nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain. The most dangerous long-term consequence is extensive liver damage caused by the accumulation of these toxins. This damage can progress to a condition known as hepatic veno-occlusive disease or venous obstruction syndrome. Such complications can permanently impair organ function if the plant is ingested.
