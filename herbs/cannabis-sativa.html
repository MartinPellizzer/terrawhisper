Renowned for its rich profile of cannabinoids, including CBD and THC, it is widely utilized in modern medicine to manage pain, anxiety, and epilepsy, offering significant potential for holistic healthcare and wellness.

Regarding its symbiotic relationships with soil fungi, the plant does not exhibit mycorrhizal associations, meaning it functions without the presence of mycorrhiza.

Genetics

The genetics of Cannabis sativa are characterized by significant chromosomal variation and complex polyploidy patterns. The species exhibits diverse chromosome numbers, typically recorded as 20, 40, or 80, which reflects the evolutionary complexity and varying levels of genomic doubling within different cultivars. Despite this variability, the fundamental ploidy level for many standard specimens is 2, indicating a diploid state that serves as the basis for its genetic inheritance. This genomic structure facilitates the high degree of phenotypic diversity observed in the species, particularly concerning the regulation of secondary metabolites and morphological characteristics.

Life history

The life history of Cannabis sativa is characterized by an annual lifecycle, meaning it completes its entire biological development—from germination to seed production—within a single growing season. As a therophyte, the plant functions by completing its life cycle rapidly and surviving unfavorable environmental conditions in the form of dormant seeds. This life form strategy allows it to exploit seasonal resources effectively, utilizing its rapid growth phase to establish a robust structure before senescence. Throughout its developmental stages, it maintains this therophytic habit, relying on the ephemeral availability of moisture and light to fuel its progression from seedling to a mature, reproductive adult.

Morphology

The morphology of Cannabis sativa is characterized by its growth form as a non-woody herb, specifically classified as a forb. This plant is a self-supporting species, meaning it does not function as a climber (such as a vine or liana) nor does it exhibit epiphytic behavior, as it is strictly terrestrial in its habitat. As an independent organism, it does not act as a parasite on other plants. In terms of physical stature, the plant exhibits significant variability in height, with individual specimens ranging from a minimum of 0.2 m to a maximum of 5 m, maintaining a mean plant height of approximately 1.925 m.

The physiology of Cannabis sativa is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, which dictates its carbon fixation processes and metabolic efficiency. Reproduction

The reproduction of Cannabis sativa is primarily sexual, characterized by a dioecious mating system where self-fertilization is absent. The flowering period typically begins in July and concludes in August, utilizing both biotic and abiotic pollination syndromes, specifically involving both insect and wind-mediated processes. The plant produces small fruits classified as achenes, which reach a maximum length of 0.5 cm and a minimum length of 0.4 cm. These fruits are indehiscent, meaning they do not split open at maturity to release seeds. The resulting seeds are uniform in dimension, with a mean length and width of 3 mm (ranging from a minimum of 3 mm to a maximum of 3 mm). These seeds possess a mean mass of 0.01029 g, with individual seed masses varying between a minimum of 0.00622 g and a maximum of 0.019 g. For dispersal, the species employs an anemochorous syndrome, relying on wind to transport its offspring. Asexual reproduction is absent in this species.
