Lavandula × intermedia

Lavandula ×intermedia

Lavandula × intermedia, commonly known as Lavandin, is a highly aromatic hybrid between true lavender and spike lavender. Renowned for its intense essential oil content, it is widely utilized in aromatherapy, perfumery, and traditional medicine to promote relaxation, soothe skin irritations, and alleviate mild symptoms of anxiety and insomnia.

  • Scientific name: Lavandula × intermedia
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae, the order Lamiales, and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Lavandula, specifically identified as the hybrid species Lavandula × intermedia.

Distribution

This plant, known scientifically as Lavandula × intermedia, exhibits a specific geographical distribution within the Mediterranean region. Its presence is notably concentrated in the southwestern part of Europe. Specifically, it can be found growing across various landscapes in France. Additionally, this hybrid species is widely distributed throughout the territory of Spain. These two countries serve as the primary hubs for its cultivation and natural occurrence.

Plant Parts

This plant, Lavandula × intermedia, is primarily valued for its highly aromatic flowers, which are arranged in dense, vibrant spikes that attract pollinators. These blossoms serve as the vital source for the extraction of its potent essential oil, a fragrant liquid produced through steam distillation. The oil captured from these floral parts contains the complex chemical compounds responsible for the plant's therapeutic properties, making it a cornerstone in aromatherapy and natural medicine.
