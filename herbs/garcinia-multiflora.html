Garcinia multiflora

Garcinia multiflora, commonly known as the "Wild Mangosteen," is a versatile medicinal plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, it offers significant antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Traditionally used to treat various ailments, this plant holds immense potential for modern pharmacological research and therapeutic applications.

  • Scientific name: Garcinia multiflora
  • Family: Clusiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales and the family Clusiaceae, it is classified under the genus Garcinia. Specifically, it is identified as the species Garcinia multiflora.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Garcinia multiflora, is referred to by various common names depending on the region and language. In Vietnamese, it is called Dọc, while in Chinese, it is known as 木竹子 (zh) or 木竹子 (zh-cn). In Taiwan, the traditional Chinese name 福木 (zh-tw) is used, and in Japanese, it is identified as タイワンフクギ (ja).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of habitats across several key regions in Asia. In China, its distribution is notably widespread, covering South-Central, Southeast, and the tropical island of Hainan. The species is also documented in Eastern Asia, specifically within the territory of Taiwan. Moving into the Indo-China region, it can be found growing in Laos. Together, these locations highlight the plant's preference for subtropical and tropical environments across the continent.
