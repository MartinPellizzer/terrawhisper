Rheum sublanceolatum

Rheum sublanceolatum, commonly known as Himalayan rhubarb, is a potent medicinal herb native to the high-altitude regions of the Himalayas. Renowned for its therapeutic properties, it is primarily used in traditional medicine to treat digestive ailments, reduce inflammation, and support detoxification, making it a vital component of herbal pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Rheum sublanceolatum
  • Family: Polygonaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, specifically categorized within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Caryophyllales and the family Polygonaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Rheum, which includes the species Rheum lanceolatum.

Plant Parts

This plant, Rheum lanceolatum, is highly valued in traditional medicine, particularly for the therapeutic properties found within its underground structures. The medicinal efficacy of the species is primarily concentrated in the rhizoma and the radix, which are harvested and processed to extract bioactive compounds. While the radix refers to the main taproot system that anchors the plant, the rhizoma consists of the thickened, horizontal stem-like structures that store nutrients and essential medicinal constituents. Together, these parts serve as the primary source for pharmacological applications.
