Ceylon hill-cherry

Rhodomyrtus tomentosa · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (96 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Ceylon hill-cherry, scientifically known as Rhodomyrtus tomentosa, is a versatile evergreen shrub native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its edible, antioxidant-rich berries, this medicinal plant offers significant therapeutic potential. Its leaves and bark are traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat various ailments, highlighting its profound ethnobotanical importance.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Rhodomyrtus tomentoza is driven by biotic pollination syndromes, specifically relying on insects for the transfer of pollen. The plant produces flowers that vary in length, ranging from a minimum of 2 cm to a maximum of 4 cm. Following successful fertilization, the plant develops indehiscent fruit categorized as a berry. These berries exhibit specific morphological dimensions, with a mean length of 1.75 cm (ranging from 1.5 cm to 2 cm) and a mean width of 1.25 cm (ranging from 1 cm to 1.5 cm). The dispersal of these fruits is zoochorous, meaning they are spread by animals. Within the fruit, the seeds are quite small and uniform in mass, with a mean, minimum, and maximum seed mass all recorded at 0.0021 g.
