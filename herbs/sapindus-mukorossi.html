Sapindus mukorossi

Indian soapberry

Sapinduus mukorossi, commonly known as Soapberry, is a versatile medicinal plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its saponin-rich fruits, it serves various traditional uses, ranging from skin ailments to digestive support. This botanical treasure offers significant pharmacological potential, making it a subject of interest in modern ethnobotanical research.

  • Scientific name: Sapindus mukorossi
  • Family: Sapindaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Sapindales and the family Sapindaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is established within the genus Sapindus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range across several key regions of East Asia. In China, it is widely distributed throughout the south-central provinces and the southeast coastal areas. Its presence is also noted on the tropical island of Hainan. Moving further north and east, the species can be found naturally occurring in Japan. Finally, its distribution extends into the Korean Peninsula, completing its regional footprint.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Sapindus mukorossi, offers various components that are utilized for their medicinal and bioactive properties. Within the seed, the seed kernel contains concentrated bioactive compounds that are often studied for their pharmacological potential.
