Indian soapberry

Sapindus mukorossi · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (16 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Indian soapberry, scientifically known as Sapindus mukorossi, is a versatile evergreen tree cherished in traditional medicine. Renowned for its saponin-rich fruit, it serves as a natural cleansing agent and therapeutic tool. Historically used to treat skin ailments, digestive issues, and inflammation, this plant remains a vital botanical resource.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range across several key regions of East Asia. In China, it is widely distributed throughout the south-central provinces and the southeast coastal areas. Its presence is also noted on the tropical island of Hainan. Moving further north and east, the species can be found naturally occurring in Japan. Finally, its distribution extends into the Korean Peninsula, completing its regional footprint.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Sapindus mukorossi, offers various components that are utilized for their medicinal and bioactive properties. The seed is encased within a fleshy, somewhat toxic fruit pulp, but it is the internal components that hold significant interest for research. Within the seed, the seed kernel contains concentrated bioactive compounds that are often studied for their pharmacological potential. Furthermore, the defatted seed, which is the material remaining after the lipids have been extracted, is frequently used in studies to isolate specific non-fatty constituents, such as saponins, which are characteristic of this species.
