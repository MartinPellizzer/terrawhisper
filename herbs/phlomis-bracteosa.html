Phlomis bracteosa

What's the taxonomical classification of Phlomis bracteosa?

Phlomis bracteosa belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is organized under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. More specifically, it is categorized within the genus Phlomis, ultimately reaching its specific identification as the species bracteos.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Phlomis bracteosa has a woody, perennial base with upright, square-shaped stems that are densely covered in fine, grayish-white hairs. The leaves are oppositely arranged, oblong to lanceolate in shape, and possess a soft, velvety texture due to their pubescence. Its inflorescence consists of dense, verticillate whorls of flowers situated in the upper leaf axils. The individual flowers are tubular and bilabiate, often displaying a pale color that contrasts with the surrounding bracts. These prominent, leaf-like bracts subtend the flower clusters and contribute to the plant's overall structural appearance.

These specialized environments dictate the narrow range in which the species can successfully establish itself. According to a study published by "International journal of nanomedicine", in vitro-derived plantlets of the endangered medicinal species Phlomis bracteosa are used for the biomimetic synthesis of antimicrobial silver nanoparticles. These plantlets provide an aqueous extract rich in total phenolic and flavonoid content to facilitate the reduction and capping of the nanoparticles. The resulting face-centered cubic silver nanoparticles have a uniform spherical size of 22.41 nm and are synthesized within 1 hour at room temperature.
