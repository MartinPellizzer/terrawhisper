Euphorbia milii

Crown of thorns

Euphorbia milii, commonly known as the Crown of Thorns, is a resilient, succulent shrub prized for its striking, colorful bracts. While primarily grown as an ornamental plant, its milky latex possesses traditional medicinal properties. However, extreme caution is required, as the sap is toxic and can cause skin irritation.

  • Scientific name: Euphorbia milii
  • Family: Euphorbiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, it is classified under the family Euphorbiaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Euphorbia.

Moving toward the Western Indian Ocean, it can be found inhabiting both Comoros and Madagascar. The species also extends its reach into East Asia, specifically within China South-Central. Finally, its distribution includes the tropical island province of Hainan in China.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Crown of Thorns, possesses distinct morphological features that are central to its characterization. The roots form a sturdy anchoring system that allows the succulent to thrive in arid conditions, absorbing necessary nutrients and moisture from the soil. Most notably, the plant contains a specialized internal substance known as latex, a milky, white sap that flows through its stems and leaves. This substance is a key identifying feature of the Euphorbia genus and is often noted for its caustic properties when it comes into contact with skin or eyes.
