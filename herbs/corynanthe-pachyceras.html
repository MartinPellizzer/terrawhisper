Corynanthe pachyceras belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further categorized under the order Gentianaales. This plant is a member of the family Rubiaceae, which is situated within the genus Corynanthe, and is specifically identified by the species name pachyceras.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Corynanthe pachyceras is primarily utilized for its medicinal bark in traditional healing practices. The bark is harvested from the woody stems to extract bioactive compounds used to treat various ailments. Practitioners often decoct the bark to create infusions that address specific health concerns. These preparations are valued for their potential therapeutic properties in regional medicine. Detailed studies focus on the chemical constituents found specifically within this outer layer.

According to a study published by "Planta medica", five indole alkaloids including corynantheidine, corynantheine, dihydrocorynantheine, alpha-yohimbine and corynanthine were isolated from the bark of Corynanthe pachyceras K. Schum. (Rubiaceae). These alkaloids showed pronounced activity against Leishmania major promastigotes with an IC50 at the micromolar level. There was no significant in vitro antiplasmodial activity recorded against chloroquine-sensitive Plasmodium falciparum. Cytotoxicity was assessed using drug sensitive KB-3-1 and multidrug-resistant KB-V1 cell lines and found to be low. The alkaloids are apparently not substrates for the P-glycoprotein (P-170) efflux pump.

This plant is studied for its potential to improve reproductive performance and sexual health in androgen-deficient subjects.
