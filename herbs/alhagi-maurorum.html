Persian manna

Alhagi maurorum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (82 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Persian manna, scientifically known as Alhagi maurorum, is a resilient desert shrub valued for its medicinal properties. Traditionally used in various cultures, it produces a sweet, resinous exudate utilized to treat respiratory issues, digestive ailments, and inflammation. This plant remains a significant subject in ethnobotanical and pharmacological research today.

Plant Parts

This plant, Alhagi maurorum, is a medicinal shrub whose various components offer distinct therapeutic properties. The roots are frequently utilized in traditional medicine for their healing attributes, often serving as a primary source of treatment. A unique characteristic of this species is the production of manna, a sweet, gummy substance exuded from the plant that is harvested for its medicinal uses. The leaf structures also play a role in the plant's physiological functions and contribute to its overall chemical profile. During the early stages of growth, the hypocotyl serves as a vital structural link between the seed and the developing stem. Furthermore, the plant contains essential oil, which provides a concentrated source of aromatic and bioactive compounds used in various medicinal preparations.
