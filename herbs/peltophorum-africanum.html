Peltophorum africanum

Peltophorum africanum, commonly known as the African Copperpod, is a versatile medicinal tree native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its vibrant yellow blooms, its various parts—including bark, leaves, and roots—are traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat inflammatory conditions, skin ailments, and digestive issues through potent bioactive compounds.

  • Scientific name: Peltophorum africanum
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fabales, it is a member of the Fabaceae family. Its taxonomic classification is completed by its placement in the genus Peltophorum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across various tropical regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found specifically within the borders of Nigeria. Its range extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, where it inhabits the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Moving toward the east, the species is documented in the East Tropical Africa regions of both Kenya and Tanzania. Finally, its distribution reaches into South Tropical Africa, notably within the nation of Angola.
