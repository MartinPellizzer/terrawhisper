The ecology of Smilax campestris is characterized by its remarkable adaptability across diverse Brazilian biomes and significant altitudinal gradients. This species inhabits a wide variety of environments, ranging from anthropogenic areas (Área Antrópica) and the semi-arid Caatinga (strictu sensu) to the specialized rocky fields of Campo Rupestre. It is also commonly found within the Cerrado (lato sensu), coastal forests (Floresta Ciliar ou Galeria), and ombrophilous forests (Floresta Ombrófila/Pluvial), demonstrating a broad ecological niche.
