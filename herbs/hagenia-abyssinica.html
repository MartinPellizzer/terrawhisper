Hagenia abyssinica

Hagenia abyssinicica, commonly known as Kosso, is a majestic medicinal tree native to the high-altitude forests of East Africa. Renowned for centuries in traditional medicine, its bark is primarily utilized to treat intestinal parasites, specifically tapeworms, making it a vital ethnobotanical resource in Ethiopian highland ecosystems.

  • Scientific name: Hagenia abyssinica
  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Hagenia abyssinica, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Rosales and the family Rosaceae. Its taxonomic identity is completed by its placement within the genus Hagenia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Hagenia abyssinica, is recognized by various regional and international names depending on the language. In Ethiopia, it is commonly referred to as ኮሶ (koso) in Amharic, while in Rwanda, it is known as Umugeshi. In German-speaking regions, it is called Kosobaum, and in Russian, it is referred to as Хагения. Additionally, in Portuguese, the plant is simply called Hagenia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across several key regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in Burundi and Rwanda. The species is also documented within the vast landscapes of the DR Congo. Moving toward Northeast Tropical Africa, its presence extends into Eritrea. Finally, it is well-established in the highland regions of Ethiopia.
