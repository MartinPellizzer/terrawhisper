Clematis yunnanensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Clematis yunnanensis?

Clematis yunnanensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Ranunculales. This plant is a member of the family Ranunculaceae and is further categorized into the genus Clematis, eventually reaching its specific identification as the species yunnanensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Clematis yunnanensis has woody climbing stems that produce opposite, trifoliate leaves characterized by three ovate to elliptical leaflets with serrated margins. The foliage is typically a deep green color and serves as the primary photosynthetic structure for this perennial vine. Its flowers are large and bell-shaped, featuring four to six creamy white to pale yellowish tepals that create a striking visual display. These blossoms emerge from terminal clusters and are supported by slender pedicels. After flowering, the plant often develops feathery, achene-based seed heads that aid in wind dispersal.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the mountainous regions of southwestern China, specifically within the provinces of Yunnan and Sichuan. It typically thrives in temperate forest environments and high-altitude shrublands. Within these habitats, the species is often found growing along rocky slopes or near the edges of woodlands. The specific elevation ranges vary depending on the local topography of the Hengduan Mountains. Its distribution remains concentrated in these specific subtropical and temperate zones of East Asia.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-drained, fertile soil with a neutral to slightly alkaline pH and a location that offers full sun to partial shade with cool, moist roots.

Because it is a vigorous climber, you must provide a sturdy trellis or support structure to accommodate its rapid growth. Regular watering is essential during the growing season to prevent the soil from drying out completely. Applying a layer of organic mulch around the base helps maintain consistent moisture levels and regulates soil temperature.

Once established, it typically thrives with minimal intervention other than occasional pruning to manage its sprawling vines.

What traditional systems uses this plant?
