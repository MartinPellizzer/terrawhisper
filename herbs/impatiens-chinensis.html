Impatiens chinensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Impatiens chinensis?

Impatiens chinensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the hierarchy of the order Ericales, the plant is placed within the family Balsaminaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Impatiens, and its unique species designation, chinensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Impatiens chinensis has succulent, herbaceous stems that are often reddish or green, along with lanceolate to ovate leaves with serrated margins and fleshy, water-storing tissues. The foliage typically features prominent venation and a smooth, somewhat waxy texture. Small, five-petaled flowers emerge from the leaf axils, displaying colors that range from pale yellow to orange. These blossoms are characterized by a distinct nectar spur located at the rear of the corolla. The plant's reproductive structures eventually develop into small, capsule-like seed pods that burst when touched.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to East Asia, specifically spanning across China, Japan, Korea, and parts of Southeast Asia. It thrives in moist, shaded environments such as forest edges, stream banks, and damp roadside ditches. Due to its ability to adapt to various humid climates, its range extends through subtropical and temperate regions of the continent. In some areas, it has become naturalized in gardens and disturbed soils where moisture is consistently available. Its distribution is largely dictated by the presence of adequate water and filtered sunlight.
