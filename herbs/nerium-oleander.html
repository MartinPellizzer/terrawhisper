oleander

Nerium oleander · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (513 studies) · ★★★★★

Oleander, scientifically known as Nerium oleander, is a striking evergreen shrub celebrated for its vibrant, trumpet-shaped blooms. While highly ornamental in landscaping, it is notoriously toxic, containing cardiac glycosides. Understanding its potent chemical properties is essential, as it holds both significant danger and potential medicinal interest in pharmacology.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific presence across various regions of Southwestern Europe. Within this area, it can be found growing in the Baleares. It also inhabits the island of Corse and extends its reach into France. Additionally, its distribution includes the coastal areas of Portugal. Finally, the species is documented to grow in Sardegna.

Plant Parts

This plant, Nerium oleander, is a hardy evergreen shrub that utilizes various components for its biological functions, though all parts are noted for their high toxicity. The large, leathery, lanceolate leaves are perhaps the most recognizable feature, often appearing in dense clusters along the twig structures that support the plant's growth. These slender twigs emerge from sturdy, woody stems that provide the necessary structural integrity to hold the heavy foliage and vibrant flowers. Beneath the soil, a robust system of roots anchors the shrub and absorbs essential nutrients, completing the complex anatomy of this potent medicinal species.
