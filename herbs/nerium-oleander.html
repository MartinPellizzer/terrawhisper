oleander

Nerium oleander · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (513 studies) · ★★★★★

Oleander, scientifically known as Nerium oleander, is a striking evergreen shrub celebrated for its vibrant, trumpet-shaped blooms. While highly ornamental in landscaping, it is notoriously toxic, containing cardiac glycosides. Understanding its potent chemical properties is essential, as it holds both significant danger and potential medicinal interest in pharmacology.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Nerium oleander involves both sexual and asexual mechanisms, with the sexual process being characterized by bisexual flowers. The flowering period is extensive, typically starting in April and continuing through December, though the specific timing can be variable throughout the year. The plant relies on biotic pollination, specifically utilizing an insect pollination syndrome to facilitate fertilization; notably, self-fertilization is absent, necessitating these external agents. Following successful pollination, the plant produces fruits with a mean length of approximately 18 cm, which contain a high volume of seeds, often exceeding 1,000 seeds per fruit. The seeds are quite small, with a minimum mass of 0.001 g, a mean mass of 0.004083 g, and a maximum mass of 0.00945 g. These seeds are dispersed via an anemochorous syndrome, meaning they are specialized for dispersal by wind.
