Tridax procumbens

Tridax procumbens, commonly known as coatbuttons, is a versatile medicinal herb widely distributed in tropical regions. Renowned for its potent anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and wound-healing properties, it is frequently used in traditional medicine to treat skin infections, promote tissue regeneration, and alleviate various systemic ailments through its bioactive compounds.

  • Scientific name: Tridax procumbens
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the order Asterales and is a member of the Asteraceae family. Its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Tridax.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several distinct tropical regions. Within the Macaronesian islands, it can be found specifically in Cape Verde. Its presence extends significantly into West Tropical Africa, where it is documented in Benin. Furthermore, the species thrives in the landscapes of Burkina and Guinea. Finally, its range includes the territory of the Ivory Coast.
