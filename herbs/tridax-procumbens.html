Coat buttons, scientifically known as Tridax procumbens, is a resilient creeping herb widely recognized for its significant therapeutic potential. Renowned in traditional medicine, this plant possesses potent anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and wound-healing properties. Its versatile applications in treating various ailments make it a vital subject in ethnopharmacological studies.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the order Asterales and is a member of the Asteraceae family. Its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Tridax.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several distinct tropical regions. Within the Macaronesian islands, it can be found specifically in Cape Verde. Its presence extends significantly into West Tropical Africa, where it is documented in Benin. Furthermore, the species thrives in the landscapes of Burkina and Guinea. Finally, its range includes the territory of the Ivory Coast.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as coatbuttons, is a versatile medicinal herb where various components are utilized for therapeutic purposes. While the leaves are often studied for their specific phytochemical properties, much of the traditional healing power is derived from the whole plant or the whole aerial parts, which include the stems and flowers. Researchers frequently utilize plant extracts obtained from these components to isolate bioactive compounds for pharmacological studies. Ultimately, the efficacy of the medicinal treatments often depends on whether the aerial parts are used in isolation or as part of a more comprehensive preparation.
