Actinidia kolomikta

What's the taxonomical classification of Actinidia kolomikta?

Actinidia kolomikta belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. It follows the order Ericales and is a member of the family Actinidiaceae. More specifically, the plant is categorized under the genus Actinidia, with its specific designation being the species kolomikta.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Actinidia kolomikta has large, heart-shaped leaves that can reach up to twenty centimeters in length and possess long, drooping petioles. These deciduous vines produce striking, creamy white flowers with numerous prominent stamens that bloom in late spring. The foliage often takes on a deep bronze or reddish hue during the growing season, adding significant ornamental value. Following pollination, the plant develops small, oval-shaped berries that transition from green to a translucent yellowish color when ripe. Its woody stems are capable of climbing through surrounding vegetation using specialized twining mechanisms.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant is used medicinally through its leaves and roots to treat various health conditions. The leaves are often prepared as a tea or infusion to help manage inflammation and support digestive health. Meanwhile, the roots are harvested and processed to create decoctions used for their purported antioxidant and sedative properties. These specific botanical parts contain bioactive compounds that contribute to the plant's overall therapeutic potential. Utilizing both the aerial and underground portions allows for a broader range of medicinal applications in traditional practices.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", the leaves of Actinidia kolomikta contain actinoside C. The analysis of these leaves was performed at 25 degrees C using a ZORBAX Extend C18 column measuring 4.6 mm x 250 mm and 5 microm. A mobile phase of methanol and water in a 51:49 ratio was used with a flow rate of 1.2 mL x min(-1) at a wavelength of 267 nm. The results showed that actinoside C reaches its highest content in the middle ten days of June. This content then decreases slightly in the middle ten days of July before reaching its lowest content in the middle ten days of August. Therefore, the optimal collective date for Actinidia kolomikta is in the middle ten days of June.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, known as Arctic kiwi, has been traditionally utilized in East Asian folk medicine to treat respiratory ailments and digestive issues.

Indigenous populations in regions such as Korea and Japan historically gathered its fleshy fruits and leaves for their perceived nutritional and healing properties. The high vitamin content made it a valued dietary supplement for maintaining overall health during harsh seasonal changes. Some traditional practices also involved using the plant's components to soothe throat irritations or as a tonic for general vitality.

These historical applications laid the groundwork for contemporary interest in its functional food potential.

What are the pharmacological activities of Actinidia kolomikta?

This plant has antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-hyperglycemic, anti-proliferative, and anti-tumor pharmacological activities. Its high concentration of polyphenols and flavonoids enables it to neutralize free radicals and reduce oxidative stress in biological systems. These bioactive compounds also help modulate immune responses to mitigate chronic inflammation. Furthermore, the plant exhibits significant potential in regulating blood glucose levels for metabolic health. Finally, its ability to inhibit cell growth makes it a subject of interest for its anti-proliferative and anti-tumor properties.

According to a study published by "Pakistan journal of pharmaceuctical sciences", Actinidia kolomiktas ethanol extract from the root, known as AKE, showed concentration-dependent inhibition effects on rat intestinal maltase and rat intestinal sucrase with IC50 values of 1.83 mg/mL and 1.03 mg/mL, respectively. In normal rats loaded with maltose, sucrose, and glucose, the administration of AKE significantly reduced postprandial hyperglycemia in a manner similar to acarbose. The AKE contained high amounts of total phenolics at 80.49 ± 0.05 mg GAE/g extract and total flavonoids at 430.69 ± 0.91 mg RE/g extract. These anti-hyperglycemic effects may be attributed to these high contents of phenolic and flavonoid compounds and are associated with the inhibition of α-glucosidase. The study also suggests possible improvements on insulin release and/or insulin sensitivity.

According to a study published by "Experimental and therapeuic medicine", Actinidia kolomikta is a species of wild plant growing in the northern part of Indochina that can be used as a natural health food with antioxidant actions. Water extracts from the root of Actinidia kolomikta produced under different temperatures were investigated for polysaccharide and polyphenol contents, SOD-like activity, and DPPH radical-scavenging activity. Extracts produced using the 100°C extraction procedure revealed higher extraction yields and antioxidant activities than those produced using the 40°C extraction procedure. The water extraction-ethanol precipitate (WE-EP) and water extraction-ethanol supernatant (WE-ES) fractions exhibited anti-proliferative effects on DLD-1 colon cancer cells. The WE-ES polyphenol-enriched fraction possessed more potent anti-proliferative effects on the DLD-1 cells by inducing apoptosis compared to the WE-EP polysaccharide fraction.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause allergic reactions, digestive distress, and potential kidney strain in certain individuals. The high concentration of organic acids found in its berries may irritate the stomach lining if consumed in excess. Some people might experience skin irritation or hives when coming into direct contact with the plant's sap. Additionally, the significant amount of oxalates in the fruit could contribute to the formation of kidney stones for those predisposed to the condition. Regular consumption should be approached with caution by anyone with sensitive digestive systems or known allergies.
