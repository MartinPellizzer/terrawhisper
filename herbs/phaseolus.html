Phaseolus

Phaseolus, commonly known as the genus for beans, holds significant medicinal value in traditional practices. Beyond being a nutritional powerhouse, various species offer bioactive compounds with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Exploring its phytochemical profile reveals potential therapeutic applications in managing metabolic health and supporting overall wellness through natural supplementation.

  • Scientific name: Phaseolus
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales, falling into the family Fabaceae. Ultimately, it is identified by its genus, Phaseolus.

Common Names

This plant, belonging to the genus Phaseolus, is known by various common names depending on the region and language. In English, it is most frequently referred to as the bean, while in Spanish, it is commonly called el frijol. In French, the plant is known as le haricot, and in German, it is referred to as die Bohne.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across various regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its distribution can be observed within the borders of Great Britain. Moving into Middle Europe, the species is found in both Germany and the areas of Czechia and Slovakia. Its range extends into Southeastern Europe, specifically within the country of Bulgaria. Finally, the plant is also documented in Eastern Europe, particularly in Belarus.
