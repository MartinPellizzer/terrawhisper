Holy basil

Ocimum tenuiflorum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1437 studies) · ★★★★★

Holy basil, scientifically known as Ocimum tenuiflorum, is a sacred perennial herb central to Ayurvedic medicine. Renowned for its potent adaptogenic properties, it helps the body manage stress and promotes holistic wellness. This aromatic plant offers diverse therapeutic benefits, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial effects, making it truly extraordinary.

It typically colonizes tropical and subtropical environments, favoring regions with warm temperatures and high humidity, though it demonstrates significant adaptability to various soil types ranging from sandy to loamy. The plant is often found in disturbed sites, forest edges, and open grasslands where it can access ample sunlight, which is essential for its physiological development and the synthesis of its bioactive compounds. Its distribution is heavily influenced by seasonal monsoon patterns, as it requires adequate moisture during its growth phases but can withstand periods of moderate heat.

These flowers vary in size, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 0.2 cm to a maximum of 0.45 cm. Following pollination, the plant develops fruit classified as a nut, which is characterized by being indehiscent, meaning it does not spontaneously split open at maturity to release its contents. These fruits are quite small, measuring between 0.08 cm and 0.12 cm in length.
