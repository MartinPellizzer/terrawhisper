Shagbark hickory, scientifically known as Carya ovata, is a majestic deciduous tree native to North America. Renowned for its distinctive peeling bark and nutrient-dense nuts, it holds significant ethnobotanical value. Historically utilized by Indigenous cultures, its various components offer diverse medicinal properties, ranging from antioxidant to anti-inflammatory benefits.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fagales, it is classified under the family Juglandaceae. Finally, it is placed within the genus Carya, specifically identified as Carya ovata.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution that spans both the European and North American continents. In Middle Europe, it can be found growing within the borders of Poland. Its range extends into Southeastern Europe, specifically throughout the country of Romania. The species is also established in the Caucasus region, occurring in both the North Caucasus and the Transcaucasus.
