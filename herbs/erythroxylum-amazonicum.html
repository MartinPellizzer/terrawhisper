Physiology

The physiology of Erythroxylum amazonicum is fundamentally characterized by its carbon assimilation strategy, specifically utilizing the C3 photosynthetic pathway. In this metabolic process, the initial fixation of atmospheric carbon dioxide is mediated by the enzyme ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase (RuBisCO), which attaches CO2 to a five-carbon sugar, ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP), to form two molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA). This direct fixation occurs within the mesophyll cells without the specialized anatomical adaptations found in C4 plants or the temporal separation of gas exchange seen in CAM species. Consequently, the plant's energy production and biomass accumulation are heavily dependent on the efficiency of its stomatal conductance and the management of photorespiration, particularly in the humid, often shaded environments of its native tropical habitats.
