Olga Bay Larch, scientifically known as Larix gmelinii var. olgensis, is a resilient conifer native to subarctic regions. While primarily valued for its timber and ecological role, its bark and needles contain bioactive compounds. Historically, such hardy species have been explored for their potential antimicrobial and restorative medicinal properties.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Pinidae and the order Pinales, it is a member of the family Pinaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Larix as the variety gmelinii var. olgensis.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning across several distinct regions of the northern hemisphere. In Northern Europe, its presence is specifically noted in Denmark and Sweden. Moving toward the eastern part of the continent, it can be found in the Russian Far East within the Primorye region. Its range extends further into China, where it is documented in the Manchuria area. Finally, the species is also distributed throughout Eastern Asia, specifically within Korea.
