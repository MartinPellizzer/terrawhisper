Bauhinia championii

What's the taxonomical classification of Bauhinia championii?

Bauhinia championii belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is organized under the order Fabales. It is a member of the family Fabaceae, which places it within the well-known legume group. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is defined by the genus Bauhinia and the species championii.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Bauhinia championii has distinctive bilobed leaves that are shaped like a butterfly wing with two symmetrical lobes meeting at a central point. The foliage is typically bright green and possesses a smooth texture on the upper surface. Its woody stems produce clusters of fragrant, five-petaled flowers that can range in color from white to soft pink. The plant also features small, elongated seed pods that develop as the flowers fade. These structural characteristics make the species easily identifiable among other members of the legume family.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Bauhinia championii utilizes its bark, leaves, and stem for various medicinal purposes. The bark is frequently processed to treat inflammatory conditions and skin ailments. Leaves are often prepared as infusions or topical applications to manage fever and promote healing. The stem is also utilized in traditional remedies to address internal discomfort. These diverse parts work together in folk medicine to provide holistic health benefits.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", the ethanolic extract of the vine stem of Bauhinia championii was analyzed for its chemical constituents. Through silica gel column chromatography, 5 compounds were isolated from the plant. These identified compounds include 2,4,6-trimethoxyphenol 1-O-beta-D-(6'-O-galloyl)-glucopyranoside (1), (+/-)-lyoniresinol (2), daucosterol (3), beta-sitosterol (4), and gallic acid (5). Compounds 1 through 4 were isolated from Bauhinia championii for the first time.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant Bauhinia championii has been utilized in Taiwan folk medicine to treat various physical ailments through the application of its leaves and bark. Local practitioners often prepare decoctions from the plant to address inflammatory conditions and digestive issues. In certain historical contexts, the botanical extracts were believed to possess properties that could aid in wound healing. These traditional practices rely on specific methods of harvesting and drying the plant material to preserve its perceived medicinal potency. Such ancestral knowledge reflects a deep cultural understanding of the local flora's therapeutic potential.

According to a study published by "The American journal of Chinese medicine", the plant Bauhinia championii Benth. was evaluated using an electron spin resonance (ESR) technique. Researchers measured the scavenging activity of superoxide radical (O2-) and hydroxyl radical (OH) from water extracts of the crude drug. These radicals were detected as spin adducts of spin traps 5,5-dimethylpyrroline-N-oxide (DMPO). The extract of B. championii showed the greatest superoxide radical and hydroxyl radical scavenger activity.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains several distinct medicinal compounds including bauchampine A, bauhichamines, bauhiniasstatins, bauhinoxepins, and various flavonoids. These specialized phytochemicals are primarily isolated from the bark and leaves of the species. The specific alkaloids like bauchampine A and the bauhichamines group contribute to its unique chemical profile. Additionally, the presence of bauhiniasstatins and bauhinoxepins suggests complex biological activities. These diverse secondary metabolites, alongside a rich array of flavonoids, provide the basis for its pharmacological potential.

According to a study published by "Natural product research", a new polyoxygenated abietane diterpenoid called bauchampine A (1) was isolated from the rattans of Bauhinia championii (Benth.) Benth. This new compound 1 was found alongside 7 known compounds, numbered 2-8. The study evaluated compound 1 for its anti-rheumatoid arthritis activity by examining its anti-proliferative effect on synoviocytes in vitro. Compound 1 exhibited an inhibitory effect on the proliferation of synoviocytes with an IC50 value comparable to that of methotrexate.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is used to create medicinal herbal preparations such as ethyl acetate extracts and water extracts to harness its bioactive compounds. The ethyl acetate extract is specifically utilized to isolate non-polar phytochemicals that demonstrate significant antioxidant properties. In contrast, water extracts are commonly prepared to derive polar constituents for traditional therapeutic applications. These various preparation methods allow researchers to study the diverse pharmacological potential of the species. By utilizing different solvents, scientists can isolate specific medicinal components for targeted health benefits.

According to a study published by "Evidence-based complementary and alternative medicine : eCAM", Bauhinia championii (Benth.) Benth. is a traditional medicinal plant used in China to treat rheumatoid arthritis, particularly in the She ethnic minority group. Active constituents were isolated from the ethyl acetate extract of the rattan of Bauhinia championii using a conventional phytochemical separation method. This process resulted in the isolation of nine flavonoids and five other compounds, including beta-sitosterol (1), 5,6,7,3',4',5'-hexamethoxyflavone (2), 3',4',5,7-tetrahydroxyflavone (3), 5,7,3',4',5'-pentamethoxyflavone (4), 4'-hydroxy-5,7,3',5'-pentamethoxyflavone (5), apigenin (6), liquiritigenin (7), 5, 7-dihydroxylcoumarin (8), 3',4',5,7, -pentamethoxyflavone (9), n-octadecanoate (10), lupine ketone (11), dibutylphthalate (12), dihydrokaempferol (13), and 5,7,3',5'-tetrahydroxy-6-methylflavanone (14). Among these, compounds 5-14 were isolated from Bauhinia championii for the first time. The study specifically evaluated the abundant dihydrokaempferol found in the plant, which exhibited inhibitory effects on the proliferation of synoviocytes and effectively promotes apoptosis.
