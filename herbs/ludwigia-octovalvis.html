Mexican primrose-willow, scientifically known as Ludwigia octovalvis, is a versatile perennial herb native to tropical regions. Renowned in traditional medicine, it possesses significant anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant properties. Often used to treat skin ailments and digestive issues, this plant offers valuable therapeutic potential for modern pharmacological research and development.

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several distinct regions. In the Macaronesian islands, its presence is specifically noted in Cape Verde. The reproduction of Ludwigia octovalvis is characterized by a highly flexible phenology, with flowering and fruiting periods that can occur throughout nearly the entire year, spanning from January to December depending on environmental variables. The plant produces distinct yellow flowers, and the process of fertilization is facilitated by the presence of self-fertilization capabilities.
