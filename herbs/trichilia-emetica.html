Christmas-bells, scientifically known as Trichilia emeetica, is a versatile medicinal tree native to Africa. Renowned for its therapeutic properties, various parts of the plant are utilized in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to digestive issues. Its bioactive compounds offer significant potential for modern pharmacological research.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further organized into the order Sapindales and the family Meliaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic designation falls under the genus Trichilia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across the West Tropical Africa region. It can be found growing within the borders of Benin and Burkina. The species also extends its range into the nation of Gambia. Furthermore, its presence is documented in Ghana. Finally, its geographical reach includes Guinea-Bissau.

Plant Parts

This plant, Trichilia emeetica, is highly valued for its various components that offer significant medicinal and economic benefits. The leaves, specifically each individual leaf and the collection of plant leaves, are frequently utilized in traditional medicine for their bioactive properties. Furthermore, the plant is a vital source of fat, as the processed seed butter serves as a versatile base for many applications, while the extraction of aril oil provides additional aromatic and therapeutic qualities.
