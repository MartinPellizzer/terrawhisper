Plukenetia conophora

Plukenetiia conophora, commonly known as the African nutmeg, is a significant medicinal plant native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, it is traditionally used to treat various ailments, including inflammation and infections. This versatile species holds great promise for pharmacological research and modern therapeutic applications.

  • Scientific name: Plukenetia conophora
  • Family: Euphorbiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, it is classified under the family Euphorbiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Plukenetiia, with its specific species being Plukenetiia conophora.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Plukenetia conophora, is referred to by various common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In English, it is most frequently called the bush jacaranda, while in French, it is known as le jacaranda sauvage. Within local contexts, it is identified as sanya in Hausa and as akpanyin in Yoruba.

Moving toward the West-Central Tropical Africa zone, the species can also be found in the Central African Republic. Additionally, it is known to grow within the borders of Cameroon. Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the African peach, offers various components that are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The leaf is frequently used in traditional medicine to treat ailments such as inflammation, fever, and digestive issues due to its rich bioactive compounds. Furthermore, the seeds are highly valued for their nutritional and medicinal potential, often being processed to extract seed oil. This oil is prized for its high concentration of essential fatty acids and antioxidant properties, making it useful in both topical applications for skin health and as a dietary supplement to support overall wellness.
