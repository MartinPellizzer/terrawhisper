Morphology

The morphology of Plukenetia conophora is characterized by its growth form as a woody liana, which functions as an obligatory climber. While sometimes described within the broader context of shrub-like growth, its primary habit is that of a climbing vine. The plant exhibits significant vertical development, with a minimum height of approximately 3.05 meters, a mean height of 6 meters, and a maximum height reaching roughly 6.10 meters. As a terrestrial species, it grows independently rather than acting as a parasite or an epiphyte, establishing itself firmly in the soil to support its woody structure as it ascends.

Physiology

The physiology of Plukenetia conophora is characterized by a complex metabolic framework geared toward the efficient synthesis and storage of high-density lipids, particularly alpha-linolenic acid, within its large, oil-rich seeds. Its vegetative growth involves robust photosynthetic pathways that support the development of its climbing or scrambling habit, utilizing specialized physiological mechanisms to optimize light interception through its foliage. In terms of nutrient cycling and soil interaction, the plant does not function as a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria, such as Rhizobium, necessary to convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms, meaning its nitrogen requirements must be met through the uptake of nitrates or ammonium from the soil substrate. This metabolic profile dictates a reliance on soil fertility and organic matter decomposition to sustain its protein synthesis and cellular structural integrity.
