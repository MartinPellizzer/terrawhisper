Physiology

The physiology of Sedum sarmentoense is characterized by a specialized metabolic adaptation to arid and rocky environments, primarily utilizing the C3 photosynthetic pathway. While many members of the Crassulaceae family are known for Crassulacean Acid Metabolism (CAM) to conserve water, Sedum sarmentoense typically functions via C3 photosynthesis, which involves the direct fixation of carbon dioxide by the enzyme RuBisCO during the day. This metabolic strategy allows for rapid growth when moisture is available, although the plant compensates for potential water loss through morphological adaptations such as succulent, fleshy leaves that serve as specialized water-storage tissues. These tissues facilitate high hydraulic capacitance, allowing the plant to maintain turgor pressure and physiological functions during periods of transient drought. The combination of C3 carbon fixation and high succulence enables the plant to balance efficient photosynthetic rates with the necessity of managing osmotic stress in its native habitats.
