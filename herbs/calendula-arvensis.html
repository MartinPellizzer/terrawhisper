Field marigold, scientifically known as Calendula arvensis, is a resilient annual herb belonging to the Asteraceae family. Often found in disturbed soils and agricultural fields, this plant is characterized by its bright yellow, daisy-like blooms. Historically valued for its diverse botanical properties, it remains a subject of interest in ethnobotany.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as corn marigold, is characterized by its various morphological features used in botanical study. The aerial parts consist of an erect, branching stem covered in fine hairs, supporting several lanceolate leaves that grow alternately along the stalk. The flowers are prominent, featuring bright yellow ray florets surrounding a central disc, which serve as the primary visual attractant for pollinators. Finally, the fruits develop as small, cylindrical achenes, each topped with a pappus that facilitates wind dispersal to ensure the continued spread of the species.
