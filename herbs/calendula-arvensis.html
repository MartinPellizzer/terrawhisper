Field marigold, scientifically known as Calendula arvensis, is a resilient annual herb belonging to the Asteraceae family. Often found in disturbed soils and agricultural fields, this plant is characterized by its bright yellow, daisy-like blooms. Historically valued for its diverse botanical properties, it remains a subject of interest in ethnobotany.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Calendula arvensis is primarily a sexual process, as asexual reproduction is absent in this species. The plant features bisexual flowers that are characterized by an orange color and range in size from 0.25 to 0.4 cm in length and 0.7 to 2.5 cm in width. The flowering period typically begins in February and concludes in April. Pollination is driven by biotic factors, specifically through an insect pollination syndrome. While self-fertilization is present, the plant relies on these insect visitors to facilitate its reproductive cycle. Following successful pollination, the plant produces dry, indehiscent fruits categorized as achenes. These fruits are prolific, yielding between 101 and 1000 seeds per fruit. The seeds themselves are relatively uniform in dimension, with a mean length of 8.5 mm and a mean width of 3 mm. The seed mass varies slightly, ranging from a minimum of 0.0026 g to a maximum of 0.00988 g, with a calculated mean mass of approximately 0.00534 g. Dispersal of the seeds is achieved through a combination of unspecialized methods and zoochorous mechanisms, allowing the species to spread within its environment.
