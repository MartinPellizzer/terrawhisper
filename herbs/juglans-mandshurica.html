Juglans mandshurica

What's the taxonomical classification of Juglans mandshurica?

Juglans mandshurica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is further categorized into the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the biological hierarchy, it is placed in the order Fagales and the family Juglandaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by the genus Juglans and is specifically distinguished as the species mandshurica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Juglans mandshurica has large, odd-pinnately compound leaves measuring 30 to 60 centimeters in length, consisting of 11 to 25 leaflets with serrated margins and acute tips. The leaflets are typically dark green on the upper surface and slightly paler underneath, often displaying a smooth or slightly rugose texture. Its bark is characterized by deep, longitudinal furrows that provide a rugged texture to the mature trunk. The plant produces small, inconspicuous flowers in catkins, which are monoecious and emerge in early spring. The fruit is a large, fleshy drupe containing a hard, wrinkled nut that is encased in a thick green husk.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to a broad region across Northeast Asia, including parts of northern China, the Korean Peninsula, and the Russian Far East. It typically inhabits temperate deciduous forests where it can thrive in varied mountainous and lowland terrains. The species is frequently found in provinces such as Heilongjiang and Jilin in China as well as throughout the Sakhalin and Primorsky regions of Russia. Its range extends southward into the northernmost reaches of the Korean Peninsula. These specific environmental conditions allow the plant to maintain stable populations across its fragmented eastern distribution.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant Juglans mandshurica requires well-drained, deep, and fertile soil with a neutral to slightly acidic pH to ensure successful establishment and growth.

It thrives best in temperate climates where it can receive full sunlight to support its large canopy development. During the early stages of cultivation, consistent moisture is necessary, though the mature tree is relatively drought-tolerant once its extensive root system is established. Caretakers should monitor for common pests and fungal diseases that can affect walnut species in humid environments.

Regular mulching around the base of the tree helps retain soil moisture and suppresses competing vegetation.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Juglans mandshurica provides medicinal benefits through its bark, husk, leaf, root, and branch. The bitter bark and the thick outer husk are frequently harvested to treat digestive issues and skin inflammations. Medicinal preparations also utilize the green leaves to help regulate blood flow and reduce swelling. Extracts from the root are often employed in traditional formulas to address various systemic ailments. Finally, the woody branches can be processed into decoctions used for their calming and healing properties.

According to a study published by "Journal of separation science", Juglandis Mandshuricae Cortex is the bark of Juglans mandshurica Maxim. which is used as a folk medicine plant in China and India. A total of 113 compounds were characterized using an ultra-high performance liquid chromatography-quadrupole/orbitrap high-resolution mass spectrometry method. Seven flavonoids were simultaneously quantified in 15 min, including myricetin, myricetrin, taxifolin, kaempferol, quercetin, quercitrin, and naringenin. All calibration curves showed a good linear relationship with r > 0.9990 within test ranges. Intra- and inter-day relative standard deviations were less than 2.16%, while accuracy validation showed recovery between 95.6 and 101.3% with relative standard deviation values below 2.85%. The validated method was applied to determine the contents of seven flavones in Juglandis Mandshuricae Cortex from seven sources.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, known as Manchurian walnut, has been utilized in Traditional Chinese Medicine for centuries to treat various ailments by leveraging the medicinal properties of its nuts and husks. Practitioners traditionally use the seeds to address conditions such as asthma, coughs, and digestive issues like constipation. The shells are often prepared to help relieve skin conditions or to reduce swelling in certain parts of the body. Historically, different parts of the tree have also been applied topically to treat dermatological problems or used in dietary practices to promote overall health. These long-standing applications demonstrate the plant's central role in regional herbal pharmacology.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Cortex Juglandis Mandshuricae is the dry branch or stem bark of Juglans mandshurica Maxim. that is widely used as a traditional Chinese medicine in Asia and Africa. Its use was first recorded in Kaibao Bencao to treat a wide range of diseases such as dysentery, acute conjunctivitis, bacterial infections, and cancer. A total of 249 compounds have been isolated from this plant, including quinones, flavonoids, tannins, diarylheptanoids, triterpenoids, coumarins, phenylpropanoids, and volatile oils. A clinical preparation known as Compound Muji Granules is utilized in the treatment of various liver diseases.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains a diverse array of bioactive secondary metabolites including naphthoquinone, various flavonoids, kaempferol, quercitrin, and tannins. The presence of naphthoquinone is particularly significant for its potential antimicrobial and antifungal properties. Within the flavonoid group, specific compounds such as kaempferol and quercitrin contribute to the plant's antioxidant and anti-inflammatory capabilities. Additionally, the tannins found in Juglans mandshurica assist in astringent activities and help protect the plant from environmental stressors. Together, these medicinal compounds form a complex chemical profile that supports many of the plant's traditional therapeutic uses.

Different organs of the plant have shown anti-tumor activity in both clinical and laboratory settings. Through analysis, 111 compounds were identified, which consist of 56 tannins, 29 flavonoids, 13 organic acids, 8 naphthalene derivatives, and 5 anthracenes. Only six compounds, including four organic acids and two tannins, were present in all three plant extracts.

What herbs are paired with Juglans mandshurica?

This plant, Juglans mandshurica, is most frequently paired with herbs such as Glycyrrhiza uralensis, Panax ginseng, and Angelica sinensis to balance its warming and astringent properties.

Pairing it with Panax ginseng helps to enhance its ability to tonify the kidneys and strengthen the essence of the body. Additionally, combining it with Angelica sinensis can support blood circulation and alleviate stagnation caused by dampness.

These specific combinations are designed to optimize the therapeutic effects on the digestive and reproductive systems.
