Manjack, scientifically known as Cordia alliodora, is a versatile evergreen tree native to tropical regions. Renowned for its fragrant, bell-shaped flowers and medicinal properties, it is used in traditional healing to treat skin ailments, inflammation, and digestive issues. This resilient species holds significant value in both ethnobotany and ecology.

This plant, Cordia alliodora, is classified within the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It belongs to the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, falling under the order Boraginales. Finally, it is a member of the family Boraginaceae within the genus Cordia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across several distinct tropical regions. Within the Western Indian Ocean, its range includes the islands of Mauritius and the Seychelles. Moving toward the Malesian region, it can be found growing in Borneo. Its distribution extends into Papuasia, specifically across the Solomon Islands. Finally, the species is also documented in the Southwestern Pacific within Vanuatu.
