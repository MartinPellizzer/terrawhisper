Nut-grass

Cyperus rotundus · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (559 studies) · ★★★★★

Nut-grass, scientifically known as Cyperus rotundus, is a perennial sedge widely recognized in traditional medicine. Renowned for its aromatic rhizomes, it possesses potent bioactive compounds. Used extensively in Ayurvedic and Chinese practices, this versatile plant offers therapeutic benefits, including anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antispasmodic properties for various health conditions.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Poales, it is a member of the family Cyperaceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Cyperus, where it is known as Cyperus rotundus.

Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as nut grass, possesses a complex underground structure essential for its survival and medicinal potency. The morphological architecture begins with a creeping rhizome that spreads horizontally through the soil, acting as a primary storage organ. From these rhizomes, a network of fibrous roots develops to anchor the plant and absorb nutrients. The most distinctive feature of Cyperus rotundus is the formation of small, nut-like tubers that develop at the ends of the underground structures, which are frequently harvested for their therapeutic properties. While the visible portion of the plant consists of a slender, triangular stem, the true biological complexity lies within these interconnected rhizome and tuber systems.
