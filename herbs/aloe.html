Aloë, scientifically known as Aloe, is a succulent plant renowned for its remarkable healing properties. Famous for its thick, gel-filled leaves, it has been used for centuries in traditional medicine. Primarily utilized to soothe skin irritations and burns, this versatile plant remains a staple in modern dermatological care.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asparagales, it is further classified under the family Asphodelaceae. Ultimately, it is categorized under the genus Aloe.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of habitats across several regions in Europe. In Middle Europe, its presence can be noted specifically within Austria. Moving toward the Mediterranean, it is found throughout various parts of Southwestern Europe. This distribution includes the islands of Baleares and Corse. Additionally, the species is widely established in both France and Portugal.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as aloe, is widely celebrated for its remarkable healing properties found within its unique structure. The most valuable part of the leaf is the clear, soothing gel located deep inside the fleshy tissue. To access this substance, one must carefully slice through the tough outer skin of the leaves to reach the hydrating core.
