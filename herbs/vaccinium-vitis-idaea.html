lingonberry

Vaccinium vitis-idaea · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (671 studies) · ★★★★★

Lingonberry, scientifically known as Vaccinium vitis-idaea, is a resilient perennial shrub valued for its tart, crimson berries. Rich in antioxidants, polyphenols, and vitamin C, this potent medicinal plant offers significant anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties. Traditionally used to support urinary tract health, it remains a powerhouse of natural wellness.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and order Ericales, it is a member of the Ericaceae family. Specifically, it is identified by the genus Vaccinium.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several regions in Northern Europe. It can be found growing naturally within the territories of Denmark and Finland. Its range also extends to the island of Iceland and the Faroe Islands, known as Føroyar. Additionally, the species is present throughout the landscape of Great Britain. These locations highlight its preference for the specific environmental conditions found in northern latitudes.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as highbush blueberry, is highly valued for its medicinal and nutritional properties, particularly through the consumption of its berries. These small, antioxidant-rich berries are the primary part of the plant used in various health supplements and natural remedies. Often harvested at peak ripeness, the berries are utilized to combat oxidative stress and support overall wellness due to their dense concentration of bioactive compounds.
