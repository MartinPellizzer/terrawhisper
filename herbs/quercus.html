Quercus

Quercus, commonly known as oak, is a majestic genus of trees celebrated for both its ecological significance and medicinal potential. Rich in tannins and polyphenols, various species offer potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties. Historically used in traditional medicine, oak bark remains a valuable resource for dermatological treatments.

  • Scientific name: Quercus
  • Family: Fagaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fagales and the family Fagaceae, it is classified under the genus Quercus.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Quercus, offers various components that are utilized in traditional medicine. One of the most distinctive parts harvested is the galls, specifically oak galls, which are rich in tannins and often used for their astringent properties. The medicinal value is also heavily concentrated in the cortex, a botanical term referring to the outer layers of the plant's structure. Specifically, the bark is the primary material collected for therapeutic preparations, and this protective Bark layer is frequently processed into decoctions to treat various ailments.
