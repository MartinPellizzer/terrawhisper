Artemisia

Mugwort

Artemisia, commonly known as wormwood, is a diverse genus of aromatic plants celebrated for their potent medicinal properties. Renowned for their bitter compounds, these herbs have been used for centuries in traditional medicine to support digestive health, combat parasites, and provide soothing relief from various inflammatory conditions worldwide.

  • Scientific name: Artemisia
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales, it is a member of the Asteraceae family. Specifically, it is categorized under the genus Artemisia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a distinct presence across several regions within Northern Europe. It can be found growing in the landscapes of Denmark and Finland. Its distribution also extends to the island of Iceland and the archipelago of Føroyaar. Furthermore, the species is documented throughout Great Britain. Together, these locations highlight its specific range across the northern territories.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Artemisia, utilizes various components for its medicinal properties. The most frequently harvested parts include the aromatic leaves, which are often gathered when they are most potent. Specifically, the young (non-elongated) shoots are highly valued for their concentrated essential oils and therapeutic compounds. While the larger stems provide the structural framework for the herb, the most common way to consume the plant is by brewing dried leaf pieces or whole sprigs into soothing teas.
