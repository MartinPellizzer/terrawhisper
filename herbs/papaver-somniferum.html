Papaver somniferum

Opium poppy

Papaver somniferum, commonly known as the opium poppy, is a highly significant medicinal plant prized for its potent alkaloids. Renowned for producing morphine and codeine, it serves as a cornerstone in modern pharmacology for pain management. While medically invaluable, its cultivation requires strict regulation due to its profound psychoactive properties.

  • Scientific name: Papaver somniferum
  • Family: Papaveraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Ranunculales, it is classified under the family Papaveraceae. Ultimately, the medicinal species is identified by its genus, Papaver.

Plant Parts

This plant, Papaver somniferum, is a versatile medicinal source where many of its various organs contain potent alkaloids. The most significant part is the latex, a milky substance extracted from the unripe fruit that serves as the primary source of opiates. The seeds are contained within a distinctive capsule, which is harvested after maturity, while the dried stalks, often referred to as straw, can also be utilized in certain preparations. Ultimately, while specific components are often targeted, the whole plants have historically been studied for their complex chemical profiles and therapeutic potential.
