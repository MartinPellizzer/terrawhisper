Vitex agnus-castus, scientifically known as Vitex polygama, is a perennial shrub celebrated in herbal medicine for its hormonal balancing properties. Often called Chasteberry, it is primarily utilized to support reproductive health, alleviate PMS symptoms, and regulate menstrual cycles by influencing prolactin levels and stabilizing endocrine functions naturally.

Physiology

The physiology of Vitex agnus-castus (often referred to in botanical contexts as Vitex polygama) is characterized by a complex metabolic framework designed for survival in Mediterranean-style climates, utilizing specialized photosynthetic pathways to manage transpiration in semi-arid environments. Its physiological processes are heavily centered on the production of secondary metabolites, specifically diterpenoids and flavonoids, which are synthesized through complex biosynthetic pathways to provide chemical defense and hormonal modulation. Regarding its nutrient acquisition, the plant does not possess the physiological mechanisms required for symbiotic nitrogen fixation; therefore, it is not a nitrogen fixer, as it lacks the specialized root nodules and the necessary symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria to convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms. Instead, the plant relies on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium ions from the soil via its root system to support its growth and the synthesis of proteinaceous structures. Its water relations are governed by highly regulated stomatal conductance, allowing the plant to maintain turgor pressure and metabolic activity even during periods of moderate drought stress.
