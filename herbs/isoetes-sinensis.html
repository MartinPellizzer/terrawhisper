Morphology

The morphology of Isoetes sinensis is characterized by a distinctive aquatic growth habit, consisting of a compact rosette of leaves that emerge from a central, somewhat elongated rhizome. The plant is fundamentally non-woody, lacking any lignified tissues or secondary thickening, which allows it to remain flexible within its submerged or semi-submerged habitats. The leaves, often referred to as microphylls, are linear, elongated, and somewhat fleshy, typically arranged in a dense spiral around the base. These leaves are equipped with fine, longitudinal lines known as striations that run from the base toward the apex, often containing small, dark spots or lacunae. The root system is composed of several slender, filiform roots that emerge from the base of the rosette to anchor the plant in the substrate. Reproduction occurs through the production of heterosporous sporangia, which are located in the axils of the leaves, typically appearing as small, dark, or brownish structures during the sporophytic phase.
