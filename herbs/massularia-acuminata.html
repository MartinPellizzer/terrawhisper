Massularia acuminata

Massularia acuminata, commonly known as the "Seaweed of the Sands," is a resilient halophytic plant found in coastal regions. Renowned for its unique medicinal properties, it is traditionally utilized in various cultures to treat skin ailments, inflammation, and digestive issues, offering significant potential for modern pharmacological research and development.

  • Scientific name: Massularia acuminata
  • Family: Rubiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Gentianaales, it falls under the family Rubiaceae. Its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Massularia, with the species being Massularia acuminata.

Common Names

This plant, Massularia acuminata, is known by various names across different linguistic and regional contexts. In English, it is commonly referred to as the sea moss or sea mosses, while in Spanish, it is known as musgo de mar. Within the context of local languages, it is called moshu in Swahili.

Distribution

This plant, known as Massularia acuminata, is primarily distributed across the West Tropical Africa region. Its presence is specifically documented in the country of Ghana. It can also be found growing within the borders of Guinea-Bissau. Furthermore, the species inhabits parts of Guinea and Liberia. Finally, its geographical range extends into the Ivory Coast.
