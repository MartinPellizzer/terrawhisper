The life history of Crepis incana is characterized by its classification as a hemicryptophyte, a life form where the perennating buds are located at or just below the soil surface, protected by leaf litter or soil during unfavorable seasons. Within the various botanical life form frameworks, it is specifically identified as a hemicryptophyte, distinguishing it from other growth strategies such as phanerophytes, chamaephytes, or therophytes. This developmental strategy allows the plant to persist through seasonal changes by maintaining its regenerative structures at the ground level, ensuring its survival and ability to re-emerge during favorable growing periods.

The morphology of Crepis incana is characterized by a perennial growth habit with a root system that is primarily terrestrial, as it lacks aquatic or semiaquatic adaptations.
