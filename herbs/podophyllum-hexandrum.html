Podophyllum hexandrum

Podophyllum hexandrum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (381 studies) · ★★★★★

Podophyllum hexandrum, scientifically known as Podophyllum hexandrum, is a perennial herb native to the Himalayan regions. Commonly called the Himalayan Mayapple, this medicinal plant is highly valued in pharmacology for its potent podophyllotoxin content, which plays a critical role in developing various anti-cancer and antiviral therapeutic treatments.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Himalayan Mayapple, derives its medicinal potency primarily from its underground structures. The most significant part used in pharmacology is the rhizomes, which are often harvested for their high concentration of podophyllotoxin. While the rhizome acts as the primary storage organ, the roots also play a role in the plant's overall nutrient distribution. Although the root system helps anchor the plant in its moist, forest habitats, the therapeutic value lies in the fleshy, tuberous nature of the rhizoemes. Finally, although the plant produces seeds for reproduction, these are not typically the focus of medicinal extraction compared to the potent underground tissues.
