Podophyllum hexandrum

Podophyllum hexandrum, commonly known as the Himalayan Mayapple, is a perennial herb native to the high-altitude regions of the Himalayas. Renowned in traditional medicine, its rhizomes contain potent alkaloids like podophyllotoxin, which possess significant antiviral and anticancer properties, making it a vital subject in modern pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Podophyllum hexandrum
  • Family: Berberidaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Ranunculales and the family Berberidaceae. Its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Podophyllum.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Himalayan Mayapple, derives its medicinal potency primarily from its underground structures. The most significant part used in pharmacology is the rhizomes, which are often harvested for their high concentration of podophyllotoxin. While the rhizome acts as the primary storage organ, the roots also play a role in the plant's overall nutrient distribution. Although the root system helps anchor the plant in its moist, forest habitats, the therapeutic value lies in the fleshy, tuberous nature of the rhizoemes. Finally, although the plant produces seeds for reproduction, these are not typically the focus of medicinal extraction compared to the potent underground tissues.
