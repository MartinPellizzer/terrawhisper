Phyllanthus emblica

What's the taxonomical classification of Phyllanthus emblica?

Phyllanthus emblica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its hierarchical descent, the plant is placed in the order Malpighiales and the family Phyllanthaceae. Finally, it is defined by its specific taxonomic rank within the genus Phyllanthus, specifically as the species emblica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Phyllanthus emblica has a medium-sized tree structure characterized by a spreading crown, pinnately compound leaves arranged on branchlets, and globose, light green fruits that hang from the branches. The leaves are small, oblong, and arranged in a manner that mimics a compound leaf structure to facilitate efficient photosynthesis. Its bark is typically grayish-brown and becomes somewhat fissured as the tree matures. The fruits are fleshy, ribbed, and possess a sour taste, hanging individually or in small clusters from the woody stems. This morphology allows the plant to thrive in tropical environments by maximizing light capture and fruit dispersal.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical and subtropical regions of South and Southeast Asia, including India, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, and Indonesia. It thrives primarily in moist deciduous forests and along the banks of rivers where soil moisture is consistent. The species is also frequently found in the wild throughout parts of China and even extends into parts of Africa due to human introduction. Because it prefers warm, humid climates, it is rarely found in temperate zones or arid desert environments. Cultivation has spread the plant to various other tropical regions across the globe for medicinal and culinary purposes.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, scientifically known as Phyllanthus emblica and commonly called amla, requires well-drained loamy soil and a tropical or subtropical climate with moderate rainfall to thrive.

It is best propagated through seeds or grafting techniques during the spring season to ensure strong root establishment. The tree prefers full sunlight and can tolerate various soil types as long as they do not remain waterlogged for extended periods. Regular watering is necessary during the early stages of growth, although mature trees are relatively drought-tolerant.

Once established, the tree requires minimal pruning and can produce fruit for several decades with consistent care.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant, Phyllanthus emblica, provides medicinal benefits through its fruit, bark, leaves, roots, and stem. The vitamin C-rich fruit is most commonly utilized to boost immunity and treat digestive issues. Bark and stem extracts are often prepared to address inflammatory conditions or skin ailments. The leaves are frequently applied topically or consumed to manage fever and respiratory concerns. Additionally, the roots are processed into decoctions to treat various systemic ailments and infections.

According to a study published by "Plant foods for human nutrition (Dordrecht, Netherlands)", a large scale screening of approximately 1,000 kinds of herbs was conducted using an electron spin resonance (ESR)-spin trapping method. Among these, the extract from the Phyllanthus emblica fruit was identified as one of four edible herbal extracts with prominently potent ability to reduce the signal intensity of 5,5-dimethyl-1-pyrroline-N-oxide (DMPO)-OOH. This spin adduct is formed by DMPO and superoxide anion. The superoxide scavenging potential of the Phyllanthus emblica fruit extract was found to be comparable to that of L-ascorbic acid. Polyphenol determination indicates that this activity is at least in part attributable to polyphenols.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, known as amla, has been utilized for centuries as a cornerstone medicinal ingredient within the Ayurvedic, Siddha, and Unani traditional systems of medicine. In Ayurveda, it is revered as a potent rejuvenator and a primary component of Rasayana formulations used to promote longevity and balance the three doshas. The Siddha practitioners employ the fruit to treat various ailments related to the blood and respiratory system, often utilizing its cooling properties. Unani medicine integrates the plant to address digestive issues and to act as a natural tonic for overall vitality. These diverse historical practices collectively highlight the plant's significant role in managing chronic health conditions through its high antioxidant content.

According to a study published by "Natural product research", Phyllanthus emblica is one of the medicinal herbs commonly used in the Ayurvedic system of Sri Lanka. This plant was prioritized for further assessment after showing notable activity with a p value less than or equal to 0.05. Its methanolic extracts contained alkaloids, flavonoids, tannins, and terpenoids, but lacked saponins and steroids. GC-MS analysis of the plant annotated 10 compounds, 8 of which possessed anti-diabetic properties. Additionally, LC-HRMS analysis annotated 30 metabolites, which included 14 anti-diabetic compounds.

What are the pharmacological activities of Phyllanthus emblica?

This plant has potent pharmacological activities including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-diabetic, antibacterial, and antiviral properties. Its high concentration of vitamin C and polyphenols allows it to neutralize free radicals and reduce oxidative stress in the body. The bioactive compounds also inhibit inflammatory enzymes to help manage systemic swelling and pain. Furthermore, specific phytochemicals in the fruit assist in regulating blood glucose levels by improving insulin sensitivity. Finally, the plant demonstrates a capacity to inhibit the growth of various pathogenic bacteria and suppress the replication of several viral strains.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains various medicinal compounds such as gallic acid, phyllanthin, ascorbic acid, emblicin, and chebulagic acid. These bioactive constituents contribute to the significant antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties associated with the species. Gallic acid and ascorbic acid serve as potent antioxidants that help protect cells from oxidative stress. The lignan phyllanthin and the hydrolyzable tannins chebulagic acid and emblicin further enhance its therapeutic potential in traditional medicine. Together, these diverse chemical elements make the plant a valuable source of natural pharmacological agents.

According to a study published by "Zhong yao cai = Zhongyaocai = Journal of Chinese medicinal materials", 13 compounds were isolated and identified from Phyllanthus emblica. These identified constituents include Triacontanol (1), Triacontanoic acid (2), β-Amyrin ketone (3), Betulonic acid (4), Daucosterol (5), Lupeol acetate (6), β-Amyrin-3-palmitate (7), Gallic acid (8), Betulinic acid (9), Ursolic acid (10), Oleanolic acid (11), Quercetin (12) and Rutin (13). Specifically, compounds 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10 and 11 are obtained from Phyllanthus emblica for the first time. The chemical constituents were isolated and purified using silica gel, polyamide and Sephadex LH-20 chromatography.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is Phyllanthus emblica, which is processed into diverse medicinal preparations including Amalaki Rasayana, Triphala, fruit juice, powder, and 50% alcoholic extract. Amalaki Rasayana serves as a potent rejuvenative tonic in Ayurvedic medicine to promote longevity and vitality. The fruit can be dried and ground into a fine powder or expressed as a fresh juice for direct consumption. Triphala is a classic herbal formulation that utilizes the dried fruit of this plant alongside two other species to support digestive health. Additionally, a 50% alcoholic extract is often prepared to concentrate its specific bioactive compounds for therapeutic use.

According to a study published by "PloS one", a 0.5% (weight/volume) supplement of the herbal derivative Amalaki Rasayana, which is made with Phyllanthus emblica, was tested using at least 200 larvae or flies for each assay. This formulation affected life-history and other physiological traits, including the size of salivary glands and hnRNP levels in larval tissues. The median life span and starvation resistance improved only with this herbal preparation. Feeding larvae and adults with this supplement increased fecundity. Additionally, feeding adults on this supplement had no effect on fecundity.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause adverse health reactions including biliousness, diarrhea, itching, jaundice, and vaginal irritation in some individuals. These side effects may manifest differently depending on the dosage or the method of consumption used. Some users might experience digestive distress such as diarrhea or an increase in bile production known as biliousness. Skin sensitivities can also arise, leading to uncomfortable sensations of itching. Furthermore, more severe complications like jaundice or localized vaginal irritation have been noted during use.

According to a study published by "Pakistan journal of biological sciences : PJBS", Phyllanthus emblica is utilized for specific medicinal purposes. This plant is used as a diuretic to assist with bodily functions. It is also employed in the treatment of anemia. Additionally, the plant is used to address biliousness. These uses are part of a recorded inventory that includes 95 species from 38 families in the Samahni valley.
