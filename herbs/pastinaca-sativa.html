Pastinaca sativa

Pastinacica sativa, commonly known as wild parsnip, is a perennial herbaceous plant native to Eurasia. While its sweet roots are edible, the plant poses risks; its sap contains furanocoumarins, which can cause severe phytophotodermatitis when skin is exposed to sunlight. It is often categorized as a problematic invasive species.

  • Scientific name: Pastinaca sativa
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Pastinaca sativa, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Apiales and the family Apiaceae. Finally, it is classified under the genus Pastinaca.

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across various regions of Northern Europe. Specifically, it can be found growing throughout the coastal and inland areas of Denmark.
