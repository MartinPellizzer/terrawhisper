Angel's-trumpet, scientifically known as Brugmansia suaveolens, is a striking tropical shrub renowned for its large, pendulous, bell-shaped flowers. While celebrated for its intoxicating fragrance and ornamental beauty, this plant contains potent tropane alkaloids, making it highly toxic and requiring extreme caution when studied for its medicinal properties.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Brugmansia suaveolens is characterized by its bisexual sexual reproduction system, which relies on biotic pollination mechanisms, specifically through insects. The plant produces large, striking red flowers that measure between 20 cm and 25 cm in length, with a maximum width of 13 cm. The flowering season is extensive and variable, beginning as early as April and continuing through to December. To ensure genetic diversity, self-fertilization is absent in this species. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fruit categorized as a berry, which is indehiscent. These fruits are highly prolific, containing more than 1000 seeds per fruit, with an average individual seed mass of 0.012 g. The dispersal of these seeds follows an unspecialized syndrome, though it also exhibits anthropochorous tendencies, meaning dispersal is often influenced by human activity.
