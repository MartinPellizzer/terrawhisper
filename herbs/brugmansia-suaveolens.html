Brugmansia suaveolens

Brugmansia suaveolenz, commonly known as Angel's Trumpet, is a striking tropical shrub prized for its large, pendulous, bell-shaped flowers. While celebrated for its ornamental beauty, it contains potent tropane alkaloids. These compounds offer significant medicinal potential in controlled doses but pose serious toxicity if misused or ingested improperly.

  • Scientific name: Brugmansia suaveolens
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Solanales and the family Solanaceae, it is classified under the genus Brugmansia. Specifically, this species is identified as Brugmansia suaveolens.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across parts of Northern Africa and the Macaronesian islands. In the African continent, its presence is documented within the territories of Morocco and Tunisia. Moving toward the Atlantic Ocean, the species can be found inhabiting the Azores. It also extends its range to the Canary Islands. Finally, its distribution includes the island of Madeira within the Macaronesian archipelago.

Plant Parts

This plant, Brugmansia suaveolenes, possesses distinct morphological features in its medicinal components, most notably its large, green leaves and pendulous flowers. The leaves are typically ovate or elliptical, providing a broad surface area that is often studied for its complex alkaloid content. Complementing the foliage are its large, trumpet-shaped flowers, which hang gracefully from the branches and are highly fragrant, especially during the evening. Both the leaves and the flowers contain potent bioactive compounds, making them the primary parts of interest in ethnobotanical research and traditional medicinal practices.
