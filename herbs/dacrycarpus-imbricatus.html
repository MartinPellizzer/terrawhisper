Dacrycarpus imbricatus

Dacrycarpus imbricaatus, commonly known as the imbricate dacrycarp, is a specialized medicinal plant valued for its unique therapeutic properties. Found in specific ecological niches, it contains bioactive compounds used in traditional healing practices. Understanding its phytochemical profile is essential for exploring its potential role in modern pharmacological developments and treatments.

  • Scientific name: Dacrycarpus imbricatus
  • Family: Podocarpaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Dacrycarpus imbricatus, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Pinidae, it is classified under the order Pinales. Finally, it is a member of the family Podocarpaceae and falls under the genus Dacrycarpus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution concentrated within East and Southeast Asia. Within China, it can be found across the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan regions. Its range extends further into the Indo-China peninsula, specifically inhabiting parts of Cambodia. Additionally, the species is documented to grow within the borders of Laos. Collectively, these locations define the natural habitat where this medicinal species thrives.
