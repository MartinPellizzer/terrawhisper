Hoodia gordonii

Hoodia gordonii, commonly known as the Hoodia plant, is a succulent native to the arid regions of Southern Africa. Historically used by the San people to suppress hunger and thirst during long hunts, this remarkable botanical specimen is now widely researched for its potential natural appetite-suppressant properties in weight management.

  • Scientific name: Hoodia gordonii
  • Family: Apocynaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Gentianaales, it is classified under the family Apocynaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Hoodia.

Distribution

This plant is indigenous to the arid regions of Southern Africa. Its primary distribution can be found within the Cape Provinces, where it thrives in specific desert climates. Additionally, the species is widely spread across the landscape of Namibia. It also maintains a presence within the Free State region of the continent. Consequently, its habitat is concentrated in these specific southern territories.
