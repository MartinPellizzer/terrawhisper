Combretum hereroense

Russet bushwillow

Combretum hereroense, commonly known as the Silver Combretum, is a resilient medicinal shrub native to southern Africa. Renowned for its therapeutic properties, various parts of this plant are traditionally utilized to treat ailments such as digestive issues, skin infections, and inflammation, offering significant potential for modern pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Combretum hereroense
  • Family: Combretaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Myrtales, it is situated within the family Combretaceae. Specifically, it is identified by the genus Combretum and carries the species name Combretum hereroense.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Combretum hereroense, is referred to by various names depending on the region and language. In English, it is commonly called the Russet bushwillow, while in Afrikaans, it is known as the Kierieklapper, and in Tshivenda, it is called Xikhavi.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across the African continent, primarily concentrated within the tropical regions. In Northeast Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in Ethiopia, Somalia, and the territories of Sudan and South Sudan. Moving toward East Tropical Africa, the species can also be found growing in Kenya. Furthermore, its range extends southward into Tanzania. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's preference for the diverse landscapes of East and Northeast Africa.
