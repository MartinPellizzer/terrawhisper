What's the taxonomical classification of Dryopteris peninsulae?

Dryopteris peninsulae belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this group, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Polypodiinae. Following its taxonomic lineage further, it falls under the order Polypodiales and is a member of the family Polypodiaceae. Finally, its specific classification is defined by the genus Dryopteris and the species peninsulae.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Dryopteris peninsulae has large, bipinnate to tripinnate fronds that typically measure between 30 and 80 centimeters in length, featuring a dark brown to blackish stipe covered in pale, lanceolate scales. The pinnae are arranged alternately along the rachis and possess deeply lobed, serrated margins with acute tips. Each pinnule is broadly ovate or oblong in shape, exhibiting a texture that is often slightly leathery or coriaceous. The underside of the fertile fronds contains prominent, circular sori arranged in two neat rows near the midvein of each segment. These reproductive structures are protected by a characteristic kidney-shaped indusium that eventually withers as the spores mature.
