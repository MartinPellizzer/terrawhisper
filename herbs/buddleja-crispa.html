Buddleja crispa

What's the taxonomical classification of Buddleja crispa?

Buddlejia crispa belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, the plant is placed in the order Lamiales and the family Scrophulariaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Buddleja, and its specific species name, crispa.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Buddlejia crispa has an erect, woody shrub habit characterized by lanceolate, serrated leaves and dense, terminal panicles of small, tubular, pale lilac to white flowers. The foliage is notably rugose with a textured surface and a distinctively wrinkled appearance. Its stems are often branched and can become somewhat woody at the base as the plant matures. The inflorescences consist of numerous tiny florets tightly packed along elongated stalks. These flowers are typically accompanied by small, globose fruit that develop after successful pollination.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude mountainous regions of western China, specifically within the provinces of Sichuan, Yunnan, and Gansu. It typically thrives in subalpine environments where the terrain is rocky and well-drained. These botanical specimens are often found growing along mountain slopes and in forest margins at elevations ranging from 2,500 to 4,000 meters. The specific microclimates of these eastern Himalayan foothills provide the necessary moisture and temperate conditions for its survival. Because of its specialized habitat requirements, its natural range remains relatively localized to these specific Asian highlands.

According to a study published by "Journal of enzyme inhibition and medicinal chemistry", phytochemical investigations on the ethyl acetate-soluble fraction of the whole plant of Buddlejia crispia led to the isolation of iridoids 1-7. Compound 2 displayed significant inhibitory potential against the enzyme lipoxygenase in a concentration-dependent fashion with an IC50 value of 39.7 +/- 0.02 microM. This same compound also demonstrated DPPH radical scavenging activity with an IC50 value of 0.638 mM. What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds agnuside, buddlejoside A, buddlejoside B, iridoids 1, and iridoids 2. These specific chemical constituents are classified as secoiridoid glycosides which contribute to the plant's pharmacological profile. Researchers study these molecules to understand their potential therapeutic properties in various biological systems. The presence of these diverse iridoid derivatives highlights the complex chemical makeup of Buddleja crispa. Such specialized compounds are often the focus of investigations into natural drug development.

According to a study published by "Journal of natural products", two alleged iridoid galactoside compounds from Buddlejia crispa named buddlejoside A and B, designated as 12a and 12b, were analyzed. The compound buddlejoside A (12a) was shown to be identical to agnuside (13a). Additionally, buddlejoside B (12b) was identified as 3,4-dihydroxybenzoylaucubin (13b). This specific iridoid glucoside (13b) had not been previously published.
