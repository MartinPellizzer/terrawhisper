Buddleja crispa

Buddleja crispa, commonly known as the crisp-leaved butterfly bush, is a versatile medicinal shrub native to East Asia. Renowned for its striking, fragrant blooms, it is traditionally utilized in herbal medicine to treat inflammatory conditions, skin ailments, and respiratory issues, offering a rich profile of bioactive phytochemical compounds.

  • Scientific name: Buddleja crispa
  • Family: Scrophulariaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Lamiales, it is a member of the family Scrophulariaceae. Specifically, Buddleja crispa is identified by the genus Buddleja.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Buddleja crispa, is referred to by various names across different linguistic contexts. In Chinese, it is commonly called 皱叶醉鱼草, a name that is consistently used in the zh, zh-hans, and zh-cn language codes. Interestingly, in the Azerbaijani language, the plant is documented under the names Buddlejia acosmia and Buddlejia agathosma.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across parts of Asia. In Western Asia, its range includes the countries of Afghanistan and Iran. Within China, it is found across the South-Central and North-Central regions. Additionally, the species extends into the high-altitude areas of Tibet. These diverse locations highlight its presence in both Middle Eastern and East Asian landscapes.
