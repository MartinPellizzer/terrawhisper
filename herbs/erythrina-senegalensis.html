Erythrina senegalensis

Erythrina senegalensis, commonly known as the Senegal coral tree, is a versatile medicinal plant native to Africa. Renowned for its vibrant red flowers, it holds significant ethnobotanical importance. Various parts of the tree are traditionally utilized to treat ailments ranging from skin infections and inflammation to digestive and respiratory issues.

  • Scientific name: Erythrina senegalensis
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales, it is a member of the Fabaceae family. Finally, it is identified by the genus Erythrina.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Erythrina senegalensis, is referred to by various common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In Arabic, it is called حمرية سنغالية, while in the Dagbani language, it is known as Taŋjibga. In Bambara, the plant is called Ntenbilen, and in Chinese, it is identified as 塞内加尔刺桐.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the diverse landscapes of West Tropical Africa. Specifically, its presence can be documented within the borders of Benin. It also grows naturally throughout the territories of Burkina and Gambia. Furthermore, the species is found in the regions of Ghana. Finally, its geographical range extends into Guinea-Bissau.
