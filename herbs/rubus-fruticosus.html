Bramble, scientifically known as Rubus fruticosus, is a versatile, thorny shrub renowned for its nutrient-dense fruits. Beyond its culinary appeal, this plant holds significant medicinal value. Rich in antioxidants, vitamins, and tannins, it is traditionally used to support digestive health, reduce inflammation, and promote skin healing and vitality.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several regions within Northern Europe. In the British Isles, it can be found growing throughout both Great Britain and Ireland. Moving further north, the species is also established in the country of Denmark. Its presence extends into the Scandinavian Peninsula, where it is common in Norway. Additionally, the plant is frequently documented across the landscapes of Sweden.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as blackberry, contains various components that are utilized for their diverse therapeutic properties. The nutrient-rich leaves are frequently used in herbal preparations due to their antioxidant content, while the juicy fruit serves as a primary source of vitamins and polyphenols. In modern biotechnology, researchers utilize suspension cultures to mass-produce specific bioactive compounds in a controlled laboratory environment, bypassing the need for seasonal harvesting. Furthermore, the concentrated berries extract is highly valued in both the pharmaceutical and cosmetic industries for its potent anti-inflammatory and skin-protective qualities.
