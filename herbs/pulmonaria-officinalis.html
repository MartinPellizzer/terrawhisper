Pulmonaria officinalis

Lungwort

Pulmonaria officinalis, commonly known as lungwort, is a perennial herb prized in traditional herbalism. Characterized by its distinctive spotted leaves and bell-shaped flowers, it has historically been used to support respiratory health. This medicinal plant serves as a vital remedy for soothing coughs and supporting overall lung function.

  • Scientific name: Pulmonaria officinalis
  • Family: Boraginaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following its placement in the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Boraginales, it is a member of the Boraginaceae family. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Pulmonaria.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Pulmonaria officinalis, is recognized by various common names across different languages, reflecting its traditional medicinal uses. In Poland, it is called Miodunka plamista, while in France, it is referred to as Pulmonaire officinale. The Hungarian name, orvosi tüdőfű, highlights its historical association with lung health, a connection also seen in the Latvian name, dziedniecības lakaciis, and the Estonian name, Harilik kopsurohi.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several key regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence is well-documented in Denmark and Great Britain. It can also be found growing within the landscapes of Ireland and Sweden. Moving toward the central part of the continent, the species extends into Middle Europe. Specifically, its geographical range includes the territory of Austria.
