Reproduction

The reproduction of Terminalia superbba is characterized by a biotic pollination strategy, primarily involving insects, with bees playing a specific and significant role in the process. The species exhibits an absence of self-fertilization, suggesting a reliance on cross-pollination to maintain genetic diversity. Once fertilization occurs, the resulting seeds are dispersed via an anemochorous syndrome, utilizing wind for movement. The seeds themselves are relatively small and uniform in size, with a recorded seed volume that remains constant at a mean, minimum, and maximum of 64 mm³. In terms of weight, the seed mass varies slightly, ranging from a minimum of 0.061 g to a maximum of 0.112 g, with an average seed mass of 0.09735 g.
