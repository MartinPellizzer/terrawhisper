Pulsatilla alpina subsp. apiifolia

Pulsatilla sulphurea

Pulsatilla alpina subsp. apiifolia, commonly known as Alpine Pasqueflower, is a resilient perennial native to high-altitude European meadows. Renowned for its striking violet blooms, this botanical treasure has long been studied for its bioactive compounds, offering significant traditional medicinal potential in treating inflammatory conditions and various skin ailments.

  • Scientific name: Pulsatilla alpina subsp. apiifolia
  • Family: Ranunculaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Ranunculales and the family Ranunculaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic designation is within the genus Pulsatilla.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Pulsatilla alpina subsp. apiifolia, is recognized by several distinct common names depending on the language and region. In German, it is frequently referred to as the Gelbe Alpen-Kuhschelle, Schwefelanemone, Schwefel-Anemone, or Schwefel-Küchenschelle. In French, it is commonly called la Pulsatille soufrée.
