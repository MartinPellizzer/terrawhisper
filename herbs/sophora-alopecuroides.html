Sophora alopecuroides

What's the taxonomical classification of Sophora alopecuroides?

Sophora alopecuroides belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant falls under the order Fabales. It is further categorized into the family Fabaceae, which is characteristic of many nitrogen-fixing legumes. Within this family, it is placed in the genus Sophora, ultimately being identified by its specific species name, alopecuroides.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Sophora alopecuroides has pinnately compound leaves with numerous small, oblong leaflets and small, pale yellow flowers that emerge from axillary clusters. The foliage is characterized by a dense, hairy texture on both the surfaces of the leaflets and the stems. Its stems are woody and can develop a somewhat scrambling or climbing habit depending on the environment. The plant produces small, elongated legume pods that contain the seeds. These morphological traits are adapted to help the plant thrive in specific tropical or subtropical habitats.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Sophora alopecuroides is used medicinally through its aerial parts, leaf, root, seed, and stem. The roots are frequently utilized in traditional remedies to treat various ailments. Both the leaves and the stems are harvested to prepare infusions or topical applications. The seeds also contain bioactive compounds that contribute to its therapeutic profile. By utilizing these diverse components, practitioners can target specific health concerns effectively.

According to a study published by "Journal of natural products", the aerial parts of Sophora alopecuroides were used to isolate a new matrine-type alkaloid named 7alpha-hydroxysophoramine (1). This specific compound was found alongside 8 known alkaloids. These include 12beta-hydroxysophocarpine (2), sophoramine (3), 14beta-hydroxymatrine, matrine, sophoridine, sophocarpine, adenocarpine, and baptifoline. The structures of compounds 1-3 were confirmed through single-crystal X-ray diffraction analysis.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant Sophora alopecuroides is used in Traditional Chinese Medicine to clear heat, resolve toxicity, and reduce swelling through its root bark known as Ku Shen. Historically, practitioners have prescribed it to treat various dermatological conditions such as itchy skin, eczema, and fungal infections. The plant's bioactive alkaloids provide potent antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties that have been central to its medicinal applications for centuries. Beyond topical uses, it has also been incorporated into formulas to address internal issues like damp-heat in the lower abdomen. These longstanding practices continue to inform modern pharmacological research into its therapeutic potential.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the quinolizidine alkaloids matrine, sophocarpine, oxymatrine, oxysophocarpine, and sophoridine. These bioactive chemical constituents are responsible for many of the therapeutic properties attributed to the species. Researchers have studied these specific compounds for their potential pharmacological effects on the central nervous system and various metabolic processes. The concentration of these alkaloids can vary significantly depending on the environmental conditions during growth. Understanding the chemical profile of these substances is essential for developing standardized herbal medicines.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", seven alkaloids were isolated from the seed of Sophora alopecuroides. These specific compounds were identified as oxymatrin, oxysophocarpine, cytisine, matrine, sophocarpine, sophoridine, and nicotine. This study marked the first time that nicotine was isolated from Sophora. The research involved carrying out in vitro tests to evaluate the activity of the extracts and alkaloids against cancer, virus, dermatophytes, and bacteria.

According to a study published by "Microscopy research and technique", six different solvents were used to prepare Sophora alopecuroides seed extracts. The methanol extract contained 93.76 ± 2.71 GAE/mg of phenolic contents and 77 ± 3.60 QE/mg of flavonoid contents, while also demonstrating a highest DPPH scavenging potential of 82%. For LOSM, a total antioxidant capacity of 90.60 ± 1.55 alpha amylase enzyme [AAE]/mg and a total reducing power of 94.44 ± 1.38 AAE/mg were determined. The SASM showed the highest alpha amylase inhibition at 78.20 ± 1.58%. Additionally, the n-hexane extract (SASH) had a highest LD50 of brine shrimp at 13.03 μg/ml.

According to a study published by "Journal of proteomics", Sophora alopecuroides is a medicinal plant known for its anti-tumor, anti-inflammatoroy, and antibacterial effects, as well as its anti-saline properties and resistance to drought stress. Proteomic profiling of the leaves was performed under salt stress conditions induced by 150 mM NaCl for 3 d and 7 d. The study identified a total of 434 differentially abundant proteins (DAPs) in response to these salt stress conditions. While several transporter proteins related to the secondary messenger signaling pathway were altered after 3 d, certain transferase, oxidoreductase, and dehydrogenase levels were mainly altered after 7 d. These findings were validated through parallel reaction monitoring (PRM) and data are available on ProteomeXchange under PXD027627.
