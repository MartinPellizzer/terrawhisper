Centipeda minima

What's the taxonomical classification of Centipeda minima?

Centipeda minima belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is situated under the order Asterales. It is further categorized into the family Asteraceae, which is a large and diverse group of flowering plants. Within this family, it is placed in the genus Centipeda, and its specific identification is completed by its species name, minima.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Centipeda minima has small, inconspicuous flowers with five petals that are typically white or pale pink and a distinctively narrow, linear leaf structure. The plant is characterized by its diminutive stature and slender, creeping stems that allow it to spread across the soil surface. Each leaf is lanceolate in shape and features finely serrated margins along the edges. The reproductive organs are centrally located within the small floral structure, often possessing short filaments and anthers. These morphological traits are adapted to its specific ecological niche, facilitating survival in low-growth environments.

This plant Centipeda minima thrives when grown in well-draining, sandy soil under bright, indirect sunlight and consistent moisture levels.

According to a study published by "Frontiers in oncology", Centipeda minima is a traditional Chinese herbal medicine historically used to treat rhinitis, sinusitis, relieve pain, and reduce swelling. The total extract of Centipeda minima (CME) was found to significantly reduce cell viability and proliferation in MDA-MB-231 cells in a dose and time-dependent manner. CME may potentially act via inhibition of multiple signaling pathways, including the EGFR, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, NF-κB, and STAT3 pathways. Furthermore, CME could significantly reduce tumor burden in MDA-MB-231 xenograft mice without any appreciable side effects.

Understanding the concentration of these compounds helps researchers determine the efficacy of Centipeda minima in traditional medicine.

According to a study published by "Phytomedicine : international journal of phytotherapy and phytopharmacology", Centipeda minima contains a sesquiterpene lactone known as Arnicolide C. This compound has demonstrated potential anticancer activity through the induction of ROS accumulation in HCC cells. Arnicolide C works by modulating the PI3K/Akt and MAPK pathways to downregulate the oncogene MYC. In studies, AC markedly inhibited HCC cell proliferation, triggered apoptosis, and suppressed metastatic behaviors. Furthermore, the antitumor efficacy of AC was validated in a xenograft mouse model where it reduced tumor growth without overt toxicity.

According to a study published by "Frontiers in pharmacology", Centipeda minima extract (CME) has demonstrated anti-cancer effects in human nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC). Breviliin A, a sesquiterpene lactone isolated from C. minima, was tested on NPC cell lines including CNE-1, CNE-2, SUNE-1, HONE1, and C666-1. This substance inhibited NPC cell viability in a concentration- and time-dependent manner by inducing cell cycle arrest at G2/M and inducing apoptosis. In a tumor xenograft model, brevilin A reduced tumor growth at a similar magnitude to cisplatin. Notably, treatment with brevilin A did not lead to the significant weight loss in mice observed with cisplatin treatment.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause various adverse health reactions including allergy, cough, headache, rhinitis, and sinusitis. Individuals exposed to its components may experience allergic reactions that manifest as respiratory irritation or skin sensitivities. These symptoms often include a persistent cough and inflammation of the nasal passages known as rhinitis. Furthermore, the plant can trigger sinus inflammation or sinusitis in sensitive subjects. Some people also report suffering from frequent headaches as a secondary result of these physiological responses.

According to a study published by "Phytomedicine : international journal of phytotherapy and phytopharmacology", Centipeda minima (L.) A. Braun & Asch has been used as a traditional Chinese herbal medicine to treat diseases such as sinusitis, rhinitis, headache, and allergy. The plant's extracts exhibit both anticancer and chemosensitizing effects through active components like sesquiterpene lactones. The most abundant sesquiterpene lactone, 6-O-angeloylplenolin (6-OAP, or brevilin A), targets the Skp1-Cullin1-F-box protein (SCF) E3 ubiquitin ligase and signal transducers and activators of transcription 3 (STAT3). Clinical trials have assessed the potential of 6-OAP in patients with vertex baldness and alopecia areata due to its effect on JAK-STATs signaling. Additionally, chlorogenic acid is a representative organic acid in Centipeda minima that has been approved for phase II clinical trials in patients with glioma in China.
