Scientifically known as Bupleurum flavum, is a perennial medicinal herb native to East Asia. Renowned in traditional medicine, its roots contain saikosaponins, which offer potent anti-inflammatory and hepatoprotective properties. Often used to treat liver ailments and fever, this versatile plant remains a significant subject in modern pharmacological research.

Life history

The life history of Bupleurum flavum is characterized by its classification as a therophyte, meaning it completes its annual life cycle through the production of seeds that allow it to persist through unfavorable seasons. Within the various botanical life form classifications, it is specifically identified as a therophyte under both the secondary and primary life form descriptors, indicating that the majority of its vegetative biomass dies back at the end of the growing season, leaving only seeds to ensure the continuity of the species. This strategy allows the plant to synchronize its development with favorable environmental conditions, rapidly germinating, flowering, and setting seed before the onset of drought or temperature extremes.
