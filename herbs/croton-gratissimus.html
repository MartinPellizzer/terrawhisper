Beyond its aesthetic appeal, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Various cultures utilize its leaves and extracts in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to digestive issues.

Physiology

The physiology of Croton gratissimus is characterized by complex metabolic processes involving the synthesis and storage of various secondary metabolites, particularly diterpene esters and polyphenols, which serve as chemical defense mechanisms against herbivory. Its photosynthetic pathways are optimized for growth in diverse African landscapes, allowing for efficient carbon assimilation under varying light intensities. Regarding its nutritional interaction with the soil, the plant does not function as a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria, such as Rhizobium, typically found in leguminous species, and therefore relies on the uptake of inorganic nitrogen from the soil substrate. This absence of nitrogen-fixing capabilities means its physiological development and nitrogen assimilation are directly dependent on the availability of nitrate and ammonium ions within the immediate rhizosphere.
