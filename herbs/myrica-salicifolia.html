Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across various regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found inhabiting the landscapes of Burundi, Cameroon, and Rwanda. Its presence is also noted within the vast territories of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Moving toward a different climatic zone, the species extends into Northeast Tropical Africa. Specifically, it has been documented growing in the country of Eritrea.

The morphology of Myrica salicifolia is characterized by its growth form as a tree, exhibiting a robust and woody structure. In terms of its ecological habit, the plant is primarily terrestrial, though it can exist within a spectrum of habitats ranging from semiaquatic to aquatic environments. Reproduction

The reproduction of Myrica salicifolia is characterized by the production of small, specialized fruits and seeds designed for effective propagation. The plant produces fruit classified as a drupe, which is characterized by its indehiscent nature, meaning it does not split open at maturity to release its contents. These fruits are relatively uniform in size, possessing a mean length of 0.5 cm and a mean width of 0.5 cm. Within these fruits, the seeds exhibit a highly consistent mass, with a recorded mean, minimum, and maximum value of 0.0253 g. The dispersal of the species is driven by a zoochorous syndrome, indicating that the plant relies on animals to facilitate the movement and distribution of its seeds across the landscape.
