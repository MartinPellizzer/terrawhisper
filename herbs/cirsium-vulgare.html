Cirsium vulgare

Cirsium lanceolatum

Cirsium vulgare, commonly known as bull thistle, is a hardy perennial herb recognized by its prickly leaves and purple flower heads. Historically used in traditional medicine, it possesses diuretic and anti-inflammatory properties. This resilient plant is often studied for its potential bioactive compounds and diverse ecological roles in various habitats.

  • Scientific name: Cirsium vulgare
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales, it is a member of the Asteraceae family. Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as bull thistle, possesses several distinct morphological features used in various botanical studies. The robust roots typically consist of a deep taproot system that anchors the plant firmly in the soil. Its large, prickly leaves are characterized by deep lobes and sharp spines along the margins, serving as a primary site for photosynthesis. The reproductive stage is marked by a striking, thistle-like inflorescence, which consists of dense, purple-to-pink flower heads. Following pollination, the plant produces small fruits known as achenes, which are often dispersed by the wind.
