Cirsium lanceolatum, scientifically known as Cirsium vulgare, is a hardy perennial herb belonging to the Asteraceae family. Commonly called bull thistle, this plant is recognized by its prickly leaves and purple flower heads. Historically, it has been utilized in traditional medicine for its potential anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as bull thistle, possesses several distinct morphological features used in various botanical studies. The robust roots typically consist of a deep taproot system that anchors the plant firmly in the soil. Its large, prickly leaves are characterized by deep lobes and sharp spines along the margins, serving as a primary site for photosynthesis. The reproductive stage is marked by a striking, thistle-like inflorescence, which consists of dense, purple-to-pink flower heads. Following pollination, the plant produces small fruits known as achenes, which are often dispersed by the wind. Each of these seeds is encased in a specialized fruit wall that protects the developing embryo until it is ready for dispersal.
