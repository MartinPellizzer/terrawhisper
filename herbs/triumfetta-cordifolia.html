What's the taxonomical classification of Triumfetta cordifolia?

Triumfetta cordifolia belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, the plant is part of the order Malvales and falls within the family Malvaceae. It is further defined by its genus, Triumfetta, and is specifically identified by its species name, cordifolia.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Triумfetta cordifolia utilizes its leaves for various medicinal applications in traditional healing practices. The crushed leaves are often applied topically as a paste to treat skin inflammations and minor wounds. Additionally, some cultures prepare decoctions from the foliage to address internal ailments or digestive issues. These leaves contain bioactive compounds that contribute to their therapeutic properties. Through these different methods, the plant serves as a versatile resource in folk medicine.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", the leaf extract of Triumfetta cordifolia was observed to display moderate in vitro antiplasmodial activity with an IC50 = 48.09 μg/ml. This extract was non-toxic to HEK293 cells and erythrocytes. At a dose of 400 mg/kg, T. cordifolia significantly (p<0.001) suppressed parasitemia and significantly (p<0.001) inhibited RBC depletion. Furthermore, this dosage prolonged survival in infected mice. This represents the first report of the antiplasmodial efficacy of the T. cordifolia ethanol extract.
