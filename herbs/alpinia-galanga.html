Siamese-ginger

Alpinia galanga · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (762 studies) · ★★★★★

Siamese-ginger, scientifically known as Alpinia galanga, is a versatile perennial herb native to Southeast Asia. Widely used in traditional medicine and culinary practices, this aromatic plant offers potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties. Its rhizomes are highly valued for treating digestive ailments and supporting overall metabolic health.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Alpinia galanga, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Zingiberales and the family Zingiberaceae. Its specific taxonomic classification is completed within the genus Alpinia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions of Asia. Within China, it is found spanning from the South-Central provinces to the Southeast regions. Its presence is also noted on the tropical island of Hainan. Furthermore, the species is distributed throughout Eastern Asia, specifically in Taiwan. Finally, its range extends into the Indian Subcontinent, where it is found in Assam.

Plant Parts

This plant, Alpinia galanga, is characterized by its robust structural components, most notably its aromatic rhizomes, which are the primary parts used in traditional medicine. While the rhizome serves as the main storage organ for essential oils, the plant also develops upright, leafy stems that support its growth. Although it possesses a system of roots to anchor itself in the soil, it is the fleshy, underground portions that hold the most therapeutic value, rather than the seeds produced during its reproductive cycle.
