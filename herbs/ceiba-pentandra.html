Reproduction

The reproduction of Ceiba pentandra is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically utilizing bats as primary pollinators. The flowering period typically occurs between February and March, featuring pink flowers that range in length from 1.5 cm to 2 cm. Self-fertilization is absent in this species, necessitating cross-pollination. Following successful fertilization, the plant produces dry, dehiscence-type fruits. These fruits vary in size, with a mean length of 11.25 cm (ranging from 7 cm to a maximum of 15 cm) and a width between 3.5 cm and 4 cm. The seeds produced are relatively small, with a mean length of 4 mm (ranging from 1 mm to 6 mm) and a mean mass of approximately 0.034 g, though individual seed mass can reach up to 0.0899 g. The seed volume is consistently recorded at 10 mm³. Dispersal of the seeds is achieved through anemochory (wind dispersal) and anthropochory (human-mediated dispersal).
