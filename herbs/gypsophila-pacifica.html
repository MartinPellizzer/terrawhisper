Gypsophila pacifica

What's the taxonomical classification of Gypsophila pacifica?

Gypsophila pacifica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further categorized under the order Caryophyllales. This plant is a member of the family Caryophyllaceae, falling within the genus Gypsophila, and is specifically identified by the species name pacifica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Gypsophila pacifica has a perennial growth habit characterized by slender, erect stems that typically reach heights of 10 to 40 centimeters and bear clusters of small, five-petaled white flowers. The leaves are primarily basal and lanceolate in shape, often featuring smooth margins and a slightly fleshy texture. As the plant matures, the cauline leaves become smaller and more sparse along the ascending stems. The inflorescence consists of many-flowered cymes that create a delicate, airy appearance above the foliage. Each individual flower possesses a small calyx and a central cluster of stamens that complement its dainty aesthetic.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is endemic to the coastal regions of central and northern California, specifically occurring along the Pacific shoreline. It is most commonly found inhabiting stabilized sand dunes and coastal scrub habitats. Its distribution is limited to narrow strips of land where maritime influence provides necessary moisture and soil conditions. Populations are often clustered in specific counties such as Monterey, San Luis Obispo, and Santa Cruz. Because its range is so localized, the species is highly sensitive to habitat fragmentation along the coast.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-drained, sandy or rocky soil and full sun exposure to thrive in a Mediterranean-style garden.

Because it is native to the coastal regions of California, it possesses a high tolerance for drought once its root system is established. You should avoid overwatering, as excessive moisture can easily lead to root rot. It is best sown directly into the garden site after the last frost to allow for steady development.

Regular pruning after the blooming period can help encourage more vigorous growth in subsequent seasons.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds 3β-O-sulfate gypsogenin, 3β-O-sulfate gypsogenin 28-O-β-d-glucopyranosyl ester, and 3β-O-sulfate quillaic acid. These specific chemical constituents belong to the triterpenoid saponin class often found in the Gypsophila genus. Researchers study these molecules to understand their potential biological activities and therapeutic properties. The presence of these sulfated compounds distinguishes the chemical profile of Gypsophila pacifica from other related species. Detailed analysis of these substances helps clarify how the plant interacts with biological systems.

As discussed in a study, the roots of Gypsophila pacifica Kom. contain three new sulfated triterpenoids identified as 1, 2, and 3. These newly isolated compounds include 3β-O-sulfate gypsogenin 28-O-β-d-glucopyranosyl ester (1), 3β-O-sulfate gypsogenin (2), and 3β-O-sulfate quillaic acid (3). In addition to these 3 new compounds, one known compound labeled as 4 was also isolated from the plant. The structures for these compounds were established using 1D, 2D NMR, and HR-ESI-MS methods.
