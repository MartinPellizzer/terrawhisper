Pedicularis rex belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this group, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant follows the taxonomic order Lamiales and is a member of the family Orobanchaceae. Finally, it is situated within the genus Pedicularis, specifically identified as the species rex.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Pedicularis rex has a tall, erect growth habit characterized by elongated stems that often reach heights of up to 60 centimeters and are topped with dense, terminal spikes of tubular, irregular flowers. The inflorescence consists of numerous zygomorphic blossoms that feature a prominent, hood-like upper lip and a smaller, three-lobed lower lip. Its leaves are typically basal or distributed along the stem in opposite pairs, exhibiting a deeply pinnatifid or lobed structure with serrated margins. The floral colors vary between shades of pink, purple, or reddish-violet, providing a striking contrast to the green foliage. These morphological traits are specifically adapted to attract specialized pollinators in its alpine or subalpine habitats.
