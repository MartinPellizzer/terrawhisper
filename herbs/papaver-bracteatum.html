Persian poppy

Papaver bracteatum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (258 studies) · ★★★★★

Persian poppy, scientifically known as Papaver bracteatum, is a striking perennial herb native to the Middle East. Renowned for its vibrant, cup-shaped blooms and distinct leafy bracts, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. It is widely studied for its bioactive compounds and traditional uses in various medicinal practices.

Plant Parts

This plant, Papaver bracteatum, is characterized by several distinct morphological features that contribute to its medicinal profile. The plant develops a robust system of roots, which often includes a specialized hairy root structure that aids in nutrient absorption from the soil. When the stem or tissues are incised, the plant exudes a milky latex, a substance known for containing various bioactive alkaloids. Furthermore, the striking appearance of the species is defined by its large, colorful petals, which serve as a primary visual identifier for this poppy species.
