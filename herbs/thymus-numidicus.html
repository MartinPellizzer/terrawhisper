Thymus numidicus

Thymus numidicus, commonly known as Numidian thyme, is a distinctive aromatic shrub native to the Mediterranean landscapes of North Africa. Characterized by its resilient structure and potent essential oils, this medicinal plant is traditionally valued for its antimicrobial, digestive, and antiseptic properties within various regional folk medicine practices.

  • Scientific name: Thymus numidicus
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales and family Lamiaceae, it is classified under the genus Thymus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Thymus numidicus, is referred to by various common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In English, it is frequently called Numidian thyme, while in Arabic, it is known as al-za'tar al-numidi (الزعتر النمدي). Additionally, in French, the species is identified as thym de Numidie.

Distribution

This plant is a medicinal species specifically found within the regions of Northern Africa. Its natural habitat is primarily limited to the territories of Algeria and Tunisia. Within these two countries, it grows according to its unique environmental requirements. The geographical range of Thymus numidicus is thus quite restricted to this particular part of the continent. Consequently, conservation efforts must focus on these specific North African locations to protect the species.
