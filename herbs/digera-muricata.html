False Amaranth, scientifically known as Digera muriata, is a versatile annual herb widely recognized for its profound medicinal properties. Often found in tropical regions, this plant is traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat various ailments, including digestive issues, skin inflammations, and kidney disorders, thanks to its rich bioactive compounds.

The physiology of Digera muricata is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, meaning it utilizes the Calvin cycle to fix carbon dioxide directly through the enzyme RuBisCO without the specialized carbon-concentrating mechanisms found in C4 or CAM plants. This metabolic process implies that the plant is most efficient in environments with moderate temperatures and sufficient water availability, as it is susceptible to photorespiration under high-heat or drought conditions. Furthermore, the plant does not function as a nitrogen fixer, lacking the symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms, and thus relies on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium from the soil to support its protein synthesis and metabolic activities.
