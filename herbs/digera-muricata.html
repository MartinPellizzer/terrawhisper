Digera muricata

Digera muricata, commonly known as the wild melon or cucamelon, is a versatile medicinal plant prized in traditional healing. Rich in bioactive compounds, it possesses significant antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties. Often used to support digestive health and skin vitality, this resilient species offers profound therapeutic potential.

  • Scientific name: Digera muricata
  • Family: Amaranthaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Caryophyllales, it is classified under the family Amaranthaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Digera.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Digera muricata, is recognized by different names depending on the linguistic context. In Traditional Chinese (zh-tw), it is commonly referred to as 瘤果莧, while in Simplified Chinese (zh), it is known as 长序苋.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning multiple continents and climate zones. In Middle Europe, its presence can be documented within the borders of Germany. Moving toward the African continent, it is found across Northern Africa, specifically in Egypt. The species also extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, with occurrences noted in Burundi. Finally, its range reaches into Northeast Tropical Africa, where it is located in both Djibouti and Eritrea.
