Plumbago europaea

Plumbago europaea, commonly known as the Leadwort, is a perennial herb prized in traditional herbalism. Characterized by its delicate blue flowers, this plant contains bioactive compounds with significant medicinal potential. Historically utilized for its antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties, it remains a subject of interest in modern ethnobotanical research.

  • Scientific name: Plumbago europaea
  • Family: Plumbaginaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Plumbago europaea, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Caryophyllales. Finally, it is a member of the family Plumbaginaceae and the genus Plumbago.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several regions of the European continent. In Middle Europe, its presence is noted within the territories of Germany as well as the Czechia-Slovakia region. Moving toward the Mediterranean, it can be found throughout various parts of Southwestern Europe. Specifically, the species is documented in France and on the island of Corse. Additionally, its range extends to the island of Sardegna within the same southwestern territory.
