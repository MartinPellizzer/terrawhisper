Physiology

The physiology of Kalanchoe laciniata is primarily characterized by its highly specialized photosynthetic pathway, specifically utilizing Crassulacean Acid Metabolism (CAM). This metabolic adaptation allows the plant to thrive in arid or semi-arid environments by decoupling the processes of gas exchange and light-dependent reactions. During the nocturnal phase, the plant opens its stomata to minimize transpirational water loss, facilitating the uptake of carbon dioxide which is then chemically fixed and stored as malic acid within the vacuoles of its succulent cells. During the subsequent diurnal period, the stomata remain closed to conserve moisture, while the stored malic acid is decarboxylated to release carbon dioxide internally for fixation via the C3 cycle using sunlight. This temporal separation of CO2 uptake and fixation optimizes water-use efficiency and enables the plant to maintain metabolic activity under significant water stress.
