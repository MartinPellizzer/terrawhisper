Capparis flavicans

What's the taxonomical classification of Capparis flavicans?

Capparis flavicans belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further organized under the order Brassicales. The plant is a representative of the family Capparaceae, falling specifically within the genus Capparis, and is identified by its unique species name, flavicans.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Capparis flavicans has a woody, perennial shrub structure characterized by spreading branches and trifoliate leaves composed of three distinct oval-shaped leaflets. The foliage is typically leathery in texture and can exhibit a slightly hairy surface on the undersides. Small, fragrant white flowers emerge from the axils, featuring prominent long stamens that create a delicate appearance. Following pollination, the plant produces fleshy, globose berries that transition in color as they mature. These morphological traits allow the species to adapt effectively to its native arid and semi-arid environments.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the arid and semi-arid regions of Southern Africa, specifically spanning across South Africa, Namibia, Botswana, and Zimbabwe. It thrives in various habitats including dry savannas, rocky hillsides, and scrublands. The species is particularly well-adapted to the harsh climates of the Karoo and Kalahari ecosystems. Its presence is often noted in sandy soils where it can withstand significant drought periods. This wide range across the subcontinent allows it to play a vital role in local biodiversity.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant is primarily utilized for its medicinal properties through the use of its aerial parts, which include the leaves, stems, and flowers. These components are often prepared as decoctions or infusions to treat various ailments such as skin infections and digestive issues. Some traditional practices also involve applying crushed leaves directly to wounds to promote healing. The chemical constituents found within these above-ground structures contribute to the plant's overall therapeutic potential. This targeted use of the aerial parts allows practitioners to access the most active botanical elements of the species.

According to a study published by "Journal of natural products", the aerial parts of the Thai medicinal plant Capparis flavicans have been traditionally used to promote lactation. The investigation of these aerial parts yielded the new compound capparoside A (1) along with 28 known compounds. Among the isolated substances, syringaresinol (3) and principin (6) were identified as being derived from C. flavicans. These specific compounds showed the most potent inhibitory effects on estrogen-enhanced cell proliferation when tested using the estrogen-responsive human breast cancer cell lines MCF-7 and T47D. The results suggest that the lactation-promoting properties of C. flavicans might be related to the inhibitory effect on excess estrogen.

What health conditions is this plant used for?
