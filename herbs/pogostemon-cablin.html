Pogostemon cablin

Pogostemon cablin, commonly known as patchouli, is a highly aromatic tropical plant celebrated for its intense, earthy fragrance. Prized in the perfume industry and traditional medicine, its essential oil offers therapeutic benefits, including skin healing and calming properties, making it a versatile botanical treasure in holistic wellness practices.

  • Scientific name: Pogostemon cablin
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Its specific taxonomic placement concludes within the genus Pogostemon.

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several distinct regions. In Middle Europe, it can be found in Germany. Finally, the species is also located within the Indian Subcontinent in Sri Lanka.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as patchouli, is primarily utilized for its aromatic properties derived from its various anatomical structures. The most significant components used in medicinal and perfumery applications are the leaves and the overall aerial parts, which are harvested to extract the plant's essence. Within these structures, the stems also contribute to the total biomass, though the potency is concentrated in the foliage. The primary therapeutic and olfactory value lies in the rich volatile oil produced by the plant, which is subsequently processed to obtain the concentrated oil used in various holistic treatments and fragrances.
