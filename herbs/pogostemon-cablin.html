Scientific literature: Very Extensive (539 studies) · ★★★★★

Patchouli-plant, scientifically known as Pogostemon cablin, is a fragrant aromatic herb native to the tropical regions of Southeast Asia. Highly valued for its essential oil, it offers profound therapeutic benefits, including skin healing and calming properties. This versatile medicinal plant remains a cornerstone in traditional aromatherapy and herbal wellness.

Physiology

The physiology of Pogostemon cablin is characterized by its metabolic processes and structural adaptations suited for tropical environments. The plant does not exhibit succulence, as its leaves and stems are not specialized for high-volume water storage, relying instead on consistent moisture availability in its native habitat. Furthermore, it is not a nitrogen fixer; it does not possess symbiotic relationships with diazotrophic bacteria to convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms, necessitating the uptake of nitrates and ammonium directly from the soil through its root system. Its physiological activity is heavily focused on the biosynthesis of essential oils, particularly patchouli alcohol, which is produced through complex secondary metabolic pathways within specialized glandular trichomes located on the leaf surfaces. These trichomes serve as the primary sites for the synthesis and storage of volatile compounds, which are released through transpiration and environmental stimuli.
