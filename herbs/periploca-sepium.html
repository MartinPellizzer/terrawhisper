Periploca sepium

Periploca sepium, commonly known as Milkweed or Devil's Ivy, is a climbing shrub native to Asia. Renowned for its milky latex, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditional medicine utilizes its leaves and stems to treat various ailments, though careful handling is essential due to its potent bioactive compounds.

  • Scientific name: Periploca sepium
  • Family: Apocynaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Gentianales, it is classified under the family Apocynaceae and is specifically identified by the genus Periploca.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions in East Asia. In the Russian Far East, it is specifically documented to occur within the Primorye region. Its presence is also well-established throughout various parts of China, including the South-Central area. Furthermore, the species can be found spanning from the northern reaches of Inner Mongolia to the Manchuria region. Finally, its habitat extends into the North-Central territories of China.
