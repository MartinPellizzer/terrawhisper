Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several regions within the tropical African continent and the surrounding ocean. In Northeast Tropical Africa, its presence is noted in Somalia. Moving southward, it can be found throughout East Tropical Africa in both Kenya and Tanzania. Its range extends further into South Tropical Africa, specifically within the territory of Mozambique. Additionally, this species is distributed across the Western Indian Ocean, notably in Madagascar.

Plant Parts

This plant, Enhalus acoroides, is a species of seagrass characterized by its specialized morphological structures adapted for life in marine environments. The leaf is the most prominent part of the plant, typically appearing as long, slender, and strap-like blades that are essential for photosynthesis and nutrient absorption from the water column. Supporting these blades is the stem, or rhizome, which grows horizontally through the seafloor substrate, providing stability and serving as a vital conduit for transporting nutrients throughout the plant's network.
