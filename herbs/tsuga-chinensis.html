Tsuga chinensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Tsuga chinensis?

Tsuga chinensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Its hierarchical lineage continues through the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Pinidae, falling under the order Pinales. Within this order, it is a member of the family Pinaceae and is categorized under the genus Tsuga, ultimately reaching its specific designation as the species chinensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Tsuga chinensis has linear, dark green needles that are approximately 15 to 30 millimeters long, arranged spirally around the twigs but appearing two-ranked due to their spreading orientation. These needles possess two distinct white stomatal bands on their undersides, which provide a characteristic silvery appearance. The tree features small, woody cones that are typically 20 to 30 millimeters in length and hang downward from the branches. Its bark becomes thick and deeply furrowed into rectangular plates as the specimen matures. The overall growth habit is conical, often reaching significant heights in montane forest environments.
