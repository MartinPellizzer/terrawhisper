Diospyros mespiliformis

Jackal berries

Diospyros mespiliformis, commonly known as the African ebony, is a versatile medicinal plant native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its profound therapeutic properties, various parts of the tree, including the bark and leaves, are utilized in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from digestive issues to inflammatory conditions.

  • Scientific name: Diospyros mespiliformis
  • Family: Ebenaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Ericales, it is classified under the family Ebenaceae. Finally, it is categorized within the genus Diospyros.

Common Names

This plant, Diospyros mespiliformis, is known by various regional names depending on the language and location. In Hausa (ha), it is referred to as Kanya, while in Russian (ru), it is called Хурма мушмуловидна. Among the Yoruba speakers (yo), the plant is commonly known as Igi dúdú. In both Dutch (nl) and Afrikaans (af), it is identified by the name Jakhalsbes or Jakkalsbessie, respectively.

Distribution

This plant, Diospyros mespiliformis, is primarily distributed throughout the West Tropical Africa region. Its presence is well-documented in the nation of Benin, where it plays a role in the local ecosystem. The species also extends its range into Burkina, contributing to the diverse flora of the area. Furthermore, it can be found growing within the borders of Gambia. Finally, the geographical footprint of this medicinal plant includes the territory of Ghana and parts of Guinea.
