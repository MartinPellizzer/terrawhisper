Mitragyna speciosa

What's the taxonomical classification of Mitragyna speciosa?

Mitragyna speciosa belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the taxonomic lineage of the order Gentianaales. It is a prominent representative of the family Rubiaceae, falling under the genus Mitragyna, with the specific designation of the species speciosa.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Mitragyna speciosa has a woody, perennial structure characterized by a large tree habit reaching heights of up to 15 meters with an erect trunk and a spreading canopy. The leaves are arranged in an opposite pattern and possess an elliptical or oblong shape with distinct pinnate venation. These leaves are typically dark green, glossy, and can grow up to 30 centimeters in length. Small, white, fragrant flowers are produced in dense, globular clusters known as cymes at the ends of the branches. The fruit consists of a fleshy, globose capsule that eventually splits to release numerous small seeds.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical regions of Southeast Asia, specifically concentrated within Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, and parts of Vietnam. It thrives in the humid, lowland environments typically found in rainforest ecosystems. The species prefers areas with consistent rainfall and warm temperatures characteristic of equatorial climates. While its primary range is restricted to these specific Southeast Asian countries, it is also found growing in certain parts of the Philippines. Due to its specific environmental requirements, its natural distribution remains tied to these tropical latitudes.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant Mitragyna speciosa requires a tropical climate with high humidity, consistent rainfall, and well-draining acidic soil to thrive.

Growers typically cultivate these trees in lowland areas where temperatures remain warm throughout the entire year. Seedlings must be carefully nurtured in shaded environments before being transplanted into open fields to ensure strong root development. What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant Mitragyna speciosa has been utilized for centuries within Thai herbal medicine to treat a variety of ailments including fever, pain, and digestive issues. Local communities in Southeast Asia historically gathered the leaves and bark to create infusions used for their stimulating and analgesic properties. These traditional practices often integrated the plant into ritualistic healing processes and daily wellness routines. Beyond its medicinal applications, historical records suggest the plant was used to increase stamina among laborers. This long history of usage established a foundation for modern scientific interest in its bioactive compounds.

According to a study published by "Life sciences", mitragynine is an indole-alkaloid isolated from the Thai medicinal herb kratom (Mitragyna speciosa). This alkaloid is used in Thai herbal medicine for its analgesic properties. The study focused on how mitragynine affects neurogenic contraction of smooth muscle. Specifically, it was found that mitragynine inhibited the contraction of the vas deferens produced by electrical transmural stimulation. The research also demonstrated that mitragynine inhibited nicotine- or tyramine-induced contraction. Through the patch-clamp technique, the substance was found to block T- and L-type Ca2+ channel currents in N1E-115 neuroblastoma cells.

What health conditions is this plant used for?

This plant is used for treating malaria, fever, pain, inflammation, and anxiety. Its active compounds act as powerful antimalarial agents that help reduce high body temperatures during infection. The leaves and bark are frequently applied or consumed to soothe physical pain and decrease systemic inflammation. Additionally, certain preparations of the plant are utilized to calm the nervous system and alleviate symptoms of anxiety. These diverse medicinal applications make it a significant resource in traditional healing practices.

According to a study published by "Life sciences", the Thai medicinal plant Mitragyna speciosa contains an indole alkaloid called mitragynine which possesses an analgesic effect. In experiments, mitragynine at concentrations of 1 nM to 3 microM inhibited the electrically stimulated contraction of the guinea-pig ileum with a pD2 value of 6.91 +/- 0.04 (n = 5). This substance was found to be 10 fold less potent than morphine, which had a pD2 of 7.68 +/- 0.11 (n = 5) when tested between 1 nM and 1 microM. While mitragynine at 3-10 microM did not affect contractions induced by acetylcholine or histamine, it did inhibit the naloxone-precipitated withdrawal contraction after a 5 min exposure of the ileum to morphine. These findings suggest that the alkaloid from Mitragyna speciosa inhibits contraction through the opioid receptor.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is processed into various medicinal herbal preparations including 7-hydroxymitragyniine-enriched Kratom preparation, Mitragyna speciosa ethanolic extract, decoctions, powder, and nanoparticle-based topical gels. Researchers utilize decoctions and finely ground powder to deliver traditional doses of the plant's alkaloids. Advanced technology has also enabled the creation of 7-hydroxymitragyniine-enriched Kratom preparation to target specific receptor interactions. Furthermore, the development of nanoparticle-based topical gels offers a localized method for transdermal drug delivery.

According to a study published by "Molecules (Basel, Switzerland)", Mitragyna speciosa contains bioactive alkaloids, notably mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine. The antioxidant properties of Mitragyna speciosa ethanolic extracts yielded EC50 values of 0.06 mg/mL for DPPH, 0.29 mg/mL for ABTS, and 55 g Fe2+/100 g for FRAP. Both alkaloid-rich extracts and purified mitragynine significantly inhibited lipid accumulation in 3T3-L1 adipocytes by up to 50-70% at non-cytotoxic concentrations of ≤25 µg/mL. These findings suggest that mitragynine is the principal anti-adipogenic constituent in Mitragyna speciosa extracts. The results support the development of Mitragyna speciosa-derived extracts and mitragynine for applications targeting subcutaneous fat and oxidative skin damage.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause serious health complications including abnormal liver tests, addiction, arrhythmia, constipation, and kidney injury. Regular consumption may lead to chemical imbalances that result in irregular heartbeats. The substances within the plant also carry a significant risk of dependency and withdrawal symptoms. Furthermore, users have reported digestive issues like constipation alongside more severe damage to renal functions. These physiological disruptions highlight the potential dangers associated with its use.

According to a study published by "Journal of investigative medicine high impact case reports", Mitragyna speciosa is a medicinal plant used for stimulant and analgesic properties through multiple alkaloid compounds. In a clinical case involving a 47-year-old male, the use of the plant led to fatigue, pruritus, and abnormal liver tests with a mixed hepatocellular/cholestatic pattern approximately 21 days after use. After a latency of 2 days following a second use 9 months after initial recovery, the patient experienced fatigue, pruritus, loss of appetite, and the same abnormal biochemical profile. This case was identified as a highly likely or definite example of drug-induced liver injury caused by Mitragyna speciosa.
