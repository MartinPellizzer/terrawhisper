Kratom, scientifically known as Mitragyna speciosa, is a tropical evergreen tree native to Southeast Asia. Traditionally used for its diverse pharmacological properties, the plant's leaves contain unique alkaloids that interact with opioid receptors. This botanical specimen is increasingly studied for its potential applications in pain management and cognitive enhancement.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Gentianaales, it is further categorized into the family Rubiaceae. Ultimately, its scientific identification is defined by the genus Mitragyna.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several key regions in Southeast Asia. Within the Indo-China region, its presence is documented in Cambodia, Thailand, and Vietnam. Moving toward the Malesia zone, the species can also be found throughout Borneo. Additionally, it is distributed across the Malay peninsula. Together, these areas highlight the tropical habitat essential for its natural growth.

Plant Parts

This plant, Mitragyna speciosa, is highly valued in traditional medicine for its diverse therapeutic properties derived from various anatomical structures. The most significant components used for medicinal extraction are the leaves, which contain the primary bioactive alkaloids. Often, the dried leaf material is prepared into various forms, such as powders or teas, to facilitate consumption. While specific preparations may focus solely on the foliage, some traditional remedies utilize the whole plant material, including the bark and roots, to achieve a broader range of effects. Additionally, the botanical identification of the species often relies on the presence of distinctive stipules, which are small, appendage-like structures located at the base of the leaf stalks.
