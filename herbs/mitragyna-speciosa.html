Kratom, scientifically known as Mitragyna speciosa, is a tropical evergreen tree native to Southeast Asia. Traditionally used for its diverse pharmacological properties, the plant's leaves contain unique alkaloids that interact with opioid receptors. This botanical specimen is increasingly studied for its potential applications in pain management and cognitive enhancement.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Gentianaales, it is further categorized into the family Rubiaceae. Ultimately, its scientific identification is defined by the genus Mitragyna.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several key regions in Southeast Asia. Within the Indo-China region, its presence is documented in Cambodia, Thailand, and Vietnam. Moving toward the Malesia zone, the species can also be found throughout Borneo. Additionally, it is distributed across the Malay peninsula. Together, these areas highlight the tropical habitat essential for its natural growth.

Life history

The life history of Mitragyna speciosa is characterized by its status as a perennial species, allowing it to persist in its ecosystem over many years through successive growing seasons. As a phanerophyte, the plant maintains its photosynthetic organs on elevated woody branches that remain above the ground level, even during unfavorable environmental conditions. This phanerophyte life form is further supported by its structural development, which ensures its survival and continued growth within its tropical habitat.

Morphology

The morphology of Mitragyna speciosa is characterized by its growth form as a woody tree, which functions as a self-supporting structure rather than a climber or vine. As a terrestrial species, it is neither aquatic nor an epiphyte, growing independently in its environment without parasitic tendencies. The plant reaches significant dimensions, with an average height of approximately 20 m and the potential to reach a maximum height of up to 25 m.

Physiology

The physiology of Mitragyna speciosa is characterized by a standard metabolic framework typical of many tropical woody perennials. It utilizes the C3 photosynthetic pathway, a process where carbon dioxide is fixed directly into a three-carbon compound during the Calvin cycle, which is well-suited to the humid, shaded, and high-moisture environments in which the species typically thrives. Furthermore, this plant is not a nitrogen fixer, meaning it does not possess a symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria to convert atmospheric nitrogen into a usable form, and instead relies on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium from the soil through its root system.
