Mentha dahurica

What's the taxonomical classification of Mentha dahurica?

Mentha dahurica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic descent, it falls into the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by its genus, Mentha, and its specific species designation, dahurica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Mentha dahurica has erect, square-shaped stems that typically reach heights of 20 to 50 centimeters and bear opposite, ovate to lanceolate leaves with serrated margins. The foliage is characterized by a distinct bright green color and a textured surface due to prominent venation. Small, tubular flowers emerge from the upper leaf axils in dense, terminal clusters known as verticillasters. These floral structures are usually light purple or white and contain the plant's aromatic essential oils. The root system consists of creeping rhizomes that allow the plant to spread effectively across moist soil.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires moist, well-draining soil with a slightly acidic to neutral pH and partial shade to thrive in temperate climates.

It is typically propagated through division of the rhizomes or by planting stem cuttings during the spring growing season. Because it spreads aggressively through underground runners, it is best contained within dedicated garden beds or large containers to prevent it from overtaking other species. Regular watering is essential to maintain consistent soil moisture, especially during periods of high heat.

Periodic harvesting of the leaves encourages bushier growth and prevents the stems from becoming overly woody.
