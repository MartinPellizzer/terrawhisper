Indian pennywort

Centella asiatica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1600 studies) · ★★★★★

Indian pennywort, scientifically known as Centella asiatica, is a perennial herb revered in traditional Ayurvedic and Chinese medicine. Renowned for its remarkable skin-healing properties and cognitive-enhancing benefits, it contains active triterpenoids. Today, this versatile botanical is a cornerstone ingredient in modern dermatological formulations and neurological health supplements.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Centella asiatica involves both sexual and asexual mechanisms, with asexual reproduction being present to facilitate its spread. During the sexual phase, the plant produces small, pink-colored flowers characterized by a width ranging from a minimum of 0.15 cm to a maximum of 0.25 cm. The flowering period is concentrated, with both the start and end of the flowering season occurring in July. These flowers rely on biotic pollination syndromes, specifically utilizing insects as the primary pollinators. Once fertilization occurs—a process where self-fertilization is present—the plant develops fruits that are quite small, with a mean length of 0.2775 cm (ranging from 0.21 cm to 0.3 cm) and a mean width of 1.91 cm (ranging from 0.22 cm to 3.6 cm). The resulting seeds are minute, maintaining a consistent mean, minimum, and maximum mass of 0.001501 g. The dispersal of these seeds is categorized as autochorous, meaning the plant employs an autochorous dispersal syndrome to distribute its offspring.
