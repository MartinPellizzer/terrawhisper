Urtica triangularis

What's the taxonomical classification of Urtica triangularis?

Urtica triangularis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, the plant is part of the order Rosales and falls under the family Urticaceae. Finally, it is categorized within the genus Urtica, with its specific designation being the species triangularis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Urtica triangularis has triangular-shaped leaves with serrated margins and a stem covered in stinging, hollow hairs called trichomes. The foliage typically exhibits a bright green color and grows in an opposite arrangement along the plant body. Small, inconspicuous flowers are produced in clusters that emerge from the leaf axils. These floral structures are often greenish or yellowish and lack large, showy petals. The overall structure is herbaceous and relatively low-growing compared to other members of the nettle family.

    What side effects this plant can have?

    This plant can cause immediate skin irritation, redness, and intense stinging sensations due to the presence of tiny, needle-like hairs that inject irritating chemicals into the flesh. These hairs, known as trichomes, act like miniature hypodermic needles to deliver a cocktail of histamine and formic acid upon contact. Most individuals will experience localized swelling and itchy bumps that resemble a nettle rash. While these dermatological reactions are typically temporary, some people may suffer from more prolonged inflammation or allergic sensitivity. Avoiding direct physical contact with the foliage is the most effective way to prevent these uncomfortable symptoms.
