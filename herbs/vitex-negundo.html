Vitex negundo

Vitex negundo, commonly known as Chinese Chaste Tree, is a versatile medicinal shrub revered in traditional Ayurvedic and Chinese medicine. Renowned for its potent anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antimicrobial properties, its leaves and seeds are widely utilized to treat respiratory ailments, skin disorders, and various inflammatory conditions across diverse cultures.

  • Scientific name: Vitex negundo
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, situated within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae, eventually falling into the genus Vitex.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Vitex negundo, is referred to by various common names across different languages and regions. In Malaysia, it is known as Pokok Lemuni Kuning (ms), while in China, it is called 黄荆 (zh). In Bengali, the plant is identified as নিশিন্দা (bn), and in the Philippines, it is commonly called Lagundi (tl). Additionally, in Hungary, it is recognized by the name kínai barátcserje (hu).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several continents and climatic zones. In Southeastern Europe, its presence can be documented in Bulgaria. Moving toward the African continent, it is found in Northeast Tropical Africa within Somalia and in East Tropical Africa in Tanzania. Its range extends further south to include Mozambique in South Tropical Africa. Finally, the species is also located in the Western Indian Ocean on the island of Mauritius.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Chinese Chaste Tree, utilizes various components for its medicinal properties. The large, serrated green leaves are the most frequently used part in traditional remedies, often prepared as a decoction or topical poultice. Each individual leaf contains bioactive compounds that contribute to its anti-inflammatory effects. While the seeds are sometimes employed in specific preparations, much of the therapeutic focus remains on the vegetative structures. Additionally, the woody stem provides structural support and contains secondary metabolites, while the root is often harvested for its potent bioactive constituents used to treat various ailments.
