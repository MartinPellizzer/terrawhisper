Acanthus ebracteatus

What's the taxonomical classification of Acanthus ebracteatus?

Acanthus ebracteatus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Moving through its hierarchical lineage, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Further taxonomic refinement places the species in the order Lamiales and the family Acanthaceae. Within this family, it is categorized under the genus Acanthus, ultimately reaching its specific identification as the species ebracteatus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Acanthus ebracteatous has deeply lobed, dark green leaves with spiny margins and long, tubular flowers that emerge from bractless spikes. The foliage is characterized by prominent veins and a rugged, architectural texture typical of the Acanthaceae family. Its inflorescence is distinctive because it lacks the large, leafy bracts found in many other Acanthus species. The flowers are often white or pale lavender and are shaped to facilitate pollination by specific insects. This plant generally maintains a subshrub to shrub-like growth habit with woody stems at the base.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Acanthus ebracteatus utilizes its aerial parts, specifically the leaf, midrib, and stem, for various medicinal purposes. The entire leafy structure is often processed to create decoctions or topical applications. Within the leaf, the midrib contains concentrated compounds that contribute to the plant's therapeutic profile. The stem is also harvested to extract bioactive elements used in traditional remedies. These specific components work together to provide the medicinal benefits associated with the species.

According to a study published by "Phytochemistry", the aerial part of Acanthus ebracteatus contains several medicinal compounds. From this part of the plant, 1 megastigmane glycoside known as ebracteatoside A was isolated. Additionally, 3 aliphatic alcohol glycosides called ebracteatosides B-D were identified. The study also isolated 7-chloro-(2R)-2-O-beta-D-glucopyranosyl-4-hydroxy-2H-1,4-benzoxazin-3(4H)-one, also referred to as 7-Cl-DIBOA-Glc. These substances were found alongside 22 known compounds.

According to a study published by "BMC complementary medicine and therapies", Acanthus ebracteatus is used for its antibacterial, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory properties to treat acne vulgaris. This skin condition is characterized by inflammation and is frequently linked to the bacteria Cutibacterium acnes and Staphylococcus spp. In a combination treatment with a 15:5:10 GM:CC:AE ratio, the mixture displayed high selectivity indices of 26.87 against Staphylococcus epidermidis and 4.99 against C. acnes. The study suggests this approach is effective for controlling acne vulgaris by inhibiting the bacterial enzyme topoisomerase II alpha and regulating the human nuclear factor kappa B subunit 1.
