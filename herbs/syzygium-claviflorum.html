Syzygium claviflorum

Syzygium claviflorum, commonly known as the clove flower, is a significant medicinal plant belonging to the Myrtaceae family. Renowned for its potent antioxidant and antimicrobial properties, it is traditionally used in various cultures to treat inflammatory conditions, digestive ailments, and skin infections, offering vast potential for modern pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Syzygium claviflorum
  • Family: Myrtaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Myrtales and the family Myrtaceae. Finally, it is placed within the genus Syzygium under the species name Syzygium claviflorum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range across several key regions in Asia. In China, its distribution is specifically noted in the South-Central areas as well as on the island of Hainan. The species also extends into the Indian Subcontinent, where it can be found in Assam and throughout Bangladesh. Furthermore, it inhabits the rugged landscapes of the East Himalaya. Together, these locations highlight a broad natural habitat spanning from Southern China to the eastern reaches of the Indian Subcontinent.
