Caryopteris nepetifolia

What's the taxonomical classification of Caryopteris nepetifolia?

Caryopteris nepetifolia belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is organized under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Its taxonomic hierarchy concludes with the genus Caryopteris and the specific epithet nepetifolia, which together form its scientific name.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Caryopteris nepetifolia has woody, mounding stems that produce small, lanceolate, grayish-green leaves with finely serrated margins. The foliage is typically arranged in opposite pairs along the branches and possesses a slightly textured surface. During the blooming season, the plant produces dense, terminal clusters of small, tubular, blue-to-violet flowers. These blossoms emerge from terminal panicles that rise above the dense mound of greenery. The overall structure remains compact and shrub-like, making it a sturdy perennial in temperate gardens.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant grows best in well-drained soil within full sun to partial shade environments and requires USDA hardiness zones 6 through 9.

You should plant it in a location that avoids soggy feet, as it is highly susceptible to root rot in overly wet conditions. Regular watering is necessary during the first growing season to establish the root system, but the mature shrub is quite drought-tolerant. Pruning the stems back by half in early spring promotes vigorous new growth and a profusion of blue flowers.

Providing adequate air circulation around the foliage helps to prevent fungal issues throughout the summer.
