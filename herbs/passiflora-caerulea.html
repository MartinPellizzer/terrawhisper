Passiflora caerulea

Blue passionflower

Passiflora caerulea, commonly known as the blue passionflower, is a stunning climbing vine celebrated for its intricate, exotic blooms. Beyond its ornamental beauty, this botanical treasure is highly valued in traditional medicine for its sedative and anxiolytic properties, often used to promote relaxation and support healthy sleep patterns.

  • Scientific name: Passiflora caerulea
  • Family: Passifloraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Malpighiales and the family Passifloraceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Passiflora, specifically as the species Passiflora caerulea.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Passiflora caerulea, is recognized by various common names across different languages and cultures. In Guarani, it is referred to as Mburukuja, while in Polish, it is called Męczennica błękitna. In Bosnian, the name used is Pasijonka, and in Russian, it is known as Страстоцвет голубой. Finally, in Korean, this medicinal species is called 시계꽃.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across various regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence can be documented within the territories of Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward the center of the continent, it is also found growing in Germany. The species extends into Southwestern Europe, specifically throughout Spain. Finally, its range reaches into Southeastern Europe, where it is located in Albania.
