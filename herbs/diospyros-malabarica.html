The physiology of Diospyros malabarica is characterized by a standard C3 photosynthetic pathway, where carbon fixation occurs through the Calvin cycle without the specialized adaptations found in C4 or CAM plants. This metabolic process involves the direct fixation of atmospheric CO2 by the enzyme RuBisCO, making the plant's energy production highly dependent on efficient stomatal regulation and water availability. Furthermore, the species does not function as a nitrogen fixer, meaning it lacks the specialized symbiotic relationships with diazotrophic bacteria (such as Rhizobium) required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms; instead, it relies entirely on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium present in the soil substrate to meet its nutritional requirements for protein synthesis and growth.
