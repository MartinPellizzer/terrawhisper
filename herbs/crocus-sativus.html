Crocus sativus

Saffron crocus

Crocus sativus, commonly known as saffron, is a perennial plant prized for its vibrant crimson stigmas. Beyond its status as the world's most expensive spice, it holds significant medicinal value. Renowned for its antioxidant and antidepressant properties, it is traditionally used to enhance mood and support overall neurological health.

  • Scientific name: Crocus sativus
  • Family: Iridaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the order Asparagales and is a member of the family Iridaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Crocus.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Crocus sativus, is a perennial herb highly valued for its unique reproductive structures. The most precious part of the plant comes from its purple flowers, which contain three long, crimson-colored stigmas. Each individual flower produces these distinct stigma threads, which are carefully hand-harvested to produce the world's most expensive spice, saffron. Once the dried stigmas are collected and processed, they are used globally for their intense flavor, vibrant coloring properties, and significant medicinal benefits.
