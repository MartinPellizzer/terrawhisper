The physiology of Curcuma aeruginosa is characterized by its metabolic processes as a perennial rhizomatous herb, focusing heavily on the synthesis and storage of bioactive secondary metabolites within its underground structures. The plant utilizes a complex photosynthetic pathway to convert solar energy into chemical energy, which is subsequently directed toward the development of fleshy rhizomes. These rhizomes serve as primary physiological sinks for nutrient accumulation and the production of specific terpenoids and phenolic compounds. Regarding its interaction with soil nutrients, the plant does not possess the biological mechanisms required for symbiotic or free-living nitrogen fixation; therefore, it is classified as a non-nitrogen fixer (trait_1: nitrogen fixer, trait_value: no). Consequently, its physiological growth and nitrogen assimilation are entirely dependent on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium ions from the surrounding soil environment through its root system.
