What's the taxonomical classification of Rhamnella gilgitica?

Rhamnella gilgitica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Rosales. Its taxonomic lineage continues through the family Rhamnaceae, within the genus Rhamnella, ultimately reaching its specific designation as the species gilgitica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Rhamnella gilgitica has woody stems, simple elliptical leaves with entire margins, and small tubular flowers arranged in dense axillary clusters. The foliage typically exhibits a leathery texture and a deep green coloration on the upper surface. Its leaves are arranged oppositely along the branches, often featuring distinct venation patterns. The reproductive structures consist of small fruits that develop from the fertilized ovaries of the flowers. These morphological characteristics help distinguish the species within its specific high-altitude habitat.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Rhamnella gilgitica provides medicinal benefits through the utilization of its bark, leaves, and roots. Various phytochemicals are concentrated within these tissues to treat ailments such as inflammation and digestive issues. Researchers examine the vascular structure, including the xylem, to understand how nutrients and medicinal compounds are transported throughout the plant. These specific parts are often processed into decoctions or extracts for therapeutic use. Studying these components helps clarify the plant's overall pharmacological potential.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", the xylem parts of the traditional Tibetan medicinal herb Shengdeng (Rhamnella gilgitica) are used for medicinal purposes. A reversed-phase HPLC method was developed to determine the flavonoid maesopsin in these parts. The method utilized an ODS column and a mobile phase of methanol-water (28:27) with 0.05 mol/L KH2PO4 at pH 4. Detection was performed at a UV wavelength of 290 nm. The recovery rate was 98.9% with an RSD of 2.1% for n = 6. The content of maesopsin in Rhamnella gilgitica was measured at 1.02% with an RSD of 0.52% for n = 3.

This traditional Tibetan medicinal plant has been used for the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis (RA) for many years. To evaluate its effects, rats were orally administered ethyl acetate extract of Rhamnella gilgitica at doses of 9.71, 19.43, or 38.85 mg/kg for 23 days. The treatment addressed conditions such as collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) by suppressing the arthritis index and paw swelling. It also influenced multiple inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6, IL-17, INF-γ, IL-4, and IL-10.
