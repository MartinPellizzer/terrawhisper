Dioscorea caucasica

Dioscorea caucasica, commonly known as the Caucasian yam, is a perennial climbing plant native to the Caucasus region. Renowned for its tuberous roots, this medicinal species is valued in traditional ethnobotany. It contains bioactive compounds that offer potential therapeutic benefits, making it a subject of interest for pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Dioscorea caucasica
  • Family: Dioscoreaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Dioscoreales, specifically within the family Dioscoreaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement in the genus Dioscorea.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Dioscorea caucasica, is recognized by various common names depending on the region and language. In Russia, it is referred to as Диоскорея кавказская, while in Bulgaria, it is called кавказка диоскорея. In Azerbaijan, the name used is Qafqaz dioskoreyası, and in Poland, it is known as Jams kaukaski. Additionally, in China, the plant is identified by the name 高加索薯蓣.

Distribution

This plant is native to the specific mountainous regions of the Caucasus. Its presence is primarily documented within the North Caucasus area. Additionally, it can be found extending into the Transcaucasus region. These two distinct zones define its natural geographical range. Consequently, the species is highly characteristic of this particular Caucasian landscape.
