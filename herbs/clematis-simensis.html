Clematis simensis

Clematis simensis, commonly known as a high-altitude climber, is a medicinal plant indigenous to the Ethiopian highlands. Valued in traditional medicine, it is frequently utilized to treat various ailments, including respiratory issues and inflammatory conditions. Understanding its botanical properties and ethnobotanical significance is essential for future pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Clematis simensis
  • Family: Ranunculaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Clematis simensis, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Ranunculales and the family Ranunculaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement within the genus Clematis.

Common Names

This plant, Clematis simensis, is known by various names depending on the local language. In Amharic, it is commonly referred to as ተክለ ወይዘሮ (Tekle Weyzero), while in Tigrinya, it is known as ተክለ ወይዘሮ (Tekle Weyzero).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in both Nigeria and Sierra Leone. Moving toward the central regions, its presence extends into West-Central Tropical Africa. Specifically, it is documented in the countries of Burundi, Cameroon, and Congo. Consequently, the species spans a wide latitudinal range throughout these tropical territories.
