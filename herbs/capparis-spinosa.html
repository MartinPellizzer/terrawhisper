Capparis spinosa

Caper bush

Capparis spinosa, commonly known as the caper bush, is a resilient, evergreen shrub native to the Mediterranean region. Renowned for its edible flower buds, this plant offers significant therapeutic potential. Rich in antioxidants and bioactive compounds, it is traditionally used to support digestive health and combat inflammatory conditions.

  • Scientific name: Capparis spinosa
  • Family: Capparaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Following its subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Brassicales and the family Capparaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Capparis.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern throughout the Mediterranean region of Southwestern Europe. It is found growing in the coastal and inland areas of France and Portugal. Additionally, its presence is noted across several significant island territories. Specifically, it inhabits the Baleares and Corse archipelagos. Finally, the species can also be located within the territory of Sardegna.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the caper bush, is a versatile medicinal species where various components offer unique benefits. The small, fleshy fruits, often referred to as capers when harvested as buds, are widely used for their antioxidant properties, while the mature fruit itself provides a different nutritional profile. Within these structures, the tiny seeds are also known to contain bioactive compounds. The thick, woody roots are frequently utilized in traditional remedies to treat inflammation and digestive issues, and even the hardy leaves contribute to the plant's overall therapeutic potential.
