Citrus medica

What's the taxonomical classification of Citrus medica?

Citrus medica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this group, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving further down the taxonomic hierarchy, it falls under the order Sapindales and the family Rutaceae. Finally, its specific identity is defined by the genus Citrus and the species medica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Citrus medica has a large, evergreen shrub or small tree morphology characterized by spreading branches, prominent spines, and large, leathery, oblong leaves with serrated margins. The foliage is often fragrant and possesses a distinctively coarse texture compared to other citrus species. Its flowers are white, solitary, and possess five petals accompanied by numerous stamens. The resulting fruit is typically large, globose or oval, and features a very thick, pitted rind containing numerous oil glands. This thick rind is a primary distinguishing morphological feature of the species.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, commonly known as the citron, requires a subtropical climate with well-drained, slightly acidic soil and consistent warmth to thrive.

It is typically propagated through grafting or seed sowing in controlled environments before being transplanted into sunny locations. Because the species is sensitive to frost, growers often provide protection or use greenhouses in cooler regions. Regular irrigation is necessary to prevent soil moisture fluctuations, though overwatering must be avoided to prevent root rot.

Adequate sunlight and nutrient-rich soil ensure the development of its thick, fragrant rind.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant, Citrus medica, provides medicinal value through its fruit, leaves, peel, roots, and seeds. The fruit is frequently used to treat digestive issues and respiratory ailments. Essential oils extracted from the peel are applied topically or ingested to manage various inflammatory conditions. Medicinal preparations often incorporate the leaves to soothe nervous system disorders and promote relaxation. Additionally, the roots and seeds are utilized in traditional remedies to address specific systemic imbalances.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", the edible fruit juice of Citrus medica L. is used to prepare Citron syrup for treating migraine headache. In a clinical trial involving 90 patients, participants received 15ml of this syrup three times daily after a meal for 4 weeks. The syrup was evaluated using a visual analogue scale (VAS) with a 0-10 score to measure headache intensity. Results showed that Citron syrup was superior to placebo in reducing headache attack intensity (P<0.01) and duration (p<0.0001). The effectiveness of the fruit juice syrup was found to be as effective as 20mg of propranolol in terms of pain reduction (P>0.05).

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains limonene, limonin, limonol, nomilinic acid, and a high vitamin C content. The terpene limonene provides significant antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties to the fruit. Limonin and its derivative limonol are bitter limonoids that contribute to the plant's potential medicinal effects. Nomilinic acid is another important bioactive compound found within the citrus structure. Additionally, the high vitamin C content supports immune function and overall cellular health.

According to a study published by "Dental research journal", the essential oil of Citrus medica contains limonene as its major chemical component at 92.31%. The research utilized the peels of Citrus medica for the analysis of its chemical composition. While the plant possesses notable antimicrobial properties against oral pathogens, Streptococcus salivarius was resistant to the Citrus medica essential oil. This specific microorganism also exhibited the highest minimum inhibitory concentration when tested.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is used to create diverse medicinal preparations including Citri Fructus, citron syrup, essential oils, alcoholic extracts, and chloroform extract. Citri Fructus serves as the primary dried fruit used in various traditional remedies. Citron syrup is often formulated to soothe coughs and aid digestion through its sweet, liquid consistency. Essential oils are extracted from the peel to provide concentrated aromatic and therapeutic properties. Finally, both alcoholic extracts and chloroform extracts are utilized to isolate specific bioactive compounds for medicinal purposes.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", the ripe dried fruit of Citrus medica serves as one of the important sources of the Chinese herb Citri Fructus. The investigation into intraspecific variation involved 11 citron fruits, which included species such as C. medica var. ethrog, C. medica var. yunnanensis, and C. medica var. muliensis. Research identified main production areas in Yunnan, Sichuan, Guangxi, Chongqing, Hubei, and Zhejiang, while natural communities are mainly distributed in southwestern and western Yunnan and southeastern Tibet. To support orderly and effective production of medicine, variety selection and classification management based on medicinal purposes should be implemented.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause nausea, stomach discomfort, diarrhea, and gastric irritation in individuals who consume it. These gastrointestinal issues often arise due to the high concentration of citric acid and essential oils found within the fruit and rind. Excessive intake may lead to a burning sensation in the digestive tract or significant abdominal cramping. Such symptoms are typically linked to the plant's potency when used in medicinal or concentrated forms. Monitoring dosage is essential to minimize the risk of these unpleasant digestive reactions.
