Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern primarily concentrated within the Southeastern European region. It can be found growing across various terrains in Albania, Bulgaria, and Greece. Furthermore, its presence extends into the Northwestern Balkan Peninsula. The range also reaches into Eastern Europe, specifically within the Krym area. Together, these locations define the distinct geographical footprint of this particular variety.

Plant Parts

This plant, Pinus sylvestris var. hamaata, is a gymnosperm, which means it does not produce traditional flowers like angiosperms; instead, its reproductive structures consist of male and female cones. The male reproductive organs, often referred to in a broad botanical sense as the "flowers" of the conifer, are small, pollen-bearing structures that release vast amounts of wind-dispersed pollen to fertilize the larger, woody female cones.
