Common dayflower, scientifically known as Commelina communis, is a versatile herbaceous plant cherished in traditional medicine. Renowned for its vibrant blue blooms, it offers therapeutic benefits, including anti-inflammatory, diuretic, and antimicrobial properties. Often used to treat skin ailments and digestive issues, this resilient plant remains a vital botanical resource.

Physiology

The physiology of Commelina communis is characterized by a metabolic framework governed by the C3 photosynthetic pathway, a process where carbon dioxide is fixed directly into a three-carbon compound during the Calvin cycle. This C3 mechanism involves the enzyme Rubisco facilitating the carboxylation of ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate, which is typical for many herbaceous plants growing in temperate to tropical environments. The plant utilizes this pathway to convert light energy into chemical energy, supporting its rapid growth and succulent stem structure. Because it relies on the C3 pathway, its photosynthetic efficiency is highly dependent on ambient temperature and moisture availability, as it must keep its stomata open for gas exchange, which inherently leads to transpirational water loss. This physiological trait dictates its ecological preference for moist, shaded, or partially sunny habitats where water loss can be managed effectively.
