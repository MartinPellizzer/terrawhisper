Physiology

The physiology of Annona exsucca is fundamentally characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, a metabolic strategy common to most members of the Annonaceae family. This process involves the direct fixation of atmospheric carbon dioxide by the enzyme RuBisCO within the mesophyll cells, leading to the initial formation of a three-carbon compound, 3-phosphoglycerate. Due to this C3 mechanism, the plant's metabolic efficiency and gas exchange processes are highly sensitive to environmental variables such as light intensity, temperature, and water availability. Under conditions of high irradiance or significant water stress, the plant may experience increased rates of photorespiration, as the oxygenase activity of RuBisCO becomes more prominent. Consequently, its physiological performance is closely tied to its ability to regulate stomatal conductance to balance carbon assimilation with transpirational water loss.
