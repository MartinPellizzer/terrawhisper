Ocotea aurantiodora

Ocotea aurauntiodora, commonly known as cinnamonwood, is a versatile medicinal plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its aromatic bark, it contains essential oils with potent antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties. Traditionally used to treat digestive issues and infections, this species remains a significant resource in ethnobotanical research and wellness.

  • Scientific name: Ocotea aurantiodora
  • Family: Lauraceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Laurales and the family Lauraceae. Finally, it is identified under the genus Ocotea.

Common Names

This plant, Ocotea aurauntiodora, is known by a variety of common names across different regions and languages. In English, it is frequently referred to as Brazilian bay or Brazilian laurel, while in Spanish, it is commonly called laurel de Brasil or laurel de la costa. In Portuguese, it is known as canela-de-jacare or canela-rosa, reflecting its aromatic properties and regional presence.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the tropical regions of Central and South America. Within Central America, its presence is documented in the nations of Costa Rica, Nicaragua, and Panamá. Moving into Northern South America, the species expands its range into Venezuela. Additionally, it can be found growing in the tropical forests of French Guiana. These specific locations highlight the plant's preference for the humid environments characteristic of these territories.
