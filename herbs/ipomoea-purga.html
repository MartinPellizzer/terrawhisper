Physiology

The physiology of Ipomoea purga is characterized by specialized metabolic processes focused on the synthesis and accumulation of ergoline alkaloids within its tuberous roots. These alkaloids are produced through complex biochemical pathways that respond to environmental stimuli, serving as secondary metabolites that facilitate the plant's chemical defense mechanisms. The plant exhibits a robust subterranean growth pattern, where the enlarged taproots function as primary storage organs for both water and nutrients, enabling survival in semi-arid environments. Regarding its nutrient acquisition strategies, Ipomoea purga does not engage in symbiotic relationships with nitrogen-fixing bacteria; therefore, it is not a nitrogen fixer (trait_1: no). Instead, it relies on the uptake of inorganic nitrogen directly from the soil through its root system to support its vegetative development and the synthesis of nitrogenous alkaloid compounds.
