Tabernaemontana divaricata

What's the taxonomical classification of Tabernaemontana divaricata?

Tabernaemontana divaricata belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic descent, it falls under the order Gentianales and is a member of the family Apocynaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by its specific genus, Tabernaemontana, and its unique species designation, divaricata.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Tabernaemontana divaricata has an evergreen, woody shrub morphology characterized by opposite, oblong, glossy green leaves with entire margins and an erect growth habit. The foliage typically features a leathery texture and prominent pinnate venation. Its inflorescences consist of fragrant, white, pinwheel-shaped flowers that emerge from the terminal ends of the branches. These blossoms possess five to eight spreading petals that create a distinct star-like appearance. The plant often produces small, fleshy, green drupes that eventually turn black upon maturity.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical and subtropical regions of South and Southeast Asia, spanning from India through much of the Indomalayan realm. It flourishes in warm climates characterized by high humidity and consistent rainfall. Due to its ornamental value, it has been widely introduced and naturalized in other tropical zones, including parts of Africa and the Pacific Islands. It is commonly cultivated in gardens across the Caribbean and various coastal areas worldwide. This widespread distribution is largely a result of human intervention for landscaping purposes.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-draining soil, consistent moisture, and bright, indirect sunlight to thrive in tropical or subtropical environments.

It grows best when planted in slightly acidic soil that allows for efficient drainage to prevent root rot. Regular watering is essential, though the plant can tolerate brief periods of drought once its root system is established. Providing organic mulch around the base helps maintain stable soil temperatures and moisture levels.

Periodic pruning can be used to manage its bushy shape and encourage more frequent white blooms.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Tabernaemontana divaricata utilizes its flowers, leaves, roots, branches, and stems for various medicinal applications. The white flowers are often processed to treat skin conditions or used in topical preparations. Leaf extracts are frequently applied to reduce inflammation and promote wound healing. The roots are employed in traditional remedies to address digestive issues and fever. Both the stem and the branches contain bioactive compounds that are used to treat various ailments such as coughs or minor aches.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", ethanolic extracts of roots, stems, leaves and flowers of Tabernaemontana divaricata were studied using an observational screening method in rats. Extracts of all plant parts caused sedation, decreased respiration and decreased skeletal muscle tone. Except for the flower extract, analgesic activity was found for all extracts of Tabernaemontana divaricata. Lethal doses of the extracts caused the animals to die from respiratory paralysis. The intensity of pharmacological activities was greater with the root and stem extracts.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, Tabernaemontana divaricata, is utilized in Ayurveda and Thai traditional medicine to treat various ailments through its diverse pharmacological properties. In the Ayurvedic system, different parts of the plant are employed to manage conditions such as fever, skin disorders, and inflammatory issues. Practitioners of Thai traditional medicine also rely on the plant to address various physical discomforts and digestive concerns. The roots, leaves, and flowers are frequently prepared in specific decoctions or topical applications to harness their medicinal benefits. This long-standing historical use highlights the plant's significant role in regional healing practices across South and Southeast Asia.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Tabernaemontana divaricata is one of the psychoactive alkaloid containing species mentioned in recipes for Amrita-Prâsa clarified butter and Amrita Oil. These recipes, which are part of the Bower Manuscript recorded in the 6th century A.D., contain around 100 herbal ingredients in total. Tabernaemontana divaricata is noted as being still important in Ayurvedic, Chinese, and Thai medicine. The species contains several notorious and potential psychoactive and psychedelic alkaloids such as tryptamines, 2-phenylethylamine, ephedrine, aporpines, ibogaine, and L-DOPA. Additionally, the plant contains protoberberine alkaloids, tetrahydro-β-carbolines, and tetrahydroisoquinolines with monoamine oxidase inhibitor activity and neurotoxic properties.

What are the pharmacological activities of Tabernaemontana divaricata?

This plant has demonstrated diverse pharmacological activities including analgesic, anti-inflammatory, anti-nociceptive, antibacterial, and antioxidant properties. Research indicates that its chemical constituents work effectively to reduce pain perception and mitigate inflammatory responses in various biological models. The plant also exhibits significant anti-nociceptive effects by interrupting pain signaling pathways within the nervous system. Furthermore, its antibacterial capabilities allow it to inhibit the growth of specific pathogenic microorganisms. These combined therapeutic attributes are largely attributed to the presence of bioactive alkaloids and flavonoids within its tissues.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", ethanolic extracts of roots, stems, leaves and flowers of Tabernaemontana divaricata were studied using an observational screening method in rats. These extracts caused sedation, decreased respiration, and decreased skeletal muscle tone. All plant part extracts caused vasodilatation of ear vasculature, with the exception of the T. pandacaqui leaf extract. Analgesic activity was observed in all extracts of T. divaricata except for the flower extract. Lethal doses of these extracts caused death from respiratory paralysis. The intensity of these pharmacological activities was greater with the root and stem extracts.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the indole alkaloids catharanthine, voacamine, voacangine, tabernaelegantine A, and 16α-hydroxyl-ibogamine. These specific medicinal compounds are primarily extracted from the latex and leaves of the species. Researchers study these alkaloids for their potential pharmacological activities, including anti-malarial and anti-tumor properties. The chemical structures of these molecules play a crucial role in their biological interactions within the human body. Understanding these constituents helps scientists develop new therapeutic treatments derived from natural sources.

According to a study published by "Food and chemical toxicology : an international journal published for the British Industrial Biological Research Association", the flowers of Tabernaemontana divaricata (L.) R. Br. contain medicinal properties used in traditional medicine for analgesic purposes. Phytochemical analysis of the Tabernaemontana divaricata flower methanolic extract led to the isolation of 3 indole alkaloids, specifically Voacangine, Catharanthine, and O-acetyl Vallesamine. This methanolic extract was tested in doses of 125, 250, and 500 mg/kg, p.o. The anti-nociception produced by the extract was significantly attenuated (p< 0.001) by the intra-peritoneal pretreatment of naloxone, L-Arginine, and glibenclamide. These results indicate that the anti-nociception action is mediated by opioid, nitric oxide, K+-ATP, and glutamate mechanisms and is largely related to the indole alkaloids.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is used to create various medicinal preparations including ethyl alcoholic extract, cough syrup, and methanolic extracts for therapeutic purposes. Researchers often utilize methanolic extracts to isolate specific alkaloids and bioactive compounds from the leaves and roots. Ethyl alcoholic extract serves as a common solvent base for studying the plant's pharmacological properties in laboratory settings. Additionally, the plant's components are processed into cough syrup to treat respiratory ailments and soothe irritation. These diverse preparations allow for the systematic exploration of the species' traditional healing applications.

According to a study published by "Indian journal of dental research : official publication of Indian Society for Dental Research", many parts of Tabernaemontana divaricata, including its leaf, are used for analgesic and antibacterial purposes by Adivasis of Kerala. The research conducted at Biogenix Research Lab, Thiruvananthapuram, evaluated the antibacterial potential of the ethyl alcoholic extract of Tabernaemontana divaricata leaves against common caries pathogens like S. mutans and L. acidophilus. This study assessed the leaves' antibacterial activity, acid production of bacteria, Minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC), minimum bacterial concentration (MBC), and colony forming units (CFU) levels. Statistical analyses used to evaluate the results included mean, standard deviation, Mann-Whitney U test, Independent Students T-test, Student Paired T-test, One-way ANOVA, and Turkeys Post hoc analysis. It was found that Tabernaemontana divaricata has substantial antibacterial action against S. mutans and L. acidophilus.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe physiological harm including cytotoxicity, sedation, decreased respiration, respiratory paralysis, and death. The presence of specific alkaloids within the plant can lead to cellular damage through cytotoxic effects. Once ingested, these compounds may induce deep sedation that suppresses the central nervous system. This suppression often leads to decreased respiration, which can escalate into fatal respiratory paralysis. Ultimately, these cumulative toxic effects can result in the death of the organism.

According to a study published by "Evidence-based complementary and alternative medicine : eCAM", Tabernaemontana divaricata methanolic extract is rich in TPC at 62.32 ± 4.02 and TFC at 24.53 ± 0.61. The extract showed a cytotoxic potential of 10% towards human RBCs and a protein estimation of 22.82 ± 4.6. Antioxidant potential was measured via DPPH at 15.9 ± 2.33%, CAT at 65.57 ± 13.4%, SOD at 3.02 ± 3.4, and POD at 15.87 ± 0.5 and 0.74 ± 0.2. Regarding antidiaabetic potential through glucose uptake by yeast cells, the plant showed levels of 75.11 ± 1.44% for α-amylase, 41.81 ± 3.75% for α-glucosidase, and 35.9 ± 1.24%. These findings indicate that the plant is a powerful source of natural antioxidants and antidiaabetic activity.
