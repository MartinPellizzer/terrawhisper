Red-berried Elder, scientifically known as Sambucus racemosa, is a deciduous shrub native to North America. Highly valued in traditional herbalism, its vibrant red berries and fragrant flowers possess potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Often utilized to support immune health, this versatile plant remains a cornerstone of botanical medicine.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Sambucus racemosa is characterized by a sexual cycle that begins with a flowering period starting in April and concluding in July. The pollination process is primarily biotic, relying on an insect pollination syndrome to facilitate fertilization, as self-fertilization is noted to be absent. Following successful pollination, the plant produces fleshy, indehiscent fruits classified as drupes, which have an average length of 0.565 cm and an average width of 0.4833 cm. These fruits facilitate seed dispersal through a zoochorous syndrome, specifically via endozoochory, where animals consume the fruit and disperse the seeds. The seeds themselves are quite small, with a mean mass of approximately 0.00513 g (ranging from a minimum of 0.001232 g to a maximum of 0.006502 g) and a consistent mean length of 2 mm. The seed volume is recorded at a mean of 3 mm³, which serves as both the minimum and maximum value in this dataset. Notably, asexual reproduction is absent in this species, making sexual reproduction through these insect-mediated processes essential for its life cycle.
