What's the taxonomical classification of Ilex purpurea?

Ilex purpurea belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, it falls under the order Aquifoliales and is a member of the family Aquifoliaceae. Finally, the plant is defined by its genus, Ilex, and its specific species designation, purpurea.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Ilex purpurea has small, elliptical leaves that are dark green on the upper surface and slightly paler underneath. These leaves are typically arranged oppositely along the stems and possess smooth, entire margins. The plant produces small, inconspicuous flowers that emerge from the leaf axils during the blooming season. Following pollination, the plant develops small, fleshy drupes that turn a distinct shade of purple or black when fully ripe. The overall growth habit is characterized by a compact, shrubby structure with woody stems.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the subtropical and temperate regions of East Asia, specifically ranging across central and southern China, Japan, and Korea. It typically thrives in moist, shaded environments such as riverbanks, marshes, and the edges of forests. Within these habitats, it prefers areas with high humidity and well-drained but consistently damp soil. Its distribution is primarily limited to these specific climatic zones where seasonal rainfall supports its growth. What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Ilex purpurea is primarily utilized for its medicinal leaves. These leaves are frequently harvested to create infusions or decoctions used in traditional healing practices. They are often employed to address various inflammatory conditions and skin ailments. Some practitioners also use the plant extracts to support digestive health or manage fever symptoms. The specific bioactive compounds found within the foliage contribute to its overall therapeutic potential.

According to a study published by "Yao xue xue bao = Acta pharmaceuutica Sinica", the leaves of Ilex purpurea Hassk contain pedunculoside, ziyuglycoside I, and rutundic acid. An HPLC-ELSD method was used to quantify these components using an Allsphere ODS-2 column measuring 250 mm x 4.6 mm ID with 5 microns. The calibration curves were linear in the range of 2.56-25.60 micrograms for pedunculoside, 1.64-16.40 micrograms for ziyuglycoside I, and 3.74-37.40 micrograms for rutundic acid. Average recovery rates for the leaves were 96.3% for pedunculoside, 97.3% for ziyuglycoside I, and 97.7% for rutundic acid. Precision RSDs were 1.59% (n = 5), 3.82% (n = 5), and 2.11% (n = 5), with all RSDs being less than 4% (n = 6) and reproducibilities less than 5% (n = 6).

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause potential health side effects including gastrointestinal distress, allergic skin reactions, and possible interactions with anticoagulant medications.
