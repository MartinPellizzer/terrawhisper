Valeriana amurensis, scientifically known as Valeriana amurensis, is a perennial herb native to East Asian landscapes. Renowned for its delicate flowering stems and distinctive foliage, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Distribution

This plant is primarily found throughout the diverse landscapes of the Russian Far East. Within this region, its presence is specifically noted in the Amur, Khabarovsk, and Primorye territories. Beyond the Russian borders, the species extends into the Manchuria region of China. Its geographical reach further encompasses the easternmost parts of the Asian continent. Ultimately, the distribution of Valeriana amurensis includes the entire Korean peninsula in Eastern Asia.

Plant Parts

This plant, Valeriana amurensis, relies on its specialized underground structures for both survival and medicinal potency. The primary components utilized are the roots and the rhizomes, which form an extensive network beneath the soil surface. These subterranean parts serve as the main storage organs for the plant, concentrating the essential bioactive compounds that characterize the species. In many medicinal applications, it is these very rhizomes and fibrous roots that are harvested, dried, and processed to extract the therapeutic properties inherent to the plant.
