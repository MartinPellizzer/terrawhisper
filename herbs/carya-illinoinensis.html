Carya illinoinensis

Carya illinoensis

Carya illinoinenensis, commonly known as the pecan tree, is a vital North American species valued for its nutrient-dense nuts. Beyond its culinary importance, traditional practices utilize its bark and leaves for medicinal purposes, including treating skin ailments and inflammation, highlighting its significant role in both ecology and herbalism.

  • Scientific name: Carya illinoinensis
  • Family: Juglandaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fagales, it is classified under the family Juglandaceae. Its specific taxonomic placement is defined by the genus Carya.

Common Names

This plant, scientifically known as Carya illinoinensis, is recognized by various common names across different linguistic regions. In Simplified Chinese, it is referred to as 长山核桃 (zh-hans) and 长山核桃 (zh-cn), while in Singapore, the name used is 长山核桃 (zh-sg). In Traditional Chinese contexts, such as in Taiwan, it is known as 長山核桃 (zh-hant), and in Hong Kong, the designation is similarly 長山核桃 (zh-hk).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several distinct regions across Asia and Europe. In Central Asia, its presence can be identified specifically within Tajikistan. The species also extends into the Caucasus, inhabiting both the North Caucasus and the Transcaucasus regions. Furthermore, it is found in Western Asia, notably on the island of Cyprus. Finally, its range reaches into China, where it is documented in Southeast China.
