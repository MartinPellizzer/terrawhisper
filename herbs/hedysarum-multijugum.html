Hedysarum multijugum

What's the taxonomical classification of Hedysarum multijugum?

Hedysarum multijugum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant falls under the order Fabales. It is further categorized within the family Fabaceae, a group known for its diverse legumes. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is defined by the genus Hedysarum and the species multijugum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Hedysarum multijugum has pinnately compound leaves with multiple pairs of small, oval-shaped leaflets and woody, scrambling stems that form dense thickets. The foliage typically consists of several pairs of leaflets arranged along a central rachis, which helps the plant navigate through surrounding vegetation. Its flowers are small and clustered, often displaying colors that range from yellow to pale cream. The plant produces legume-style seed pods that are characteristic of the Fabaceae family. What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds 7-hydroxy-4'-methoxy isoflavone, aureol, beta-sitosterol, hedysarimcoumestans A-H, and soyasaponin II. These specific phytochemicals contribute to the diverse pharmacological profile of Hedysarum multijugum. The presence of various coumestans and isoflavones suggests potential bioactive properties within the plant's chemical structure. Additionally, sterols and saponins like beta-sitosterol and soyasaponin II are integral components of its chemical makeup. Researchers study these constituents to better understand the plant's therapeutic applications.

According to a study published by "Journal of natural products", ten coumestans were isolated from the roots of Hedysarum multijugum. These compounds were identified through spectroscopic analyses. The structures were elucidated as the new hedysarimcoumestans A-H, which are numbered 1-8. Additionally, the plant contains 1,3,9-trimethoxycoumestan, designated as 9, and aureol, designated as 10.
