Botanical Identification

Morphology

The morphology of Buddleja cordata is characterized by its perennial, woody growth habit, forming a robust shrub that typically reaches heights of 1 to 3 meters. The plant features a sturdy, woody stem structure that provides significant structural integrity. The leaves are simple, opposite, and distinctly cordate (heart-shaped) at the base, with an acute apex and an entire margin. The leaf surface is typically soft and pubescent, often displaying a grayish-green hue due to the presence of fine hairs on both the upper and lower surfaces. The inflorescence consists of dense, terminal panicles composed of numerous small, tubular flowers that are often pale lilac, white, or yellowish. These flowers are characterized by four spreading corolla lobes and are highly attractive to pollinators. The fruit is a small, ovoid capsule containing numerous tiny, brown seeds, which are dispersed to facilitate the plant's reproduction within its natural habitat.
