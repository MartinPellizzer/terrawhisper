Alpinia tonkinensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Alpinia tonkinensis?

Alpinia tonkinensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Zingiberales, which is a group known for many aromatic and medicinal plants. It is further categorized into the family Zingiberaceae, a family commonly referred to as the ginger family. Within this family, the plant is placed in the genus Alpinia, and its specific identity is defined by its species name, tonkinensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Alpinia tonkinensis has large, elliptical, bright green leaves that grow from a thick, creeping rhizome and reach heights of up to two meters. The foliage is characterized by prominent parallel venation and a smooth, waxy texture on the upper surface. From the center of these leafy stalks, erect inflorescences emerge to produce small, delicate flowers. These blossoms typically feature tubular shapes with subtle color variations ranging from white to pale pink. The plant's robust structure is supported by sturdy pseudostems formed by tightly overlapping leaf sheaths.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude tropical regions of the Andes mountains in South America, specifically ranging from Venezuela through Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, and Bolivia. It thrives primarily in cloud forests and montane ecosystems where moisture levels are consistently high. The species is typically found at elevations between 2,000 and 3,500 meters above sea level. Because it relies on these specific microclimates, its distribution is often fragmented across isolated mountain ridges. This restricted range makes its presence highly dependent on the preservation of intact Andean forest corridors.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds 2 alpha-(p-hydroxycinnamoyl) cineole, isorrhamnetin-3-O-beta-D-galactosyl-(6-->1)-alpha-L-rhamnoside, and trans-cinnamyl methyl ester. These specific chemical constituents contribute to the diverse biological activities associated with the species. Researchers study these molecules to understand their potential therapeutic applications in various medical fields. The presence of such unique phytochemicals distinguishes Alpina tonkinenensis from other related botanical species. Detailed analysis of these compounds helps in evaluating the plant's overall pharmacological profile.

According to a study published by "Journal of Asian natural products research", two new compounds, 2 alpha-(p-hydroxycinnamoyl) cineole (2) and (E)-15-nor-16-oxo-8(17),12-labdadiene (4), were isolated from Alpinia tonkinesis Gangep. In addition to these 2 new substances, three known compounds were identified within the plant. These 3 known compounds are trans-cinnamyl methyl ester (1), 4(15)-cadinene-6,10-diol (3) and isorrhamnetin-3-O-beta-D-galactosyl-(6-->1)-alpha-L-rhamnoside (5). The structures of all 5 isolated compounds were elucidated by spectral analysis.
