common walnut

Juglans regia · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1077 studies) · ★★★★★

Common walnut, scientifically known as Juglans regia, is a majestic deciduous tree revered for its nutritional and medicinal properties. Beyond its edible kernels, its leaves and hulls contain potent bioactive compounds, including tannins and polyphenols, offering significant antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial benefits used in traditional and modern herbal medicine.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fagales, it is classified under the family Juglandaceae. It is further identified by the genus Juglans, specifically as the species Juglans regia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across several regions of Europe. In Northern Europe, its presence is noted in Denmark and the islands of Great Britain and Ireland. It can also be found further north within the borders of Norway. Moving toward the center of the continent, the species is documented in Middle Europe. Specifically, its range extends into the country of Austria.

Plant Parts

This plant, Juglans regia, commonly known as the English walnut, possesses various components that contribute to its medicinal profile. The leaves, or a single leaf, are often utilized in traditional remedies for their astringent properties, while the thick bark can be harvested for its therapeutic potential. The most well-known part is the seed, which is rich in nutrients and bioactive compounds. Furthermore, scientific studies have highlighted that the plant contains a significant amount of total flavonoids, which contribute to its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory capabilities.
