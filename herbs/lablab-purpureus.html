Lablab purpureus

What's the taxonomical classification of Lablab purpureus?

Lablab purpureus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic progression, it falls under the order Fabales and is a member of the family Fabaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by the genus Lablab and is specifically designated as the species purpureus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Lablab purpureus has a climbing, herbaceous growth habit characterized by trifoliate leaves with ovate leaflets and a twining stem structure. The foliage typically features a soft, pubescent texture on both the upper and lower surfaces. Its inflorescences are produced in axillary racemes, displaying papilionaceous flowers that range in color from white to deep purple. The plant produces large, flat, indehiscent pods that are often hairy and contain several seeds. These seeds are typically kidney-shaped and can vary in color depending on the specific cultivar.

What are the pharmacological activities of Lablab purpureus?

This plant has anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, hypoglycemic, hypolipidemic, and hepatoprotective pharmacological activities that contribute to its therapeutic potential. Its antioxidant properties help neutralize free radicals, thereby reducing oxidative stress within the body's cells. The plant demonstrates hypoglycemic effects by assisting in the regulation of blood glucose levels, which is crucial for managing diabetes. Furthermore, its hypolipidemic action supports cardiovascular health by helping to lower lipid concentrations in the bloodstream. Finally, its hepatoprotective qualities serve to shield the liver from damage caused by toxins or disease.

According to a study published by "Scientific reports", the crude methanolic extract of Lablab purpureus seeds exhibited potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and cytotoxic activities. Antioxidant activity was demonstrated using the DPPH, hydroxyl radical scavenging, ferric oxide reducing, and ABTS radical reducing assays. Anti-inflammatory activity was confirmed through RBC membrane stabilization and protein denaturation assays. The extract exhibited significant in vitro cytotoxicity towards MCF-7 and A549 cell lines, causing apoptosis and cell cycle arrests at the S and G2/M phases. Furthermore, the extract showed minimal cytotoxicity towards normal HEK-293 cell lines. Molecular docking using Schrodinger v14.1 identified ligands like N-Acetyl-D-glucosamine forming stable complexes with proteins to indicate potent anti-breast and anti-lung cancer agents.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause favism in individuals with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency, a genetic condition that leads to the destruction of red blood cells upon ingestion. This specific reaction occurs because the plant contains certain bioactive compounds that trigger oxidative stress within the bloodstream. Such oxidative damage can result in hemolytic anemia, characterized by jaundice, dark urine, and extreme fatigue. While the plant is a valuable source of protein, careful monitoring is necessary for vulnerable populations to avoid these acute hematological complications. Consuming unprocessed seeds may also pose risks due to the presence of naturally occurring anti-nutrients.

According to a study published by "The Journal of applied bacteriology", the toxic glycosides vicine and convicine present in Lablab purpureus have been implicated in favism, which is an anaemic disease of humans. These glycosides are identified as the components responsible for this condition. The concentrations of vicine and convicine can be reduced through the growth of Lactobacillus plantarum on Lablab purpureus suspensions. Additionally, the glycosides are eliminated from the Lablab purpureus substrate by the growth of the filamentous fungus Fusarium graminearum. Using a concentrated culture filtrate of Aspergillus oryzae can also result in the complete degradation of these glycosides.
