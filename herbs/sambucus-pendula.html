Sambucus pendula

Sambucus pendula, commonly known as the weeping elder, is a graceful deciduous shrub distinguished by its arching, pendulous branches. While primarily celebrated for its ornamental beauty in landscapes, this species belongs to the elderberry family, traditionally valued in various cultures for its unique botanical characteristics and potential medicinal properties.

  • Scientific name: Sambucus pendula
  • Family: Viburnaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, placing it within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Dipsacales and the family Viburnaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Sambucus, specifically as the species Sambucus pendula.

Distribution

This plant possesses a very specific and limited geographical range. Within the vast region of Eastern Asia, its presence is quite localized. Specifically, the species can be found growing in the territory of Korea. This indicates that its natural habitat is restricted to this particular part of the continent. Consequently, the distribution of Sambucus pendula is characterized by this single, distinct location.
