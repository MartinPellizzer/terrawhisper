Coccoloba uvifera

Sea grape

Coccoloba uvifera, commonly known as sea grape, is a resilient coastal shrub native to tropical regions. Renowned for its fleshy, edible fruits and leathery leaves, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. It is traditionally utilized in various cultures for its medicinal properties, including treating inflammation, wounds, and digestive ailments.

  • Scientific name: Coccoloba uvifera
  • Family: Polygonaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Caryophyllales. Finally, it is a member of the family Polygonaceae and falls under the genus Coccoloba.

Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as Coccoloba uvifera or sea grape, features distinct and highly characteristic leaves that serve as a primary identifier for the species. The leaves are large, leathery, and broadly orbicular in shape, often appearing somewhat kidney-shaped with a rounded or slightly indented apex. They possess a thick, waxy cuticle that helps the plant retain moisture in coastal environments, and they are often characterized by prominent, palmate venation that radiates from the base. In many medicinal applications, these robust leaves are the parts most frequently studied or utilized for their various bioactive properties.
