Xanthocercis zambesiaca

Nyala tree

Xanthocercis zambesiaca, commonly known as the African Wild Plum, is a resilient tree native to Southern Africa. Beyond its ecological importance, this plant holds significant medicinal value. Various parts, including its bark and leaves, are traditionally utilized in various cultures to treat ailments ranging from skin irritations to digestive issues.

  • Scientific name: Xanthocercis zambesiaca
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales. Finally, it is a member of the Fabaceae family within the genus Xanthocercis.

Common Names

This plant, scientifically known as Xanthocercis zambesiaca, is recognized by various local and regional names across different languages. In English, it is commonly referred to as the nyala tree, while in Afrikaans, it is known as the njalaboom. Its diverse nomenclature also extends to Spanish, where it is identified by the names Xanthocercis zambesiana, Pseudocadia zambesiaca, and Sophora zambesiaca.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the regions of South Tropical Africa. Specifically, it can be found growing within the borders of Malawi and Mozambique. Its presence also extends into the territories of Zambia and Zimbabwe. Additionally, the species inhabits parts of Southern Africa, particularly in Botswana. These diverse locations highlight the plant's wide range across the southern portion of the continent.
