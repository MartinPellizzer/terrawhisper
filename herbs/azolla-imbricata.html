Azolla imbricata

Azolla imbricata, commonly known as a floating water fern, is a remarkable aquatic plant valued for its rapid growth and nitrogen-fixing capabilities. Often used in aquaculture and organic farming, it serves as a nutrient-rich biofertilizer. Its medicinal potential lies in its diverse bioactive compounds and traditional therapeutic applications.

  • Scientific name: Azolla imbricata
  • Family: Salviniaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Polypodiinae, it is organized under the order Salviniales and the family Salviniaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Azolla.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Azolla imbricaata, is referred to by various common names depending on the language and region. In Japanese, it is called アカウキクサ (akaukikusa), while in Korean, it is known as 물개구리밥 (mulgaeguribap). In Chinese, the plant is widely recognized by the name 满江红 (mǎnjiānghóng), which is specifically written as 满江红 in Simplified Chinese (zh-cn) and as 滿江紅 in Traditional Chinese (zh-tw).

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several key regions within East Asia. In China, its presence is noted in the South-Central areas as well as throughout the Manchuria region. It can also be found growing in the North-Central parts of the country. Furthermore, its range extends into the Southeast portion of China. Finally, this species is documented to inhabit the island nation of Japan.
