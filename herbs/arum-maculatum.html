Lords and ladies

Arum maculatum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (17 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Lords and ladies, scientifically known as Arum maculatum, is a captivating yet deceptive woodland perennial. Renowned for its striking spathe and spadix, this plant holds a complex place in ethnobotany. While historically used in traditional medicine, its potent calcium oxalate crystals require extreme caution due to their significant toxicity.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Arum maculatum, features distinct leaves that are central to its morphological identification. The leaves are typically large, heart-shaped, or arrow-shaped, characterized by a prominent pattern of dark green blotches or spots on their surface, which gives the species its name. These leaves are arranged in a basal rosette, growing directly from the underground rhizome, and they serve to capture sunlight for the plant's energy production while providing a lush, textured appearance to its natural habitat.
