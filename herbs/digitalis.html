Digitalis

Digitalis, commonly known as foxglove, is a striking perennial plant famous for its bell-shaped flowers. While aesthetically beautiful, it contains powerful cardiac glycosides. Historically significant in medicine, it is used to treat heart failure, yet requires extreme caution due to its high toxicity if ingested incorrectly or in excess.

  • Scientific name: Digitalis
  • Family: Plantaginaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales and family Plantaginaeceae, it is specifically classified under the genus Digitalis.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical range across several key regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence is well-documented in Denmark and Great Britain. It can also be found growing within the landscapes of Ireland and Sweden. Moving toward the central part of the continent, the species is distributed through Middle Europe. Specifically, its habitat extends into the mountainous and lowland regions of Austria.
