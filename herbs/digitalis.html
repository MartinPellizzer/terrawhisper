the foxglove genus

Digitalis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (52 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

The foxglove genus, scientifically known as Digitalis, is a group of striking perennial plants renowned for their bell-shaped flowers. While celebrated for their ornamental beauty, they contain potent cardiac glycosides. These compounds are vital in modern medicine for treating heart failure, though the plant remains highly toxic if ingested.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as foxglove, is a potent medicinal herb where the most significant components are its leaves. While the plant can produce small seeds for reproduction, it is the foliage that contains the vital cardiac glycosides required for pharmaceutical use. In clinical and traditional preparations, the leaves are typically harvested and processed into dried leaves to ensure stability and standardized potency, as fresh material can be unpredictably toxic.
