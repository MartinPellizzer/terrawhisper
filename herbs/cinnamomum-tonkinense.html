Cinnamomum tonkinense

What's the taxonomical classification of Cinnamomum tonkinense?

Cinnamomum tonkinense belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows a taxonomic lineage that places it in the order Laurales. It is further categorized under the family Lauraceae, which defines its botanical relationships. Ultimately, the plant is identified by its genus, Cinnamomum, and its specific epithet, tonkinense.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Cinnamomum tonkinense has elliptical to ovate leaves that are typically 5 to 12 centimeters long, possessing an entire margin and an acute apex. The foliage exhibits a leathery texture with a glossy upper surface and a paler underside. Its woody stems and branches are characterized by a smooth bark that may become slightly fissured with age. Small, inconspicuous flowers are arranged in axillary panicles, which eventually develop into small, fleshy drupes. These fruits are typically globose and change color as they reach maturity.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is endemic to the mountainous regions of the central highlands in Vietnam. It is primarily found growing in high-altitude evergreen forests where the climate remains consistently humid. The species is specifically concentrated within the provinces of Lam Dong and Dak Lak. These habitats provide the necessary shade and moisture required for its survival. Its range is quite restricted compared to other members of the Cinnamomum genus.
