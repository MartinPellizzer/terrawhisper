What's the taxonomical classification of Typha davidiana?

Typha davidiana belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, the plant is organized under the order Poales and the family Typhaceae. Finally, its specific classification is defined by the genus Typha and the species davidiana.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Typha davidiana has long, linear, sword-shaped leaves that emerge directly from a creeping rhizome and reach heights of up to two meters. These leaves are characterized by a prominent midrib and a smooth, somewhat leathery texture that helps them withstand aquatic environments. The plant produces a distinct cylindrical spike composed of densely packed, small, brownish flowers located atop a sturdy, upright stem. This flowering spike is typically divided into a more compact upper section of fertile flowers and a more elongated lower section of sterile, feathery bristles. The overall structure of the plant is adapted for life in marshes and wetlands, allowing it to stabilize itself in soft sediment.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Typha davidiana utilizes its female flowers, leaves, and pollen for various medicinal purposes. The female flowers are often harvested to treat inflammatory conditions and digestive issues. Its leaves can be processed to aid in topical applications or to support metabolic health. Additionally, the pollen provides concentrated nutrients that are used to bolster the immune system. These different components work together to offer a diverse range of therapeutic benefits.

According to a study published by "Yao xue xue bao = Acta pharmaceutica Sinica", the medicinal parts of Typha davidiana used in the Chinese traditional drug "Puhuang" include pollen, filaments, female flowers, and leaves. Five flavonoids within these specific plant parts were determined using HPLC with a mu-Bondapak C18 column. The analytical process utilized a mobile phase consisting of water, isopropanol, and tetrahydrofuran in a ratio of 83.5:14:2.5. Detection of these components was performed at a wavelength of 287 nm. The study compared the qualities of the Typha species and eight commercial "Puhuang" samples to discuss quality control criteria.
