Mallotus philippensis

Mallotus phillipensis (misspelling)

Mallotus philippensis, commonly known as Kamala, is a medicinal shrub native to South and Southeast Asia. Renowned for its resinous fruit, it serves as a potent traditional remedy for treating intestinal worms, skin ailments, and inflammation. Its bioactive compounds offer significant antimicrobial and antioxidant properties in various therapeutic applications.

  • Scientific name: Mallotus philippensis
  • Family: Euphorbiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Mallotus philippensis, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Malpighiales and the family Euphorbiaceae. Finally, it is classified under the genus Mallotus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical presence across several key regions of Asia. In China, it is widely distributed throughout the South-Central areas and the Southeast. Its range also extends to the tropical island of Hainan and the high-altitude regions of Tibet. Furthermore, the species can be found within the Nansei-shotto islands of Eastern Asia.
