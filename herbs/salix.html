Willow, scientifically known as Salix, is a versatile genus of shrubs and trees renowned for its medicinal properties. Long used in traditional healing, its bark contains salicin, a natural precursor to aspirin. This potent botanical resource remains fundamental in managing pain, reducing inflammation, and treating various febrile conditions worldwide.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malpighiales. Finally, it resides in the family Salicaceae and is identified by the genus Salix.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across various regions of Northern Europe. Specifically, it can be found growing in the landscapes of Denmark and Finland. Its presence extends to the island of Iceland as well. Furthermore, the species is common throughout Great Britain. Finally, its geographical range includes the Faroe Islands, known as Føroyar.
