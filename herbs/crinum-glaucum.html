Crinum glaucum

Crinum glaucum, commonly known as the Poison Bulb, is a perennial herb valued in traditional medicine. Native to coastal regions, its large, fleshy bulbs contain bioactive alkaloids used to treat skin ailments and inflammation. While therapeutically significant, its potent chemical properties require careful handling to avoid toxicity and adverse effects.

  • Scientific name: Crinum glaucum
  • Family: Amaryllidaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified within the order Asparagales and the family Amaryllidaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Crinum.

Distribution

This plant, known as Crinum glaucum, exhibits a specific geographical distribution across the West Tropical Africa region. It can be found growing within the borders of Benin, where it thrives in local ecosystems. The species is also documented throughout the coastal and inland areas of Ghana. Furthermore, its presence extends to the territories of Guinea-Bissau and the Ivory Coast. Finally, the plant is widely distributed within Nigeria, completing its range across these five West African nations.
