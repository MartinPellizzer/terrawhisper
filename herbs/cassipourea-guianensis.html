Cassipourea guianensis

Cassipourea guianenensis, commonly known as a vital medicinal herb in regional folk medicine, holds significant ethnobotanical importance. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, this plant is frequently utilized to treat diverse ailments. Understanding its chemical properties and therapeutic applications is essential for advancing modern pharmacological research and natural healing.

  • Scientific name: Cassipourea guianensis
  • Family: Rhizophoraceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, it is classified under the family Rhizophoraceae and the genus Cassipourea.

This plant, Cassipourea guianenis, is known by various names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In English, it is commonly referred to as the Guianan Cassipourea. In Portuguese, it is identified as Cassipourea-guianense, while in French, the name used is Cassipourea-guianense.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found across several distinct regions within the Caribbean and Northern South America. Its distribution begins in the Caribbean, where it is specifically recorded in Trinidad and Tobago. Moving into the mainland, the species occupies significant territories throughout the Guianas, including French Guiana, Guyana, and Suriname. Furthermore, its presence extends into the northern reaches of Venezuela. Together, these locations define the specific geographical range of Cassipourea guianenensis.
