Phaeanthus ophthalmicus

Phaeanthus ophthalmicus, commonly known as the "eye plant," is a distinctive medicinal herb native to tropical regions. Renowned for its unique leaf patterns resembling eyes, it is traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat various ailments, particularly those affecting vision, skin inflammation, and digestive wellness through its bioactive compounds.

  • Scientific name: Phaeanthus ophthalmicus
  • Family: Annonaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and order Magnoliales, it is a member of the family Annonaceae. Specifically, it is categorized under the genus Phaeanthus.

Distribution

This plant is native to the diverse tropical regions within the Malesia botanical zone. Its distribution includes the large island of Borneo, where it thrives in humid environments. It can also be found scattered throughout the Lesser Sunda Islands. Furthermore, the species extends its reach into the Malay Peninsula and the Maluku archipelago. Finally, its geographical range encompasses the various islands of the Philippines.
