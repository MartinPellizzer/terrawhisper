Rauvolfia serpentina

Rauvolfia serpentina, commonly known as Indian Snakeroot, is a perennial medicinal shrub native to the Indian subcontinent. Renowned in Ayurvedic medicine, it contains unique alkaloids like reserpine. Historically used to treat snake bites, it is most celebrated today for its potent sedative and antihypertensive properties in managing high blood pressure.

  • Scientific name: Rauvolfia serpentina
  • Family: Apocynaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified within the order Gentianales and the family Apocynaceae. Finally, it is identified under the genus Rauvolfia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several key regions in Asia. In China, it can be found specifically throughout the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan areas. Its presence extends into the Indian Subcontinent, where it is notably located in Assam. Additionally, the species is documented to grow within the borders of Bangladesh. Together, these locations highlight the plant's significant footprint across diverse subtropical landscapes.

Plant Parts

This plant, Rauvolfia serpentina, is a medicinal species where various anatomical components hold significant pharmacological value. The most potent part is the root, specifically the elongated, twisted roots that are harvested for their alkaloid content. In some botanical descriptions, the texture of the hairy root system is noted, distinguishing its physical structure. Beyond the underground portions, the leaves are used in various traditional applications, while the small seeds serve as a key component in the plant's reproductive cycle.
