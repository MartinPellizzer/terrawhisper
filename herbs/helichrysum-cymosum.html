Helichrysum cymosum

Helichrysum cymosum, commonly known as the Everlasting flower, is a resilient medicinal shrub native to Southern Africa. Renowned for its potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, this plant is traditionally used in herbal remedies to support skin health, promote wound healing, and alleviate various inflammatory conditions through its essential oils.

  • Scientific name: Helichrysum cymosum
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asterales, it is classified under the family Asteraceae and is identified by the genus Helichrysum.

Common Names

This plant, Helichrysum cymosum, is known by various regional names across different linguistic landscapes. In English, it is commonly referred to as the strawflower, a name that reflects its durable, paper-like blooms. Within South Africa, where it is native, it is frequently called dieblom in Afrikaans. Additionally, its local identity is captured in Xhosa as imivula and in Zulu as imivula, highlighting its deep-rooted connection to the botanical heritage of the region.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning across multiple regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence is noted around the Gulf of Guinea Islands. Moving toward the south, it can be found in Zambia within the South Tropical Africa region. Its distribution extends further into the Southern Africa region, specifically encompassing the Cape Provinces and Lesotho. Additionally, the species is documented as growing within the KwaZulu-Natal area of Southern Africa.
