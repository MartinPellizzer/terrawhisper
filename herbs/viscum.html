Viscum

Viscum, commonly known as mistletoe, is a unique hemiparasitic plant found globally. While often seen as a decorative symbol, it holds significant botanical interest. Traditionally, various species have been explored in herbal medicine for their potential therapeutic properties, though careful study is essential due to their complex chemical compositions.

  • Scientific name: Viscum
  • Family: Santalaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Santalales, it is classified under the family Santalaceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Viscum.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as mistletoe, is a hemiparasitic species where the medicinal properties are primarily derived from its green parts. The leafy, evergreen shoots constitute the most significant portion used in traditional and alternative medicine, as they contain the bioactive lectins and viscotoxins required for therapeutic applications. Because the plant relies on its green parts to perform photosynthesis while simultaneously drawing water and nutrients from its host tree, these succulent, verdant stems serve as the essential reservoir for its active chemical compounds.
