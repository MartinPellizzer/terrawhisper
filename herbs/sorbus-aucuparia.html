Sorbus aucuparia

Rowan

Sorbus aucuparia, commonly known as the mountain ash or rowan, is a resilient deciduous tree valued for its medicinal properties. Rich in antioxidants and vitamin C, its berries are traditionally used to support digestive health and immune function, offering a natural remedy rooted in ancient herbal wisdom and folklore.

  • Scientific name: Sorbus aucuparia
  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Under the subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified in the order Rosales and the family Rosaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Sorbus.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as the mountain ash, possesses several distinct components used in various traditional applications. Each individual leaf is pinnate, meaning it is composed of multiple small leaflets arranged along a central stem, and these leaves collectively form a lush, green canopy. The most notable medicinal and nutritional part of the species is the fruit, which appears in dense clusters. These small, red berries are highly valued for their vitamin content, and while a single fruit may seem insignificant, the abundance of these fruits provides a significant source of antioxidants.
