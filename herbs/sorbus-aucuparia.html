Reproduction

The reproduction of Sorbus aucuparia is primarily sexual, utilizing bisexual flowers that facilitate genetic diversity. The flowering period typically commences in June and extends through July. During this stage, the plant relies on biotic pollination syndromes, specifically involving insects, to ensure successful fertilization, as self-fertilization is noted to be absent. Following pollination, the plant develops fleshy, indehiscent fruits classified as berries. These fruits generally measure between 0.6 cm and 0.85 cm in length and maintain a width ranging from 0.6 cm to 0.8 cm. The dispersal of the plant is characterized by a zoochorous syndrome, specifically endozoochory, where animals consume the fleshy fruit and disperse the seeds. The seeds themselves are quite small, with a mean mass of approximately 0.0297848834 g (ranging from a minimum of 0.00066 g to a maximum of 0.188 g) and a consistent seed volume of 3.4 mm³. No asexual reproduction methods are present for this species.
