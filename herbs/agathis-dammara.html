Agathis alba, scientifically known as Agathis dammara, is a majestic coniferous tree native to the rainforests of Indonesia. Renowned for producing high-quality dammar resin, it holds significant medicinal value. This aromatic substance is traditionally used for its antiseptic, anti-inflammatory, and wound-healing properties, playing a vital role in holistic wellness.

The physiology of Agathis dammara is characterized by a specialized metabolic framework typical of many coniferous species. It utilizes the C3 photosynthetic pathway, a process in which carbon dioxide is fixed directly into a three-carbon compound during the initial stage of the Calvin cycle, making it highly efficient in stable, moist environments but sensitive to photorespiration under high-temperature stress. Furthermore, the plant does not function as a nitrogen fixer, meaning it lacks a symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria to convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms, and instead relies on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium from the soil through its root system to sustain its growth and development.
