Silphium perfoliatum

Silphium perfoliatum, commonly known as Cup Plant, is a striking perennial native to North American prairies. Renowned for its large, bowl-shaped leaves that collect rainwater, this botanical marvel offers ecological benefits. While primarily valued by pollinators, its unique structure and hardy nature make it a fascinating subject in herbalism.

  • Scientific name: Silphium perfoliatum
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Under the subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified in the order Asterales and falls within the family Asteraceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Silphium.

Plant Parts

This plant, Silphium perfoliatum, commonly known as cup plant, possesses distinct anatomical features that serve various ecological roles. Its large, green leaves are notable for being perfoliate, meaning they appear to wrap around the stem, creating a cup-like structure that often collects rainwater. Additionally, the plant produces abundant pollen within its bright yellow flower heads, which serves as a vital nutritional resource for a wide variety of pollinating insects.
