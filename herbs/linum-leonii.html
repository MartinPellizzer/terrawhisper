Plant Parts

This plant, Linum leonii, serves as a fascinating subject in plant biotechnology due to its capacity for diverse in vitro growth patterns. Researchers often utilize callus induction to initiate cell proliferation, resulting in undifferentiated masses of calli that can be further manipulated for secondary metabolite production. By transitioning from these cellular masses to more organized structures, scientists can establish root cultures that mimic natural growth. Specifically, the induction of hairy roots through Agrobacterium rhizogenes-mediated transformation allows for the development of stable hairy root cultures, which are highly valued for their ability to produce significant quantities of medicinal compounds in a controlled environment.
