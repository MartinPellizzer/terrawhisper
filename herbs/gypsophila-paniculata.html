Gypsophila paniculata

Common gypsophila

Gypsophila paniculata, commonly known as baby's breath, is a perennial herb celebrated for its delicate, cloud-like white blossoms. While primarily used in floristry, this plant holds traditional significance in various cultures. Its ethereal beauty and hardy nature make it a beloved staple in both ornamental gardens and floral arrangements.

  • Scientific name: Gypsophila paniculata
  • Family: Caryophyllaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Caryophyllales, it is classified under the family Caryophyllaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Gypsophila.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Gypsophila paniculata, is identified by various common names across different Chinese linguistic contexts. In Simplified Chinese, it is referred to as 圆锥石头花 (zh-hans) and specifically as 圆锥石头花 in mainland China (zh-cn) and Singapore (zh-sg). In Traditional Chinese contexts, it is known as 圓錐石頭花 (zh-hant), which is also the designation used in Hong Kong (zh-hk).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution concentrated within the northern regions of Europe. In the Scandinavian peninsula, it can be found growing across Norway and Sweden. Its presence also extends to the island of Great Britain, marking a significant part of its western range. Moving further east, the species is documented within the borders of Finland. Finally, its northern European footprint is completed by its occurrence in Denmark.
