Cornus chinensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Cornus chinensis?

Cornus chinensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this classification, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the taxonomic hierarchy further, it falls under the order Cornales and is a member of the family Cornaceae. Ultimately, this plant is identified by the genus Cornus and is specifically recognized by its species name, chinensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Cornus chinensis has opposite, decussate leaves that are ovate to elliptical in shape, featuring entire margins and prominent pinnate venation. The leaf surfaces are typically smooth and dark green, while the undersides may possess a slightly different texture or color. The plant produces small, creamy white flowers arranged in dense, flat-topped cymes that emerge in late spring. These inflorescences are followed by the development of fleshy, drupe-like fruits that transition from green to a deep blue or black hue when ripe. Its woody stems and branches can develop a distinctive grayish bark as the shrub matures.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

Its distribution is closely tied to areas that provide consistent moisture and well-drained soils. How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-drained, acidic soil and partial shade to thrive in temperate climates.
