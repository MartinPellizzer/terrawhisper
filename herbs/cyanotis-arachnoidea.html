What's the taxonomical classification of Cyanotis arachnoidea?

Cyanotis arachnoidea belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. At the class level, it is categorized as Equisetopsida, falling under the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant is further organized into the order Commelinales and is a member of the family Commelinaeaecae. Within this family, it is assigned to the genus Cyanotis, ultimately reaching its specific designation as the species arachnoidea.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Cyanotis arachnoidea has slender, creeping stems that produce small, lanceolate leaves with distinctively hairy or arachnoid surfaces. Tiny, three-petaled flowers emerge from the leaf axils, typically displaying a pale blue or whitish coloration. These floral structures are supported by delicate pedicels that allow the blooms to sit lightly against the vegetation. According to a study published by "International journal of molecular sciences", Cyanotis arachnoidea contains a rich array of phytoecdysteroids, including 20-hydroxyecdysone (20E), which displays important agrochemical, medicinal, and pharmacological effects. The comparative transcriptome of Cyanotis arachnoidea leaf and root was constructed using 86.5 million clean reads. These reads were assembled into 79,835 unigenes, of which 39,425 unigenes were successfully annotated. In the roots, which have a higher accumulation of 20E, the expression levels of 2427 unigenes were up-regulated. The study identified 49 unigenes referring to the phytoecdysteroid backbone biosynthesis, consisting of 15 mevalonate pathway genes, 15 non-mevalonate pathway genes, and 19 genes for biosynthesis from farnesyl pyrophosphate to cholesterol.
