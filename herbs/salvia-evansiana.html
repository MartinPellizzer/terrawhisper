Salvia evansiana

What's the taxonomical classification of Salvia evansiana?

Salvia evaansiana belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. As a member of the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae, this plant is further categorized under the genus Salvia, ultimately reaching its specific designation as the species evansiana.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Salvias evaansiana has upright, square-shaped stems that support oppositely arranged, rugose leaves with serrated margins and a distinctively textured surface. The foliage typically presents a deep green hue and a somewhat lanceolate to ovate shape. Its inflorescences consist of dense, terminal spikes featuring tubular, two-lipped flowers that transition through shades of purple and blue. These blossoms are supported by branched calyces that provide structural integrity to the flowering heads. The overall architecture of the plant is herbaceous and bushy, reaching a moderate height in suitable growing conditions.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude regions of western China, specifically within the mountainous provinces of Yunnan and Sichuan. It thrives in temperate, montane environments where rocky terrain and well-drained soils are prevalent. These specific habitats are typically located at elevations ranging from 2,000 to 3,500 meters above sea level. The species prefers moist, shaded forest edges or alpine meadows that receive filtered sunlight. Because of its localized range in these eastern Himalayan foothills, it is often considered a specialist of these particular mountain ecosystems.

According to a study published by "Yao xue xue bao = Acta pharmaceutica Sinica", methanol extracts from the roots of Salvia evansiana were tested using an in vitro screening model. This study analyzed 17 Salvia species to evaluate their inhibitory activities on protein tyrosine phosphatase 1B. The inhibition rates for the crude extracts of these samples ranged from 9.76% to 100% at a concentration of 30 microg x mL(-1). Among the tested species, Salvia evansiana demonstrated one of the most convincing effects on human PTP1B. HPLC analysis further identified seven components shared by the samples that could be related to these inhibitory activities.
