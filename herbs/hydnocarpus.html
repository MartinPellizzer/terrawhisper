Hydnocarpus

Hydnocarpus, commonly known as Chaulmoogra, is a significant medicinal plant celebrated for its historical role in treating leprosy. Extracted from its seeds, chaulmoogra oil contains unique fatty acids that possess potent antimicrobial properties. Today, it remains a vital subject in ethnobotany and traditional pharmacology for various skin ailments.

  • Scientific name: Hydnocarpus
  • Family: Achariaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Malpighiales and the family Achariaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic designation is placed within the genus Hydnocarpus.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Hydnocarpus wightianus, is highly valued for its therapeutic properties derived primarily from its reproductive structures. The most significant medicinal component is found within the seeds, which serve as the source of its potent bioactive compounds. Through specialized extraction processes, the oil of hydnocarpus is obtained, providing a concentrated form of the plant's healing essence. This valuable seed oil, commonly referred to as chang oil, is widely utilized in dermatological applications to treat various skin conditions such as scabies and leprosy.
