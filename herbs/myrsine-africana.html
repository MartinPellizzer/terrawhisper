Myrsine africana

Myrsine africanica, commonly known as the African Myrtle, is a versatile evergreen shrub native to various African regions. Renowned for its medicinal properties, it is traditionally used to treat skin ailments, digestive issues, and inflammation. Its bioactive compounds make it a significant subject in ethnobotanical and pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Myrsine africana
  • Family: Primulaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Ericales and the family Primulaceae, it is classified under the genus Myrsine.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Myrsine africanica, is referred to by various names across different languages and regions. In Simplified Chinese (zh-hans), it is called 铁仔, while in Traditional Chinese (zh-hant) and the specific Cantonese context of Hong Kong (zh-hk), it is known as 鐵仔. In Amharic (am), the plant is called ቀጨሞ, and in the Hsbin language (hsb), it is referred to as Chinska myrsina.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution that spans multiple continents and climates. In the northern reaches of its range, it can be found in Great Britain within Northern Europe. Its presence extends to the Macaronesian islands, specifically documented in the Azores. Within West-Central Tropical Africa, the species is located in both Rwanda and the DR Congo. Finally, its distribution reaches into Northeast Tropical Africa, where it is found in Eritrea.
