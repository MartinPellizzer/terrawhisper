Douglas hawthorn, scientifically known as Crataegus douglasii, is a resilient deciduous shrub native to western North America. Renowned for its dense foliage and delicate blossoms, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditionally used by Indigenous communities, it offers potential medicinal properties, particularly in supporting cardiovascular health and wellness.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning several continents and diverse climates. In North America, it is commonly found throughout the subarctic region of Alaska. Its presence extends further south into Western Canada, specifically within the provinces of Alberta and British Columbia. Moving across the Atlantic, the species can be located in the Baltic States of Eastern Europe. Finally, its range reaches into Northern Europe, where it is documented in Finland.

Plant Parts

This plant, Crataegus douglasii, commonly known as Douglas hawthorn, is recognized for its medicinal potential, which is primarily concentrated in its fruit. These small, fleshy pomes are often harvested for their nutritional and therapeutic properties, as they are rich in various bioactive compounds. In traditional herbal practices, the fruit is utilized for its ability to support cardiovascular health and digestive wellness, making it a significant part of the plant's medicinal profile.
