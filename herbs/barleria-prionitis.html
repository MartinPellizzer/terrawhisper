Barleria prionitis

What's the taxonomical classification of Barleria prionitis?

Barleria prionitis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the evolutionary lineage of the order Lamiales. This plant is a representative of the family Acanthaceae, specifically falling under the genus Barleria, with the specific epithet prionitis denoting its unique species identity.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Barleria prionitis has an erect, thorny shrub morphology characterized by stiff, woody stems that typically reach heights of up to two meters. The plant features long, straight, and sharp spines that emerge from the nodes along the branches. Its leaves are pinnately compound with several pairs of leaflets that possess an elliptical or oblong shape and entire margins. The flowers are bright yellow and grow in axillary clusters, often appearing in dense cymes. These botanical structures are complemented by small, globose fruits that develop after successful pollination.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Barleria prionitis Linn. is an important Ayurvedic medicinal plant in India used to treat ailments including swellings, gout, arthritic and rheumatic disorders, nervine and skin diseases, and as an immunorestorative. Research into its n-butanol fraction of methanol extract from aerial parts (IFBp) showed that doses of 50, 100 and 200μg/ml significantly (P≤0.01) increased the intracellular killing activity of stimulated neutrophils. The IFBp contained main iridoids shanzhiside methyl ester and barlerin at contents of 21.55±2.40 and 10.03±1.69 % w/w, respectively, based on a mean±SD where n=3. This investigation reveals that IFBp is a potent immunostimulant that stimulates both specific and non-specific immune mechanisms.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause significant reproductive toxicity characterized by antifertility through the disruption of male reproductive health. Scientific observations indicate that it leads to decreased preleptotene spermatocytes and direct interference with the process of spermatogenesis. These physiological changes are accompanied by low testicular glycogen contents within the tissue. Furthermore, the plant exposure results in a reduced cross-sectional surface area of Sertoli cells. These combined effects collectively compromise the structural and functional integrity of the testes.

According to a study published by "Journal of the Chinese Medical Association : JCMA", Barleria prionitis is one of several medicinal plants reviewed for its potential use as an antifertility agent in females. The study notes that many of these medicinal plants appear to act through an antizygotic mechanism. Researchers conducted an extensive bibliographic review by analyzing classical textbooks and peer reviewed papers through searches in CENTRAL, Embase, and PubMed. While the review identifies Barleria prionitis as a plant traditionally used to facilitate antifertility, it suggests that its active constituents should be further investigated for their mechanisms. This research creates a foundation to study the efficacy of plants used by women as traditional medicines.
