Labisia pumila, scientifically known as Labisia pumila, is a creeping herb native to the tropical rainforests of Southeast Asia. Often referred to as "Kacip Fatimah," this medicinal plant is highly revered in traditional medicine for its potent phytoestrogens, widely used to support female reproductive health and hormonal balance.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions of Southeast Asia. Within the Indo-China region, it can be found growing in both Cambodia and Thailand. Its presence extends significantly into the Malesia zone, where it inhabits the island of Borneo. Additionally, the species is distributed throughout the islands of Jawa and the Malay Peninsula. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in various tropical environments across the region.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Labisia pumila, utilizes its leaves as a primary component in traditional medicinal practices. These leaves are typically characterized by their elliptical shape and distinct veining, which contain various bioactive compounds and phytochemicals. In many Southeast Asian cultures, the leaves are harvested and prepared through decoctions or infusions to treat a variety of ailments, making them a vital part of the plant's therapeutic profile.
