Actinidia polygama, scientifically known as Actinidia polygama, is a vigorous deciduous vine native to East Asia. Physiology

The physiology of Actinidia polygama is fundamentally characterized by its reliance on the C3 photosynthetic pathway, a mechanism common to most woody climbing vines that involves the direct fixation of atmospheric carbon dioxide via the enzyme RuBisCO within the mesophyll cells. This metabolic process dictates the plant's resource requirements, as it necessitates consistent access to moisture and moderate temperatures to minimize photorespiration, which occurs when oxygen competes with carbon dioxide at the enzyme's active site. Consequently, the physiological efficiency of its carbon assimilation is highly dependent on environmental humidity and light interception through its expansive foliage. Beyond carbon fixation, its physiological processes include complex vascular transport systems designed to move water and nutrients upward through its climbing stems to support rapid vegetative growth, alongside specialized cellular respiration to maintain metabolic homeostasis throughout its seasonal life cycle.
