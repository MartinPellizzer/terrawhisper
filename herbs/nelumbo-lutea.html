Physiology

The physiology of Nelumbo lutea is characterized by a highly specialized adaptation to aquatic environments, governed primarily by a C3 photosynthetic pathway. This metabolic strategy involves the direct fixation of carbon dioxide via the enzyme RuBisCO during the Calvin cycle, which is typical for most temperate aquatic macrophytes. To support its rapid growth and large, floating lamina, the plant employs advanced anatomical adaptations, such as extensive aerenchyma tissue that facilitates the internal diffusion of gases, including oxygen and carbon dioxide, between the aerial leaves and the submerged rhizomes. This internal gas transport system is crucial for maintaining aerobic respiration in the roots despite the hypoxic conditions of the benthic substrate. Additionally, the plant manages transpiration and gas exchange through specialized stomata located exclusively on the upper surface of the leaves (epistomatous), allowing for efficient CO2 uptake from the atmosphere while minimizing water loss and avoiding the drowning of photosynthetic tissues.
