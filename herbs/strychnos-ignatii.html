Strychnos ignatia, scientifically known as Strychnos ignatii, is a tropical climbing shrub native to the West Indies. Renowned for its potent seeds, commonly called Saint Ignatius beans, this plant contains strychnine and brucine. Historically used in pharmacology, it serves as a powerful stimulant and a significant subject in toxicology.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Gentianales and the family Loganiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Strychnos.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across parts of East and Southeast Asia. In China, its presence is noted in the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan regions. Moving into the Indo-China peninsula, the species can be found growing in Thailand. It is also documented as being native to the landscapes of Vietnam. Together, these locations define the primary geographical range for Strychnos ignatii.
