Rhodiola yunnanensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Rhodiola yunnanensis?

Rhodiola yunnanensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the evolutionary lineage of the order Saxifragales. This plant is further categorized under the family Crassulaceae, falling into the genus Rhodiola, and is specifically identified by its species name, yunnanensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Rhodiola yunnanensis has a perennial rhizome that is thick, fleshy, and often branched, supporting a rosette of leaves that are lanceolate to elliptical in shape. The leaves are typically leathery in texture and possess entire margins with slightly serrated edges toward the apex. During the flowering stage, the plant produces small, actinomorphic flowers that are clustered in dense, terminal cymes. These inflorescences often display a range of colors from yellowish to reddish-pink depending on the specific environmental conditions. The stem structure is relatively short, keeping the foliage close to the ground to survive high-altitude habitats.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude regions of Yunnan Province in southwestern China. It primarily inhabits alpine meadows and rocky slopes found within the Hengduan Mountains. The species thrives in specific microclimates characterized by cool temperatures and well-drained soils. Its distribution is closely tied to the unique topographical features of the Tibetan Plateau's southeastern edge. Because of these specialized environmental requirements, the plant remains localized to these mountainous habitats.

According to a study published by "Yao xue xue bao = Acta pharmaceutica Sinica", the plant Rhodiola yunnanensis was included in the analysis of 10 samples of the Rhodiola genus. The study utilized a high performance liquid chromatographic method to determine the presence of the medicinal compound salidroside. This determination was performed using a mu Bondapak ODS column with 10 microns, measuring 3.9 mm x 300 mm. The process involved a methanol-water mobile phase at a 2:8 ratio and a flow rate of 1.0 ml/min. A Waters 484 detector was utilized at a wavelength of 276 nm to facilitate the quantitative analysis of salidroside.
