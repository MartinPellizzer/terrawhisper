Toxicodendron radicans

Poison ivy

Toxicodendron radicans, commonly known as poison ivy, is a notorious perennial plant characterized by its distinctive three-leaf structure. Renowned for containing urushiol, a potent allergenic oil, it causes severe skin irritation upon contact. Understanding its growth patterns and chemical properties is essential for safe identification and avoiding painful reactions.

  • Scientific name: Toxicodendron radicans
  • Family: Anacardiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Sapindales and the family Anacardiaceae. Finally, it is classified under the genus Toxicodendron.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a remarkably wide geographical distribution spanning multiple continents and climate zones. In Middle Europe, it can be found growing within the borders of Germany. Its range extends into Southeastern Europe, specifically documented in Italy. Within the region of Central Asia, the species is present in both Tajikistan and Uzbekistan. Finally, its habitat reaches the northern reaches of Subarctic America in the Yukon territory.
