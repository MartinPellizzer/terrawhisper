Poison ivy, scientifically known as Toxicodendron radicans, is a notorious perennial plant characterized by its distinctive trifoliate leaves. Renowned for containing urushiol, a potent allergenic oil, it triggers severe skin reactions upon contact. Understanding its growth patterns and identification features is crucial for avoiding accidental exposure and irritation.

This plant exhibits a remarkably wide geographical distribution spanning multiple continents and climate zones. In Middle Europe, it can be found growing within the borders of Germany. Its range extends into Southeastern Europe, specifically documented in Italy. Within the region of Central Asia, the species is present in both Tajikistan and Uzbekistan.
