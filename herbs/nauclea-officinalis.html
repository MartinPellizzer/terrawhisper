Nauclea officinalis

Nauclea officinalis, commonly known as the African peach or yellowwood, is a versatile medicinal plant native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its potent pharmacological properties, it is traditionally utilized to treat malaria, inflammation, and fever. Modern research continues to explore its alkaloids for significant therapeutic potential in global medicine.

  • Scientific name: Nauclea officinalis
  • Family: Rubiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and order Gentianales, it is a member of the Rubiaceae family. Its taxonomic identity is further defined by its placement in the genus Nauclea.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several key regions across Asia. In China, its presence is specifically noted in Hainan and throughout China Southeast. Moving into the Indo-China region, the species can be found growing in the landscapes of Cambodia. It also maintains a significant presence within the borders of Laos. Finally, its natural range extends into the tropical environments of Thailand.
