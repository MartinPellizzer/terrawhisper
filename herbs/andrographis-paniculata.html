Andrographis paniculata

King of bitter

Andrographis paniculata, commonly known as King of Bitters, is a versatile medicinal herb prized in Ayurvedic and traditional Chinese medicine. Renowned for its intense bitterness, this plant offers powerful anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and immune-boosting properties, making it a vital natural remedy for treating respiratory infections and supporting overall wellness.

  • Scientific name: Andrographis paniculata
  • Family: Acanthaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Andrographis paniculata, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Lamiales and the family Acanthaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Andrographis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Andrographis paniculata, is recognized by various common names across different linguistic and cultural contexts. In Hindi, it is widely referred to as कालमेघ (Kalmegh), while in Portuguese, it is simply known as Andrographis. In Chinese-speaking regions, the names vary slightly by script: it is called 穿心莲 in Simplified Chinese (zh-hans), 穿心蓮 in Traditional Chinese (zh-hant), and 穿心蓮 in the Hong Kong regional variant (zh-hk).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions in Asia. Within China, it can be found specifically in the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan areas. Its range also extends significantly into the Indian Subcontinent. In this region, the species is documented to grow in Assam and throughout Bangladesh. Together, these locations highlight the diverse habitats where the plant thrives.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as King of Bitters, is a versatile medicinal herb where multiple components hold therapeutic value. The most frequently utilized portion is the aerial parts, which encompass the stems and the foliage. Specifically, the green leaves are highly valued in traditional medicine, and even a single leaf can contain significant concentrations of bioactive andrographolides. While the whole plant is often harvested and dried for various preparations, the roots also contribute to the plant's medicinal profile, though they are used less commonly than the above-ground structures.
