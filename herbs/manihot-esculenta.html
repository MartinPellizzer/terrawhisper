Cassava, scientifically known as Manihot esculenta, is a vital starchy root vegetable essential to global food security. Primarily grown in tropical regions, it serves as a primary energy source for millions. Beyond nutrition, its various parts offer medicinal potential, demonstrating unique bioactive properties used in traditional healing practices.

Distribution

This plant, known scientifically as Manihot esculenta, exhibits a diverse geographical range across several continents. In Southeastern Europe, its presence can be identified within the country of Italy. Moving toward the African continent, the species is widely distributed throughout West Tropical Africa. Specifically, it is found in the nations of Benin, Burkina, and Gambia. Additionally, its distribution extends to the coastal region of Guinea-Bissau.

Plant Parts

This plant, scientifically known as Manihot esculenta, utilizes different parts of its anatomy for various nutritional and medicinal purposes. The cassava tuber, which is the enlarged adventitious root, serves as a primary energy storage organ and is the most widely consumed part of the plant. Additionally, the leaf plays a crucial role in photosynthesis and is often utilized in traditional medicine and culinary practices due to its specific nutrient profile.
