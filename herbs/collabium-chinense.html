Collabium chinense

What's the taxonomical classification of Collabium chinense?

Collabium chinense belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Asparagales. This plant is further categorized into the family Orchidaceae, which defines its botanical characteristics. Within this family, it is identified by the genus Collabium, and its specific biological designation is the species chinense.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Collabium chinense has large, heart-shaped leaves that are bright green and can reach up to thirty centimeters in length. The leaf blades feature prominent venation and smooth margins, often growing in a dense basal rosette pattern. Long, fleshy petioles extend from the center of the plant to support the broad foliage. The plant typically produces small, inconspicuous flowers that emerge from the central stem during its blooming period. This herbaceous perennial also develops a thick, tuberous root system used for nutrient storage.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, commonly known as Chinese cabbage, requires well-draining soil with a pH between 6.0 and 7.5 and consistent moisture to thrive.

Sowing seeds is most effective in early spring or late summer to avoid the intense heat of midsummer. Regular applications of nitrogen-rich fertilizer are necessary to promote the development of dense, compact heads. Maintaining a consistent watering schedule prevents the heads from splitting or becoming bitter due to stress.

Providing full sunlight for at least six to eight hours daily ensures optimal growth and nutrient accumulation.
