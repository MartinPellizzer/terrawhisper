Brassica oleraceae, scientifically known as Brassica oleracea, is a versatile medicinal plant belonging to the Brassicaceae family. Renowned for its nutritional density, it contains bioactive compounds like glucosinolates and antioxidants. These properties offer significant health benefits, including anti-inflammatory effects and support for immune function within traditional herbal practices.

Reproduction

Reproduction in Brassica oleracea is primarily sexual, characterized by bisexual flowers that facilitate efficient propagation. The flowering period typically begins in April and extends through August, with the plant displaying distinctive yellow flowers that range in length from 1.5 cm to 2.5 cm. The pollination syndrome is biotic, relying heavily on insects, specifically bees, to ensure successful fertilization, although self-fertilization is also present. Following pollination, the plant develops dry, capsule-type fruits (specifically siliquae) that exhibit dehiscence. These fruits vary in size, with a mean length of 7.5 cm (ranging from 5 cm to 9 cm) and a mean width of 0.45 cm (ranging from 0.3 cm to 0.5 cm). Each fruit is highly productive, containing more than 1,000 seeds. The seeds themselves have a mean mass of approximately 0.003269 g (ranging from 0.0023 g to 0.00719 g), a mean length of 55 mm, a mean width of 3.5 mm, and a consistent mean volume of 2.6 mm³. Dispersal is achieved through an autochorous syndrome, where seeds are spread via self-dispersal mechanisms. No asexual reproduction methods were observed in this species.
