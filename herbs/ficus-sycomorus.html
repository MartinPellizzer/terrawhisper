Physiology

The physiology of Ficus sycomorus is characterized by a standard C3 photosynthetic pathway, which dictates its carbon fixation process and metabolic efficiency under varying environmental conditions. Unlike specialized desert plants, it does not utilize C4 or CAM pathways, relying instead on the direct fixation of atmospheric carbon dioxide through the enzyme RuBisCO during the light-independent reactions. Furthermore, the species does not function as a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria, such as Rhizobia, typically found in leguminous plants, and therefore depends entirely on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium available within the soil matrix to satisfy its nutritional requirements for protein synthesis and cellular growth.

Reproduction

Reproduction in Ficus sycomorus is characterized by a highly specialized biological process involving biotic pollination and animal-mediated dispersal. The plant utilizes a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects to facilitate fertilization. The reproductive structures produce a fleshy fruit type, classified as an anthocarp, which maintains a fleshy texture to attract dispersers. These fruits are relatively uniform in size, possessing a mean length of 2.5 cm and a mean width of 2.5 cm. Seed dispersal is zoochorous, meaning the plant relies on animals to spread its progeny.
