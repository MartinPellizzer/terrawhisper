Triticum turgidum subsp. durum

Triticum turgidum subsp. durum, commonly known as durum wheat, is a vital cereal crop valued for its high protein content and nutritional density. Beyond its culinary importance in pasta production, it offers medicinal potential through antioxidant compounds and essential minerals, contributing to overall dietary wellness and metabolic health.

  • Scientific name: Triticum turgidum subsp. durum
  • Family: Poaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Poales and the family Poaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Triticum, specifically as the subspecies durum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a varied distribution across several distinct regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence can be documented in both Great Britain and Sweden. Moving toward Middle Europe, the species is found growing within the territories of Austria and Germany. Additionally, its range extends into Southwestern Europe, specifically reaching the island of Corse. Collectively, these locations highlight the broad geographical spread of this particular subspecies.
