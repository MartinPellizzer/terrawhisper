Toxicodendron succedaneum

Japanese wax tree

Toxicodendron succedaneum, commonly known as the poison sumac, is a deciduous shrub native to North American wetlands. Distinguished by its distinctive red berries and trifoliate leaves, this plant is notorious for its potent urushiol content, which can cause severe allergic contact dermatitis upon exposure to its sap or foliage.

  • Scientific name: Toxicodendron succedaneum
  • Family: Anacardiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Plant Parts

This plant, Toxicodendron succedaneum, is primarily noted for the potent chemical properties found within its sap. The milky or resinous sap serves as the primary vehicle for urushiol, the allergenic oil responsible for the plant's characteristic skin irritation. When this sap comes into contact with human skin, it triggers a strong immune response, making the fluid the most significant and hazardous part of the plant in terms of its medicinal and toxicological profile.
