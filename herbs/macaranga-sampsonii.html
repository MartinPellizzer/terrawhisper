Macaranga sampsonii

Macaranga sampsonii, commonly known as the Sampson Macaranga, is a versatile medicinal plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its bioactive properties, it is traditionally used to treat various ailments, including skin infections and inflammation. This plant holds significant ethnobotanical importance, offering promising potential for modern pharmacological research and development.

  • Scientific name: Macaranga sampsonii
  • Family: Euphorbiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Macaranga sampsonii, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae within the order Malpighiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Euphorbiaceae and falls under the genus Macaranga.

This plant exhibits a specific geographical range across parts of East and Southeast Asia. Within the borders of China, it can be found inhabiting the South-Central regions. Additionally, the species is distributed throughout the Southeast portion of China. Its range extends further into the Indo-China peninsula as well. This plant, known scientifically as Macaranga sampsonii, utilizes its leaves as a primary component in traditional medicinal practices. The leaves are typically large, heart-shaped, and serve as the main part of the plant used for extracting therapeutic properties. In various herbal applications, these leaves are often prepared as poultices or decoctions to treat various ailments, making them the most significant botanical part for medicinal use.
