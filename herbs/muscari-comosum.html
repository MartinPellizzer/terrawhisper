Muscari comosum

Muscari comosum, commonly known as Grape Hyacinth, is a charming perennial celebrated for its unique, grape-like flower clusters. Beyond its ornamental beauty in gardens, this plant holds traditional medicinal significance. Often utilized in herbal practices, it offers intriguing properties that continue to fascinate both botanists and holistic wellness enthusiasts.

  • Scientific name: Muscari comosum
  • Family: Asparagaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Under the subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified in the order Asparagales and the family Asparagaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Muscari.

Common Names

This plant, scientifically known as Muscari comosum, is recognized by several distinct common names across different languages and regions. In Poland, it is referred to as Szafirek miękkolistny, while in France, it is known as Muscari à toupet. Within the Catalan-speaking regions, it is called calabruixa grossa, and in Hungary, it goes by the name üstökös gyöngyike. Additionally, in the Basque language, this medicinal species is identified as Purrione.
