Reproduction

The reproduction of Salpichroa origanifolia involves both sexual and asexual mechanisms, with its sexual reproduction being characterized by bisexual flowers. The flowering period typically begins in April and continues through September, featuring small white flowers that measure between 0.45 cm and 0.5 cm in length. These flowers rely on biotic pollination syndromes, specifically involving insects such as bees. To ensure genetic diversity, self-fertilization is noted as absent. Following successful pollination, the plant produces black, indehiscent berry-type fruits. These fruits vary in size, with a mean length of 1.5 cm (ranging from 1 cm to 1.5 cm) and a width ranging from 0.8 cm to 1.2 cm. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing more than 1000 seeds.
