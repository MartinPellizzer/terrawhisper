Life history

The life history of Salvia majdae is characterized by its specific ecological adaptation as a chamaephyte, a life form that dictates its growth strategy and survival mechanisms within its native habitat. As a chamaephyte, the plant maintains its perennating buds very close to the soil surface, often just above it, which allows it to withstand environmental fluctuations such as temperature changes or moderate herbivory. This classification indicates that while it does not possess the tall, woody structure of a phanerophyte or the purely ephemeral nature of a therophyte, it relies on these low-lying buds to ensure perennial regrowth. Consequently, its life cycle involves navigating seasonal shifts by utilizing these protected buds to re-emerge, balancing the structural stability required to persist in its niche with the resilience necessary to survive in its specific environmental context.
