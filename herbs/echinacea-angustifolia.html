Echinacea angustifolia

Purple coneflower

Echinacea angustifolia, commonly known as narrow-leaf coneflower, is a perennial herb native to North American prairies. Renowned for its potent medicinal properties, it is widely utilized in herbalism to bolster the immune system and combat respiratory infections. This resilient plant remains a cornerstone of traditional and contemporary botanical medicine.

  • Scientific name: Echinacea angustifolia
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales, it is situated in the family Asteraceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Echinacea and the specific species angustifolia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Echinacea angustifolia, is recognized by various regional and cultural names across the globe. In German, it is called Schmalblättriger Sonnenhut, while in Polish, it is referred to as Jeżówka wąskolistna. The plant holds deep cultural significance in indigenous traditions, such as in the Chy name Mo'ôhtávêheséeo'ôtse, and is also known as equinàcia in Catalan. Additionally, in Hungarian, it is commonly called keskenylevelelű kasvirág.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution across the northern regions of North America. In Western Canada, it can be found growing within the provinces of Manitoba and Saskatchewan. Moving south into the Northwestern U.S.A., its range extends into the state of Montana. The species is also documented throughout the rugged landscapes of Wyoming. Finally, its geographical presence reaches into the mountainous terrain of Colorado.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Echinacea angustifolia, is a perennial herb where various components can be utilized for medicinal purposes. While the whole plant is sometimes used in specific preparations, the most potent therapeutic properties are concentrated within the roots. Specifically, the dried root is the primary part harvested for its immune-boosting compounds. In addition to the underground structures, the stems and the serrated leaves also contain bioactive constituents that contribute to the plant's overall medicinal profile.
