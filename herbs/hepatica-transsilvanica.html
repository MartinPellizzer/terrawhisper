Botanical Identification

Morphology

The morphology of Hepatica transsilvanica is characterized by a low-growing, perennial herbaceous habit, being strictly non-woody in its structural development. The plant emerges from a short, somewhat woody rhizome that serves as the underground storage organ, though the aerial parts remain entirely herbaceous. The leaves are the most distinctive vegetative feature, being simple, leathery, and deeply three-lobed (reniform to kidney-shaped), often appearing in clusters at the base of the plant. These leaves are typically dark green with a slightly shiny surface and prominent venation. During the flowering period, the plant produces solitary, actinomorphic flowers borne on slender, leafless pedicels. The flowers are characterized by a ring of white, petaloid sepals—as true petals are absent—surrounding a central cluster of numerous stamens and pistils. The overall architecture is compact and ground-hugging, adapted to the shaded forest floor environments it inhabits.
