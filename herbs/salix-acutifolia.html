Salix acutifolia

Salix acuifolia, commonly known as the sharp-leaved willow, is a resilient medicinal plant valued for its therapeutic potential. Rich in salicin, it offers natural analgesic and anti-inflammatory properties. Often found in specialized ecosystems, this species serves as a vital botanical resource for traditional remedies and modern pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Salix acutifolia
  • Family: Salicaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Moving through its taxonomic hierarchy, it resides in the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malpighiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Salicaceae and is identified under the genus Salix.

Common Names

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Salix acuifolia, derives its medicinal value primarily from its bark. The bark of various willow species is historically significant because it contains salicin, a compound that acts as a precursor to salicylic acid, which has natural analgesic and anti-inflammatory properties. When harvested correctly, this woody layer is processed to create infusions or extracts used to alleviate pain and reduce fever.
