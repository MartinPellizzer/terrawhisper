Aristolochia gentilis

What's the taxonomical classification of Aristolochia gentilis?

Aristolochia gentilis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the biological hierarchy, it is classified in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving further down the taxonomic ranks, the plant is organized under the order Piperales and is a member of the family Aristolochiaceae. Finally, it is defined by the genus Aristolochia and is specifically identified by its species name, gentilis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Aristolochia gentilis has opposite, cordate leaves that are typically 5 to 12 centimeters long with entire margins and a smooth, somewhat leathery texture. The stems are climbing or trailing perennials that utilize twining motions to navigate through surrounding vegetation. Its flowers are tubular and brownish-purple, featuring a distinct swollen base known as an utricle. These specialized blooms are designed to trap small insects temporarily to facilitate pollination. The plant's overall structure is characterized by its sprawling habit and the subtle, mottled appearance of its foliage.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe nephrotoxicity, renal failure, uropathy, and carcinogenicity due to the presence of toxic aristolochic acids. These compounds are known to induce significant kidney damage by destroying the functional units of the renal system. Exposure often leads to obstructive uropathy, which can result in permanent structural changes to the urinary tract. Furthermore, the long-term consumption of the plant is linked to a high risk of carcinogenicity, specifically increasing the likelihood of upper urinary tract cancers. These cumulative effects make the plant extremely dangerous to human health.

The main side effects this plant can have are shown in the list below.

  • Nephrotoxicity
  • Renal failure
  • Uropathy
  • Carcinogenicity
  • Kidney damage
