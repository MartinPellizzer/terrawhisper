Artocarpus parvus

Kwai muk

Artocarpus parvus, commonly known as the small jackfruit, is a lesser-known medicinal plant valued in traditional healing. Native to tropical regions, its various parts possess significant therapeutic properties. Research highlights its potential antioxidant and anti-inflammatory benefits, offering promising applications in modern pharmacology for treating diverse health conditions naturally.

  • Scientific name: Artocarpus parvus
  • Family: Moraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Rosalales and the family Moraceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Artocarpus, which includes the specific species Artocarpus parvus.

Common Names

This plant, Artocarpus parvus, is known by various common names depending on the language and region. In Chinese, it is referred to as 桂木 (zh), 桂木 (zh-cn), and 桂木 (zh-tw), while in English, it is commonly called kwai muk (en) or kwai muk (en-us).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several key regions in Asia. Within China, it can be found across the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan areas. Its range also extends into the Indo-China peninsula, covering significant portions of the landscape. Specifically, the species is documented to grow within the borders of Cambodia. Additionally, its presence is well-established throughout the country of Laos.
