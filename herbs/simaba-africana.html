Simaba africana

Simaba africana, commonly known as a vital medicinal shrub in various African regions, holds significant ethnobotanical value. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, this plant is traditionally used to treat inflammatory conditions, infections, and skin ailments. Exploring its chemical constituents offers promising insights into modern pharmacological developments and herbal medicine.

  • Scientific name: Simaba africana
  • Family: Simaroubaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindales. Finally, it is situated within the family Simaroubaceae and the genus Simaba.

Common Names

This plant, Simaba africana, is known by various regional names depending on the linguistic context. In English, it is frequently referred to as the African Simaba, while in French, it is commonly called Simaba africain. Within local African contexts, its names shift according to language, such as being called mnyamwezi in Swahili or simaba in several other regional dialects.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed throughout the West-Central Tropical Africa region. Specifically, it can be found growing within the borders of Cameroon and the Congo. Its natural habitat also extends to the territory of Equatorial Guinea. Furthermore, the species is documented in Gabon and the enclave of Cabinda. These diverse locations highlight its specific ecological niche within the West-Central tropical zone.
