Rhus virens

Rhus virens, commonly known as poison sumac, is a distinctive deciduous shrub native to North American wetlands. Unlike edible sumacs, this species contains urushiol, a potent oil capable of causing severe allergic skin reactions. Understanding its identification and chemical properties is essential for safety in forested and swampy habitats.

  • Scientific name: Rhus virens
  • Family: Anacardiaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindales, it is further categorized into the family Anacardiaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic designation is found within the genus Rhus.

Distribution

This plant is primarily located within the Southwestern United States, specifically found in the state of Arizona. Its range extends further into the South-Central United States, where it inhabits parts of New Mexico. Additionally, this species can be found growing across various regions of Texas. Plant Parts

This plant, known as Rhus virens or staghorn sumac, exhibits distinct morphological features in its vegetative structures. The stems are particularly characteristic, often being densely covered in soft, velvety hairs that give them a fuzzy texture and a distinctive reddish or grayish appearance. The leaves are compound and alternate, consisting of multiple leaflets that contribute to the plant's lush, spreading canopy.
