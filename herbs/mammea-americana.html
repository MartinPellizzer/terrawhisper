Mammea americana

Mammea americana, commonly known as the Brazil nutmeg, is a tropical tree native to the Caribbean and South America. Renowned for its large, edible seeds, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Beyond culinary uses, various parts of the tree are utilized in traditional medicine for their diverse therapeutic properties.

  • Scientific name: Mammea americana
  • Family: Calophyllaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Mammea americana, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Malpighiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Calophyllaceae and is situated within the genus Mammea.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide and diverse geographical distribution across several different global regions. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found specifically within the country of Guinea. Its presence extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, where it grows in the DR Congo. Moving to the Pacific islands, it is located in the Society Islands of the South-Central region and the Caroline Islands of the Northwest. Finally, its range reaches the Southeastern United States, specifically within the state of Florida.
