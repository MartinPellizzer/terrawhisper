Cyperus compressus

What's the taxonomical classification of Cyperus compressus?

Cyperus compressus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic progression, the plant falls under the order Poales and is a member of the family Cyperaceae. Finally, its specific biological identity is defined by its placement within the genus Cyperus, reaching its final classification at the species level as Cyperus compressus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Cyperus compressus has a perennial life cycle characterized by a creeping rhizomatous root system, triangular stems that reach heights of up to 1 meter, and linear, bright green leaves that emerge from the base. The stems are notably compressed and feature a distinctively triangular cross-section typical of the sedge family. Leaves are narrow and arranged in a basal rosette, often appearing somewhat stiff or leathery in texture. The inflorescence consists of dense, terminal umbels made up of multiple spikelets that transition from green to brown as they mature. Small, inconspicuous flowers are embedded within these spikelets, eventually producing tiny achene fruits.

How is this plant cultivated?

Because it thrives in wet environments, you should plant it in areas where water tends to collect or near the edges of ponds. Regular maintenance involves thinning the clumps every few years to prevent them from becoming overly dense.
