Chamomile, scientifically known as Matricaria chamomilla, is a beloved medicinal herb celebrated for its soothing properties. Renowned for its delicate, daisy-like flowers, this plant is widely used in herbal teas and essential oils to reduce anxiety, aid digestion, and promote restful sleep, making it a staple in natural wellness.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Matricaria chamomilla is characterized by sexual processes through bisexual flowers, as asexual reproduction is absent in this species. The flowering period typically begins in May and extends through September, featuring white flowers that vary in width from 1.2 cm to 2.5 cm. Pollination is achieved through biotic means, specifically via an insect pollination syndrome. While the plant is capable of self-fertilization, the reproductive cycle culminates in the development of green, indehiscent fruits classified as achenes. These fruits are quite small, with a mean length of 0.45 cm (ranging from a minimum of 0.08 cm to a maximum of 0.1 cm) and a consistent mean width of 0.3 cm. The resulting seeds are lightweight, with a mean mass of 5.18e-05 g (ranging from 4e-05 g to 4.72e-05 g), which facilitates an anemochorous dispersal syndrome, allowing the seeds to be spread by the wind.
