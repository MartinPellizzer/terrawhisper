Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several continents and distinct ecological zones. In Southeastern Europe, its presence is noted within the northwestern Balkan Peninsula. Its range extends significantly into East Tropical Africa, specifically within the countries of Kenya and Tanzania. Furthermore, the species can be found in the Western Indian Ocean, particularly around the Comoros. Finally, the plant reaches the Indian Subcontinent, where it is documented in the region of Assam.

Plant Parts

This plant, Melaleuca citrina, possesses various components that contribute to its medicinal profile. The most significant part is the leaves, which are often harvested for their essential oils known for their antiseptic properties. The flowers are typically delicate and provide aesthetic value while supporting local pollinators. Although the seeds are produced to ensure the continuation of the species, they are less commonly used in traditional remedies compared to the foliage. Ultimately, the entire aerial part of the plant is frequently utilized in herbal preparations, as the bioactive compounds are concentrated in the structures above the ground.
