Tephrosia purpurea

Wild indigo

Tephrosia purpurea, commonly known as Purple Tea, is a versatile medicinal shrub native to tropical regions. Renowned for its potent antioxidant properties, this plant is traditionally used to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and digestive issues. Its bioactive compounds offer significant therapeutic potential in modern pharmacological research and herbal medicine.

  • Scientific name: Tephrosia purpurea
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. It is further organized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales. Finally, it is a member of the family Fabaceae and falls under the genus Tephrosia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions in Africa and its surrounding islands. In Northern Africa, it can be found growing in the countries of Algeria, Egypt, and Morocco. Its range also extends to the Macaronesian region, specifically within the archipelago of Cape Verde. Furthermore, the species is documented in West Tropical Africa, particularly in Benin. Together, these locations highlight the plant's presence in both arid and tropical environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Tephrosia purpurea, is valued in traditional medicine for various therapeutic properties derived from its different anatomical structures. While many medicinal applications utilize the whole plant, much of its efficacy is concentrated within the aerial parts, which include the visible above-ground structures. Specifically, the shoots and the vibrant leaves are frequently harvested for decoctions and extracts due to their rich phytochemical content. Additionally, the root system may also be employed in certain preparations, ensuring that nearly every component of the organism contributes to its medicinal profile.
