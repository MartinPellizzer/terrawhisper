Alaska birch

Betula pendula subsp. mandshurica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (34 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Alaska birch, scientifically known as Betula pendula subsp. mandshurica, is a resilient deciduous tree native to East Asia and North Pacific regions. Renowned for its adaptability, this subspecies offers significant medicinal potential, particularly through its bark and sap, which possess antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and diuretic properties used in traditional wellness.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further organized into the order Fagales and the family Betulaceae, falling under the genus Betula. Specifically, the taxon is identified as the subspecies Betula pendula subsp. mandshurica.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across several key regions within the vast territories of Russia. In the Siberian region, its presence is notably documented in Buryatiya, Chita, and Yakutiya. Moving eastward, the species also inhabits portions of the Russian Far East. Specifically, it can be found growing in the Amur region. Additionally, its range extends further toward the Kamchatka Peninsula.
