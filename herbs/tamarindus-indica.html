Tamarindus indica

Tamarindus indica, commonly known as tamarind, is a versatile tropical tree prized for its nutritional and medicinal properties. Its tangy fruit pulp, seeds, and leaves are utilized in traditional medicine to treat digestive issues, reduce inflammation, and manage fever. This ancient plant remains a vital staple in holistic healing.

  • Scientific name: Tamarindus indica
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Fabales and the family Fabaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identification is completed within the genus Tamarindus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions. In Northern Africa, it can be found growing in both Egypt and Libya. Its presence extends into the Macaronesia region, specifically within Cape Verde. Furthermore, the species is established in West Tropical Africa. Within this tropical zone, it is documented in both Benin and Burkina.
