Musa × paradisiaca

Musa paradisiaca

Musa × paradisiaica, commonly known as the banana, is a versatile tropical plant valued for both nutrition and medicine. Beyond its edible fruit, various parts are utilized in traditional healing to treat digestive issues, inflammation, and skin ailments. Its rich nutrient profile makes it a vital staple in global wellness.

  • Scientific name: Musa × paradisiaca
  • Family: Musaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Zingiberales, it is classified under the family Musaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Musa.

Distribution

This plant, Musa × paradisiaca, exhibits a specific distribution across several regions in Africa. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found within the borders of Burkina, Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, and Guinea. Moving further south into West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence extends into the Central African Republic. These locations highlight the plant's reliance on specific tropical climates found within these nations. Consequently, its range is concentrated within these distinct West and Central African territories.

Plant Parts

This plant, scientifically known as Musa × paradisiaca, offers various therapeutic benefits depending on the specific component utilized. While many parts of the banana plant are used in traditional medicine, the fruit peels are particularly noted for their bioactive properties. These peels are rich in polyphenols, carotenoids, and dietary fibers, which can be applied topically to soothe skin irritations or consumed to aid digestive health and provide antioxidant support.
