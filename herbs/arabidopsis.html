Arabidopsis

Rockcress

Arabidopsis, commonly known as the model organism of plant biology, is a small flowering plant belonging to the Brassicaceae family. Despite its unassuming appearance, it is indispensable to scientific research, providing critical insights into genetics, development, and environmental responses, serving as a cornerstone for understanding complex plant life processes.

  • Scientific name: Arabidopsis
  • Family: Brassicaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered under Brassicales and sits in the family Brassicaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic classification is placed within the genus Arabidopsis.

Plant Parts

This plant, Arabidopsis thaliana, is a model organism in botanical research, and its structural components are meticulously studied to understand plant development. The life cycle begins with the seeds, which are small and numerous, providing the starting point for germination. Once growth commences, the roots extend into the soil to anchor the plant and absorb nutrients, a process driven by the highly active root meristem. This zone of cell division is located at the very ends of the plant's underground structure, specifically within the root tips, where rapid cellular proliferation occurs. Above the ground, the plant develops green leaves that serve as the primary sites for photosynthesis, fueling the overall growth of the organism.
