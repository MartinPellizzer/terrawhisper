Martynia annua

Devil's claws

Martynia annua, commonly known as devil's claw, is a unique herbaceous plant recognized by its distinctive, hooked seed pods. Widely distributed across tropical regions, it holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditional medicine utilizes its various parts to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and digestive issues, showcasing its diverse pharmacological potential.

  • Scientific name: Martynia annua
  • Family: Martyniaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Lamiales, it is specifically situated in the family Martyniaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Martynia, with the species name annua.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution spanning across multiple regions of Africa and the Indian Ocean. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in several countries including Benin and Burkina. It is also found within the borders of Nigeria and Togo. Beyond the African mainland, the species extends its range to the Western Indian Ocean. Specifically, this medicinal plant can be located on the island of Mauritius.
