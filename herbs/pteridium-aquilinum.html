Pteridium aquilinum

Common bracken

Pteridium aquilinum, commonly known as bracken, is a widespread, resilient fern found across many global habitats. While historically utilized in various traditional medicinal practices, it contains significant compounds like ptaquiloside. This requires careful handling, as its potential toxicity necessitates scientific caution regarding its medicinal application and consumption.

  • Scientific name: Pteridium aquilinum
  • Family: Dennstaedtiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Polypodiinae, it falls under the order Polypodiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Dennstaedtiaceae and is identified by the genus Pteridium.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Pteridium aquilinum or bracken fern, possesses various structures, but its young leaves are particularly notable in certain traditional contexts. These young leaves, often referred to as fiddleheads when they are tightly coiled, emerge from the rhizomes during the spring growing season. While the mature fronds become large, tough, and highly invasive, the tender texture of the young leaves distinguishes them from the more fibrous and chemically complex older foliage.
