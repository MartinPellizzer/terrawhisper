Prickly lettuce, scientifically known as Lactuca serriola, is a versatile annual herb belonging to the Asteraceae family. Often found in disturbed soils and meadows, this plant is valued for its edible leaves and traditional medicinal properties, frequently utilized in herbal remedies to support digestive health and reduce inflammation.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Lactuca serriola is primarily sexual, characterized by bisexual flowers arranged in a panicle inflorescence. The flowering period is relatively long, typically beginning in April and extending through October. The flowers are yellow in color and utilize a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects for successful pollination. While self-fertilization is present, the plant is classified as a monocarp, meaning it completes its reproductive cycle once. The resulting fruit is a dry, indehiscent achene, which typically measures an average of 0.325 cm in length and 0.1 cm in width (with a maximum length of 0.4 cm and a minimum of 0.3 cm). Each fruit is highly productive, containing more than 1,000 seeds. These seeds are quite small, with a mean mass of approximately 0.00052572725 g (ranging from 0.0002 g to 0.00096 g), a mean length of 2.8 mm, and a mean volume of 0.51 mm³. Dispersal is achieved through an anemochorous syndrome, utilizing wind to spread the seeds effectively. Asexual reproduction is absent in this species.
