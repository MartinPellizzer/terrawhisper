Reproduction

The reproduction of Rhododendron groelandicum is characterized by a sexual reproductive cycle that lacks any recorded instances of asexual reproduction. The flowering period typically commences in May and extends through June. The plant utilizes a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects to facilitate the transfer of pollen. Following successful fertilization, the plant develops fruit in the form of a capsule, which is a dehiscence type, meaning it opens at maturity to release its seeds. These seeds are extremely minute, with a consistent seed mass of approximately 2e-05 g (both the minimum, maximum, and mean values are recorded at this level). Dispersal of the seeds is classified as zoochorous, involving animals in the movement of the reproductive units.
