Pongamia

Pongamia pinnata · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Moderate (147 studies) · ★★★☆☆

Pongamia, scientifically known as Pongamia pinnata, is a versatile, nitrogen-fixing legume widely distributed across tropical regions. Renowned for its medicinal properties, it is used in traditional healing to treat skin ailments, inflammation, and infections. Its oil is also highly valued in biofuels and sustainable organic pesticide formulations.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across several diverse regions within the African continent. In Northern Africa, its range extends into the country of Egypt. Moving toward the center of the continent, it is found throughout West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically in the DR Congo. Its distribution also covers Northeast Tropical Africa, including both Sudan and South Sudan. Finally, the species is well-established in East Tropical Africa, spanning across Tanzania and Uganda.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Karanja, offers a variety of therapeutic components that are utilized in traditional medicine. The seeds are highly valued for producing a potent oil used to treat skin diseases and as a natural pesticide. The leaf and the broader collection of leaves are often processed into pastes or decoctions to address inflammatory conditions and various dermatological issues. Additionally, the stem bark is frequently employed for its astringent properties, while the roots are utilized in specific herbal formulations to treat ailments such as fever and digestive disturbances.
