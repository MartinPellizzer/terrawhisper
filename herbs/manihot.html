Manihot, scientifically known as Manihot, is a versatile genus of flowering plants in the spurge family. Renowned for its medicinal properties, species like Manihot esculenta are vital globally. Beyond nutrition, various extracts are studied for their bioactive compounds, offering potential therapeutic benefits in treating inflammation and oxidative stress.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution that spans across both European and African continents. In Europe, its presence can be found in Spain within the Southwestern region. Moving toward Southeastern Europe, the plant is also located in Italy. Its reach extends significantly into West Tropical Africa, where it thrives in Benin. Finally, the species is documented in Burkina and Gambia, further illustrating its wide tropical range.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as cassava, is primarily valued for its edible tuberous roots, but its reproductive structures also play a role in its biological lifecycle. The flower of the Manihot esculenta is typically small, inconspicuous, and cream-colored, appearing in clusters that emerge from the leaf axils. While the floral parts are not the main focus of its medicinal or nutritional use, they are essential for the plant's pollination and the eventual production of seeds.
