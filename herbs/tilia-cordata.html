Tilia cordata

Small-leaved lime

Tilia cordata, commonly known as the small-leaved lime, is a deciduous tree cherished for its fragrant, pale-yellow blossoms. Historically utilized in herbal medicine, its flowers are brewed into soothing teas to reduce anxiety, ease insomnia, and alleviate digestive discomfort, making it a cornerstone of traditional European botanical healing.

  • Scientific name: Tilia cordata
  • Family: Malvaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malvales, falling into the family Malvaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Tilia.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the small-leaved lime, is primarily valued for its fragrant flowers and the specialized structures surrounding them. The individual flower is a small, yellowish, and nectar-rich component of the inflorescence, which serves to attract pollinators during the summer months. These flowers are nestled within a distinctive, leaf-like pale green bract, which is often referred to as part of the flower cluster in medicinal preparations. When harvested, both the flowers and their accompanying bracts are dried to create infusions used for their calming and soothing properties.
