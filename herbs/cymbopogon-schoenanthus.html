Cymbopogon schoenanthus

Camel grass

Cymbopogon schoenanthus, commonly known as lemongrass, is a versatile aromatic grass cherished in traditional medicine. Renowned for its uplifting citrus scent, it possesses potent antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and digestive properties. Often used in herbal teas and essential oils, this plant offers therapeutic benefits for relaxation and holistic wellness.

  • Scientific name: Cymbopogon schoenanthus
  • Family: Poaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further organized into the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Poales, falling under the family Poaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Cymbopogon.

Distribution

This plant, Cymbopogon schoenanthus, is primarily distributed throughout the northern regions of the African continent. Its presence is well-documented across the diverse landscapes of Algeria and Egypt. Furthermore, it can be found growing within the territories of Libya and Morocco. The species also extends its reach into the coastal and inland areas of Tunisia. Consequently, its geographical range is concentrated within these five specific North African nations.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as citronella grass, utilizes various components for its medicinal and aromatic properties. The long, slender green leaf is the most prominent part used in applications, as it serves as the primary source for harvesting the plant's potent constituents. Within these leaves are concentrated essential oils, which provide the characteristic citrus scent and possess significant insect-repellent and antiseptic qualities. While the plant can reproduce via the seed, the therapeutic value is almost exclusively derived from the aromatic compounds extracted from the foliage.
