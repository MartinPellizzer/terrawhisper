Chinese Yew, scientifically known as Taxus chinensis, is a versatile evergreen conifer native to East Asia. Renowned for its ornamental beauty and dense foliage, it also holds significant pharmacological importance. The plant contains bioactive alkaloids, which are extensively researched for their potential therapeutic properties in modern medicinal applications.

Plant Parts

This plant, Taxus chinensis, is characterized by several distinct morphological features used in various traditional applications. The annual leaves are needle-like and densely arranged along the stems, which provide the structural support for the evergreen growth. While the leaves themselves are often studied for their complex chemical compounds, other parts of the plant are equally significant in botanical descriptions. The barks of the tree are typically used in specialized preparations, and although the plant produces distinctive red, fleshy arils, the seeds contained within are known to be highly toxic and must be handled with extreme caution.
