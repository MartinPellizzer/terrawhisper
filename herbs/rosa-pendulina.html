Rosa alpina

Rosa pendulina · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (10 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Rosa alpina, scientifically known as Rosa pendulina, is a charming perennial shrub native to the mountainous regions of Europe. Renowned for its delicate, pale pink blossoms and graceful arching stems, this medicinal plant is traditionally valued in herbalism for its soothing properties and its ability to support overall wellness.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Rosales and the family Rosaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement within the genus Rosa.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across various regions of Middle Europe. In the western part of this territory, it can be found growing in Belgium. Moving eastward, its presence is well-documented throughout Germany and Austria. The species also inhabits the central landscapes of Czechia and Slovakia. Finally, its geographical range extends further into the region of Hungary.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Rosa pendulina, is often studied for its various therapeutic properties, with much of the traditional interest focused on the beautiful and fragrant flower. The flower serves as a primary component in many herbal preparations, where its petals are harvested to create infusions, oils, or tinctures used to soothe the nervous system or support skin health.
