Parkinsonia aculeata

Parkinsonia aculeata, commonly known as the thorny Parkinsonia, is a distinctive deciduous shrub native to the tropical regions of Africa and Asia. Characterized by its prominent spines and delicate flowers, this resilient plant holds significant ethnobotanical value, frequently utilized in traditional medicine to treat various ailments and infections.

  • Scientific name: Parkinsonia aculeata
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fabales, it is classified under the family Fabaceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Parkinsonia and the species name Parkinsonia aculeata.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Parkinsonia aculeata, is referred to by several different names depending on the regional Chinese linguistic standards. In Simplified Chinese, it is commonly called 扁轴木 (zh-hans) or 扁轴木 (zh-cn), which is also the standard nomenclature used in Singapore (zh-sg). In Traditional Chinese, the name is written as 扁軸木 (zh-hanth), a form also utilized in Hong Kong (zh-hk).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across the Mediterranean region. In Southwestern Europe, it can be found growing in Portugal as well as Spain. Its presence is also noted in the island of Sardegna within the same southwestern territory. Moving toward Southeastern Europe, the species is located in Greece. Finally, its range extends to the island of Sicilia in the southeastern part of the continent.
