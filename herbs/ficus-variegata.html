Ficus variegata

Ficus variegata, commonly known as the Variegated Fig, is a versatile tropical plant celebrated for its aesthetic beauty and medicinal potential. Beyond its ornamental appeal, various parts of this species are utilized in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from inflammation to skin disorders, showcasing its significant ethnobotanical value.

  • Scientific name: Ficus variegata
  • Family: Moraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Rosales and the family Moraceae. Ultimately, it is identified by its genus, Ficus.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several specific regions in East Asia. Within China, it can be found throughout the South-Central areas as well as the Southeast. Its presence extends to the tropical island of Hainan in the southern part of the country. Additionally, the species is documented in Taiwan and the Nansei-shoto islands. This diverse range highlights its established ecological footprint across these coastal and subtropical territories.
