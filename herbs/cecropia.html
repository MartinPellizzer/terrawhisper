Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across several regions within the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its distribution includes the nations of Ghana, Ivory Coast, and Senegal. Furthermore, the species extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically within Cameroon. Its range also encompasses the coastal areas of the Gulf of Guinea Islands. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's significant footprint throughout the tropical African landscape.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Cecropia, utilizes its large, deeply lobed leaf as a primary site for both photosynthesis and the expression of its medicinal properties. In various traditional practices, the leaf is the most frequently harvested part, often used in decoctions or poultices due to its specific chemical composition. The structure of the leaf is distinct, often featuring a palisade layer that supports the plant's rapid growth in tropical environments, while its surface area is optimized to capture sunlight efficiently.
