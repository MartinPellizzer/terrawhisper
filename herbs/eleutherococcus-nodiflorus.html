Eleutherococcus nodiflorus

Eleutherococcus nodiflorus, commonly known as Siberian ginseng or devil's bush, is a powerful adaptogenic herb valued in traditional medicine. Renowned for its ability to bolster the immune system and reduce fatigue, this resilient plant helps the body manage physiological stress, enhancing overall vitality and promoting long-term wellness and resilience.

  • Scientific name: Eleutherococcus nodiflorus
  • Family: Araliaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Apiales. Finally, it resides in the family Araliaceae under the genus Eleutherococcus.

Common Names

This plant, Eleutherococcus nodiflorus, is known by various common names depending on the linguistic context. In Chinese, it is referred to as 细柱五加 (zh), 糙毛五加 (zh), or simply 五加 (zh). Specifically, in Mainland China, the name used is 细柱五加 (zh-cn), while in Taiwan, it is identified as 細柱五加 (zh-tw).

Distribution

This plant is naturally distributed across several key regions of East Asia. Within mainland China, its presence is documented throughout the South-Central territories. It also extends into the North-Central provinces of China. Furthermore, the species can be found growing within the Southeast regions of the country. Finally, its geographical range includes the island of Taiwan in Eastern Asia.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Siberian ginseng or eleuthero, contains various components that are utilized for their medicinal properties. The woody stems provide the structural framework for the shrub, while the root bark is often harvested and processed for its adaptogenic compounds. Additionally, the plant produces small, dark fruits that appear as berries, completing the botanical profile of this significant medicinal species.
