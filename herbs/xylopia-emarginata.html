Xylopia emarginata

Xylopia emarginata, commonly known as a potent medicinal shrub in various tropical regions, is highly valued for its therapeutic properties. Rich in bioactive alkaloids and essential oils, this species is traditionally used to treat inflammation, digestive issues, and infections, offering significant potential for modern pharmacological research and herbal medicine.

  • Scientific name: Xylopia emarginata
  • Family: Annonaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Magnoliales and the family Annonaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Xylopia, specifically as the species Xylopia emarginata.

Common Names

This plant, Xylopia emarginata, is known by various regional names across different languages. In English, it is commonly referred to as the African nutmeg, while in French, it is called le petit noyau. In Swahili, the species is known as mchele, and in Zulu, it is called umfino.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of locations across the South American continent. In Northern South America, its presence is documented within the borders of Venezuela. Moving toward the western regions, it can be found in both Colombia and Bolivia. Within Brazil, the species is distributed across the West-Central area.
