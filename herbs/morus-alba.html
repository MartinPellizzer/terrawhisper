Morus alba

White mulberry

Morus alba, commonly known as the White Mulberry, is a versatile deciduous tree prized for its medicinal and nutritional properties. Historically used in traditional medicine, its leaves and berries offer potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and hypoglycemic benefits, making it a vital botanical resource for supporting metabolic health and wellness.

  • Scientific name: Morus alba
  • Family: Moraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Morus alba, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Rosales and the family Moraceae. Its taxonomic journey concludes in the genus Morus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of locations across the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence can be identified within the territories of Denmark and Sweden. Moving toward Middle Europe, the species is also documented throughout Germany. Furthermore, its distribution extends into the regions of Austria and the Czechia-Slovakia area. These specific locations highlight the varied geographical footprint of Morus alba.

Plant Parts

This plant, Morus alba, commonly known as the white mulberry, possesses various components that are utilized for their medicinal properties. The leaves (or individual leaf specimens) are widely used in traditional medicine to help manage blood sugar levels and support cardiovascular health. The fruits are not only nutritious but also contain antioxidants that contribute to overall wellness. Furthermore, the therapeutic profile of the plant extends to its underground structures, where both the roots and the root bark are harvested to treat ailments such as inflammation, fever, and digestive issues.
