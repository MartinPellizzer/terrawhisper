Vachellia tortilis subsp. raddiana

Vachellia tortilis subsp. raddiaana, commonly known as the twisted euphorbia, is a resilient medicinal shrub native to arid African landscapes. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, various parts of this plant are traditionally utilized to treat skin ailments, inflammation, and digestive issues, offering significant potential for pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Vachellia tortilis subsp. raddiana
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fabales, it is classified under the family Fabaceae and the genus Vachellia. Specifically, this subspecies is identified as Vachellia tortilis subsp. raddiana.

Common Names

This plant, Vachellia tortilis subsp. raddiana, is known by several different common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In Arabic, it is frequently referred to as al-mishmish, while in English, it is commonly called the umbrella thorn acacia. Additionally, it is recognized by the name akasia in Swahili.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across the diverse landscapes of Northern Africa. Its presence can be observed in the expansive territories of Algeria and Morocco. Additionally, it grows within the unique environmental conditions found in Tunisia and Libya. The species also extends its reach into the arid regions of Egypt. Together, these five countries represent the primary geographical range for this specific subspecies.
