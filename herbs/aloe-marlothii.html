Mountain aloe

Aloe marlothii · Accepted scientific name

Mountain aloe, scientifically known as Aloe marlothii, is a striking succulent native to the rocky slopes of Southern Africa. Characterized by its robust, fleshy leaves and vibrant orange-red flowers, this resilient species is highly valued in traditional medicine for its soothing, anti-inflammatory, and wound-healing properties in diverse herbal remedies.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions in Africa. Within South Tropical Africa, it can be specifically found in Mozambique. Its presence extends further into Southern Africa, where it inhabits the country of Botswana. In the South African region, the species is located in both KwaZulu-Natal and the Free State. Finally, the plant is also documented as growing within Eswatini.

Plant Parts

This plant, Aloe marlothii, is highly valued for the various therapeutic components found within its succulent structure. The whole-leaf is often utilized in traditional practices, though it is frequently processed to isolate more specific medicinal elements. Within the leaf, two distinct substances are harvested: the bitter, yellowish leaf exudate, known as aloin, which is primarily used for its potent laxative properties, and the clear, soothing gel found in the inner parenchyma, which is widely applied to treat burns, wounds, and skin irritations.
