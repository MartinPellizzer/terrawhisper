Berberis vulgaris · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (974 studies) · ★★★★★

Common barberry, scientifically known as Berberis vulgaris, is a deciduous shrub celebrated for its medicinal properties. Renowned for containing berberine, it offers potent anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial benefits. Historically used to treat digestive issues and skin conditions, this resilient plant remains a cornerstone in traditional herbal medicine and wellness practices.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Ranunculales, it is further categorized into the family Berberidaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Berberis.

Distribution

This plant, known as Berberis vulgaris, is widely distributed across several regions in Northern Europe. It can be found growing naturally throughout the landscapes of Denmark. Additionally, this medicinal species is present within the territories of Finland and Norway. The plant also establishes itself across the islands of Great Britain and Ireland. Consequently, its geographical range covers a significant portion of the northernmost European continent.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Berberis vulgaris, contains various therapeutic components distributed throughout its structure. The woody stem provides the necessary framework for the shrub, while the serrated leaves are often used in traditional preparations. Specifically, the leaf and the leaves of the plant contain alkaloids like berberine, which are central to its medicinal properties. Furthermore, the most potent medicinal compounds are frequently concentrated in the root, making the extraction of the entire roots essential for many pharmacological applications.
