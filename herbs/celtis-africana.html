Highly valued for its medicinal properties, it is used in traditional healing to treat ailments ranging from digestive issues to skin inflammations. Its robust nature and ecological importance make it a significant botanical resource.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Celtis africana is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, primarily facilitated by insects and specifically by bees. The plant produces fruits classified as drupes, which are fleshy in texture and indehiscent, meaning they do not naturally split open at maturity to release their contents. These fruits are relatively small, with a mean length and width of approximately 0.6 cm. The seeds contained within the fruit are quite consistent in size, possessing a mean volume of 92 mm³ (with both minimum and maximum values recorded at this volume), a mean length of 5.4 mm, and a maximum height and width of 4.5 mm. The seed mass varies slightly, ranging from a minimum of 0.03182 g to a maximum of 0.0504 g, with a calculated mean mass of 0.04111 g. Regarding the movement of genetic material and offspring, the dispersal syndrome is identified as anemochorous, indicating that wind plays a significant role in the dispersal process.
