Butea superba

What's the taxonomical classification of Butea superba?

Butea superba belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is situated in the subclass Magnoliidae and falls under the order Fabales. As a member of the family Fabaceae, this plant is categorized under the genus Butea, with its specific designation being the species superba.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Butea superba has trifoliate leaves consisting of three leaflets that are typically elliptic to obovate in shape and possess an entire margin. The plant is characterized by its bright orange-yellow flowers that emerge in dense, terminal racemes. Each flower features a distinct papilionaceous structure with a large, prominent standard petal. The woody stems and branches are often covered in a fine pubescence that provides protection. Its fruit is a flat, indehiscent pod that remains on the plant for an extended period.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical regions of India, where it is found primarily within the deciduous forests of the central and southern states. Its distribution is closely tied to specific environmental conditions found in the dry and moist deciduous biomes of the subcontinent. It frequently inhabits hilly terrains and scrublands that provide the necessary drainage for its root systems. While its presence is most documented in regions like Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra, it remains a characteristic component of local forest ecosystems. Localized populations may vary based on seasonal rainfall patterns and soil composition.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant Butea superba requires well-drained sandy or loamy soil and thrives in semi-arid climates with moderate sunlight and seasonal rainfall.

Farmers typically propagate the species through seeds or stem cuttings to ensure vigorous growth in tropical regions. During the initial stages, consistent moisture is necessary to establish a strong root system before the plant adapts to drought conditions. Regular weeding and minimal fertilization are sufficient to maintain its health throughout the growing cycle.

Once established, the plant is highly resilient and demands very little maintenance to produce its characteristic medicinal properties.

This plant Butea superbilla utilizes its rootbarks, stem, and tuberous roots for various medicinal applications. What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant Butea superbosa has been utilized for centuries in Ayurveda and Thai traditional medicine to treat various ailments including respiratory issues, skin diseases, and digestive disorders. In the Ayurvedic system, different parts of the plant such as the flowers, bark, and seeds are prepared into decoctions or powders to balance bodily humors. Thai traditional practitioners often employ the plant to manage inflammation and treat fever by utilizing its specific medicinal properties. Beyond these systems, the various components of the plant have historically served as ingredients in complex herbal formulations meant to restore physical equilibrium. This long-standing history of use underscores its significant role in ancient pharmacological practices.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Butea superba has a traditional use as an aphrodisiac and for male sexual disorder (MSD). This plant is among the Indian medicinal plants reported as aphrodisiacs in Ayurveda and modern scientific literature. The study suggests that the use of Butea superba for these conditions might be in part due to its ROCK-II inhibitory potential. While the study screened methanolic and successive aqueous extracts of 30 plants, it noted that 15 herbal extracts showed inhibition ranging between 50 and 88% at 50 μg/mL.

What are the pharmacological activities of Butea superba?

This plant has diverse pharmacological activities including androgenic, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, vasorelaxant, and erectile dysfunction management properties. Its androgenic effects contribute to enhancing testosterone levels and supporting male reproductive health. The antioxidant and anti-inflammatory actions help mitigate oxidative stress and reduce systemic inflammation within the body. Additionally, its vasorelaxant properties facilitate improved blood flow by relaxing smooth muscle tissues. These combined mechanisms make it a significant subject of study for treating various physiological dysfunctions.

According to a study published by "Natural product communications", Butea superba (Red Kwao Khruea) is used as a rejuvenating herb in traditional medicine in many tropical countries. This specific species is used for specific purposes and effects within the Kwao Khruea group. In contrast to other species, Butea superba shows androgenic effects in males. The study utilized a cycleave PCR based on the sequence of the chloroplast matK gene to differentiate this species. This method can be a specific, sensitive, and rapid way to identify medicinal plants.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds (-)-epicatechin, prunetin, procyanidin B2, and procyanidin B5. These specific phytochemicals are found within the tissues of Butea superba. Research suggests that these polyphenolic substances contribute to the plant's various biological activities. The presence of these antioxidants is a key characteristic of its chemical profile. Scientists study these individual molecules to understand how they support human health.

According to a study published by "Phytochemical analysis : PCA", the Thai medicinal plant Butea superba Roxb contains specific antioxidant compounds. The method utilized a decrease in absorbance at 517 nm to monitor negative peaks indicating antioxidant activity. Through the use of HPLC coupled on-line to ES1-MS and a DPPH-based assay, the antioxidant compounds in a fraction of an extract were identified. These identified medicinal compounds in Butea superba include procyanidin B2, (-)‐epicatechin, and procyanidin B5.

What health conditions is this plant used for?

This plant is used for treating depression, various cancers including gastric cancer, erectile dysfunction, and impaired sexual performance. Researchers have explored its bioactive compounds to evaluate their potential in managing psychological distress and mood disorders. In oncology, studies focus on its ability to combat malignant cell growth, specifically targeting gastric cancer. Furthermore, its medicinal properties are investigated for their capacity to improve reproductive health and address sexual dysfunction. These diverse applications highlight the plant's significant pharmacological potential in modern medicine.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Butea superba is a Thai medicinal plant used as folk medicine to improve physical and mental conditions and to prevent impaired sexual performance in middle-aged or elderly males. It has been reported to improve cognitive deficits and depression-like behavior in olfactory bulbectomized mice, which serve as an animal model for dementia and depression. In experiments involving mice subjected to unpredictable chronic mild stress, the plant was administered at a daily dose of 300 mg of dried herb weight/kg. This treatment attenuated symptoms of anhedoia and depression-like behavior, as well as an elevation of serum corticosterone levels. Furthermore, the administration of Butea superba reversed neurochemical and histological abnormalities, including the UCMS-induced decrease in expression levels of BDNF mRNA, CREB, and a phosphorylated form of NR1.

The powdered version is often incorporated into dietary supplements or used in traditional decoctions. According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", the red Butea superba plant extract was evaluated for its anti-proliferation effect on the growth of MCF-7 cells after 4 days of incubation. This plant led to no proliferation and demonstrated an anti-proliferation effect at concentrations of 10, 100, and 1000 microg/mL. The ED50 value for Butea superba was calculated to be 370.91 microg/mL. The results indicated that Butea superba exhibited only anti-proliferation effects on the growth of MCF-7 cells, potentially due to a possible anti-estrogen mechanism or a potent cytotoxic effect.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can induce significant androgenic effects and cause interference with ecdysis in various biological organisms. When consumed, the bioactive compounds in Butea superba may stimulate the production of male hormones, potentially leading to hormonal imbalances. In certain insect species, these chemicals act as endocrine disruptors that hinder the natural process of molting. This disruption of the molting cycle can result in developmental failures or death for the affected organisms. Such physiological changes highlight the potent influence this botanical species exerts on endocrine systems.
