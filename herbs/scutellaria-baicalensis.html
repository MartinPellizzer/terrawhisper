Baikal skullcap

Scutellaria baicalensis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1397 studies) · ★★★★★

Baikal skullcap, scientifically known as Scutelariia baicalensis, is a perennial herb native to East Asia. Renowned in traditional medicine, it contains bioactive flavonoids like baicalin. These compounds offer potent anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and neuroprotective properties, making it a significant subject of modern pharmacological research for treating various inflammatory conditions.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a notable geographical presence across diverse regions of the Eurasian continent. In Middle Europe, it can be found growing within the borders of Germany. Its distribution extends significantly into the vast territories of Siberia. Specifically, it is located in the regions of Buryatiya, Chita, and Irkutsk. Additionally, the plant's range reaches even further north into Yakutiya.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Scutellaria baicalensis, is a perennial herb where various components offer significant therapeutic value. The most pharmacologically active part is the roots, which are harvested and dried to extract baicalin. While the singular root is often the focus of medicinal preparation, the entire underground system contributes to the plant's vitality. Above the ground, the stem supports the structure of the herb, leading to the development of the leaf, which is typically lanceolate in shape. Ultimately, the aerial parts, encompassing the stems, leaves, and flowers, represent the visible growth of the plant above the soil surface.
