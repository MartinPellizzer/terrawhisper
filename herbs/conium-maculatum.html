Conium maculatum

Hemlock

Conium maculatum, commonly known as poison hemlock, is a highly toxic perennial herb belonging to the Apiaceae family. Often mistaken for edible parsley or carrots, this dangerous plant contains potent alkaloids like coniine. While historically significant in toxicology, it serves primarily as a lethal poison rather than a medicine.

  • Scientific name: Conium maculatum
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Conium maculatum, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Apiales and the family Apiaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Conium.

This plant, known as hemlock, exhibits a widespread presence across several regions of Northern Europe. It is commonly found growing throughout the coastal and inland areas of Denmark. In the northernmost reaches of the continent, the species is also established in Finland. Its distribution continues westward, where it is frequently documented across Great Britain.
