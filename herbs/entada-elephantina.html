Entada elephantina

Entada elephantina, commonly known as the elephant bean, is a robust woody climber native to tropical African forests. Renowned for its massive, woody pods, this medicinal plant is traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat various ailments, including skin infections and inflammation, offering significant pharmacological potential for modern research.

  • Scientific name: Entada elephantina
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified within the order Fabales and the family Fabaceae, specifically placed in the genus Entada. As a member of the Entada genus, Entada elephantina represents a specialized lineage of medicinal legumes.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several distinct regions within the southern portion of the continent. In South Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in both Mozambique and Zimbabwe. Moving toward the southern reaches, it can be found throughout Botswana. The species also extends into the Cape Provinces of Southern Africa. Finally, its geographical range includes the mountainous terrain of Lesotho.
