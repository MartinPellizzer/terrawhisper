Lagopsis supina

What's the taxonomical classification of Lagopsis supina?

Lagopsis supina belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further categorized under the order Lamiales. It falls within the family Lamiaceae, specifically nested in the genus Lagopsis, with the specific epithet supina identifying the species.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Lagopsis supina has a prostrate, creeping growth habit characterized by slender, spreading stems that form dense mats along the ground. The leaves are small, typically elliptic or lanceolate in shape, and are arranged alternately along the stems. These leaves feature entire margins and a somewhat fleshy texture that aids in moisture retention. The plant produces small, inconspicuous flowers that emerge from the axils of the leaves. Its root system is shallow but efficient at anchoring the sprawling stems to the substrate.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Lagopsis supina has been used as a traditional medicinal herb for centuries in China. In folk medicine, the plant is utilized for diuretic activities, anti-inflammatory activities, and for promoting blood circulation and removing blood stasis. Modern pharmacological investigations have demonstrated that Lagopsis supina possesses antioxidative, myocardioprotective, and improvements in blood and lymphatic microcirculation. In a study using 30 male Sprague-Dawley rats, an ethanol extract of Lagopsis supina was administered orally at 460 mg/kg once daily for 7 successive days. This treatment resulted in a significant increase in total urine output (p < 0.01) and a significant decrease in the expression levels of AQP-1, 2, and 3 proteins. These findings embody the traditional Chinese medicine application principle of Huo xue li shui.
