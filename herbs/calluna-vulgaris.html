Reproduction

The reproduction of Calluna vulgaris is primarily sexual, utilizing bisexual flowers to facilitate genetic exchange. The flowering period typically begins in July and extends through September. The pollination process is a biotic mechanism, specifically driven by insects, with bees acting as key pollinators. In terms of reproductive success, self-fertilization is absent, emphasizing the importance of cross-pollination. Following fertilization, the plant produces dry, dehiscent capsules as its fruit type. These capsules release a high volume of seeds, with each fruit containing more than 1,000 seeds. The individual seeds are characterized by a mean mass of approximately $2.93 \times 10^{-5}$ g (ranging from $1 \times 10^{-5}$ g to $5 \times 10^{-5}$ g) and a consistent volume of $0.027 \text{ mm}^3$. Dispersal is an anemochorous process, meaning the seeds are distributed by the wind. There is no recorded evidence of asexual reproduction for this species.
