Ferula moschata

Ferula moschata, commonly known as musk ferula, is a perennial plant highly valued in traditional medicine. Renowned for its aromatic resin, it possesses significant antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties. Often used in Ayurvedic and Middle Eastern practices, this potent botanical offers therapeutic potential for various complex health conditions.

  • Scientific name: Ferula moschata
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Apiales, placing it in the family Apiaceae. Ultimately, it is identified by the genus Ferula and the specific species Ferula moschata.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution centered primarily within the regions of Central Asia. It can be found growing naturally across the mountainous landscapes of Kyrgyzstan. Its habitat also extends into the territories of Tajikistan, where it is part of the local flora. Furthermore, the species is documented within the borders of Uzbekistan. Finally, its range stretches eastward into the Xinjiang region of China.
