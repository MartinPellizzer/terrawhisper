Eryngium triquetrum subsp. triquetrum

Eryngium triquetrum subsp. triquetrum, commonly known as Sea Holly, is a resilient perennial native to coastal regions. Renowned for its striking, spiny blue bracts, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Historically used in traditional medicine, its bioactive compounds offer potential therapeutic benefits, particularly for treating inflammatory and skin conditions.

  • Scientific name: Eryngium triquetrum subsp. triquetrum
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Apiales and the family Apiaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Eryngium.

Common Names

This plant, Eryngium triquetrum subsp. triquetrum, is known by various names depending on the region and language. In English, it is frequently referred to as the sea eryngo or sea holly. In French, it is commonly called eryngo marin, while in Spanish, it is known as eryngo marino.
