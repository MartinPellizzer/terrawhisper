Black-cumin

Nigella sativa · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1887 studies) · ★★★★★

Black-cumin, scientifically known as Nigella sativa, is a versatile medicinal plant celebrated for its profound therapeutic properties. Often referred to as "black seed," this nutrient-dense spice contains thymoquinone, a powerful compound known for its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial effects, making it a cornerstone in traditional and modern wellness.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions of the European continent. In Middle Europe, its presence is noted within the territories of Germany as well as the Czechia-Slovakia region. Moving toward the Mediterranean, it can be found growing throughout Southwestern Europe, specifically in France. The species also extends into Southeastern Europe, where it is established in Bulgaria. Finally, its range continues further south into the coastal and inland areas of Greece.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as black cumin, is highly valued for its therapeutic properties derived from various components. The most significant part used in traditional medicine is the seeds, which are small and dark in color. Each individual seed contains a concentrated amount of bioactive compounds. When these Seeds are pressed, they yield a potent oil known for its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects. Consequently, the extracted seed oil is frequently used in topical applications and dietary supplements to promote overall wellness.
