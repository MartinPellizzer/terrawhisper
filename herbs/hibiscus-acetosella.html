Hibiscus acetosella

Hibiscus acetosella, commonly known as False Roselle, is a versatile medicinal herb prized for its distinctively tart, acidic flavor. Rich in vitamins and antioxidants, this plant is traditionally used to support digestive health and reduce inflammation. Its vibrant leaves and potent properties make it a valuable botanical resource.

  • Scientific name: Hibiscus acetosella
  • Family: Malvaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Malvales and the family Malvaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Hibiscus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse distribution across several regions within the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in Benin, Guinea, and the Ivory Coast. Moving into West-Central Tropical Africa, the species can also be found in Burundi. Furthermore, its range extends into Cameroon, which is part of the West-Central region. Together, these locations highlight the plant's presence in both West and West-Central tropical ecosystems.

Plant Parts

This plant, known for its distinctively shaped foliage, primarily utilizes its young leaves for medicinal purposes. These tender, reddish-tinted young leaves are highly valued in traditional herbal practices due to their concentrated nutrient profile and specific chemical properties. When harvested early in their development, they are often consumed to provide a tart, acidic flavor that is characteristic of the species, serving both as a culinary additive and a therapeutic agent.
