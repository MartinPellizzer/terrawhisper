Cunila spicata, scientifically known as Cunila spicata, is a fragrant, aromatic herb belonging to the Lamiaceae family. Commonly called mountain mint, this resilient perennial thrives in open woodlands. Renowned for its essential oils, it offers significant medicinal potential, particularly for its antimicrobial, antioxidant, and soothing therapeutic properties.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed throughout the southern regions of South America. It can be found growing in the northeast of Argentina and across the territory of Paraguay. Furthermore, its presence extends into the country of Uruguay. Within the continent, it is also specifically documented in the southern part of Brazil. These regions collectively form the core geographical range for Cunila spicata.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as tansy, utilizes its aromatic leaves as the primary component for medicinal and culinary applications. These leaves are characterized by their deeply serrated edges and a distinct, pungent scent that is released when crushed. In traditional herbalism, the essential oils contained within the leaf tissue are often the focus of preparations used to stimulate digestion or act as an insect repellent.
