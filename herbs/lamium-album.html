White dead-nettle, scientifically known as Lamium album, is a versatile perennial herb found across Europe and Asia. Characterized by its square stems and white, hooded flowers, it belongs to the mint family. Traditionally used in herbal medicine, it offers various therapeutic properties, supporting skin health and digestive wellness.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as white dead-nettle, is characterized by its distinctive morphological features used in medicinal preparations. The aerial parts of the specimen are typically collected for their therapeutic properties, encompassing the stems and leaves. Specifically, the flowering tops are highly valued, as they contain a high concentration of active compounds. The flowers themselves are small, white, and bilabiate, arranged in whorls along the upper nodes of the stem. When harvesting for herbal purposes, the entire upper portion of the plant, including the flower and the surrounding foliage, is often gathered to ensure maximum potency.
