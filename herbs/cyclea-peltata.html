Cyclea peltata

Abuta amara

Cyclea peltata, commonly known as the "climbing vine" or "heartleaf," is a versatile medicinal plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its bioactive compounds, it is traditionally used in folk medicine to treat skin inflammations, wounds, and digestive issues, offering significant therapeutic potential for modern pharmacological research and development.

  • Scientific name: Cyclea peltata
  • Family: Menispermaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Ranunculales, it is classified under the family Menispermaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Cyclea, specifically as the species Cyclea peltata.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several regions of South and Southeast Asia. Within the Indian Subcontinent, it can be found growing in India, Bangladesh, and Sri Lanka. Specifically, its presence is also noted in the state of Assam. Furthermore, the species extends its range into the Indo-China region, particularly within the Andaman Islands. Together, these locations highlight the diverse habitats where this medicinal plant thrives.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Cyclea peltata, is a climbing vine that is highly valued in traditional medicine, particularly for its bioactive properties. The leaf serves as the most significant anatomical component used in therapeutic applications, characterized by its distinct peltate shape. Most medicinal preparations are derived from the leaf extract, which is processed to isolate various phytochemicals believed to possess antioxidant and anti-inflammatory qualities.
