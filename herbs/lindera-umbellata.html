Lindera umbellata

What's the taxonomical classification of Lindera umbellata?

Lindra umbellata belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is situated in the order Laurales. It is further categorized under the family Lauraceae, which places it within the genus Lindra, eventually reaching its specific designation as the species umbellata.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Lindera umbellata has deciduous, finely textured foliage consisting of small, lanceolate leaflets arranged in an alternate pattern along slender woody stems. The leaves typically feature serrated margins and transition to vibrant shades of red or orange during the autumn season. In early spring, the plant produces small, inconspicuous yellowish flowers that emerge before the foliage fully develops. The stems possess a smooth bark that becomes slightly more textured as the shrub matures into its woody structure. These botanical characteristics define its elegant, airy appearance within woodland environments.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the southwestern part of Western Australia, specifically occurring within the kwongan heathlands of the Geraldton Sandplains and Swan Coastal Plain bioregions. It thrives in nutrient-poor, sandy soils characteristic of these mediterranean-type ecosystems. The species is primarily found in coastal and near-coastal shrublands where seasonal rainfall is moderate. Its distribution is relatively restricted to these specific ecological niches within its home state. This localized presence makes it a distinct component of the regional biodiversity.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, commonly known as Umbellata or Umbrella Plant, thrives when grown in well-draining, sandy soil under bright, indirect sunlight and maintained with consistent moisture without waterlogging.

According to a study published by "The Journal of organic chemistry", the bark of Lindera umbellata contains a tricyclic hexahydrodibenzofuran constituent known as (+/-)-Linderol A. This specific constituent from the plant bark exhibits potent inhibitory activity on the melanin biosynthesis of cultured B-16 melanoma cells. The total synthesis of this component was achieved through 19 steps of reaction. This chemical process resulted in a 6.6% overall yield.
