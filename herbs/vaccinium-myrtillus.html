Reproduction

The reproduction of Vaccinium myrtillus is primarily a sexual process facilitated by bisexual flowers, with asexual reproduction being absent. The flowering period typically commences in April and extends through June. The plant relies on biotic pollination syndromes, specifically utilizing insects, with bees being a key pollinator. Notably, self-fertilization is absent, highlighting the importance of cross-pollination. Following successful fertilization, the plant develops fleshy, indehiscent fruit classified as a berry. These berries typically measure between 0.6 cm and 1 cm in both length and width, with average dimensions of 0.72 cm in length and 0.735 cm in width. The dispersal syndrome is zoochorous, specifically via endozoochory, where animals consume the fruit and disperse the seeds. The seeds themselves are quite small, characterized by a mean mass of approximately 0.016478 g (ranging from 0.00026 g to 0.0004 g) and a consistent volume of 0.17 mm³.
