Conioselinum tataricum

Conioselinum tataricum, commonly known as Tataricum or Siberian Conioselinum, is a perennial herb native to East Asia. Valued in traditional medicine, it contains bioactive compounds utilized for their therapeutic potential. This plant is primarily studied for its unique phytochemical profile and its role in treating various inflammatory conditions.

  • Scientific name: Conioselinum tataricum
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. It falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Apiales, specifically residing in the family Apiaceae. Within this family, it is identified by the genus Conioselinum.

Common Names

This plant, known by several names depending on the region and specific classification, is referred to as Russekjeks in Norwegian (nn), while in Polish (pl) it is identified as both Conioselinum tataricum Hoffm. and Conioselinum vaginatum (Spreng.) Thell. In Czech (cs), the species is called šabřina tatarská, and in Swedish (sv), it is commonly known as Ryssilja.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a distinct distribution across several European regions. In Northern Europe, its presence can be documented in Norway. Moving into Middle Europe, it is found within the territories of Austria, Poland, and the Czechia-Slovakia border region. Additionally, the species extends into Southeastern Europe, specifically within Romania. Together, these locations define the primary geographical range of Conioselinum tataricum.
