, scientifically known as Dendrobium officinale, is a renowned medicinal orchid cherished in Traditional Chinese Medicine. Often called "Shihu," this epiphytic plant is prized for its restorative properties. It is widely used to nourish the lungs, clear heat, and support overall vitality, serving as a cornerstone in herbal wellness.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across various regions of Eastern Asia. In China, its presence is specifically noted in the South-Central and Southeast territories. The species also extends its range to include the islands of Japan and the Nansei-shoto archipelago. Furthermore, it can be found growing within the natural landscapes of Taiwan. Together, these locations define the primary geographical footprint of Dendrobium officinale.

Morphology

The morphology of Dendrobium officinale is characterized by its epiphytic growth habit, featuring pendulous or creeping stems that lack traditional secondary thickening, categorized as non-woody in nature. These stems are succulent, cylindrical, and often multi-jointed, serving as the primary storage organs for water and nutrients. The stems are typically green to yellowish-green, possessing a fleshy texture that allows them to thrive in humid environments. The leaves are typically distributed along the stems, appearing as lanceolate or linear shapes, often being somewhat leathery or fleshy depending on the hydration levels of the plant. The inflorescence emerges from the nodes of the stems, bearing small, delicate flowers that are typically white or pale yellow with subtle markings. The root system consists of specialized aerial roots that are adventitious, fleshy, and capable of adhering to host surfaces or substrates to facilitate moisture absorption and anchorage.
