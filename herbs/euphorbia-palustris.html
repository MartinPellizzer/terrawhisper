Marsh spurge, scientifically known as Euphorbia palustris, is a striking perennial herb cherished in traditional herbalism. Native to European wetlands, this plant features delicate, airy foliage and yellowish-green bracts. While used historically for its diverse medicinal properties, it must be handled with care due to its caustic, milky sap.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the northern regions of Europe. Its presence can be documented throughout the Scandinavian peninsula, specifically in Norway and Sweden. Furthermore, it is commonly found in the coastal and inland areas of Denmark. The species also extends its range into the northern territories of Finland. Finally, its geographical footprint includes the various landscapes of Great Britain.

Plant Parts

This plant, Euphorbia palustris, is characterized by its unique physiological structures typical of the Euphorbiaceae family, most notably the presence of a milky, white latex. This substance is contained within specialized cells throughout the stem and leaves and is released when the plant tissue is damaged or broken. While this sap serves as a natural defense mechanism against herbivores, it is also a key component of the plant's chemical profile, containing various secondary metabolites that define its medicinal and biological properties.
