velvet bean

Mucuna pruriens · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (291 studies) · ★★★★★

Velvet bean, scientifically known as Mucuna pruriens, is a tropical climbing legume renowned for its potent medicinal properties. Rich in L-dopa, it is widely used in traditional medicine to support neurological health, manage Parkinson's symptoms, and balance hormones. This versatile plant offers significant potential for modern therapeutic applications.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Fabales and the family Fabaceae. Specifically, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Mucuna, which includes the medicinal species Mucuna pruriens.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several distinct tropical regions. In the Macaronesian islands, it can be specifically found in Cape Verde. Moving to the mainland, it is widely distributed throughout West Tropical Africa. Its presence is documented in several countries, including Benin, Burkina, and Ghana. Additionally, the species extends its range into Guinea-Bissau within the same West Tropical African zone.

Plant Parts

This plant, known for its potent therapeutic properties, utilizes various components for medicinal purposes. The most widely recognized part is the seeds, often referred to as the single seed or the collective seeds, which are highly valued for their high concentration of L-dopa. Additionally, the leaves and roots are frequently studied and used in traditional practices to treat various ailments. The plant also produces distinct pods that protect the developing seeds, making the entire structure of Mucuna pruriens a subject of great interest in herbal medicine.
