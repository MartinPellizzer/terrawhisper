Aleppo oak

Quercus infectoria · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (317 studies) · ★★★★★

Aleppo oak, scientifically known as Quercus infectoria, is a deciduous tree highly valued for its medicinal properties. Renowned for its exceptionally high tannin content, the plant's galls are widely used in traditional medicine to treat inflammation, wounds, and digestive issues, serving as a potent natural astringent and antimicrobial agent.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Quercus infectoria, is highly valued in traditional medicine primarily for its unique botanical formations. The most significant parts utilized are the galls, which are abnormal growths formed on the plant due to insect activity. These specific structures are often referred to as the nut gall, a term that highlights their dense, hard texture. In medicinal preparations, the dried gall is often processed to create a concentrated gall extract, which is rich in tannins and used for its potent astringent properties. While the plant produces various botanical elements, it is these specialized fruits—the insect-induced growths—that hold the greatest therapeutic value.
