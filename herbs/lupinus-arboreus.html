Coastal bush lupine, scientifically known as Lupinus arboreus, is a resilient perennial shrub native to the Pacific coast. Renowned for its striking, upright flower spikes and nitrogen-fixing capabilities, it plays a vital role in stabilizing dunes. This hardy plant thrives in sandy soils, offering beauty and ecological benefits.

Physiology

The physiology of Lupinus arboreus is characterized by its highly efficient metabolic processes, specifically utilizing a C3 photosynthetic pathway for carbon fixation. This pathway involves the direct fixation of atmospheric carbon dioxide through the enzyme Rubisco within the mesophyll cells, a method common among many temperate woody shrubs. Furthermore, the plant exhibits a specialized symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria, typically residing within root nodules. This biological nitrogen fixation allows the plant to convert atmospheric nitrogen into a bioavailable form, such as ammonia, significantly enhancing its ability to thrive in nitrogen-limited soils and contributing to its overall vigor and ecological success.
