Persian lilac

Melia azedarach · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (781 studies) · ★★★★★

Persian lilac, scientifically known as Melia azedarach, is a versatile evergreen tree valued in traditional medicine. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, it is frequently used to treat skin conditions, parasitic infections, and inflammatory ailments. While medicinal, its seeds contain toxins, requiring careful handling and precise therapeutic application.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindales. Finally, it is a member of the family Meliaceae and falls under the genus Melia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical presence across several regions in Europe. In Southwestern Europe, it can be found growing throughout France, Portugal, and Spain. Its range also extends into Southeastern Europe, where it is established in Albania. Additionally, the species is widely distributed within the country of Italy. These locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in various Mediterranean-influenced climates.

Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as Chinaberry, possesses various components that are utilized in traditional medicine. The leaves, specifically each individual leaf, are often employed for their various bioactive properties. The bark and the roots are also significant parts of the plant, frequently used in decoctions or extracts for their therapeutic potential. Additionally, the fruits, which appear as small drupes, are a distinct feature of the species, though they must be handled with caution due to their potency.
