Persian lilac

Melia azedarach · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (781 studies) · ★★★★★

Persian lilac, scientifically known as Melia azedarach, is a versatile evergreen tree valued in traditional medicine. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, it is frequently used to treat skin conditions, parasitic infections, and inflammatory ailments. While medicinal, its seeds contain toxins, requiring careful handling and precise therapeutic application.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Melia azedarach is characterized by a specific flowering phenology and complex fruit and seed development. The plant produces violet-colored flowers arranged in panicles, with inflorescence lengths ranging from a minimum of 0.2 m to a maximum of 0.25 m. These flowers are relatively small, with widths measuring between 0.2 cm and 0.3 cm. The flowering period typically begins in April and concludes in May. Following pollination, the plant develops fleshy, brown drupe-type fruits that are indehiscent. These fruits vary in size, with a mean length of 1.6 cm and a width that ranges from 0.6 cm to a maximum of 1.5 cm (averaging 1.2 cm). The seeds contained within these fruits exhibit significant variation in mass, ranging from a minimum of 0.014 g to a maximum of 1.2089 g, with a mean seed mass of approximately 0.373185975 g. Seed dimensions include a maximum length of 3.5 mm and a maximum width of 1.6 mm, while the seed volume is recorded at a consistent 760 mm³. Dispersal of the progeny is achieved through both zoochorous and anthropochorous syndromes, utilizing animals and human activity to spread the seeds.
