Dracaena cambodiana

What's the taxonomical classification of Dracaena cambodiana?

Dracaena cambodiana belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Asparagales. Further taxonomic refinement places the plant in the family Asparagaceae, specifically within the genus Dracaena, and it is identified by the specific epithet cambodiana.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Dracaena cambodiana has slender, upright woody stems that support clusters of narrow, lanceolate leaves arranged in dense terminal rosettes. The foliage typically exhibits a deep green color with subtle longitudinal stripes or variegation depending on the specific cultivar. Each leaf is characterized by a smooth, entire margin and a tapering tip that extends into a fine point. The plant's growth habit is characterized by its slow development and the formation of a single or branched trunk over time. These structural features allow the plant to maintain a compact yet elegant architectural form in various environments.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires bright, indirect sunlight, well-draining soil, and a consistent watering schedule that allows the top inch of substrate to dry between applications.

It thrives in humid environments with temperatures ranging from 60 to 80 degrees Fahrenheit. To prevent root rot, avoid overwatering and ensure the pot has adequate drainage holes. Providing a steady supply of balanced liquid fertilizer during the growing season will encourage lush, green foliage.

Proper air circulation is also essential to maintain its health and prevent fungal issues.

This plant Dracaena cambodianan utilizes its resin, leaves, stem, and trunk for various traditional medicinal purposes. The dark red resin is frequently harvested to treat wounds and skin irritations due to its antiseptic qualities. Decoctions made from the leaves are often prepared to alleviate internal ailments or digestive issues. Additionally, the inner bark from the stem and the larger trunk provides essential components for more complex herbal remedies. These diverse plant parts allow practitioners to address a wide range of therapeutic needs.

According to a study published by "BMC plant biology", Dracaena cambodiana is the key plant source of dragon's blood, which is a well-known traditional Chinese medicine. This medicinal substance is derived from the defensive metabolites of the species, featuring flavonoids as its major bioactive components. The study identified 19 DFR genes, designated as DcDFRs, from the reference genome of the plant. These genes are distributed across 6 chromosomes and are phylogenetically classified into 4 subfamilies. One specific cluster, TDGCs-1, comprises 5 genes from DcDFR1 to DcDFR5 and is localized on chromosome 5.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the unique medicinal compound cambodianol along with the specific Loureirin derivatives Loureirin A, Loureirin B, and Loureirin D. These bioactive constituents are primarily found within the resinous substance known as dragon's blood. Researchers study these chemical profiles to understand the plant's potential pharmacological properties. The presence of these particular Loureiirins distinguishes this species from other members of its genus. Such chemical complexity makes the plant a subject of interest in natural product chemistry.

According to a study published by "Journal of Asian natural products research", the stems of Dracaena cambodiana contain a new homoisoflavonoid named cambodianol (1). This plant also contains two known flavanes, (2S)-7,3'-dihydroxy-4'-methoxy-8-methylflavane (2) and (2R)-7,4'-dihydroxy-8-methylflavane (3). The compound 1 exhibited significant cytotoxic activities against K562 and SGC-7901 cells. These activities were measured with IC50 values of 1.4 microg/ml and 2.9 microg/ml, respectively.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, drooling, skin irritation, and eye irritation if ingested or touched. When a person consumes parts of the plant, they may experience severe gastrointestinal distress including stomach cramps and digestive upset. Excessive salivation or drooling often occurs as the body attempts to react to the toxic compounds. Contact with the foliage can also lead to redness or itching on the skin. If the sap or leaves come into contact with the face, it may result in painful eye irritation.
