Vangueria infausta subsp. infausta

Vangueria infausta subsp. infausta, commonly known as the Wild Medlar, is a resilient medicinal shrub native to Southern Africa. Renowned for its therapeutic properties, various parts of the plant are traditionally utilized to treat ailments ranging from digestive issues to skin inflammation, offering significant ethnobotanical value in local healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Vangueria infausta subsp. infausta
  • Family: Rubiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified within the order Gentianaales and the family Rubiaceae. Specifically, it is identified as a member of the genus Vangueria under the subspecies name Vangueria infausta subsp. infausta.

Common Names

This plant, Vangueria infausta subsp. infausta, is known by various vernacular names depending on the region and language. In English, it is commonly referred to as the wild medlar, while in Afrikaans, it is known as wilde medel. Within local South African languages, it carries the name mofeng in Sesotho and is called mofeng in Setswana.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across several distinct regions within the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in both Rwanda and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Moving toward the eastern part of the continent, it can also be found in East Tropical Africa within Tanzania. The species extends further south into the South Tropical Africa region, specifically inhabiting Angola. Finally, its geographical range continues into Malawi, completing its spread across these diverse tropical zones.
