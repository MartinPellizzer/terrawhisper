Bloodroot

Sanguinaria canadensis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (272 studies) · ★★★★★

Bloodroot, scientifically known as Sanguinaria canadensis, is a perennial herb native to eastern North America. Renowned for its vibrant red sap, it has been used historically in traditional medicine for its potent alkaloid, sanguinarine. While valued for its antimicrobial properties, its intense biological activity requires careful, expert application.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as bloodroot, derives its primary medicinal potency from its underground structures. The most significant parts utilized for therapeutic purposes are the thick, fleshy rhizomes, which contain high concentrations of alkaloids. Whether harvested as individual rhizomes or collected from the sprawling network of rhizomes, these parts are carefully processed to extract their bioactive compounds.
