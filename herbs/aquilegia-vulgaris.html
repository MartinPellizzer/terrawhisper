Aquilegia vulgaris

What's the taxonomical classification of Aquilegia vulgaris?

Aquilegia vulgaris belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Its evolutionary path continues through the order Ranunculales and the family Ranunculaceae. Ultimately, this plant is identified by its specific genus, Aquilegia, and its species designation, vulgaris.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Aquilegia vulgaris has compound, lobed leaves that are divided into three leaflets, each further subdivided into smaller, delicate segments. The stems are typically erect and can reach heights of up to one meter in length. Its most distinctive feature is the spurred, bell-shaped flower that displays a range of colors from deep blue to violet with prominent yellow centers. These complex blossoms are held aloft on slender pedicels above the foliage. The plant's overall structure is characterized by a graceful, airy appearance due to its fine-textured leaves and pendulous blooms.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, commonly known as celandine, was historically utilized in European folk medicine as a topical treatment for skin ailments such as warts, ulcers, and various dermatological inflammations.

Traditional practitioners often applied the bright orange latex extracted directly from the stems to lesions to induce cauterization or healing. In some regional traditions, decoctions of the herb were ingested in small quantities to address digestive issues or liver complaints, though its toxicity required extreme caution. Historical herbalists also valued the plant for its supposed ability to clear vision and treat eye infections when used in diluted preparations.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe gastrointestinal distress including nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal pain. Central nervous system toxicity may manifest through dizziness, confusion, hallucinations, seizures, and convulsions. Cardiovascular complications such as tachycardia can also occur following ingestion. In extreme cases, the plant's toxins may lead to life-threatening respiratory depression.
