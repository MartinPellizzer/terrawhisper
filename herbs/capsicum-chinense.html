Physiology

The physiology of Capsicum chinense is characterized by a complex metabolic framework focused on C3 photosynthetic pathways, where carbon dioxide is fixed via the enzyme RuBisCO to produce carbohydrates essential for biomass and fruit development. A defining physiological feature is the biosynthesis of capsaicinoids, secondary metabolites produced in the placental tissue of the fruit through a specialized pathway involving the amino acids phenylalanine and valine. This metabolic process serves as a chemical defense mechanism against various herbivores and pathogens. Regarding nutrient cycling and soil interaction, the plant does not possess a symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria such as Rhizobium; therefore, it is categorized as a non-nitrogen fixer (trait_1: Nitrogen fixer; trait_2: Nitrogen_fix_1; trait_value: no; trait_units: yes, no), meaning it relies entirely on the uptake of nitrates or ammonium from the soil through its extensive root system to satisfy its nitrogen requirements for protein synthesis and enzymatic functions. Additionally, its physiological responses are highly sensitive to transpiration rates and osmotic stress, which can significantly influence the concentration of its bioactive compounds and overall reproductive success.
