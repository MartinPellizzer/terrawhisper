Capsicum chinense

Yellow lantern chili

Capsicum chinense, commonly known as habanero or Scotch Bonnet, is a potent medicinal plant celebrated for its intense heat. Rich in capsaicin, it offers powerful anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antioxidant properties. Beyond culinary spice, it is utilized in traditional medicine to stimulate circulation, alleviate pain, and support metabolic health.

  • Scientific name: Capsicum chinense
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Solanales and the family Solanaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Capsicum, which includes the species Capsicum chinense.

Common Names

This plant, Capsicum chinense, is known by various names depending on the region and language, though it is consistently referred to as 黄灯笼辣椒 in several Chinese dialects and scripts. Specifically, in Simplified Chinese used in mainland China (zh-cn) and Singapore (zh-sg), as well as in Traditional Chinese used in Taiwan (zh-hant) and Hong Kong (zh-hk), it is called 黃燈籠辣椒 or 黄灯笼辣椒. This common name, which translates to "yellow lantern chili," reflects its distinctive appearance and is used across different linguistic contexts including zh-hans and zh-hk.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning several distinct regions across the Americas and Africa. In North America, it is widely found throughout Mexico, specifically within the Central and Southeast territories. Its presence extends into Central America, where it grows in countries such as Belize and Costa Rica. Additionally, the species has established a presence in West Tropical Africa, notably in Guinea-Bissau. Together, these locations highlight the diverse environmental range inhabited by this medicinal species.
