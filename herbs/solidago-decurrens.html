Solidago decurrens

What's the taxonomical classification of Solidago decurrens?

Solidago decurrens belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales. This plant is a member of the family Asteraceae, which places it within the genus Solidago, and is specifically identified by its species name, decurrens.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Solidago decurrens has stems that are typically glabrous and characterized by leaf bases that decurrently extend down the stem, forming prominent wings. The leaves are primarily lanceolate to linear in shape and are arranged alternately along the length of the plant. During the flowering stage, it produces terminal, compact panicles of small, bright yellow flower heads. Each individual capitulum consists of several disc florets and occasionally includes ray florets. The overall structure is that of an upright perennial herb with a sturdy, branching habit.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the southeastern United States, specifically spanning from North Carolina and South Carolina through Georgia and into Alabama and Mississippi. It is frequently found within the coastal plain regions and Piedmont provinces of these states. Populations often thrive in open woodlands, savannas, and moist prairies where sunlight can penetrate the canopy. The species avoids much of the higher elevation mountainous terrain found further north and west. Its range is largely restricted to these humid, subtropical southeastern environments.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Solidago decurrens Lour. (SdL) is used to control lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced acute lung injury (ALI), a lethal respiratory disease. The plant exhibits anti-inflammatory and antibacterial activities by modulating the TLR4/NF-κB/NLRP3 signaling pathway. Through screening with UHPLC-QE-Orbitrap-MS and TCMSP databases, 19 compounds in SdL were identified, which provided 209 potential gene targets. When compared to the 1,932 gene targets of ALI obtained from OMIM, GeneCards, TTD, and DisGeNET databases, a total of 140 common gene targets were found. Specifically, SdL treatments reduced COX-2 and iNOS while suppressing LPS-induced inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-6.
