Indian-plum, scientifically known as Flacourtia indica, is a versatile tropical tree celebrated for its profound medicinal properties. Renowned in traditional practices, its leaves, bark, and fruits offer therapeutic benefits, ranging from treating digestive ailments to skin conditions. This resilient species serves as a vital natural resource in many ecosystems.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution concentrated within the tropical regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in the countries of Burundi, Congo, and Rwanda. Its presence is also well-documented within the vast territory of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Furthermore, the species extends its reach into Northeast Tropical Africa. Specifically, this particular region includes the nation of Chad as part of its natural habitat.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Indian Clause, possesses various medicinal components that are utilized in traditional practices. The bark is frequently employed for its therapeutic properties, often used in decoctions to treat ailments such as skin diseases or digestive issues. Its leaves are equally significant, frequently prepared as poultices or infusions to address inflammation and other minor ailments. The fruits, which are small and edible, can be used both as a food source and for their specific medicinal benefits in local folk medicine. Collectively, these aerial parts—comprising the bark, leaves, and fruits—serve as the primary elements harvested for their bioactive potential.
