The ecology of Mimosa caesalpiniifolia is characterized by its broad adaptability across diverse Brazilian biomes, allowing it to thrive in environments with varying moisture availability and vegetation structures. It is prominently distributed within the Caatinga (strictu sensu), where it must endure semi-arid conditions and seasonal droughts, as well as throughout the Cerrado (lato sensu), occupying various successional stages of this tropical savanna. Furthermore, its ecological range extends into the Floresta Ombrófila (also known as Floresta Pluvial), indicating a significant capacity to inhabit humid, high-precipitation rainforest environments. Physiology

The physiology of Mimosa caesalpiniifolia is characterized by a highly efficient symbiotic relationship with soil-borne bacteria, most notably within the genera Rhizobium or Bradyrhizobium, which facilitates its role as a nitrogen fixer. Through the development of specialized root nodules, the plant engages in biological nitrogen fixation, a process where atmospheric nitrogen ($N_2$) is converted into ammonia ($NH_3$), a form usable for plant protein synthesis and metabolic functions. This nitrogen fixation capability is a critical physiological adaptation that allows the species to thrive in nutrient-poor or nitrogen-deficient soils, providing a competitive advantage in diverse ecosystems. The metabolic demand for this process is supported by a steady supply of carbohydrates produced via photosynthesis, which are transported to the root nodules to fuel the energy-intensive nitrogenase enzyme activity. Consequently, the plant exhibits a positive nitrogen balance that promotes vigorous vegetative growth and efficient nutrient cycling within its immediate environment.
