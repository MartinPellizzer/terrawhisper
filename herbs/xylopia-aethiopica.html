Xylopia aethiopica

Xylopia aethiopica, commonly known as African nutmeg or Grains of Selim, is a versatile medicinal shrub native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its aromatic seeds, it is widely utilized in traditional medicine to treat respiratory ailments, digestive issues, and inflammation, offering potent antioxidant and antimicrobial therapeutic properties.

  • Scientific name: Xylopia aethiopica
  • Family: Annonaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Magnoliales, it falls into the family Annonaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Xylopia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Xylopia aethiopica, is recognized by various local and international common names across different languages and regions. In Portuguese, it is referred to as Pimenta-da-áfrica, while in Czech, it is called habzelie etiopská. Within certain West African linguistic contexts, specifically in Hausa, it is known as Kimba. The German name for this species is Mohrenpfeffer, and in Japanese, it is identified as ギニアペッパーグローブ.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across various regions of West Tropical Africa. Specifically, it can be found growing in the country of Benin. Its presence is also documented within the borders of Burkina Faso. Furthermore, the species inhabits parts of Gambia and Ghana. Finally, the geographical range extends to the nation of Guinea-Bissau.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Niger coast nutmeg, utilizes various components for its medicinal properties. The fruits are often harvested for their aromatic qualities, and within each fruit, one can find the potent seeds. These seeds, or the individual seed found inside, are frequently used in traditional remedies due to their concentrated bioactive compounds. Additionally, the stem bark is highly valued in herbal medicine, offering a different set of therapeutic benefits compared to the flavorful fruits and their contents.
