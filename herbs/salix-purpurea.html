Purple willow, scientifically known as Salix purpurea, is a versatile shrub prized for both its ornamental beauty and medicinal properties. Historically, its bark has been utilized to treat inflammation and pain, containing salicin—the precursor to aspirin. This resilient plant remains a vital subject in both traditional and modern herbalism.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the purple willow, is a species where the most significant medicinal component is found within its bark. The bark of Salix purpurea contains salicin, a chemical compound that serves as a natural precursor to salicylic acid, which has been used historically for its analgesic and antipyretic properties. When harvested, this bark is typically dried and processed to create decoctions or extracts intended to help alleviate pain and reduce inflammation.
