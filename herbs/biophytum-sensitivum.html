Biophytum sensitivum

What's the taxonomical classification of Biophytum sensitivum?

Biophytum sensitivum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Oxalidales. This plant is further categorized into the family Oxalidaceae, within the genus Biophytum, and is specifically identified by the species name sensitivum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Biophytum sensitivum has a creeping rhizomatous stem, large heart-shaped leaves that exhibit rapid nyctinastic movement, and solitary flowers with five petals. The leaves are characterized by their sensitive nature, folding inward quickly when touched or during the night to protect themselves. These leaves emerge from a thick, fleshy rootstock that allows the plant to spread across the forest floor. The flowers are typically pale yellow or white and appear on long stalks rising above the foliage. Small, fleshy fruits develop from the flowers after successful pollination.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-drained, humus-rich soil and consistent moisture to thrive in warm, humid environments with filtered sunlight.

Because it is a tropical species, it is sensitive to frost and should be grown in containers or tropical greenhouses to avoid cold damage. Frequent watering is essential to prevent the delicate leaves from wilting, though the soil must not remain waterlogged to avoid root rot. Providing high humidity through misting or a pebble tray will mimic its natural habitat and encourage vigorous growth.

Once established, the plant can be propagated easily through division or by collecting seeds from mature specimens.

According to a study published by "Phytotherapy research : PTR", the medicinal antiinflamatory activity of Biophytum sensitivum was evaluated using aqueous and methanol extracts of aerial parts, an aqueous extract of roots, and ultrafiltration fractions of a methanol extract of roots. These studies were conducted using the carrageenin induced rat paw oedema model.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant Biophytum sensitivum is utilized in various traditional medicinal practices across Asia, including traditional Nepalese folk medicine, to treat diverse ailments. In many regional systems, the succulent leaves and roots are prepared as poultices or decoctions to address skin inflammations and wounds. Practitioners often employ the plant for its purported ability to reduce swelling and alleviate localized pain. Beyond topical applications, some cultures have historically used preparations of the plant to manage digestive issues or fever. These long-standing customs rely on the plant's unique physiological properties to provide relief for common physical discomforts.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Biophytum sensitivum is an annual herb used in traditional Nepalese folk medicine for the treatment of hyperglycemic patients. An optimum dose of 200 mg/kg body weight was identified for hypoglycemia. When a single administration of this dose was given to 16-h fasted non-diabetic rabbits, it caused a 16.1% fall in fasting plasma glucose at the end of 1 and 2 h. This hypoglycemic effect persisted at the end of 6 h with a 13.8% fall. Additionally, the extract attenuated the plasma glucose response to an oral glucose load of 3 g/kg body weight. Following this administration, serum insulin levels showed a rise of 13.7% at the end of 2 h and a 12.6% rise at the end of 6 h.

What health conditions is this plant used for?

This plant is used for treating Dalton's lymphoma ascites (DLA), Ehrlich ascites carcinoma (EAC), Ehrlich ascites tumor, solid tumor, and various other types of tumors. Researchers have investigated its chemical compounds for their potential to inhibit the growth of these specific malignant cells. Studies often focus on its ability to reduce tumor volume and suppress the progression of both liquid and solid cancerous growths. By targeting these specific conditions, the plant serves as a significant subject in pharmacological studies regarding anti-tumor activity.

According to a study published by "Asian Pacific journal of cancer prevention : APJCP", Biophytum sensitivum extract showed 100% toxicity at a concentration of 0.5 mg/ml against Dalton's lymphoma ascites (DLA) and Ehrlich ascites carcinoma (EAC) cells. The extract was also cytotoxic to L929 cells at a concentration of 0.1 mg/ml. Administration of 500 microg/dose/animal inhibited solid tumor development in mice with DLA cells and increased the lifespan of mice with EAC tumors by 93.3%. In ascites tumor bearing animals, the extract significantly (p<0.001) reduced tumor cell glutathione (GSH), serum gamma glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT), and nitric oxide (NO) levels. Furthermore, the total WBC count increased to 14,087 cells/mm(3) on the 12th day in BALB/c mice, while the number of plaque forming cells also enhanced significantly (p<0.001).
