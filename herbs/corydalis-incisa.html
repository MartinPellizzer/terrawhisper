Corydalis incisa

Corydalis incisa, commonly known as the Japanese corydalis, is a perennial herb native to East Asia. Renowned for its delicate, creeping foliage and vibrant spring blooms, this medicinal plant contains bioactive alkaloids. It is traditionally utilized in herbal medicine to soothe pain and support various neurological and digestive functions.

  • Scientific name: Corydalis incisa
  • Family: Papaveraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Ranunculales, it is classified under the family Papaveraceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic designation is within the genus Corydalis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Corydalis incisa, is referred to by various common names depending on the region and language. In Japan, it is called ムラサキケマン (ja), while in Korea, it is known as 자주괴불주머니 (ko). The Sorbian name for this species is Cuzy kokorč (hsb). Furthermore, in Chinese-speaking regions, it is identified as 刻叶紫堇 (zh) in simplified characters and 刻葉紫菫 (zh-tw) in traditional characters.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several key regions within Eastern Asia. In China, its presence is noted throughout the South-Central, North-Central, and Southeast territories. Beyond the Chinese mainland, its range extends into the island nation of Japan. Additionally, the species can be found growing naturally across the Korean Peninsula. These diverse locations highlight the plant's significant footprint throughout the eastern part of the continent.
