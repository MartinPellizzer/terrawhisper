The physiology of Tamarix gallica is characterized by specialized adaptations for survival in arid and saline environments, primarily driven by its efficient water management systems. A fundamental aspect of its metabolic process is its photosynthetic pathway, which is classified as C3. Despite being a C3 plant, which typically faces challenges with photorespiration in high-temperature environments, Tamarix gallica compensates through advanced xerophytic mechanisms. It utilizes specialized salt glands located on its scale-like leaves to actively excrete excess sodium and chloride ions, a process that prevents toxic accumulation in the photosynthetic tissues and maintains osmotic balance. This salt excretion often results in a visible crystalline crust on the foliage, which can also serve to increase leaf reflectance, thereby reducing the thermal load on the plant.
