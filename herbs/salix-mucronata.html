This metabolic process is typical of woody dicotyledonous shrubs within the Salicaceae family, relying on the stomatal uptake of CO2 for biomass production. In terms of nutrient cycling and symbiotic associations, the species does not function as a nitrogen fixer, meaning it lacks the specialized root nodules or symbiotic relationships with diazotrophic bacteria required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms. Consequently, its nutritional requirements and growth patterns are heavily dependent on the availability of nitrogenous compounds, such as nitrates and ammonium, directly present within the soil matrix.
