Loquat, scientifically known as Eriobotrya japonica, is a versatile evergreen tree prized for its medicinal and nutritional properties. Native to East Asia, its fleshy fruits and antioxidant-rich leaves are used traditionally to soothe coughs, reduce inflammation, and support respiratory health, making it a cornerstone of holistic herbal remedies.

Distribution

This plant, known scientifically as Eriobotrya japonica, exhibits a specific distribution across several regions of Southwestern Europe. It can be found growing within the Balearic Islands, which are part of the Baleares archipelago. Its presence is also noted in the French territories and along the coastlines of Portugal. Additionally, this species inhabits the island of Corse. Finally, the plant is documented to grow in the Mediterranean region of Sardegna.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the loquat, utilizes various components for its medicinal and nutritional purposes. The large, serrated leaves are frequently used in traditional remedies to treat respiratory ailments, often prepared as a decoction from a single dried leaf. During the spring, the plant produces fragrant white flowers that attract pollinators and eventually develop into fruit. Within these fruits, the hard seeds contain compounds that are studied for their bioactive properties, though the seed itself must be handled with care due to its potency.
