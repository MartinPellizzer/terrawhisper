Duck

Aristolochia littoralis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (5 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Duck, scientifically known as Aristolochia littoralis, is a captivating climbing perennial native to tropical regions. Renowned for its unique, heart-shaped leaves and distinctive tubular flowers, this plant holds significant historical interest in traditional medicine. However, users must exercise extreme caution due to the presence of potent nephrotoxic compounds.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of habitats across several distinct regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence can be noted within the borders of Gambia. Moving into West-Central Tropical Africa, the species is documented in Burundi and the Central African Republic. It also extends its reach to the islands located within the Gulf of Guinea. Finally, the distribution continues into Northeast Tropical Africa, specifically within the country of Chad.

Plant Parts

This plant, Aristolochia littoralis, is characterized by its distinct morphological features, most notably its large, heart-shaped leaves. These leaves are typically arranged alternately along the stem and serve as the primary site for photosynthesis, providing the energy necessary for the plant's growth and the development of its unique, tubular flowers. In the context of medicinal botany, the chemical compounds found within the leaf tissue are often studied, though it is important to note that many species in this genus contain aristolochic acid, which requires careful scientific consideration.
