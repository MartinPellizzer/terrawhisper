Aristolochia littoralis

Aristolochia littoralis, commonly known as the coastal birthwort, is a striking perennial climber native to tropical regions. Characterized by heart-shaped leaves and unique, tube-like flowers, this plant holds significant botanical interest. While historically used in traditional medicine, modern science cautions against its consumption due to potential nephrotoxic properties.

  • Scientific name: Aristolochia littoralis
  • Family: Aristolochiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Piperales, it is situated in the family Aristolochiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Aristolochia, specifically as the species Aristolochia littoralis.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of habitats across several distinct regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence can be noted within the borders of Gambia. Moving into West-Central Tropical Africa, the species is documented in Burundi and the Central African Republic. It also extends its reach to the islands located within the Gulf of Guinea. Finally, the distribution continues into Northeast Tropical Africa, specifically within the country of Chad.
