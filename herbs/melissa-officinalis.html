Melissa officinalis

Common balm

Melissa officinalis, commonly known as lemon balm, is a perennial herb celebrated for its refreshing citrus aroma and calming properties. Belonging to the mint family, it has been used since ancient times to alleviate anxiety, improve sleep, and aid digestion, making it a staple in traditional herbal medicine today.

  • Scientific name: Melissa officinalis
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae, it is classified under the genus Melissa.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Melissa officinalis, is recognized by a variety of common names across different languages. In Poland, it is called melisa lekarska, while in France, it is known as Mélisse. In Belarus, it is referred to as Меліса лекавая, and in Spain, it is called Toronjil. Additionally, in Hungary, this fragrant herb is frequently called citromfű.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across several regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence is well-documented in Denmark and Sweden. It is also frequently found throughout the islands of Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward Central Europe, the species can be located within the borders of Austria. Together, these locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in diverse temperate environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as lemon balm, is primarily valued for its aromatic properties found throughout its various structures. The most therapeutically active components are the leaves and the overall aerial parts, which are frequently harvested for medicinal use. Within these structures, the plant produces a potent essential oil that carries its signature citrus scent. This oil is extracted to create various remedies, often utilized for its calming effects. While the stems provide the necessary structural support for the plant's growth, the concentration of aromatic compounds remains highest in the foliage.
