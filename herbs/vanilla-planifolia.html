Vanilla planifolia

Flat leaved vanilla

Vanilla planifolia, commonly known as vanilla, is a fragrant orchid native to Mexico. Renowned for producing the world's most coveted spice, its cured beans offer a complex, sweet aroma. Beyond culinary uses, this medicinal plant possesses antioxidant and antimicrobial properties, making it a valuable component in traditional wellness practices.

  • Scientific name: Vanilla planifolia
  • Family: Orchidaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Vanilla planifolia, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Asparagales. Ultimately, it is a member of the Orchidaceae family within the genus Vanilla.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Vanilla planifolia, is recognized by various common names across different linguistic traditions. In Czech, the name used is vanilovník plocholistý (cs), and in Croatian, it is known as Prawa wanilija (hsb). Additionally, in Haitian Creole, the plant is called Vaniy (ht).

Its presence extends into the Western Indian Ocean, specifically reaching the Comoros. Additionally, the species is located in the Mauritius island region. Plant Parts

This plant, Vanilla planifolia, is a climbing orchid characterized by its lush, succulent green leaves. Each individual leaf is lanceolate in shape and grows along a long, trailing vine, contributing to the dense foliage of the climbing plant. While the most economically significant part of the plant is the seed pod, the leaves play a vital role in the plant's overall health and photosynthetic process.
