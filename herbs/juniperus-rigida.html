Temple juniper, scientifically known as Juniperus rigidica, is a resilient coniferous species native to high-altitude regions. Renowned for its robust structure and aromatic foliage, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. It is frequently utilized in traditional medicine for its antiseptic properties and its ability to support respiratory health.

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range across several distinct regions of East Asia. In the Russian Far East, its presence is specifically noted in the areas of Primorye and Sakhalin. Moving into China, the species can be found within the territories of Inner Mongolia and Manchuria. Additionally, the distribution extends into the China North-Central region. Plant Parts

This plant, Juniperus rigida, possesses several distinct morphological features that are utilized in various medicinal and practical applications. Its structure is characterized by resilient twigs that provide a sturdy framework for its growth. The foliage consists of sharp, pointed needles that are often used to describe its texture, though in botanical terms, these needles function as the plant's primary leaves. Finally, the plant produces small, fleshy fruits, often referred to as cones, which contain the essential oils and bioactive compounds central to its medicinal properties.
