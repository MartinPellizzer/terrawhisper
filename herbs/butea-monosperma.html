This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales, it is further categorized into the family Fabaceae. Finally, it is placed within the specific genus Butea.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range across parts of Asia. In China, it can be found specifically throughout the South-Central and Southeast regions. Its distribution extends into the Indian Subcontinent, where it is native to Assam. Furthermore, the species is well-documented within the borders of Bangladesh. Finally, it also thrives in the rugged terrains of the East Himalaya.

Plant Parts

This plant, Butea monospermum, commonly known as Palash or Flame of the Forest, possesses various parts that are utilized for their medicinal properties. The leaves are often employed in traditional remedies to treat skin diseases and fever, while the stems and the stem bark are frequently used in decoctions to address ailments such as inflammation and digestive issues. The seeds are highly significant in Ayurvedic practice, often used to treat various parasitic infections. Furthermore, the seed oil, extracted from the harvested seeds, is valued for its topical applications and its role in managing specific dermatological conditions.
