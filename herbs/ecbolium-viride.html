Green shrimp plant, scientifically known as Ecbolium viride, is a striking tropical perennial celebrated for its unique, shrimp-like blossoms. Beyond its ornamental beauty, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical importance, traditionally utilized in various cultures for its diverse medicinal properties, particularly in treating inflammatory conditions and supporting overall wellness.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found throughout the Northeast Tropical Africa region. Its natural distribution includes the territories of Djibouti and Eritrea. Furthermore, it can be located across the vast landscapes of Ethiopia and Somalia. The species also extends its reach into the areas of both Sudan and South Sudan. Consequently, its geographical range is concentrated within these specific African nations.

Plant Parts

This plant, Eclipta prostrata (often referred to in similar contexts as Eclipta viride), utilizes its leaf as a primary component in traditional medicinal practices. The green, lanceolate leaf is frequently crushed or processed into a paste to treat various skin ailments, hair loss, and inflammatory conditions. Because the chemical constituents are concentrated within the foliage, the leaf serves as the most vital part of the plant for topical applications and herbal decoctions.
