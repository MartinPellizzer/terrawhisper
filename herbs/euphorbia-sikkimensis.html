Euphorbia sikkimensis

Euphorbia sikkimensis, commonly known as the Sikkim Spurge, is a medicinal plant native to the Himalayan regions. Renowned in traditional medicine, it possesses significant therapeutic potential due to its bioactive compounds. This species is frequently studied for its anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties, offering valuable insights into natural pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Euphorbia sikkimensis
  • Family: Euphorbiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, it is classified under the family Euphorbiaceae and the genus Euphorbia. Specifically, its taxonomic identity is established as Euphorbia sikkimensis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Euphorbia sikkimensis, is recognized by several distinct common names across different linguistic contexts. In Chinese, it is most frequently referred to as 黄苞大戟 (including the simplified forms 黄苞大戟 and 黄苞大戟 used in zh-hans and zh-cn), as well as the more poetic names 五虎下西川 and 水黄花.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning across several distinct regions. In Europe, its presence is noted within Great Britain. Its distribution extends significantly into Asia, where it can be found in South-Central and Southeast China. Additionally, the species is documented inhabiting the high-altitude terrains of Tibet. Finally, it is also widely distributed throughout the East Himalaya region of the Indian Subcontinent.

Plant Parts

This plant, Euphorbia sikkimensis, is primarily utilized in traditional medicinal practices through the use of its aerial part. This includes the entire structure visible above the ground, such as the succulent stems, leaves, and inflorescences. These above-ground components contain the bioactive compounds and milky latex characteristic of the Euphorbia genus, which are harvested to prepare various therapeutic formulations.
