Cerbera manghas

Cerbera manghas, commonly known as the sea mango, is a coastal evergreen tree native to tropical regions. While its striking flowers and fruits are aesthetically pleasing, the plant is highly toxic, containing potent cardiac glycosides. In traditional medicine, it is used cautiously, though its extreme toxicity requires careful handling.

  • Scientific name: Cerbera manghas
  • Family: Apocynaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Gentianales, falling into the family Apocynaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Cerbera.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across several key regions of the tropical belt. In East Tropical Africa, its distribution can be traced to the nation of Tanzania. It is also extensively found throughout the Western Indian Ocean, including the islands of Madagascar and Mauritius. Furthermore, its range extends to the Comoros and the remote Chagos Archipelago. These various locations highlight the plant's specific ecological niche within the Indian Ocean maritime environment.
