Lotus pedunculatus

Lotus uliginosus

Lotus pedunculatus, commonly known as the yellow water-lily, is a resilient aquatic perennial found in wetlands across Eurasia. Renowned for its vibrant yellow blooms, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditionally, various parts have been utilized in folk medicine to treat ailments ranging from skin inflammation to digestive issues.

  • Scientific name: Lotus pedunculatus
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae within the order Fabales. Furthermore, it is a member of the family Fabaceae and falls under the genus Lotus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Lotus pedunculatus, is recognized by various regional names across different languages. In Poland, it is called Komonica błotna, while in Estonia, it is referred to as Soo-nõiahammas. German speakers call it Sumpf-Hornklee, and in the Czech Republic, it is known as štírovník bažiinný. Additionally, in Lithuania, the plant is commonly called Pelkinis garždeniš.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution across several regions within Northern Europe. It can be found growing in the coastal and wetland areas of Denmark. Its presence extends further north into the territories of Finland and the Faroe Islands. Additionally, the species is well-documented throughout the landscape of Great Britain. Finally, its geographical range includes the island of Ireland.
