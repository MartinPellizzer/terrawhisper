Lotus uliginosus, scientifically known as Lotus pedunculatus, is a resilient perennial herb native to wetland habitats. Characterized by its distinctive trifoliate leaves and yellow flowers, this medicinal plant has long been utilized in traditional practices for its diverse therapeutic properties, offering significant potential in modern botanical pharmacology and wellness.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Lotus pedunculatus is primarily sexual, characterized by bisexual flowers that lack the capacity for self-fertilization. The flowering period typically begins in June and concludes in July, featuring yellow flowers that measure between 1 cm and 1.5 cm in length. These flowers rely on a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically utilizing insects such as bees to facilitate fertilization. Following successful pollination, the plant develops dry, brown, pod-type fruits. These pods vary in size, with a mean length of 2.5 cm (ranging from 1 cm to a maximum of 4 cm) and a mean width of 0.2 cm (ranging from 0.15 cm to a maximum of 0.25 cm). The fruits are dehiscence-based and exhibit an autochorous dispersal syndrome, meaning they disperse seeds through their own internal mechanisms. Each fruit is highly productive, containing between 101 and 1000 seeds. The seeds themselves are minute, with a consistent length of 1.1 mm and a volume of approximately 0.9 mm³. The seed mass is quite small, ranging from a minimum of 0.00048 g to a mean of 0.002544 g. Notably, asexual reproduction is absent in this species.
