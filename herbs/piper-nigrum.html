black pepper

Piper nigrum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1451 studies) · ★★★★★

Black pepper, scientifically known as Piper nigrum, is a perennial climbing vine celebrated globally as the "king of spices." Beyond its culinary warmth, this medicinal plant offers potent bioactive compounds like piperine, which possess antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and digestive properties, making it a cornerstone in traditional Ayurvedic and holistic healing.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Piperales, it is classified under the family Piperaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Piper, specifically the species Piper nigrum.

Distribution

This plant, Piper nigrum, exhibits a diverse distribution across several key tropical regions. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is noted in both Benin and Guinea. The species also extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically inhabiting Cameroon and the islands of the Gulf of Guinea. Furthermore, its range reaches into Northeast Tropical Africa within the borders of Ethiopia. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's preference for various tropical African climates.

Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as black pepper, utilizes various components for both culinary and medicinal purposes. The most widely recognized part is the dried fruit, which is harvested once the drupes reach maturity. Within these small fruits, the essential oils and piperine are concentrated, primarily located in the tiny seeds found inside. While the seed itself is the primary source of its pungent properties, the leaf is also used in some traditional practices to create infusions or topical applications.
