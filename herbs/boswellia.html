Boswellia

Boswellia, commonly known as frankincense, is a revered medicinal plant harvested from the resin of Boswellia trees. Renowned for its potent anti-inflammatory properties, it has been used for centuries in traditional medicine to soothe joints, support digestive health, and promote overall wellness through its bioactive boswellic acids.

  • Scientific name: Boswellia
  • Family: Burseraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Sapindales, it is classified under the family Burseraceae. Finally, it is categorized under the specific genus Boswellia.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as frankincense, is highly valued for the various exudates it produces when the bark of its trunk is wounded. The primary medicinal substance harvested is the resin, which is secreted in a viscous form to protect the tree. This substance is often classified as an oleo-gum-resin, a complex mixture containing essential oils, gums, and resins. Depending on the specific species and the method of collection, the harvest may be referred to as oleo-gum resin or simply gum resin, each term highlighting the multifaceted chemical composition that provides its potent anti-inflammatory properties.
