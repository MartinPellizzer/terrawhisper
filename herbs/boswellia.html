Frankincense, scientifically known as Boswellia, is a revered resin derived from several species of the Boswellia tree. Historically prized for its aromatic properties in religious rituals, modern science highlights its potent anti-inflammatory benefits. It is widely used today in traditional medicine to support joint health and manage systemic inflammation.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as frankincense, is highly valued for the various exudates it produces when the bark of its trunk is wounded. The primary medicinal substance harvested is the resin, which is secreted in a viscous form to protect the tree. This substance is often classified as an oleo-gum-resin, a complex mixture containing essential oils, gums, and resins. Depending on the specific species and the method of collection, the harvest may be referred to as oleo-gum resin or simply gum resin, each term highlighting the multifaceted chemical composition that provides its potent anti-inflammatory properties.
