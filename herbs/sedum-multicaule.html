Sedum multicaule

What's the taxonomical classification of Sedum multicaule?

Sedum multicaule belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant follows the taxonomic order Saxifragales and is a member of the family Crassulaceae. Specifically, it is situated within the genus Sedum, where it is identified as the species multicaule.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Sedum multicaule has succulent, cylindrical to slightly flattened leaves that grow in dense, overlapping rosettes along a creeping stem. These leaves are typically fleshy and vary in color from bright green to reddish hues depending on environmental stress. The plant produces small, star-shaped flowers that are usually yellow or pale pink and emerge from terminal clusters. Its stems are often prostrate or ascending, allowing it to form compact mats across rocky substrates. The overall structure is low-growing and adapted to withstand arid conditions through its water-storing foliage.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to high-altitude regions of western China, specifically within the provinces of Sichuan, Yunnan, Gansu, and Qinghai. It primarily inhabits alpine environments located on rocky slopes and mountain crevices. These plants are typically found at elevations ranging from 3,000 to 4,500 meters above sea level. The distribution is limited to these specific montane habitats where they can withstand harsh, cold climates. Its presence is closely tied to the unique topography of the eastern Tibetan Plateau.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires full sun exposure and well-draining, gritty soil to prevent root rot during its cultivation.

It thrives in rocky crevices or alpine gardens where moisture can escape quickly after rain. Because it is a succulent, you should avoid frequent watering and only hydrate the soil once it has completely dried out. This species is highly drought-tolerant and can survive harsh winters if placed in a location with proper drainage.
