Urena lobata

What's the taxonomical classification of Urena lobata?

Urena lobata belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Malvales. The plant is further categorized into the family Malvaceae, which places it within the genus Urena, and is specifically identified by its species name, lobata.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Urena lobata has an erect, perennial herb structure characterized by opposite, ovate to heart-shaped leaves that possess serrated margins and a stinging, hairy texture. The stems are often woody at the base and covered in fine, irritating trichomes that can cause skin irritation upon contact. Its inflorescence consists of dense, globose, greenish flower clusters that emerge from the leaf axils. These small, inconspicuous flowers eventually produce tiny, fleshy fruits that are typically dark in color when mature. The plant's overall morphology is adapted for rapid growth in moist, tropical environments.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Urena lobata is used medicinally primarily through its leaves, which are applied or ingested to treat various ailments. These leaves are often crushed into a paste and applied topically to soothe skin irritations or reduce swelling. In some traditional practices, decoctions made from the foliage are consumed to address internal issues like fever or digestive discomfort. The medicinal properties are concentrated within the leaf tissue, making it the most vital part for therapeutic use. This specific application highlights the plant's importance in folk medicine for managing localized inflammation.

According to a study published by "International journal of molecular sciences", the leaves of Urena lobata have been utilized as a traditional medicinal plant in India and China. An 80% ethanol extract from the leaves of Urena lobata showed effective anti-yeast activity against Saccharomyces cerevisiae strains. The isolated compound clematoside-S exhibits specific anti-yeast activity against these strains with diameter of inhibition zones in the range from 11 to 20 mm. The MIC values of clematoside-S against S. cerevisiae strains were detected to be in the range of 0.61 to 9.8 μg/mL. Additionally, the MBC values for this compound were found to be in the range of 2.42 to 9.8 μg/mL.

In the study, ethanolic extracts of this plant were administered orally at doses of 250, 500 and 750mg/kg. The treatment was applied to adult female Sprague-Dawley rats that had a drill hole injury of 0.8mm in the femur diaphysis. Following 12 days of treatment, the femur bones were collected to evaluate the effects of the plant.

What are the pharmacological activities of Urena lobata?

This plant has significant antimicrobial and anti-yeast pharmacological activities that allow it to combat various pathogenic microorganisms. Researchers have identified that its extracts can effectively inhibit the growth of several fungal species, demonstrating potent anti-yeast properties. Furthermore, the plant demonstrates broad-spectrum antimicrobial capabilities against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria. These biological effects are largely attributed to the presence of specific secondary metabolites within its chemical profile. Such medicinal properties suggest that Urena lobata could serve as a valuable natural source for developing new therapeutic agents.

According to a study published by "International journal of molecular sciences", the 80% ethanol extract from Urena lobata leaves showed effective anti-yeast activity against Saccharomyces cerevisiae strains. The plant yielded two isolated compounds, (-)-trachelogenin (1) and clematoside-S (2), through chromatographic methods. While compound 1 was inactive against all test microorganisms, compound 2 exhibited specific anti-yeast activity with inhibition zones ranging from 11 to 20 mm. The MIC values of compound 2 against S. cerevisiae strains were detected to be in the range of 0.61 to 9.8 μg/mL. Additionally, the MBC values for compound 2 were found to be between 2.42 and 9.8 μg/mL. These results suggest that Urena lobata has potential application as a natural anti-yeast agent in food preservation.

What health conditions is this plant used for?

This plant is used for reducing blood cholesterol, inhibiting cancer cell proliferation, lowering low-density lipoprotein (LDL) concentrations, decreasing urea nitrogen, and acting against yeast. According to a study published by "Veterinary world", Urena lobata L. was used as part of a medicinal plants mixture consisting of 15% of the plant. This mixture, which included the plant, was evaluated as an alternative growth promoter for forty-eight 10-week-old crossbred pigs with an average initial body weight of 30.3±1.42 kg. The study was conducted from April 2020 to June 2020 using dietary supplement levels of 0, 20, 40, and 60 g/kg of feed. The inclusion of this mixture helped decrease blood cholesterol, urea nitrogen, and low-density lipoprotein (LDL) concentrations with a significance of p<0.05. Overall, the incorporation of the plant mixture reduced serum cholesterol, LDL, and urea concentrations.
