Ashok

Monoon longifolium · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (76 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Ashok, scientifically known as Monoona longifolia, is a revered medicinal tree in Ayurveda, celebrated for its therapeutic properties. Renowned for its ability to balance hormones and support female reproductive health, it is widely used to treat menstrual irregularities, inflammation, and skin ailments, serving as a cornerstone in holistic healing.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and order Magnoliales, it is a member of the family Annonaceae. Taxonomically, it is identified under the genus Monoon as Monoon longifolium.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several key regions across Asia and Africa. In the West-Central Tropical Africa region, it can be specifically located in Cameroon. Its presence extends into China, where it is found in both the South-Central areas and on the island of Hainan. Moving toward Eastern Asia, the species is also documented in Taiwan. Finally, its range includes the Indian Subcontinent, particularly within the state of Assam.

Plant Parts

This plant, Mezoneuron longifolium (often referred to in botanical contexts similar to Monoon longifolium), utilizes various components for its medicinal properties. The most prominent parts used are the leaves, which are frequently processed into decoctions, as is each individual leaf when prepared for specific topical applications. The rugged bark is also highly valued for its bioactive compounds, while the thick Leaf structures and the woody stems contribute to the plant's overall medicinal profile in traditional practices.
