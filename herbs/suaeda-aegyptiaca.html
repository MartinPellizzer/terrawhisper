Seablite, scientifically known as Suaeda aegyptiaca, is a resilient halophyte primarily found in coastal saline environments. Renowned for its remarkable ability to thrive in harsh, salty soils, this medicinal plant offers significant therapeutic potential. It is widely studied for its unique antioxidant properties and diverse bioactive compounds.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Suaeda aegyptiaca, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Caryophyllales. Finally, it is a member of the family Amaranthaceae and the genus Suaeda.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the diverse landscapes of Northern Africa. Within this region, its presence is documented in Egypt, Libya, and Tunisia. Beyond the Mediterranean coast, its range extends into the Northeast Tropical Africa zone. Specifically, it can be found growing in the territories of Djibouti and Eritrea. Consequently, its geographical footprint covers a significant portion of the African continent.
