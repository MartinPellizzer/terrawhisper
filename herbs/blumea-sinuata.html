Cut-leaf false oxtongue, scientifically known as Blumea sinuatua, is a versatile aromatic shrub native to Southeast Asia. Renowned in traditional medicine, this plant possesses potent antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties. Often used to treat respiratory ailments and skin infections, it remains a significant subject in modern ethnopharmacological research.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across two major continents. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in the countries of Benin, Mali, and Senegal. Its presence also extends into Asia, specifically within the various regions of China. Within China, the species is documented in the South-Central areas. Additionally, the plant is known to inhabit the tropical island province of Hainan.
