Salix matsudana

What's the taxonomical classification of Salix matsudana?

Salix matsudanana belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant follows the biological order Malpighiales and is a member of the family Salicaceae. Finally, it is placed within the genus Salix, specifically identified by the species epithet matsudanana.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Salix matsudanana has lanceolate leaves that are typically 3 to 8 centimeters long, featuring finely serrated margins and a smooth, glabrous surface. The leaves are characterized by a distinctively long, tapering apex and a base that may be slightly asymmetrical. The stems of this willow species are often slender and can exhibit a yellowish-green hue during active growth periods. Small, catkin-like flowers emerge from the branches, appearing as cylindrical clusters before the foliage fully develops. This morphological structure allows the plant to thrive in moist, riparian environments.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant utilizes its leaves for various medicinal applications. These leaves contain bioactive compounds that can be processed into traditional remedies. They are often used in topical preparations to address skin inflammation or irritation. Some practitioners also utilize them in decoctions to help manage systemic discomfort. The chemical properties found within the foliage provide the primary therapeutic value for this species.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", the leaves of Salix matsudana were used to isolate 3 chemical constituents. These constituents, identified as apiagenin-7-O-beta-D-glucopyranoside, luteolin-7-O-beta-D-glucopyranoside, and luteolin-3'-methyl ether-7-O-beta-D-glucopyranoside, were studied for their influence on norepinephrine-induced lipolysis. Using rat's epididyimal lipocytes, researchers observed that 2 of these constituents can increase the release of free fatty acids (FFA) during the lipolysis process promoted by NE. All 3 of these constituents were isolated from the leaves of Salix matsudana for the first time.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, Salix matsudanana, has been utilized in traditional East Asian medicine for centuries primarily for its anti-inflammatory and analgesic properties.

People historically harvested the bark of this willow species to prepare decoctions used to treat fevers and various bodily pains. The presence of salicin within its tissues made it a natural precursor to modern aspirin treatments in folk practices. Local communities often applied crushed leaves or boiled bark to soothe skin irritations and swellings.

These long-standing medicinal applications demonstrate the species' importance in historical botanical knowledge.
