Corydalis crispa

Corydalis crispa, commonly known as creeping corydalis, is a perennial herb native to North America. Renowned in traditional herbalism, it contains various alkaloids, particularly isoquinoline derivatives. These bioactive compounds are studied for their potential sedative, analgesic, and antispasmodic properties, making this plant a subject of significant pharmacological interest.

  • Scientific name: Corydalis crispa
  • Family: Papaveraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Ranunculales and the family Papaveraceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Corydalis.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution primarily focused within high-altitude Asian regions. In China, its presence is notably documented within the Tibet region. Moving toward the Indian Subcontinent, the species can be found throughout the East Himalaya. These locations suggest that the plant thrives in mountainous environments characteristic of these areas. Consequently, its natural habitat is limited to these distinct ecological zones.
