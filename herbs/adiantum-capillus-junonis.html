Adiantum capillus-junonis

What's the taxonomical classification of Adiantum capillus-junonis?

Adiantum capillus-junonis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida, it falls under the subclass Polypodiinae and the order Polypodiales. This fern is a representative of the family Pteridaceae, specifically situated within the genus Adiantum. Its complete scientific designation is concluded by its specific epithet, identifying it as the species capillus-junonis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Adiantum capillus-junonis has delicate, bipinnate to tripinnate fronds composed of tiny, dark, fan-shaped leaflets that are attached to thin, black, hair-like stipes. The individual pinnules are characterized by their deeply lobed or laciniate margins and a dark green color. These fragile segments are arranged symmetrically along the slender, wiry rachis, giving the plant a lace-like appearance. The dark, wiry stems provide a sharp visual contrast against the translucent and finely textured foliage. Small, kidney-shaped sori are located on the undersides of the leaflets to facilitate spore production.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical and subtropical regions of South America, specifically spanning across Brazil, Paraguay, Argentina, and Uruguay. It thrives in humid environments such as rainforest understories and shaded riverbanks where moisture is abundant. Its range is largely concentrated in the Atlantic Forest biome and parts of the subtropical zones of the southern continent. Because it requires high humidity and consistent shade, it is rarely found in arid or highly exposed landscapes. This specific habitat preference limits its natural occurrence to stable, wet ecosystems within its native territory.

How is this plant cultivated?
