Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1026 studies) · ★★★★★

Olive tree, scientifically known as Olea europaea, is a cornerstone of Mediterranean culture and traditional medicine. Renowned for its longevity, this resilient evergreen offers potent therapeutic benefits. Its leaves and oil are rich in antioxidant polyphenols, widely utilized to support cardiovascular health, reduce inflammation, and combat oxidative stress.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Lamiales. Finally, it resides in the family Oleaceae under the genus Olea.

Distribution

This plant, Olea europaea, exhibits a notable presence across several key regions of Southwestern Europe. It can be found growing within the Balearic Islands, specifically known as Baleares. Furthermore, its distribution extends to the Mediterranean islands of Corsica and Sardegna. The species is also widely established throughout the mainland territory of France. Finally, its geographical range continues southward into the coastal regions of Portugal.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Olea europaea, is a versatile species where various components are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The most commonly studied parts are the leaves, which are rich in bioactive compounds like oleuropein. Whether one uses a single leaf for observation or harvests many Leaves for processing, they serve as the primary source for many health benefits. Additionally, the woody stem provides structural support for the tree and can sometimes be used in traditional applications. Ultimately, most medicinal applications are derived from concentrated plant extracts made from these parts, which are often used to support cardiovascular health and antioxidant activity.
