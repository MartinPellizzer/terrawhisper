Cardiospermum

Balloon vine

Cardiospermum, commonly known as the creeping bladder scale or balloon vine, is a versatile climbing plant belonging to the Sapindaceae family. Renowned for its delicate, fern-like foliage and ornamental bladder-like fruits, it is valued in traditional medicine for its potential anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and skin-soothing therapeutic properties.

  • Scientific name: Cardiospermum
  • Family: Sapindaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Sapindales, it is classified under the family Sapindaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Cardiospermum.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Cardamom, possesses a complex underground structure where the most therapeutically significant parts are the rhizomes. While the aerial portions include slender stems that support the leaves, the medicinal value is concentrated in the subterranean rhizome system. Specifically, the roots and the fleshy rhizomes are harvested for their essential oils and aromatic properties. In many traditional preparations, the dried rhizome is processed into a fine powder or used whole to flavor foods and serve as a digestive aid.
