Alnus incana

Grey alder

Alnus incana, commonly known as the grey alder, is a resilient deciduous shrub or tree found in temperate regions. Renowned for its ability to thrive in damp soils, it plays a vital role in nitrogen fixation. Historically, its bark and wood have been utilized for diverse medicinal and practical applications.

  • Scientific name: Alnus incana
  • Family: Betulaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fagales and family Betulaceae, it is classified under the genus Alnus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Alnus incana, is referred to by various names depending on the region and language. In Poland, it is called Olsza szara, while in Udmurt, it is known as Пурысь лулпу. In Belarusian, the species is called Вольха шэрая, and in French, it is commonly known as Aulne blanc. Finally, in Hungarian, this medicinal plant is identified as hamvas égér.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across several regions of Northern Europe. It can be found growing within the borders of Denmark and throughout Finland. The species is also present in the Faroe Islands, known locally as Føroyar. Furthermore, its range extends to the islands of Great Britain and Ireland. These locations highlight its preference for the northern European climate.
