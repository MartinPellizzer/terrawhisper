Artemisia maritima

Sea wormwood

Artemisia maritima, commonly known as sea wormwood, is a resilient perennial herb native to coastal regions. Characterized by its silvery, aromatic foliage, this plant thrives in saline environments. Historically valued in traditional medicine, it possesses potent bioactive compounds used for their antimicrobial, antioxidant, and various therapeutic properties in herbalism.

  • Scientific name: Artemisia maritima
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asterales, it is classified under the family Asteraceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Artemisia and the specific species name maritima.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across various coastal regions of Northern Europe. Its presence is specifically documented throughout the landscapes of Denmark and Great Britain. Furthermore, it can be found growing along the shores of Ireland. The species also extends its reach into the northern territories of Norway. Finally, its geographical range includes the coastal areas of Sweden.
