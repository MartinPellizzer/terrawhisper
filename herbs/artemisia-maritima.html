Ecology

The ecology of Artemisia maritima is defined by its specialized adaptation to highly saline and unstable environments, primarily colonizing the shores of coastal regions. As a halophytic species, it thrives in the harsh interface between land and sea, where it occupies salt marshes, sandy beaches, and rocky littoral zones. Its distribution is dictated by its ability to withstand high salt concentrations in the substrate and periodic exposure to salt spray, which limits competition from less tolerant plant species. By establishing itself along these shores, the plant plays a role in stabilizing coastal sediments and contributing to the unique nutrient cycling characteristic of maritime ecosystems.
