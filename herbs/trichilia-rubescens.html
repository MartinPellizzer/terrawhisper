Trichilia rubescens

Trichilia rubescens, commonly known as a medicinal species within the Meliaceae family, holds significant ethnobotanical importance. Found in tropical regions, this plant is prized for its bioactive compounds. Traditional healers utilize its bark and leaves to treat various ailments, offering a natural resource for potential pharmacological development and wellness.

  • Scientific name: Trichilia rubescens
  • Family: Meliaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Sapindales and the family Meliaceae. Ultimately, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by its membership in the genus Trichilia.

Common Names

This plant, Trichilia rubescens, is known by various regional names depending on the linguistic context. In English, it is often referred to as the red sandalwood tree, while in Spanish, it is commonly called maderilla or palo de sangre. In Portuguese, the species is known as pau-sangue, and in French, it is identified as bois de sang.

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern primarily centered within the tropical regions of Africa. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in Nigeria.
