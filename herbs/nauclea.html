Nauclea

Nauclea, commonly known as African Quinine, is a genus of flowering plants valued for its potent medicinal properties. Native to tropical Africa and Asia, its bark is rich in alkaloids, traditionally used to treat malaria, fever, and inflammation. This resilient plant remains a vital resource in ethnobotanical research today.

  • Scientific name: Nauclea
  • Family: Rubiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, specifically falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Gentianaales, it is classified under the family Rubiaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Nauclea.

This plant, commonly known as Nauclea orientalis or the African peach, possesses various components that are utilized in traditional medicine, with the bark being one of the most significant parts. Beyond the bark, other parts of the tree, including the leaves and roots, are also studied for their potential therapeutic properties, but the bark remains a central element in the plant's medicinal application.
