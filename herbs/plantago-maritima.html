Plantago maritima

Sea plantain

Plantago maritima, commonly known as sea plantain, is a resilient perennial herb native to coastal regions. Characterized by its fleshy, lance-shaped leaves, this salt-tolerant species thrives in sandy habitats. Historically valued in traditional medicine, it possesses anti-inflammatory properties, making it a significant botanical subject in coastal ecology and phytotherapy.

  • Scientific name: Plantago maritima
  • Family: Plantaginaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Lamiales and the family Plantaginaceae. Ultimately, it is identified by the genus Plantago.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Plantago maritima, is recognized by various local names across different regions and languages. In Poland, it is referred to as Babka nadmorska, while in French-speaking areas, it is called Plantain maritime. It is known as Подорожник морской in Russian, and in the Frisian language, it is identified as Südjen. Additionally, in Finland, the species is commonly known as merirataamo.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found across several regions within Northern Europe. Its distribution includes the coastal areas of Denmark and the islands of Føroyar. It also thrives in the unique environments of Iceland and Finland. Additionally, the species can be identified throughout Great Britain. These locations highlight its preference for the northernmost reaches of the continent.
