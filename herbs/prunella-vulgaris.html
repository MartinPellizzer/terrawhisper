Selfheal, scientifically known as Prunellla vulgaris, is a versatile perennial herb cherished in traditional herbalism. Renowned for its healing properties, it has long been utilized to treat wounds, respiratory issues, and inflammation. This resilient plant, characterized by its purple flowering spikes, remains a cornerstone of natural apothecary practices worldwide.

The reproduction of Prunella vulgaris is primarily sexual, characterized by bisexual flowers that allow for self-fertilization. The flowering period is variable, typically beginning in July and concluding in April. These flowers, which exhibit a blue color, generally range in length from 1.1 cm to 1.2 cm. The pollination syndrome is biotic, specifically involving insects, with bees being a primary pollinator. Following successful pollination, the plant produces dry, indehiscent fruits known as achenes. These fruits are quite small, with a mean length of 0.2 cm (ranging from 0.22 cm to 0.23 cm) and a width between 0.09 cm and 0.11 cm. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing between 101 and 1000 seeds. The seeds themselves are uniform in dimensions, with a consistent length of 2 mm and a volume of 1.7 mm³. In terms of mass, the seeds are lightweight, with a mean mass of approximately 0.00088 g, though they can range from a minimum of 8.8e-05 g to a maximum of 0.006 g. Dispersal of the seeds follows an unspecialized pattern, specifically utilizing epizoochory, where they are dispersed by attaching to passing animals.
