Prunella vulgaris

Selfheal

Prunella vulgaris, commonly known as self-heal, is a versatile perennial herb prized in traditional herbalism. Renowned for its potent antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties, this plant has long been used to treat wounds, sore throats, and skin irritations. Its unique structure and healing potential make it a botanical treasure.

  • Scientific name: Prunella vulgaris
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Prunella vulgaris, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, its classification is completed within the genus Prunella.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as self-heal, is characterized by its unique morphological structures used in traditional medicine. The most distinctive feature is the dense, dark purple flowering spike that emerges from the center of the leaves. These terminal spikes are composed of numerous small, tubular flowers clustered tightly together. While some botanical texts might colloquially or incorrectly refer to these elongated structures as a spicca or spiicas, the correct botanical term remains the spike. Below the surface, the plant develops a sturdy, creeping root system that helps it spread through the soil and anchor itself firmly.
