African myrrh

Commiphora myrrha · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (331 studies) · ★★★★★

African myrrh, scientifically known as Commiphora myrrha, is a small, resin-producing tree native to the arid regions of the Arabian Peninsula and Northeast Africa. Renowned for its aromatic oleoresin, this ancient botanical is prized in traditional medicine for its potent antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and wound-healing properties across many cultures.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution across the arid regions of the African continent. Within Northeast Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in Djibouti and Eritrea. It is also widely distributed throughout Ethiopia and Somalia. Furthermore, the species extends its range into East Tropical Africa. Ultimately, Kenya serves as another key location for its geographical presence.

Plant Parts

This plant, Commiphora myrrhra, is primarily valued for its medicinal oleo-gum-resin, which is harvested from the bark of the stem. When the plant is wounded, it produces various resins as a defense mechanism, eventually exuding a thick, aromatic oleo-gum resin that hardens upon exposure to air. This substance, commonly known as myrrh, is the most significant part of the plant used in traditional and modern pharmacology.
