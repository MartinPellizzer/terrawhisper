May apple

Podophyllum peltatum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (359 studies) · ★★★★★

May apple, scientifically known as Podophyllum peltatum, is a perennial herbaceous plant native to eastern North America. Characterized by its large, umbrella-like leaves and distinctive white flowers, it has historically been utilized in traditional medicine. However, caution is required, as the plant contains potent compounds that are highly toxic.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Mayapple, possesses a unique underground structure consisting of a thick, fleshy rhizome. This rhizome serves as the primary storage organ for the plant's nutrients and contains high concentrations of podophyllotoxin, the active medicinal compound. From this central rhizome, the plant develops its robust roots, which anchor the organism into the soil and assist in the uptake of water and essential minerals necessary for its growth.
