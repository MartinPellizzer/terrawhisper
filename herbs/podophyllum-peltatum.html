Podophyllum peltatum

May apple

Podophyllum peltatum, commonly known as Mayapple, is a perennial herbaceous plant native to North American woodlands. Renowned for its large, umbrella-like leaves and yellow flowers, it contains potent alkaloids like podophyllotoxin. While historically used in traditional medicine, its extreme toxicity requires careful handling and professional pharmaceutical application.

  • Scientific name: Podophyllum peltatum
  • Family: Berberidaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Ranunculales, it is classified under the family Berberidaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Podophyllum.

Its presence extends into the North-Central United States, where it grows in states such as Illinois and Iowa. Beyond the American continent, the species can also be located in Middle Europe, particularly in Germany.
