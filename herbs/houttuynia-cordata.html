Lizard Tail

Houttuynia cordata · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (991 studies) · ★★★★★

Lizard Tail, scientifically known as Houttuynia cordata, is a versatile perennial herb cherished in traditional medicine. Renowned for its heart-shaped leaves and potent bioactive compounds, it offers significant anti-inflammatory, antiviral, and antibacterial properties. This resilient plant is increasingly studied for its potential in supporting immune health and holistic wellness.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, placing it within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Piperales and the family Saururaceae. Ultimately, it is identified by the genus Houttuynia, specifically the species Houttuynia cordata.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence is noted in both Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward the central part of the continent, the species is also found throughout Middle Europe. Specifically, it can be located within the territories of Austria and the Czech-Slovakia region. Furthermore, the geographical range extends into Germany, solidifying its foothold in Middle Europe.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as fish mint, is a medicinal herb where various components are utilized for their therapeutic properties. While the whole plant is often cited in traditional medicine, pharmacological studies frequently focus on the aerial parts and the rhizomes to isolate active compounds. The heart-shaped leaves are a prominent feature of the foliage, often collected for processing. For medicinal applications, a water extract is commonly prepared to harness the plant's bioactive constituents, providing a concentrated form of its natural healing potential.
