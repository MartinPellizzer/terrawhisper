Berberis johannis

What's the taxonomical classification of Berberis johannis?

Berberis johannis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. At the class level, it is categorized as Equisetopsida, falling under the subclass Magnoliidae. Its taxonomic lineage continues through the order Ranunculales and the family Berberidaceae. Finally, the plant is placed within the genus Berberis, specifically identified by its species name, johannis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Berberis johannis has erect or ascending woody stems characterized by sharp, single spines located at the nodes and small, leathery, obovate leaves with entire margins. The leaves are typically arranged in clusters and exhibit a dark green color on the upper surface. Small, inconspicuous flowers emerge from the axils, often displaying a yellowish hue during the blooming period. These flowers eventually give rise to small, fleshy berries that serve as a key identifier for the species. The overall structure of the plant forms a dense, thorny shrub typical of its genus.

These populations are often found growing in rocky slopes, scrublands, and the edges of coniferous forests. The specific microclimates of these mountain ranges provide the necessary moisture and temperature fluctuations required for its survival. What are the pharmacological activities of Berberis johannis?
