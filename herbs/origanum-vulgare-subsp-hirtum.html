Origanum vulgare subsp. hirtum

Origanum vulgare subsp. hirtum, commonly known as hairy oregano, is a resilient perennial herb valued for its therapeutic properties. Rich in essential oils like carvacrol, this subspecies possesses potent antimicrobial and antioxidant qualities. Traditionally used to treat respiratory ailments and digestive issues, it remains a significant subject in ethnopharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Origanum vulgare subsp. hirtum
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Lamiales, falling into the family Lamiaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is established within the genus Origanum as the subspecies hirtum.

Plant Parts

This plant, Origanum vulgare subsp. hirtum, derives much of its therapeutic potency from its specific chemical constituents, most notably the essential oils found within its tissues. These essential oils are primarily concentrated in the glandular trichomes located on the leaves and flowering stems, where they serve as a defense mechanism for the plant. When harvested for medicinal use, these volatile oils provide the characteristic aromatic profile and contain bioactive compounds such as carvacrol and thymol, which are responsible for the plant's antimicrobial and antioxidant properties.
