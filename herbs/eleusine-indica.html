Eleusine indica

Indian crowfoot grass

Eleusine indica, commonly known as goosegrass or wiregrass, is a resilient annual grass widely distributed across tropical and subtropical regions. Often considered a persistent weed in agriculture, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value, utilized in traditional medicine for its diverse therapeutic properties, including anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activities.

  • Scientific name: Eleusine indica
  • Family: Poaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified within the order Poales and the family Poaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Eleusine.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Eleusine indica, is recognized by various common names across different regions and languages. In Chinese, it is referred to as 牛筋草 in Simplified Chinese (zh-hans), Traditional Chinese (zh-hant), and in the Hong Kong dialect (zh-hk). In the Philippines, it is commonly called Sabungsabungan in Pamantayan (pam), while in the Cebuano language (su), it is known as Carulang.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as goosegrass, features distinct leaves that are long, narrow, and linear in shape. These leaves grow in an alternate arrangement along the stem and are characterized by their prominent, parallel venation and a slightly rough texture. In medicinal applications, the leaves are often studied for their phytochemical properties and are sometimes used in traditional decoctions or topical preparations.
