Reproduction

The reproduction of Solanum mauritianum is characterized by a sexual process through bisexual flowers, which lack the capacity for asexual reproduction. The flowering period typically commences in April and concludes in June, featuring violet-colored flowers that can reach a maximum width of 2 cm. These flowers rely on biotic pollination, specifically utilizing insects as their primary pollination syndrome. Following successful pollination, the plant produces fleshy, indehiscent orange fruits classified as berries. These fruits generally measure an average of 1 cm in both length and width, with a maximum width of 1.5 cm. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing more than 1000 seeds. The seeds are quite uniform, with a mean length, minimum length, and maximum length of 1.5 mm, a mean volume of 1.9 mm³, and a mean mass of approximately 0.0010225 g (ranging from 0.00102 g to 0.00103 g). Because self-fertilization is absent, the plant's dispersal strategy is primarily zoochorous, specifically through endozoochory, where seeds are dispersed via the digestive tracts of animals.
