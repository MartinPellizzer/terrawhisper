The reproduction of Asclepia curassavica is characterized by a seasonal flowering cycle that typically begins in April and extends through November, though the timing can be variable. The plant produces orange flowers that range in length from a minimum of 0.55 cm to a maximum of 2 cm. These flowers utilize a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects, with bees serving as a primary pollinator. While the plant is capable of self-fertilization, its reproductive success is heavily supported by these insect interactions. Following successful pollination, the plant develops brown fruits classified as follicles. These fruits vary in size, with a minimum length of 5 cm and a mean length of 8 cm (reaching up to 10 cm), and a width ranging from a minimum of 0.6 cm to a mean of 1.25 cm (reaching up to 1.5 cm). The dispersal of the seeds from these follicles follows an anemochorous syndrome, meaning they are dispersed by the wind.
