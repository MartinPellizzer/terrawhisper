Daucus carota

What's the taxonomical classification of Daucus carota?

Daucus carota belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is situated in the subclass Magnoliidae and falls under the order Apiales. Its taxonomic lineage continues through the family Apiaceae, which places it within the genus Daucus, ultimately identifying it as the specific species carota.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Daucus carota has a thick, fleshy taproot that typically ranges from one to three centimeters in diameter and possesses a bright orange or white interior. The plant features highly dissected, feathery foliage consisting of compound leaves with fine, thread-like segments. It produces small, white flowers arranged in flat-topped umbels that can reach several centimeters in width. The stems are hollow and upright, often growing to a height of sixty to eighty centimeters. Small, ribbed seeds develop within the flower heads once the plant reaches maturity.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to Europe and parts of western and northern Asia but has become naturalized across much of North America and other temperate regions worldwide. It thrives in various environments ranging from open fields and roadsides to disturbed soils and coastal dunes. Because it is highly adaptable, it can be found across diverse climates provided there is sufficient sunlight and moderate moisture. Many populations have expanded rapidly due to its ability to colonize disturbed habitats. This widespread presence is often a result of human activity and the plant's efficient seed dispersal mechanisms.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, Daucus carota, is cultivated by sowing seeds directly into well-drained, sandy soil that is free of stones to allow for straight root development.

It requires full sunlight and consistent moisture to ensure the taproots grow smoothly without splitting. Farmers typically plant these seeds in the spring or early autumn when the soil temperature is moderate. Regular weeding is necessary to prevent competition for nutrients during the early stages of growth.

Once the foliage reaches a mature size, the roots can be harvested after several months of development.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant, Daucus carota, provides medicinal benefits through its leaves, roots, and seeds. The leaves are often utilized in herbal teas to act as a diuretic or to aid digestion. Its roots serve as a primary source for treating various ailments ranging from skin conditions to digestive issues. The seeds contain concentrated essential oils that can help soothe gastrointestinal discomfort and reduce inflammation. Together, these three distinct parts offer a versatile range of therapeutic applications in traditional medicine.

According to a study published by "Molecules (Basel, Switzerland)", Daucus carota L. is considered an effective medicinal herb in traditional oriental medicine. While the plant is utilized, the use of Daucus carota leaves (DCL) as therapeutic agents has not been explored in depth. Six flavone glycosides were isolated and identified from DCL, including the first elucidated structure of chrysoeriol-7-rutinoside. Using an optimized method with a relative standard deviation of <1.89% and recovery of 94.89-105.97%, the deglycosylation of DCL flavone glycosides showed conversion values of 85.8% for the luteolin group, 33.1% for the apiigenin group, and 88.7% for the chrysoeriol group. The enzyme-treated DCL demonstrated a higher inhibitory effect on TNF-α and IL-2 expression than carrot roots or untreated carrot leaves.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, Daucus carota, has been utilized across various ancient medical traditions to treat digestive ailments, skin conditions, and inflammatory issues. In Traditional Persian Medicine, the root was often valued for its ability to balance bodily humors and support gastrointestinal health. Historical practitioners in Europe also relied on the plant to manage various infections and promote wound healing through topical applications. Many cultures integrated the seeds into herbal preparations intended to stimulate menstruation or alleviate respiratory discomfort. These diverse historical uses demonstrate a widespread recognition of the plant's bioactive properties throughout antiquity.

According to a study published by "Electronic physician", Daucus carota is utilized in a traditional food product known as wild carrot halvha. This traditional food product consists of mixed Daucus carota and several herbs with honey. In a clinical trial involving 96 women aged 18-35 years, one group received 30 g of this traditional food product on a daily basis. The intervention involving the food product containing Daucus carota was administered over an eight-week period. This study was registered with the IRCT ID: IRCT2015122425681N1.

What are the pharmacological activities of Daucus carota?

This plant has anti-diabetic, anti-inflammatory, anti-cancer, antioxidant, and hepatoprotective pharmacological activities. Its high concentration of bioactive compounds like carotenoids helps neutralize free radicals to prevent oxidative stress in cells. These properties allow it to reduce systemic inflammation and potentially inhibit the progression of malignant tumors. Furthermore, the plant supports metabolic regulation by managing blood glucose levels and protects liver tissue from chemical damage. Such diverse therapeutic effects make Daucus carota a significant subject in medicinal research.

According to a study published by "Biological trace element research", Daucus carota is one of 16 anti-diabetic medicinal plants studied for its potential in managing diabetes. The plant contains elements such as Ca, K, Cr, Cu, Mn, Zn, and Se which contribute to maintaining normal glucose metabolism. Specifically, Daucus carota was identified as one of 10 plants containing significant Zn concentrations. It was also found to be one of 12 plants containing significant Se concentrations. These elements are crucial because they assist in lowering intestinal glucose absorption and increasing insulin secretion by pancreatic tissues.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds carotol, falcariol, apiogenin, carotenoids, and β-carotene. These phytochemicals contribute to various therapeutic properties ranging from antioxidant support to anti-inflammatory effects. Carotenoids serve as essential precursors for the production of vitamin A in the human body. Specifically, β-carotene functions as a powerful antioxidant that helps protect cells from oxidative stress. The presence of apiogenin and specific sesquiterpenes like carotol and falcariol further enhances the plant's bioactive profile.

According to a study published by "Journal of dietary supplements", the primary main components of Daucus carota essential oils include carotol at 46%. The study also identifies the specific medicinal composition of the plant through antimicrobial effectiveness tests. This research focuses on the evaluation of its efficacy as a natural preservative. What health conditions is this plant used for?

This plant is used for treating age-related eye diseases like cataracts, managing diabetes, alleviating diarrhea, and reducing inflammation. The high beta-carotene content in its roots supports ocular health by protecting the eyes from degenerative changes. Additionally, its medicinal properties help stabilize blood sugar levels for those dealing with diabetic complications. The plant also possesses antimicrobial and soothing qualities that assist in calming digestive distress and diarrhea. Furthermore, its antioxidant profile works effectively to combat systemic inflammation throughout the body.

According to a study published by "Critical reviews in biotechnology", Daucus carota contains lutein, which is an important xanthophyll. This substance is concentrated in the macula and suppresses oxidative stress in eye tissues. Increased consumption of lutein has a close correlation with a reduction in the incidence of cataract. Lutein may be useful in the prevention and/or mitigation of cataract and other age-related eye diseases.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is Daucus carota, a species used to create medicinal preparations including decoctions, essential oils, ethanol extracts, infusions, and juice. A decoction involves boiling the tougher root parts in water to extract soluble compounds, while an infusion uses hot water to gently steep the plant material. Essential oils are distilled from the seeds or roots to capture concentrated aromatic volatile compounds for therapeutic use. Ethanol extracts are produced by soaking the plant in alcohol to isolate specific bioactive constituents that water cannot dissolve. Fresh juice is often pressed directly from the roots to provide a concentrated dose of vitamins and minerals.

According to a study published by "Revista de gastroenterologia del Peru : organo oficial de la Sociedad de Gastroenterologia del Peru", Daucus carota is one of several plants used by Peruvian people in the popular treatment of diarrhea. In the experiments, decoction or infusion of the plants were used for the in vitro tests. The study tested the in vitro effect of Daucus carota against Vibrio cholerae 01. Results showed that Daucus carota showed no in vitro effect against V. cholerae.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause contact dermatitis and localized skin irritation when the sap or foliage comes into direct contact with sensitive skin. While most people consume the root safely, the stems and leaves contain compounds that may trigger allergic reactions in certain individuals. These dermatological issues often manifest as redness, itching, or a bumpy rash around the area of exposure. Some people may also experience sensitivity to sunlight after handling the plant, which can exacerbate these skin issues. It is important to wash hands thoroughly after gardening with this species to minimize these risks.

According to a study published by "Archives of dermatology", fifty-three patients having contact dermatitis on the fingertips showed positive patch tests with several vegetables, including carrot. The carrot was identified as one of the commonest vegetables causing these reactions in the study. Preparations made from carrot juices used as such gave the maximum number of positive patch test reactions. In some cases, the lyophilization of these juices led to false-negative patch tests.

Combining these botanicals allows for a complex balance of savory and sweet flavor profiles in culinary applications.
