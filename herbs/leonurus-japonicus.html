Leonurus japonicus

Leonurus japonicus, commonly known as Motherwort, is a perennial herb cherished in traditional medicine for its calming properties. Renowned for supporting cardiovascular health and regulating menstrual cycles, this potent plant contains bioactive compounds that alleviate anxiety and promote emotional balance, making it a staple in herbal healing practices worldwide.

  • Scientific name: Leonurus japonicus
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae, it is classified under the genus Leonurus.

Common Names

This plant, Leonurus japonicus, is known by various common names across different regions and languages. Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution that spans across multiple continents and ocean regions. In Middle Europe, its presence is documented within the territories of Germany as well as the Czechia-Slovakia region. The species also extends to the Macaronesian islands, specifically reaching Cape Verde. Furthermore, its range includes remote locations within the Middle Atlantic Ocean. These specific areas include the islands of both Ascension and St. Helena.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Leonurus japonicus, possesses several distinct morphological features that contribute to its medicinal profile. The most commonly utilized portion is the aerial parts, which include the stems and leaves. Upon closer inspection of the foliage, one can observe specialized peltate glandular trichomes that often contain the plant's bioactive compounds. Below the surface, the specimen develops a system of normal roots that anchor the plant and facilitate nutrient uptake, although specific medicinal preparations may sometimes focus on the root itself. Additionally, the plant produces a seed that ensures the continuation of its life cycle through reproduction.
