Dichrocephala integrifolia

Dichrocepala integrifolia, commonly known as the African mahogany or wild mahogany, is a versatile medicinal plant native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, its leaves and bark are traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to gastrointestinal issues and inflammation.

  • Scientific name: Dichrocephala integrifolia
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and order Asterales, it falls into the family Asteraceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Dichrocephala and the species name Dichrocephala integrifolia.

Common Names

This plant, Dichrocraphela integrifolia, is known by various common names across different linguistic contexts. In Chinese, it is frequently referred to as 鱼眼草 (yú yǎn cǎo) or 蚯疽草 (qiū jū cǎo), and it is also commonly called 茯苓菜 (fú líng cài). These names, including the specific regional designations 鱼眼草 and 茯苓菜 used in mainland China (zh-cn) and Taiwan (zh-tw), reflect its traditional identification within various herbal practices.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning across two major continental regions. In Southeastern Europe, its presence is documented in Italy and the European portion of Türkiye. Moving toward the African continent, it is widely distributed throughout West Tropical Africa. Specifically, the species can be found within the borders of Benin, Ghana, and Guinea. This wide distribution highlights the plant's ability to thrive in varied ecological environments across these regions.
