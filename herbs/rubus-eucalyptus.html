Rubus eucalyptus

What's the taxonomical classification of Rubus eucalyptus?

Rubus eucalyptus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic progression, it falls under the order Rosales and is a member of the family Rosaecae. Finally, its specific biological identity is defined by its placement in the genus Rubus and its unique species designation, eucalyptus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Rubus eucalyptus has arching, woody stems characterized by a dense covering of fine, recurved prickles and elongated, lanceolate leaves with serrated margins. The foliage typically consists of trifoliate or pentafoliate leaflets that exhibit a distinct, silvery-green pubescence on their undersides. These leaves are arranged alternately along the canes, which can grow quite long and spread outward. Small, inconspicuous flowers emerge from the axils, eventually developing into fleshy, aggregate fruits. The overall structure of the plant allows it to form thick, sprawling thickets in its natural habitat.

These habitats provide the specific environmental conditions necessary for its survival at significant elevations.
