Alchornea laxiflora

Lowveld bead-string

Alchornea laxiflora, commonly known as a medicinal shrub native to tropical regions, holds significant ethnobotanical importance. Widely utilized in traditional medicine, its leaves and bark possess potent bioactive compounds. These properties offer therapeutic benefits, particularly for treating inflammatory conditions, skin ailments, and various digestive issues throughout indigenous communities.

  • Scientific name: Alchornea laxiflora
  • Family: Euphorbiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malpighiales, it is further categorized under the family Euphorbiaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Alchornea.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Alchornea laxiflora, is referred to by several different common names depending on the language and region. Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across several regions of the African continent. Specifically, its range extends into the Central African Republic and reaches as far as Burundi. Consequently, the distribution of Alchornea laxiflora covers a vast area of tropical habitats.
