Reproduction

The reproduction of Carduus crispus is characterized by a primarily sexual process, as the plant is bisexual and lacks asexual reproduction. As a monocarp, it completes its reproductive cycle once before death. The flowering period typically begins in July and extends through September. During this time, the plant utilizes a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects to facilitate the transfer of pollen. Following successful fertilization, which can occur through self-fertilization, the plant develops fruit in the form of an achene. These fruits are indehiscent, meaning they do not split open at maturity to release seeds. The seeds themselves are quite small, with a minimum mass of 0.0016 g, a maximum mass of 0.00203 g, and an average mass of approximately 0.1191 g. For the spread of the next generation, the plant employs an anemochorous dispersal syndrome, utilizing the wind to transport its seeds to new locations.
