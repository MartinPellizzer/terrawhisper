Melastoma dodecandrum

What's the taxonomical classification of Melastoma dodecandrum?

Melastoma dodecandrum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Moving through its hierarchical lineage, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant is further categorized under the order Myrtales and falls within the family Melastomataceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Melastoma and the species dodecandrum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Melastoma dodecandrum has opposite, elliptical leaves with prominent longitudinal veins, woody stems, and showy flowers featuring ten bright yellow stamens and five pinkish petals. The leaf venation is highly characteristic, displaying several primary veins that curve from the base toward the apex in a parallel fashion. Its flowers are typically actinomorphic and emerge from terminal or axillary positions. The plant produces small, capsule-like fruits that contain numerous tiny seeds. These structural features distinguish it from many other species within the Melastomataceae family.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds casuarictin, casuariinin, nobotannin B, pedunclagin, and various ellagitannins. These specific polyphenolic substances are classified as hydrolyzable tannins that contribute to the plant's biological activities. The presence of these compounds is often linked to the antioxidant and antimicrobial properties observed in the species. Researchers study these molecules to understand how they interact with cellular structures in medicinal applications. Such chemical profiles make the plant a subject of interest in pharmacological investigations.

According to a study published by "Biological & pharmaceutical bulletin", an extract of Melastoma dodecandrum Lour. obtained with 80% aqueous acetone (MDL) inhibits nitric oxide production in RAW264.7 cells. Further fractionation showed that the 50% methanol extracts contained hydrolyzable tannins, including casuariinin, casuarictin, pedunclagin, and nobotannin B. These specific tannins exhibited strong inhibitory activities toward nitric oxide production with ID50 values between 2.0 and 5.1 microM. Additionally, these tannins inhibited nitric oxide production in macrophages preactivated for 16 h with LPS and IFN-gamma, showing IC50 values around 30-130 microM. These findings suggest that MDL and its hydrolyzable tannins possess the pharmacological ability to suppress nitric oxide production.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Melastoma dodecandrum is used as a folk remedy for the treatment of snakebites. This plant was among the 88 plant species investigated to see if they contain compounds with bacterial growth inhibition to prevent secondary infections. The crude extracts of Melastoma dodecandrum showed MIC values below 200 μg/mL against either Bacillus subtilis, Staphylococcuus aureus, Eschericia coli or Pseudomonas aeruginosa. Biochromatograms demonstrated that tannins play a main role for the bacterial growth inhibition observed for this plant.
