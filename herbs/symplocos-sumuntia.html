What's the taxonomical classification of Symplocos sumuntia?

Symplocos sumuntia belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The taxonomic hierarchy continues through the order Ericales and the family Symplocaceae, eventually narrowing down to its specific genus, Symplocos, and its unique species designation, sumuntia.

According to a study published by "Natural product research", Symplocos sumuntiia has been traditionally used to relieve inflammation in Vietnam. A chemical investigation led to the isolation and identification of 1 new and 9 known compounds from the plant. These 10 compounds, labeled 1-10, possessed inhibitory activities on NO production induced by LPS activated macrophages. Among these, compounds 2 and 3 demonstrated a strong ability to reduce NO production with IC50 values of 3.91 ± 0.05 and 3.96 ± 0.24 µM, respectively. Other compounds also reduced NO production in LPS stimulated RAW264.7 cells.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is Symplocos sumuntiia, a species used to create various medicinal herbal preparations for treating diverse ailments. Such herbal preparations represent a significant area of interest in traditional medicine and drug discovery.

According to a study published by "Plants (Basel, Switzerland)", Symplocos sumuntia Buch.-Ham. ex D. Don is a traditional medicinal herb used in Asia to treat various pathologies including cough, stomachache, tonsillitis, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia. The methanol extract of this plant, referred to as SSME, was investigated for its inhibitory effects on inflammatory responses in LPS-treated BV2 cells. SSME significantly inhibited the production of nitric oxide (NO) along with the expression of inducible nitric oxide synthase and cyclooxygenase-2. The production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, specifically interleukin (IL)-6, tumor necrosis factor-α, and IL-1β, was also suppressed by the SSME in LPS-induced BV2 cells. This anti-inflammatory effect involves the suppression of LPS-stimulated phosphorylation of mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPKs) such as JNKs and a decrease in the nuclear translocation of the nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB)/p65 protein.
