Allium ursinum

Allium ursinum, commonly known as wild garlic, is a perennial herb native to Europe. Renowned for its pungent aroma and edible leaves, it thrives in shady, moist woodlands. Beyond its culinary uses, this plant holds significant medicinal value, offering antimicrobial properties and supporting cardiovascular health through its nutrient-rich composition.

  • Scientific name: Allium ursinum
  • Family: Amaryllidaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Allium ursinum, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae within the order Asparagales. Finally, it is a member of the family Amaryllidaceae and the genus Allium.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several regions in Northern Europe. Specifically, it can be found growing throughout the country of Denmark. Its presence extends to the northern landscapes of Finland and Norway. Furthermore, the species is commonly documented across the islands of Great Britain and Ireland. These diverse locations highlight its established range within the northernmost parts of the continent.
