Annona cherimola

Annona cherimola, commonly known as cherimoya, is a tropical fruit prized for its creamy, custard-like texture and sweet flavor. Beyond its culinary appeal, this "superfruit" offers significant medicinal potential, containing bioactive compounds with antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties that support overall wellness and traditional healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Annona cherimola
  • Family: Annonaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Annona cherimola, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Magnoliales. Finally, it is a member of the family Annonaceae and falls under the genus Annona.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning multiple continents and climatic zones. In Southwestern Europe, its presence is noted within the borders of Spain. Its range extends to the Macaronesian islands, specifically including Cape Verde. Within West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found in both the Gulf of Guinea Islands and Rwanda. Finally, its distribution reaches into Northeast Tropical Africa, where it is found in Ethiopia.
