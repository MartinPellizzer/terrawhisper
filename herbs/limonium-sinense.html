Limonium sinense

Limonium sinese, commonly known as Chinese sea lavender, is a resilient perennial herb native to East Asia. Renowned for its delicate, colorful blossoms, this medicinal plant is valued in traditional practices for its bioactive properties. It is frequently studied for its potential antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and various therapeutic healing benefits.

  • Scientific name: Limonium sinense
  • Family: Plumbaginaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Caryophyllales and the family Plumbaginaceae. Finally, it is specifically identified by the genus Limonium.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Limonium sinense, is recognized by several different common names depending on the linguistic context and regional usage. In Chinese, it is frequently referred to as 中华补血草, a name also used in simplified Chinese (zh-cn) and even as zh-hans. In Taiwan, it is commonly called 石蓯蓉, while another traditional Chinese designation for the species is 白花玉钱香.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range across various regions of East Asia. Within China, its presence is noted in Manchuria as well as the North-Central and Southeast territories. The distribution extends further into the Eastern Asia region, specifically covering the Nansei-shoto islands. Additionally, the species can be found growing in Taiwan. Collectively, these locations highlight the diverse habitats inhabited by Limonium sinense.
