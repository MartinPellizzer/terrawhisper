Artemisia indica

Indian wormwood

Artemisia indica, commonly known as Indian wormwood, is a perennial herb valued in traditional medicine for its diverse therapeutic properties. Rich in bioactive compounds, it is widely used to treat digestive issues, fever, and skin ailments. This versatile plant plays a crucial role in holistic healing and pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Artemisia indica
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales. Ultimately, it is a member of the family Asteraceae within the genus Artemisia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Artemisia indica, is referred to by various common names depending on the region and language. In Chinese, it is known as 五月艾 (wǔyuè ài), which is also the designation used in both Simplified Chinese (五月艾, zh-cn, zh-hans) and Traditional Chinese (艾, zh-tw) contexts. In Japanese, the species is commonly called ニシヨモギ (nishiyomogi).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across various regions of China. It can be found growing in the South-Central areas of the country. Furthermore, its range extends to the tropical landscape of Hainan. The species also inhabits the northern territories of Inner Mongolia and Manchuria. Finally, it is documented to exist within the China North-Central region.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Artemisia indica, utilizes its various anatomical structures for both growth and medicinal efficacy. The shoot system consists of an erect, branching stem that supports the overall structure of the herb, often displaying a characteristic silvery-green hue due to fine hairs. The leaf parts are highly distinctive, being deeply lobed or pinnatifid with a fragrant, aromatic quality that is central to its therapeutic properties. Beneath the surface, the root parts provide essential anchorage and nutrient absorption, forming a sturdy foundation that allows the plant to thrive in its natural habitat.
