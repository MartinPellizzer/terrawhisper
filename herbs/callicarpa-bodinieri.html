Callicarpa bodinieri

What's the taxonomical classification of Callicarpa bodinieri?

Callicarpa bodinieri belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. This specific plant is further categorized into the genus Callicarpa, ultimately reaching its unique designation at the species level as bodinieri.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Callicarpa bodinieri has opposite, simple leaves that are ovate to elliptic in shape with serrated margins and an acute apex. The leaf surfaces are typically pubescent, providing a soft texture to the foliage. Its woody stems support small, axillary clusters of flowers that eventually develop into distinct drupes. These small, spherical fruits are notable for their vibrant color and clustered arrangement along the branches. The overall structure of the plant is characterized by its shrubby growth habit and multi-stemmed form.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is endemic to the temperate forests of Japan, specifically occurring within the islands of Honshu, Shikoku, and Kyushu. It typically occupies montane regions where the climate is sufficiently moist and cool. Within these mountainous habitats, it thrives in shaded areas such as forest edges and understory thickets. The species is most commonly found at moderate elevations where well-drained soil is available. Its range is restricted to these specific East Asian ecological zones.

Small fruits harvested from the branches serve as key components in traditional remedies. Additionally, the twigs are utilized in preparations to address specific health concerns. According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", the leaves of Callicarpa bodinieri were studied to identify its constituents. Through chromatography, 7 compounds were isolated and elucidated from this plant. These identified substances include 5-hydroxy-4',3,6,7-tetramethoxy flavone (I), luteolin-7-O-glucoside (II), chrysoeriol-4'-O-glucoside (III), luteolin-4'-O-glucoside (IV), beta-sitosterol (V), ursolic acid (VI), and betulinic acid (VII). All 7 of these compounds were isolated from this plant for the first time. Additionally, compounds I, III, and IV were isolated from the Callicarpa genus for the first time.

According to a study published by "Journal of natural products", species belonging to the genus Callicarpa are used traditionally in Chinese medicine for the treatment of inflammation, rheumatism, and pain. Investigation of the leaves and twigs of Callicarpa bodinieri resulted in the isolation of nine new abietane diterpenoids, bodinieric acids A-I (1-9), along with six known compounds (10-15). The inhibitory activities of these isolates were determined against 13 kinase enzymes to explore therapeutic potential. Eight compounds exhibited moderate inhibitory effects on spleen tyrosine kinase (SYK). Specifically, the IC50 values of compounds 2 and 6 were 7.2 and 10.7 μM, respectively.
