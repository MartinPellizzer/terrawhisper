Artemisia gilvescens

Artemisia gilvescens, commonly known as the Gilvescens wormwood, is a resilient perennial herb native to the rocky terrains of the Mediterranean. Renowned for its aromatic, silvery foliage, this medicinal plant is traditionally utilized in herbal remedies to support digestive health and possess potent antioxidant and antimicrobial properties.

  • Scientific name: Artemisia gilvescens
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales. As a member of the family Asteraceae, it is specifically identified by the genus Artemisia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning across several key regions in Asia and Europe. In Middle Europe, its presence is specifically documented within Germany. The species also occupies significant territories within China, including the South-Central, North-Central, and Southeast regions. Furthermore, its range extends into Eastern Asia, where it can be found in Japan. Together, these locations highlight the diverse environmental settings where the plant thrives.

Plant Parts

This plant, Artemisia gilvescens, is highly valued in traditional medicine primarily for its concentrated essential oil. This oil, which is extracted from the aromatic foliage and stems of the plant, contains a complex profile of bioactive compounds that provide its therapeutic properties. Because the essential oil serves as the primary source of its medicinal potency, the quality and concentration of this component are critical for its use in treating various ailments.
