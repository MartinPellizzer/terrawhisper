Mentha pulegium

Pennyroyal

Mentha pulegium, commonly known as pennyroyal, is a perennial herb within the Lamiaceae family. Renowned for its potent essential oils, particularly pulegone, this aromatic plant has been utilized extensively in traditional medicine. While valued for its antimicrobial and digestive properties, its high toxicity requires cautious and careful application.

  • Scientific name: Mentha pulegium
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales and family Lamiaceae, it is classified under the genus Mentha.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across several key regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, it can be found growing throughout Great Britain and Ireland. Its distribution continues into Middle Europe, where it is established in Austria. The species is also documented within the borders of Belgium. Finally, its geographical range extends through the territories of Czechia and Slovakia.

Plant Parts

This plant, Mentha pulegium, is a species where the whole plant is often utilized for its diverse therapeutic properties. The structural foundation of the herb is provided by its upright stem, which contains specialized stem tissues that facilitate the transport of nutrients and secondary metabolites throughout the organism. However, the most significant part of the plant is the leaves, which serve as the primary site for the synthesis of aromatic compounds. It is within these foliage structures that the highly potent pennyroyal essential oil is concentrated, providing the characteristic scent and the medicinal potency for which the species is known.
