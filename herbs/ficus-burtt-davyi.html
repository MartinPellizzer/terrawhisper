Ficus burtt-davyi

Scrambling fig

Ficus burtt-davyi, commonly known as the Burtt-Davie fig, is a rare, slow-growing succulent tree native to Baja California. While primarily valued for its unique, sculptural appearance, this resilient species holds potential in ethnobotanical studies, offering intriguing possibilities for traditional medicinal applications within its specialized arid desert ecosystem.

  • Scientific name: Ficus burtt-davyi
  • Family: Moraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across several regions of the African continent. Within South Tropical Africa, its presence is noted in Mozambique. Plant Parts

This plant, known as Ficus burtt-davyi or the Burtt-Davyi fig, is a unique succulent species where the fruits serve as a vital component of its biology and ecological role. These fruits are actually specialized structures called syconia, which function as enclosed inflorescences that house tiny flowers within a fleshy receptacle. Once matured, these fruits provide a concentrated source of nutrients for local wildlife, playing a crucial role in the plant's reproductive cycle and its ability to thrive in arid environments.
