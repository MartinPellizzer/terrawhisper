Sumac

Rhus coriaria · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (385 studies) · ★★★★★

Sumac, scientifically known as Rhus coriaria, is a versatile Mediterranean shrub prized for its medicinal and culinary properties. Rich in antioxidants and tannins, it possesses potent anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial qualities. Traditionally used to treat digestive issues and skin ailments, this vibrant plant remains a vital element in herbal medicine.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Rhus coriaria is characterized by a flowering period that typically begins in April and extends through July. The plant produces small, white flowers arranged in a panicle inflorescence, with individual flower lengths ranging from a minimum of 0.3 cm to a maximum of 0.35 cm. Following pollination, the plant develops fleshy, brown fruits classified as drupes. These fruits are relatively small, featuring a mean length of 0.52 cm and a mean width of 0.48 cm, with dimensions ranging from a minimum width of 0.4 cm to a maximum width of 0.6 cm. The fruits are indehiscent, meaning they do not split open at maturity to release seeds. The seeds contained within are characterized by a mean mass of approximately 0.01157 g (ranging from 0.01142 g to 0.01572 g) and a consistent volume of 8.6 mm³. Dispersal of the seeds is achieved through a zoochorous syndrome, specifically via endozoochory, where animals consume the fleshy fruit and disperse the seeds through their digestive tracts.
