Cuscuta australis

Cuscuta australis, commonly known as dodder, is a parasitic plant characterized by its leafless, yellowish, thread-like stems. Lacking chlorophyll, it relies entirely on host plants for nutrients via specialized structures called haustoria. Despite its parasitic nature, it holds significant ethnobotanical importance in traditional medicine for various healing applications.

  • Scientific name: Cuscuta australis
  • Family: Convolvulaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Cuscuta australis, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Solanales and the family Convolvulaceae. Its taxonomic hierarchy concludes with the genus Cuscuta.

This plant exhibits a specific distribution across various regions of the European continent. In Middle Europe, its presence can be documented in both Hungary and Switzerland.
