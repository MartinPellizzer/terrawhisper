Australian Dodder

Cuscuta australis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (20 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Australian Dodder, scientifically known as Cuscuta australis, is a parasitic climbing vine characterized by its leafless, yellowish stems. Lacking chlorophyll, it survives by extracting nutrients from host plants. This unique botanical species plays a significant role in various ecosystems, serving as a subject of interest in ethnobotanical studies.

Plant Parts

This plant, Cuscuta australis, is a parasitic climber that lacks functional roots and leaves, instead relying on a specialized structure to survive. The stem is its most prominent feature, appearing as a thin, yellowish, thread-like filament that coils tightly around a host plant to extract nutrients. Throughout its life cycle, the plant produces small seeds that are dispersed to find new hosts, ensuring the continuation of the species through its parasitic survival strategy.
