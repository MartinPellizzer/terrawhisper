Terminalia ivorensis

Black afara

Terminalia ivorensis, commonly known as the Ivory Coast Terminalia, is a significant medicinal tree native to West African rainforests. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, various parts of the plant are traditionally utilized to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to inflammatory conditions, offering valuable insights for modern ethnopharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Terminalia ivorensis
  • Family: Combretaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Myrtales and is a member of the family Combretaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Terminalia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Terminalia ivorensis, is referred to by various names across different languages and cultures. In Esperanto, it is called Eburborda terminalio, while in French, the common name is Framiré. In Thailand, it is known as หูกกระจง, and in Japan, it is referred to as フラミレ. Additionally, in the Yoruba language, it is known as Idígbó.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the diverse landscapes of West Tropical Africa. Within this broad region, it can be found growing in the country of Benin. Its presence is also documented throughout Burkina Faso. Furthermore, the species extends its reach into the nation of Gambia. Finally, its geographical range includes the territories of both Ghana and Guinea.
