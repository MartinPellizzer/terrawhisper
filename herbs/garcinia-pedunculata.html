Garcinia pedunculata

What's the taxonomical classification of Garcinia pedunculata?

Garcina pedunculata belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant follows the taxonomic order Malpighiales and is a member of the family Clusiaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Garcinia, with its specific designation being the species pedunculata.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Garcinia pedunculata has evergreen, woody branches with simple, opposite, oblong-elliptic leaves that possess a leathery texture and entire margins. The foliage typically displays a shiny dark green upper surface and a slightly paler underside. Small, fragrant flowers emerge from axillary positions on short peduncles, featuring white petals and numerous stamens. These blossoms eventually develop into fleshy, globose fruits that transition from green to a bright yellow or orange color when ripe. The plant's overall structure is characterized by a spreading canopy and a dense arrangement of its characteristic foliage.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant grows best in tropical, humid environments with well-draining, acidic soil and requires partial shade to thrive.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains several bioactive xanthones, specifically alpha-mangostin, beta-mangostin, and gamma-mangostin, which serve as its primary medicinal compounds. These specific phytochemicals are recognized for their significant antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Research indicates that these xanthones can assist in combating various oxidative stresses within biological systems. The presence of these unique chemical structures contributes to the plant's overall therapeutic potential in traditional medicine. Scientists continue to study these individual compounds to better understand their roles in pharmacological applications.

According to a study published by "Journal of AOAC International", the fruit rinds of Garcinias pedunculata contain five biologically important compounds. These medicinal compounds include 3 xanthones, specifically α-mangostin, β-mangostin, and γ-mangostin. Additionally, the plant contains 2 polyisoprenylated benzophenones known as xanthochymol and isoxanthochymol. A validated UHPLC-PDA method with a total run time of 9 min was used to analyze these extracts. The research indicates that there is a wide variation in the concentration of these 5 compounds within the species.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is Garcinia pedunculata, and its medicinal herbal preparations primarily consist of dried leaf powder, various aqueous extracts, and methanolic extracts. Researchers utilize the dried leaf powder to create concentrated formulations that harness the plant's bioactive compounds. Detailed studies often focus on methanolic extracts to isolate specific secondary metabolites for pharmaceutical investigation. These various preparations are evaluated for their potential antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties. Through these different extraction methods, the therapeutic versatility of the species is thoroughly explored.

According to a study published by "Indian journal of clinical biochemistry : IJCB", Garcinia pedunculata Roxb. is an important medicinal plant of the North Eastern region of India used against various diseases in folk medicine. A methanolic extract was prepared from the dried leaf powder using a hot continuous extraction method. The LD50 values of the Garcinia pedunculata Roxb. extract were found to be safe up to 2000 mg. In vivo biological studies on male Wistar rats were carried out at dose levels of 100 mg, 300 mg, and 600 mg/kg body weight. The extract at the dose of 600 mg/kg body weight significantly reversed elevated marker enzymes including SGOT, SGPT, and ALP. Higher dose extracts also had a pronounced effect on oxidative stress parameters such as GSH and catalase.
