Glycyrrhiza squamulosa

What's the taxonomical classification of Glycyrrhiza squamulosa?

Glycyrrhiza squamuosa belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the evolutionary lineage of the order Fabales. It is further categorized under the family Fabaceae, which is characterized by its diverse group of legumes. Within this family, the plant is placed in the genus Glycyrrhiza, and its specific biological identity is defined by its species name, squamulosa.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Glycyrrhiza squamuosa has a woody, perennial taproot that is thick, branched, and typically turns dark brown or blackish with age. The stems are erect and can reach heights of up to one meter, often exhibiting a somewhat woody base. Leaves are pinnately compound, consisting of multiple pairs of small, oval-shaped leaflets that are arranged alternately along the rachis. The plant produces small, pea-like flowers that are typically white or pale violet and bloom in clusters. Its fruit consists of small, indehiscent pods that contain several tiny, brownish seeds.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause serious health complications such as hypertension, hypokalemia, edema, muscle weakness, nausea, dizziness, heart palpitations, and weight gain. The primary mechanism behind these issues involves the consumption of glycyrrhizin, which disrupts electrolyte balance in the body. This imbalance often leads to low potassium levels, resulting in muscle weakness and dangerous heart palpitations. As the body retains excess sodium and water, patients may experience significant edema and rapid weight gain. These systemic changes frequently trigger secondary symptoms like hypertension, nausea, and dizziness.
