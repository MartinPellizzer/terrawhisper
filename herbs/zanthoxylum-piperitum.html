Japanese pepper

Zanthoxylum piperitum · Accepted scientific name

Japanese pepper, scientifically known as Zanthoxylum piperitum, is a deciduous shrub native to East Asia. Renowned for its aromatic leaves and peppercorn-like fruit, it is a staple in culinary and traditional medicine. Its unique numbing properties and antioxidant qualities make it a valuable resource for holistic wellness and flavor.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across several key regions in Eastern Asia. It can be found growing naturally throughout the various islands and mainland areas of Japan. Its presence is also well-documented across the Korean Peninsula. Moving toward the Indian Subcontinent, the species extends into the mountainous terrain of the East Himalaya. These specific locations define the core geographical range for this medicinal species.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Japanese pepper or Sanshō, utilizes several distinct components for its medicinal and culinary purposes. The fruits are frequently harvested for their aromatic zest, while the seeds contained within provide a unique pungent sensation used in various traditional remedies. Additionally, the bark of the tree is often employed in herbal preparations, contributing its own specific chemical properties to the plant's overall therapeutic profile.
