Life history

The life history of Asteriscus aquaticus is characterized by an annual lifecycle, meaning the plant completes its entire developmental process—from germination to seed production—within a single growing season. In terms of its structural growth strategy, it is classified as a therophyte, a life form defined by plants that survive unfavorable seasons as ephemeral seeds, allowing them to rapidly colonize suitable habitats when conditions become favorable. This therophytic nature ensures that the plant can effectively navigate its environmental constraints by focusing its biological energy on a quick reproductive cycle before the onset of environmental stress.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Asteriscus aquaticus is characterized by a flowering period that begins in March and extends through August, though the timing can be variable. During this cycle, the plant produces flowers that are typically yellow in color, with a width ranging from a minimum of 1.5 cm to a maximum of 3 cm. Following pollination, the plant develops fruit in the form of an achene. These fruits are indehiscent, meaning they do not open at maturity to release their contents. The seeds produced are notably small and uniform in weight, with a recorded mean, minimum, and maximum seed mass of 0.00078 g.
