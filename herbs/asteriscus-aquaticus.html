Asteriscus aquaticus

Asteriscus aquaticus, commonly known as a unique aquatic aster, is a fascinating medicinal plant found in specialized wetland ecosystems. Renowned for its distinct floral structure, this species is studied for its potential bioactive compounds. Exploring its botanical properties offers valuable insights into natural pharmacology and the preservation of aquatic biodiversity.

  • Scientific name: Asteriscus aquaticus
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales, it is further categorized into the family Asteraceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Asteriscus, which includes the species Asteriscus aquaticus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Asteriscus aquaticus, is referred to by various names depending on the language. In German, it is called Einjähriger Strandstern, while in Bulgarian, it is known as воден астерискус, and in Arabic (Arz), it is identified as استريسكوس اكواتيكوس.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across several European regions. In Middle Europe, it can be found growing in Belgium, Germany, and Poland. Its presence also extends to the mountainous landscapes of Switzerland. Moving toward the Mediterranean, the species is located in Southwestern Europe within the Baleares. Together, these locations define the natural range of Asteriscus aquaticus.

Plant Parts

This plant, Asteriscus aquaticus, is primarily utilized in traditional medicinal practices through its aerial part. This portion of the plant includes the stems, leaves, and flowering structures that grow above the ground. When harvesting the aerial part, practitioners typically collect the entire visible structure to ensure that the bioactive compounds concentrated in the foliage and blooms are captured for preparation.
