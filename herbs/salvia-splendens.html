Salvia splendens

Scarlet sage

Salvia splendens, commonly known as scarlet sage, is a vibrant ornamental perennial prized for its striking red blooms. Beyond its aesthetic appeal in gardens, this plant holds traditional significance in herbalism. While primarily grown for decoration, its unique botanical properties continue to inspire interest in natural medicinal applications.

  • Scientific name: Salvia splendens
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Salvia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Salvia splendens, is widely recognized by various names across different Chinese linguistic regions. In Simplified Chinese, including the variants used in mainland China (zh-cn), Singapore (zh-sg), and general usage (zh-hans), it is commonly called 一串红. This same name is also used in Hong Kong (zh-hk) using Traditional Chinese characters, written as 一串紅. In regions that utilize Traditional Chinese characters, such as in Taiwan (zh-hant), it is also referred to as 一串紅.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several regions of the European continent. In Middle Europe, its presence can be documented within the territories of Czechia and Slovakia. Moving toward the southern reaches, it is found throughout Southeastern Europe in both Bulgaria and Romania. The species also extends into Eastern Europe, specifically occupying areas of South European Russia. Finally, its range continues through the Eastern European landscape of Ukraine.

Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as scarlet sage, is primarily admired for its vibrant and showy flowers, which grow in dense, upright spikes. These blossoms are typically a brilliant red, though they can also be found in shades of pink, blue, or white, and they serve as a major attraction for pollinators like hummingbirds. While the plant is frequently grown for its ornamental beauty, the flowers are the most distinctive and characteristic feature of its anatomy.
