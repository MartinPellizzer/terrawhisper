Coast redwood, scientifically known as Sequoia sempervirens, is an iconic evergreen of the Pacific coast. While primarily celebrated for its immense stature, various cultures have historically utilized its bark and resins for their antimicrobial and astringent properties, offering traditional medicinal potential in treating skin ailments and inflammation.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Pinidae and order Pinales, it is a member of the family Cupressaceae. Ultimately, its scientific classification is defined by the genus Sequoiia.

This plant exhibits a highly fragmented geographical distribution across several distinct regions. In Northern Europe, its presence can be identified within Great Britain.
