Ripariosida hermaphrodita

Ripariosida hermaphrodita, commonly known as the dual-bloom riverside herb, is a versatile medicinal plant found in damp, riparian ecosystems. Renowned for its unique self-pollinating properties, it contains potent bioactive compounds used traditionally to treat inflammatory conditions and support digestive health, making it a vital subject for ethnobotanical research.

  • Scientific name: Ripariosida hermaphrodita
  • Family: Malvaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, placing it within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Malvales and the family Malvaceae. Finally, the species is identified by its genus, Ripariosida, and its specific epithet, hermaphrodita.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning both Europe and North America. In Middle Europe, its presence is documented within Germany as well as the Czechia-Slovakia region. Moving across the Atlantic, the species can be found in Eastern Canada, specifically throughout Ontario. Its range extends into the Northeastern United States, where it is located in Massachusetts. Additionally, the plant is recorded in the Midwestern state of Indiana.
