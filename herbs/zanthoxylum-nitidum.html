Zanthoxylum nitidum

What's the taxonomical classification of Zanthoxylum nitidum?

Zanthoxylum nitidum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Sapindales. Further taxonomic classification places it in the family Rutaceae, where it is categorized under the genus Zanthoxylum, ultimately reaching its specific designation as the species nitidum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Zanthoxylum nitidum has a woody structure characterized by a medium-sized tree habit, pinnately compound leaves, and small, inconspicuous flowers. The leaves consist of multiple pairs of leaflets that are typically elliptic to lanceolate in shape with serrated margins. Its bark is often textured and provides protection for the internal vascular tissues of the trunk. The plant produces small fruits that are characteristic of the Rutaceae family, often appearing as fleshy or capsule-like structures. These morphological features collectively define its physical presence within its native tropical habitats.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Zanthoxylum nitidum provides medicinal value through its branches, flowers, fruit, leaves, and root. The bark from the branches and the roots are frequently harvested to prepare decoctions for treating various ailments. Additionally, the leaves are utilized in traditional remedies to manage systemic conditions. The flowers and the fruit also contain bioactive compounds that contribute to the plant's therapeutic applications. Each of these specific parts serves a unique role in the holistic medicinal practice involving this species.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", the medicinal parts of Zanthoxylum nitidum are the roots and stems. For 1-6 years old samples, the total contents of nitidine chloride and chelerythrine in roots and stems were 0.114%-0.256% and 0.030%-0.133%, respectively. Dried samples excluding branches from three plantation bases showed total contents of 0.308%±0.123% in Yunfu, 0.192%±0.025% in Maoming, and 0.197%±0.052% in Nanning. All these values were not less than 0.15%, meaning roots and stems of plants transplanted for more than 2.5 years can meet medical requirements. To ensure clinical effectiveness, it is recommended that the root and stem be harvested for medical use after the seedlings have been transplanted for more than 3 years.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, Zanthoxylum nitidum, is utilized in Traditional Chinese Medicine and various Southeast Asian folk practices to treat digestive ailments and skin conditions. Practitioners historically employed the bark and fruit to address symptoms like stomach pain, diarrhea, and intestinal parasites. In some local traditions, decoctions made from its components are applied topically to soothe inflammatory skin issues. The historical use of this species often relies on its perceived medicinal potency within regional herbal pharmacopeias. These traditional applications reflect a long-standing reliance on the plant's chemical properties for primary healthcare.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", the dry root of Zanthoxylum nitidum (ZN), known as Zanthoxyli Radix (ZR), is referred to as Liang Mian Zhen in China. This plant has been the preferred Chinese medicine for the treatment of inflammation and cancer disease at home and abroad. ZR serves as the core ingredient in anti-inflamatory traditional medicines, including Sanjiuweitai granules and Jinji tablets. A total of 184 chemical components have been identified in ZR, which include 91 alkaloids, 22 lignans, 4 flavonoids, 19 coumarins, 17 terpenoids, and 31 other types. Pharmacological studies have proven that ZR possesses various biological activities, particularly in anti-inflammation.

What are the pharmacological activities of Zanthoxylum nitidum?

This plant has potent pharmacological activities including analgesia, anti-inflammation, antibacterial, anti-cancer, and antioxidant properties. Its bioactive compounds work to reduce pain sensations and suppress inflammatory responses in various biological models. The plant also demonstrates significant ability to inhibit the growth of harmful bacteria and neutralize free radicals through its antioxidant mechanisms. Furthermore, research indicates that its chemical constituents possess anti-cancer potential by targeting malignant cell proliferation. These diverse medicinal qualities make it a valuable subject for pharmaceutical development and natural product research.

According to a study published by "Zhong yao cai = Zhongyaocai = Journal of Chinese medicinal materials", the root and stem of Zanthoxylum nitidum both demonstrate pharmacological activities including anti-contusion injury, analgesia, and anti-inflammation. Both parts of the plant relieved the exterior and histological symptoms of rats' injury legs that were struck by hammer. Regarding analgesic effects, Zanthoxylum nitidum decreased the numbers of mice's writhing and enhanced the pain threshold of mice on a hot plate. The plant also inhibited the edema of rats induced by carrageenan and suppressed the granuloma of rats induced by cotton pellets. Ultimately, the stem of Zanthoxylum nitidum showed similar effects in anti-contusion injury, analgesia, and anti-inflammation compared to the root.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds nitidumalkaloid A, nitidumalkaloid B, nitidumalkaloid C, chelerythrine, and nitidine chloride. These specific alkaloids are primary bioactive constituents identified within the chemical profile of Zanthoxylum nitidum. Researchers study these substances to understand their potential pharmacological properties and biological activities. The presence of these complex molecules contributes to the plant's overall medicinal significance in traditional practices. Detailed chemical analysis helps isolate each compound for further therapeutic investigation.

According to a study published by "Drug metabolism and disposition: the biological fate of chemicals", the roots of Zanthoxylum nitidum (Roxb.) DC contain the pharmacologically active alkaloid nitidine chloride (NC), which is a polyaromatic hydrocarbon. This compound significantly inhibited CYP1A1- and 1B1-catalyzed ethoxyresorufin O-deethylation activity with IC50 values of 0.28 ± 0.06 μM and 0.32 ± 0.02 μM, respectively. Nitidine chloride showed only slight inhibition of CYP1A2 activity with an IC50 > 50 μM. Kinetic analysis showed NC competitively inhibited CYP1B1 with a Ki value of 0.47 ± 0.05 μM. Additionally, NC acted as a mixed type inhibitor on CYP1A1 with Ki and KI values of 0.14 ± 0.04 and 0.19 ± 0.09 μM, respectively.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is used to create various medicinal herbal preparations including EtOH extract, Jinji tablets, Sanjiuweitai granules, essential oil, and supercritical extracts. These diverse formulations leverage the chemical properties of the species for therapeutic applications. The EtOH extract and supercritical extracts are often utilized to isolate specific bioactive compounds through solvent-based processes. Additionally, the plant is processed into solid forms like Jinji tablets and Sanjiuweitai granules for easier administration. The essential oil provides a concentrated aromatic and medicinal liquid component derived from the plant material.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", the chemical constituents of the roots of Zanthoxylum nitidum were studied using column chromatography on Silica gel and Sephadex LH - 20. From the CHCl3 fraction and n-butanol fraction of the EtOH extract of the roots, 10 compounds were isolated and identified. These compounds include 2, 4-dihydroxypyrimidine (1), syringic acid (2), 2, 6-dimethoxy-1, 4-benzoquinone (3), 4-hydroxybenzoic acid (4), ethylparaben (5), (Z)-3-(2, 3, 4-trimethoxyphenyl) acrylic acid (6), 5, 6, 7-trimethoxycoumarin (7), stigmast-9 (11)-en-3-ol (8), daucosterol (9), and beta-sitosterol (10). Compounds 1-9 were isolated and identified from the roots of Zanthoxylum nitidum for the first time. This research also marks the first isolation of compound 6 as a natural product.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause skin irritation, allergic reactions, and potential gastrointestinal distress if ingested improperly. The chemical compounds within the bark and roots may trigger contact dermatitis in sensitive individuals upon direct skin exposure. When consumed, the bioactive alkaloids might lead to nausea or stomach discomfort in some users. There is also limited clinical data regarding the systemic toxicity of its long-term use in humans. Consequently, caution is necessary when handling or utilizing this species for medicinal purposes.
