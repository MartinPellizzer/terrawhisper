Ecology

The ecology of Persea major is characterized by its presence across diverse Brazilian biomes, demonstrating a significant capacity for adaptation to varying moisture regimes and vegetation structures. It is commonly found inhabiting the Cerrado (latu sensu), where it must contend with more seasonal water availability and distinct dry periods, as well as within the Floresta Estacional Semi-decidual, where its growth patterns align with the seasonal shedding of leaves. Furthermore, its distribution extends into the more humid and stable environments of the Floresta Ombrófila (also known as Floresta Pluvial), where high precipitation and consistent moisture levels support its physiological requirements. This broad ecological range allows the species to occupy multiple successional stages and environmental niches, ranging from more open, fire-adapted landscapes to dense, closed-canopy tropical rainforests.
