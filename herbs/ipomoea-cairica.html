Ipomoea cairica

Ipomea cairica, commonly known as Railroad Vine or Bindweed, is a vigorous climbing perennial found in tropical regions. Renowned for its medicinal versatility, this plant is utilized in traditional healing to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and digestive issues. Its bioactive compounds offer significant potential for modern pharmacological research and development.

  • Scientific name: Ipomoea cairica
  • Family: Convolvulaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Solanales and the family Convolvulaceae. Ultimately, the medicinal species is identified by the genus Ipomoea.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ipomoea cairica, is recognized by several distinct common names across different linguistic traditions. In Southern Min, it is referred to as Chhiok-hio̍h-khan-gû, while in Chinese, its names vary slightly by regional script and usage: it is known as 五爪金龙 (zh-hans) and 五爪金龍 (zh-hant) in formal writing, and is specifically called 五爪金龍 (zh-hk) in Hong Kong and 五爪金龙 (zh-cn) in mainland China.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across several distinct regions, including parts of Southwestern Europe. Within this area, it can be found in both Spain and the Baleares islands. Its presence also extends into Southeastern Europe, specifically documented in Sicilia. Furthermore, the species is located in Northern Africa, notably within Egypt. Finally, its range reaches the Macaronesia region, specifically documented in the Canary Is.
