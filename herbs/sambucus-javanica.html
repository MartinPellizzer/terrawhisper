Sambucus javanica

What's the taxonomical classification of Sambucus javanica?

Sambucus javanica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is classified under the order Dipsacales. It is a member of the family Viburnaceae, specifically falling within the genus Sambucus, and is identified by its unique species name, javanica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Sambucus javanica has decumbent to erect stems that reach heights of up to two meters, characterized by opposite, pinnately compound leaves with five to seven serrated leaflets. The plant features terminal cymes of creamy white, five-lobed flowers that emerge from clusters of small, inconspicuous buds. Its fruit consists of small, globose, dark purple to black berries that hang in dense bunches. The leaf petioles and stems often exhibit a reddish tint, providing a visual contrast to the green foliage. This shrubby species typically develops a woody base as it matures in its natural habitat.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Sambucus javanica is utilized medicinally through the application of its berries and leaves. The dark berries are often processed into syrups or decoctions to treat respiratory ailments and boost the immune system. Meanwhile, the leaves are frequently employed in topical preparations to reduce inflammation or skin irritations. Both parts of the plant contain various bioactive compounds that contribute to its therapeutic properties. Careful preparation is essential to ensure these botanical components are used safely for their intended healing purposes.

According to a study published by "Tropical life sciences research", the ethanol extract of Sambucus javanica berries and leaves is used for its medicinal properties. The research evaluated the immunomodulatory activities of these Sambucus javanica extracts in a chloramphenicol-induced anemia aplastic mouse model using BALB/c mice. These extracts could promote the population number of regulatory T-cells and naive cytotoxic T-cells. Furthermore, the Sambucus javanica extracts reduced the inflammation and necrotic incidence in the CMP-induced mouse aplastic anemia model. These results suggest that Sambucus javanica has a therapeutic effect on aplastic anemia by acting as an immunomodulatory agent.
