Persicaria limbata

Persicaria limbata, commonly known as the African knotweed, is a versatile perennial herb native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its medicinal properties, it is frequently utilized in traditional healing to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and gastrointestinal issues. This resilient plant serves as a vital resource in many local pharmacopeias.

  • Scientific name: Persicaria limbata
  • Family: Polygonaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Under the subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized within the order Caryophyllales and the family Polygonaceae. Finally, it is classified under the genus Persicaria.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Persicaria limbata, is recognized by various common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In English, it is frequently referred to as the limbata knotweed, while in Spanish, it is commonly called nudo de agua. In Portuguese, the plant is known as knotweed-de-água, and in French, it is identified as la renouée limbata.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution spanning across several regions of the African continent. In Northern Africa, its presence is documented within the borders of Egypt. Moving toward the western portion of the continent, it is found throughout various West Tropical African nations. Specifically, the species inhabits the territories of Benin, Burkina, and Guinea. Additionally, its range extends further south to include the Ivory Coast.
