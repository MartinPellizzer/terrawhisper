Pleiocarpa bicarpellata

Pleiocarpia bicarpellata, commonly known as a vital medicinal shrub in certain tropical regions, holds significant ethnobotanical importance. Renowned for its diverse bioactive compounds, this plant is traditionally utilized to treat various ailments. Research continues to explore its potential pharmacological properties, highlighting its value in developing modern therapeutic treatments.

  • Scientific name: Pleiocarpa bicarpellata
  • Family: Apocynaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Gentianales, it is classified under the family Apocynaceae and the genus Pleiocarpa. Specifically, the species is identified as Pleiocarpa bicarpellata.

Common Names

This plant, Pleiocarpia bicarpellata, is known by various common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In English, it is referred to as the bicarpellate pleiocarp, while in French, it is called pleiocarpia bicarpellata. In local contexts within its native regions, such as in Swahili, it is known as mnyonyo, and in certain parts of Africa, it is also referred to as kinyonyo.

Distribution

This plant is native to the West-Central Tropical Africa region. Its geographical range specifically includes the nation of Cameroon. Furthermore, it can be found growing within the borders of Gabon. The species is also distributed throughout the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Finally, its presence extends to the territories of Congo and Cabinda.
