Genista canariensis

Genista canariensis, commonly known as the Canary Island broom, is a resilient shrub endemic to the Macaronesian islands. Renowned for its vibrant yellow blooms, this botanical treasure plays a vital role in stabilizing volcanic soils and supporting local biodiversity, serving as a fascinating subject for ecological and medicinal study.

  • Scientific name: Genista canariensis
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, placing it within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Fabales and the family Fabaceae, which is characteristic of the legume group. Within this hierarchy, it is specifically identified under the genus Genista.

Common Names

This plant is known by various names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In Thai, it is referred to as บรูมกานาเรียส, while in Faroese, its name is Kanaarenskroben. In Spanish, it is identified by several terms, including Cytisus canarienis, Retamón canario, and Cytisus hillebrandii.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a highly diverse and fragmented geographical distribution across several distant regions of the world. Within the Macaronesia archipelago, it can be found growing in the Canary Islands. Its presence extends significantly into the Indian Subcontinent, specifically spanning across India and Sri Lanka. Furthermore, the species has established itself in the Northwestern United States. In this particular American region, it is documented to inhabit both Oregon and Washington.
