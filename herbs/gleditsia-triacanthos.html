According to a study published by "Natural product research", methanolic extracts of Gleditsia triacanthos L. fruits yielded two mono-carbonyl curcumin analogues, specifically (1E,4E)-1,5-bis(4-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl)penta-1,4-dien-3-one [1] and (1E,4E)-1,5-bis(4-hydroxyphenyl)penta-1,4-dien-3-one [2]. These extracts also provided beta-sitosterol [3], apiigenin [4], quercetin-3-O-methyl ether [5], rutin [6], quercetin [7], naringenin [8], eriodictyol [9], vitexin [10], isovitexin [11], and gleditsiin A [12]. The isolated compounds [1] and [2] were assayed in 2 hepatic cancer cells, namely HepG-2 and Huh-7. Compound [1] exhibited high cytotoxic activity against HepG-2 cells with an IC50 value of 14.39 µM. This activity was compared with the standard IC50 value of 12.44 µM.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

According to a study published by "Phytochemistry", extracts of the cotyledons of germinated honey locust (Gleditsia triacanthos) seeds contain galactomanan as a reserve polysaccharide in the endosperm. The study examined fractions for a specific manno-6-kinase, but no evidence for such a kinase was obtained. Instead, research identified one particulate hexokinase as the major hexose-6-kinase fraction and two soluble hexokinase fractions as the minor portion. Additionally, a soluble fructo-6-kinase fraction was identified, which was further separated into 3 fractions. Kinetic studies on 1 of these fractions indicated specific structural requirements for the monosaccharide substrate regarding the beta-D-anomeric 2-OH, a 4-OH, and a -CH2OH substituent on C2.
