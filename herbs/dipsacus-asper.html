Dipsacus asper

Dipsacus asper, commonly known as teasel, is a distinctive biennial herb recognized by its prickly, architectural foliage and spiked flower heads. Native to Europe, this resilient plant thrives in disturbed soils. Beyond its ornamental appeal, it holds traditional significance in folk medicine for its various therapeutic potential and ecological value.

  • Scientific name: Dipsacus asper
  • Family: Caprifoliaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Dipsacus asper, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae within the order Dipsacales. Finally, it is a member of the family Caprifoliaceae and is situated within the genus Dipsacus.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across several distinct regions within East and Southeast Asia. In China, its presence is noted in the South-Central and Southeast territories, as well as in the high-altitude regions of Tibet. Its range extends into the Indian Subcontinent, specifically within the Assam region. Additionally, the species can be found throughout the Indo-China peninsula. Finally, its geographical reach includes the country of Myanmar.
