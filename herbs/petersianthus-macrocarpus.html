Petersianthus macrocarpus

Petersianthus macrocarpus, commonly known as the African mahogany or African nut tree, is a versatile medicinal plant native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its diverse pharmacological properties, various parts of the tree are utilized in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to digestive and inflammatory issues.

  • Scientific name: Petersianthus macrocarpus
  • Family: Lecythidaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Petersianthus macrocarpus, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Ericales and the family Lecythidaceae. It is further identified by its specific genus, Petersianthus.

Common Names

This plant, Petersianthus macrocarpus, is known by several different common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In French, it is referred to as Essia, while in Turkish, it is commonly called Abale. In the Cerdinian language, it is identified as Ессия (Юра), and in Japanese, the name used is アバレ. Additionally, in the Akan language, the plant is known as Esia.

Distribution

This plant is primarily located within the West Tropical Africa region. Its presence is notably documented in the country of Ghana. It also grows naturally throughout the territory of Guinea. Additionally, the species can be found across the landscapes of Ivory Coast and Liberia. Finally, its distribution extends further east into Nigeria.
