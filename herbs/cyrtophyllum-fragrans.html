Cyrtophyllum fragrans

Fagraea fragrans

Cryptophyllum fragrans, commonly known as the fragrant cryptophyllum, is a distinguished medicinal shrub native to tropical regions. Renowned for its aromatic foliage, this plant is traditionally utilized in herbal medicine to address various ailments. Its unique phytochemical profile offers significant potential for pharmacological research and holistic healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Cyrtophyllum fragrans
  • Family: Gentianaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Gentianales, it is further categorized into the family Gentianaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Cyrtophyllum.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Cryptophyllum fragrans, is referred to by several different common names depending on the language and region. In English, it is called Fagraea fragrans, while in Chinese, it is known as 香灰莉木. Within the Indonesian and Javanese linguistic contexts, it is commonly called tembesu (id) or tembésu (jv), and in Malay, it is identified as tembusu padang (ms).

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed throughout the Indo-China region. Specifically, its presence can be found across the various landscapes of Cambodia and Laos. It also grows naturally within the territories of Myanmar and Thailand. Furthermore, the species extends its reach to include the Andaman Islands. This diverse range highlights its significant ecological footprint across Southeast Asia.

This plant, Cryptophyllum fragrans, utilizes various components for its medicinal properties, most notably its leaves and bark. The leaves are frequently employed in traditional remedies, often used in infusions or decoctions to treat ailments such as fever or digestive issues.
