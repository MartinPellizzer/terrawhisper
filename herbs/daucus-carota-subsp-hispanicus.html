Daucus carota subsp. hispanicus

Daucus carota subsp. hispanicus, commonly known as a wild variety of wild carrot, is a distinctive medicinal herb native to Mediterranean climates. Renowned for its therapeutic properties, this subspecies is utilized in traditional herbalism to support digestive health, promote skin vitality, and offer potent antioxidant benefits through its essential compounds.

  • Scientific name: Daucus carota subsp. hispanicus
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further organized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Apiales, falling into the family Apiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Daucus as the subspecies hispanicus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Daucus carota subsp. hispanicus, is referred to by several different common names depending on the region and language. In English, it is most frequently called wild carrot or Queen Anne's lace, while in Spanish, it is known as zanahoria silvestre. In French, it is called carotte sauvage, and in Italian, it is referred to as carota selvatica.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across several regions within Southwestern Europe. Specifically, it can be found growing in the territories of France and Spain. Its presence also extends to the island of Sardegna within the same southwestern zone. Moving toward Southeastern Europe, the subspecies is located in Italy. Finally, it inhabits the Sicilian region, which is also part of Southeastern Europe.
