Polyspora axillaris

Fried egg plant

Polyspora axillaris, commonly known as the axillary polypora, is a significant medicinal plant prized for its diverse therapeutic properties. Rich in bioactive compounds, it is traditionally utilized to treat inflammatory conditions and infections. Understanding its phytochemical profile is essential for exploring its potential in modern pharmacological advancements and natural healing.

  • Scientific name: Polyspora axillaris
  • Family: Theaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Ericales, it falls under the family Theaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is established within the genus Polyspora.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Polyospora axillaris, is referred to by various names depending on the language and region. In Chinese, it is known as 大头茶 (simplified) and 大頭茶 (traditional), while in Spanish, Vietnamese, and Italian, it is commonly referred to by its synonym, Gordonia axillaris.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning several key regions across East and Southeast Asia. Within China, its distribution includes the tropical island of Hainan as well as the broader Southeast China region. The species is also found in Eastern Asia, specifically documented on the island of Taiwan. Moving into the Indo-China peninsula, the plant extends its reach into the territory of Myanmar. Finally, its documented presence continues southward through the landscapes of Thailand.
