Ipomoea carnea

Hedge glory

Ipomoea carnea, commonly known as the creeping morning glory, is a versatile medicinal plant valued in traditional healing. Renowned for its bioactive compounds, it is frequently utilized to treat skin ailments, inflammation, and digestive issues. This resilient climber offers significant therapeutic potential for holistic wellness and natural pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Ipomoea carnea
  • Family: Convolvulaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered as Solanales and falls under the family Convolvulaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Ipomoea.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ipomoea carnean, is referred to by various common names depending on the region and language. In Hindi, it is commonly called बेहया, while in Chinese, it is identified as 藤树牵牛 (zh-hans) and 藤树牵牛 (zh-cn). The traditional characters are used in Taiwan and Hong Kong, where it is known as 藤樹牽牛 (zh-hant) and 藤樹牽牛 (zh-hk), respectively.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning across the African continent. In Northern Africa, its presence is documented within the territories of Egypt and Libya. Moving toward the western regions, it is found throughout West Tropical Africa. Specifically, its distribution includes the nations of Burkina, Guinea, and Ivory Coast. These various locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in different tropical and subtropical environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, Ipomoea carneol, offers various components that are utilized in traditional medicine. The delicate flowers, specifically each individual trumpet-shaped flower, are often noted for their aesthetic and medicinal potential. Furthermore, the foliage is highly valued, as both the potent leaf juice and a more concentrated leaf extract are frequently employed in topical or oral applications to harness the plant's healing properties.
