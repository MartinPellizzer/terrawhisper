What's the taxonomical classification of Hemsleya delavayi?

Hemsleya delaavay belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is positioned in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic progression, the plant is part of the order Cucurbitales and falls under the family Cucurbitaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Hemleysa and is specifically designated as the species delaayi.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Hemsleya delaavay has large, deeply divided, tripinnate leaves that can reach up to 60 centimeters in length and possess thin, membranous textures with serrated margins. The plant is a perennial fern characterized by a creeping rhizome that often forms compact tufts. Its fronds emerge from a central base and are supported by long, slender stipes that may show slight pigmentation. The underside of the leaflets contains specialized fertile structures called sori, which are arranged in distinct rows between the veins. These morphological features allow the species to thrive in the shaded, moist environments of high-altitude forests.
