Erigeron breviscapus

What's the taxonomical classification of Erigeron breviscapus?

Erigeron breviscapus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Its taxonomic hierarchy continues through the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, placing it under the order Asterales. As a member of the family Asteraceae, it is categorized under the genus Erigeron, with its specific identity defined by the species name breviscapus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Erigeron breviscapus has a low-growing, perennial habit characterized by short, erect stems that typically reach heights of 5 to 20 centimeters and bear small, white to pale lavender flower heads. The foliage consists of basal leaves that are generally spatulate or oblanceolate in shape and often possess fine, spreading hairs along their margins. Stem leaves are fewer in number, smaller, and more widely spaced than the basal rosette. Each inflorescence features numerous narrow ray florets surrounding a central disc of yellow tubular florets. The overall structure of the plant is compact, reflecting its adaptation to open, rocky, or montane environments.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude regions of the southwestern United States, specifically within the states of Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico, and Utah. It primarily inhabits montane and subalpine environments where rocky slopes and open woodlands provide suitable growing conditions. Its distribution is often restricted to specific mountain ranges that offer the necessary cool temperatures and well-drained soils. Because it thrives at higher elevations, its presence is frequently tied to localized microclimates found in the Rocky Mountains and the Mogollon Rim. How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-drained, sandy or rocky soil and full sun exposure to thrive in its natural alpine environment.

It should be planted in locations that prevent moisture from stagnating around its shallow root system. Because it is a high-altitude species, it benefits from cool summer temperatures and significant drainage to avoid root rot. Providing a gritty soil mix helps mimic its native rocky habitats.

Regular watering is necessary during establishment, but the plant is quite drought-tolerant once its root system is mature.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Erigeron breviscapus utilizes its flowers, leaves, roots, seeds, and stems for various medicinal applications. The dried flowers are often prepared as a soothing tea to treat digestive upset or mild inflammation. Extracts from the leaves are applied topically to help heal small skin abrasions and irritations. The roots are decocted to address internal ailments and support overall systemic health. Additionally, the seeds and stems are integrated into traditional remedies to balance bodily functions and promote general wellness.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", scutellarin is a flavonoid with remarkable pharmacological activity found in Erigeron breviscapus. HPLC analysis showed that the scutellarin content was highest in leaves, followed by the flowers and stems, while it was not detected in the root. The full-length CHS gene sequence used to study this biosynthesis was 1 270 bp and encoded 405 amino acids. Research indicated that CHS expression levels and scutellarin content in different parts of Erigeron breviscapus have a positive correlation with r = 0.761 and P < 0.05.

Hand-mazz (EB) has been used in traditional Tibetan medicine to treat meningitis, polyneuritis, hepatitis, adenolymphitis, and enteronitis. The study also found that myeloperoxidase (MPO) activity was strongly reduced in the EB-treated groups.

These diverse biological properties suggest that its chemical constituents can effectively combat oxidative stress and cellular damage. The anti-inflammatory and anti-tumor capabilities indicate a potential role in managing chronic diseases and regulating abnormal cell growth. Such multifaceted medicinal potential makes Erigeron breviuscapus a significant subject for further pharmacological research.

According to a study published by "Physiology and molecular biology of plants : an international journal of functional plant biology", the flavonoids found in Erigeron breviscapus have significant anti-inflammaroty and cardiovascular therapeutic effects. The gene EbMYBP1 was cloned from Erigeron breviscapus and positively regulates flavonoid synthesis. In studies involving EbMYBP1-OE lines, specifically OE8, OE10, and OE15, a higher level of UV-B tolerance was observed in seedlings and leaves compared to the WT line. These lines showed a significant decrease in reactive oxygen species and an increase in antioxidant enzyme expression after UV-B treatment. Overall, EbMYBP1 modulates ROS scavengers and upregulates stress response genes to increase UV-B tolerance.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds breviscapine, erigeronone C, erigeronone D, eriogsteter B, and scutellarein. These specific phytochemicals are secondary metabolites that contribute to the biological profile of Erigeron brevicapus. Research into these substances often focuses on their potential antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. The presence of these diverse chemical constituents highlights the complex chemical makeup of the species. Scientists study these compounds to better understand how they interact with human physiological systems.

According to a study published by "Yao xue xue bao = Acta pharmaceutica Sinica", the dried whole plant of Erigeron breviscapus (Vant.) Hand-Mazz contains a flavonoid mixture known as breviscapine. The main effective constituent of this mixture is scutellarin. In a study involving 18 male Sprague-Dawley rats, breviscapine injection was administered at a dose of 20 mg x kg(-1), i.v. This investigation examined the interaction between scutellarin and valsartan at a dose of 15 mg x kg-, i.g. The data showed that the plasma clearance (CL(p)) and the bile clearance (CL(b)) of scutellarin were reduced significantly when co-administered with valsartan.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is used to create several specific medicinal herbal preparations including breviscapiine injection, Yindan xinnaotong soft capsules, decoction pieces, and ethanol extract. The Erigeron breviscapus preparation process often involves drying the plant material into decoction pieces for traditional tea-like brewing. Scientists also utilize an ethanol extract to isolate active compounds for more concentrated medicinal applications. These chemical constituents are further refined to produce specialized forms such as the breviscapiine injection. Finally, the Yindan xinnaotong soft capsules represent a processed pharmaceutical delivery method for the plant's therapeutic properties.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", Erigeron breviscapus preparation was administered to 12 rats at a dose of 1.5 mg.kg-1 weight, i.p. following a 2 hour right middle cerebral artery occlusion. After reperfusion for 4 hours and 6 hours, the Erigeron breviscapus preparation group showed decreased hyperintense signals in the right cerebrum and decreased T2 values compared to the untreated group. Treatment with the preparation led to an increased level of NAA, a decreased ratio of Cr/NAA and Cho/NAA, and no lactose was observed. These results indicate that Erigeron breviscapus preparation is beneficial to the reestablishment of blood circulation and the improvement of neuronal metabolism and survival in the ischemic brain.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause allergic skin reactions such as contact dermatitis, redness, or itching if it comes into direct contact with sensitive skin. Individuals with known sensitivities to the Asteraceae family may experience more severe dermatological irritation. Inhalation of pollen from the flowers might also trigger respiratory symptoms like sneezing or coughing in highly sensitive people. While systemic toxicity is not widely documented, caution is advised when handling any wild flora to avoid unexpected physical responses. Always monitor the site of contact for any swelling or persistent discomfort after exposure.
