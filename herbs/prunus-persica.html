The reproduction of Prunus persica is characterized by a sexual process, as asexual reproduction is absent in this species. The reproductive cycle begins with the flowering stage, which typically occurs during the month of April. The flowers are relatively small, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 2.5 cm to a maximum of 4 cm. The pollination syndrome is primarily biotic, relying on insects to facilitate the transfer of pollen. Following successful pollination, the plant develops a fleshy fruit classified as a drupe. These fruits exhibit significant morphological variation, with a mean length of approximately 3.82 cm (reaching a maximum of 7 cm) and a mean width of 3.3 cm (with a minimum width recorded at 5 cm and a maximum of 7 cm). The fruit is indehiscent, meaning it does not split open at maturity. Seed dispersal follows a zoochorous syndrome, where animals play a key role in spreading the seeds.
