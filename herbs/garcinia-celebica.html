The physiology of Garcinia celebica is characterized by a metabolic framework typical of many tropical woody species, utilizing a C3 photosynthetic pathway for carbon fixation. This mechanism involves the initial fixation of CO2 into a three-carbon compound via the enzyme RuBisCO, which is the primary method through which the plant synthesizes organic compounds from light energy and atmospheric gases. In terms of nutrient acquisition, the plant does not function as a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationships with nitrogen-fixing bacteria required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms, meaning it relies on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium present in the soil through its root system to satisfy its nitrogen requirements for protein and nucleic acid synthesis.
