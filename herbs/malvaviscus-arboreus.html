Malvaviscus arboreus

Turkcap

Malvaviscus arboreus, commonly known as the Sea Hibiscus, is a resilient coastal shrub prized for its medicinal properties. Renowned for its large, fragrant flowers, this plant offers therapeutic potential in traditional healing. Its leaves and bark are frequently utilized to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and various digestive issues.

  • Scientific name: Malvaviscus arboreus
  • Family: Malvaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further categorized under the order Malvales and the family Malvaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is established within the genus Malvaviscus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning several distinct regions across Africa and Asia. In Northern Africa, its presence is specifically noted within the borders of Tunisia. Moving into the tropical zones, it can be found in West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically in Cameroon and the DR Congo. Its distribution also extends to Northeast Tropical Africa, where it grows in Chad. Finally, the species reaches the Indian Subcontinent, documented in the region of Assam.

Plant Parts

This plant, Malvaviscus arboreus, commonly known as Turk's cap, features distinct morphological characteristics across its various components. The leaves are typically simple, ovate to heart-shaped, and possess serrated margins that provide a lush, green backdrop for the plant. The flowers are perhaps its most striking feature, consisting of bright red, tubular structures that lack petals and instead showcase prominent, protruding stamens. Both the leaves and the flowers are integral to the plant's identity, with the vibrant blossoms acting as a major attractant for pollinators like hummingbirds.
