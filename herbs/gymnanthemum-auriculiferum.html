Gymnanthemum auriculiferum

Gymnanthemum auriculiferum, commonly known as the ear-leaved gymnema, is a distinctive medicinal herb native to specific mountainous regions. Renowned for its bioactive compounds, it is traditionally studied for its potential anti-diabetic properties. This plant plays a significant role in ethnobotanical research, offering insights into natural metabolic health management.

  • Scientific name: Gymnanthemum auriculiferum
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Under the subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized within the order Asterales and the family Asteraceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Gymnanthemum.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Gymnanthemum auriculiferum, is identified by various common names depending on the linguistic context. In English, it is frequently referred to as gymnanthemum, while in German, it is known as Ohriges Gymnanthemum. Moving into other regional identifiers, it is called gymnanthemum auriculiferum in French and gymnanthemum auriculiferum in Spanish.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across various regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence has been documented in Nigeria. Moving into West-Central Tropical Africa, the species can be found in Cameroon and the Congo. Additionally, its range extends to the eastern parts of West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically in Burundi and Rwanda. Together, these locations define the known habitats for this medicinal species.
