Litsea, scientifically known as Litsea cubeba, is a versatile tropical evergreen tree highly valued in traditional medicine. Native to Southeast Asia, its aromatic leaves and fruits possess potent antimicrobial and antioxidant properties. Often used to treat digestive issues and skin ailments, this plant remains a vital natural therapeutic resource.

The physiology of Litsea cubeba is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, indicating its reliance on the standard carbon fixation process common to many woody tropical species. The plant's vegetative structure is defined by its foliage, which features elliptic leaf forms with entire margins and an absence of both thorns and spines. The leaves exhibit significant morphological variation in size, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 4 cm to a maximum of 11 cm, maintaining a mean length of approximately 7.5 cm. Correspondingly, the leaf width varies from a minimum of 1.1 cm to a maximum of 2.4 cm, with an average width of 1.75 cm. Furthermore, Litsea cubeba does not function as a nitrogen fixer, meaning it relies on external soil nutrient availability rather than symbiotic nitrogen fixation to support its physiological processes.
