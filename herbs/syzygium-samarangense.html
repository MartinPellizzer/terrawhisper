Syzygium samarangense

Wax apple

Syzygium samarangense, commonly known as the Samarangan guava, is a rare medicinal plant native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its bioactive properties, it is traditionally used to treat various ailments, including digestive issues and inflammation. Exploring its phytochemical potential offers promising insights into developing new natural therapeutic treatments.

  • Scientific name: Syzygium samarangense
  • Family: Myrtaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Myrtales and the family Myrtaceae, it is classified under the genus Syzygium. Specifically, its taxonomic identity is defined as Syzygium samarangense.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution spanning across different regions of the world. Within the Western Indian Ocean, it can be found growing in the Chagos Archipelago, Mauritius, and Réunion. It is also naturally occurring in the Seychelles, further establishing its presence in the island ecosystems of that ocean. Beyond the Indian Ocean, its range extends to the continent of Asia. Specifically, the species is documented to inhabit the South-Central region of China.
