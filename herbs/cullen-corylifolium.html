Blackdot

Cullen corylifolium · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (579 studies) · ★★★★★

Blackdot, scientifically known as Cullen corylifolium, is a medicinal herb belonging to the Apiaceae family. Widely used in traditional Ayurvedic medicine, it is prized for its bioactive compounds, particularly psoralens. This plant is primarily recognized for its potential therapeutic benefits in treating skin conditions like psoriasis and vitiligo.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across several key regions in Africa and the Indian Ocean. In Northeast Tropical Africa, it can be found inhabiting the landscapes of Djibouti, Somalia, and the island of Socotra. Its distribution extends significantly into the Western Indian Ocean as well. Specifically, the species is documented growing on the island of Mauritius. Furthermore, its range reaches the island of Réunion, completing its unique geographical footprint.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Cullen corylifolium, is highly valued in traditional medicine for the various therapeutic properties found within its different structures. The medicinal potency is often concentrated in the leaves, which are frequently used in herbal formulations, and the fruit, which serves as the vessel for its reproductive components. Within this fruit, the development of the seed is a crucial stage of the plant's life cycle, and the collection of these seeds is essential for propagation and further study. Furthermore, researchers often focus on the seed extract to isolate specific bioactive compounds, such as psoralens, which are responsible for much of the plant's pharmacological activity.
