Aconitum heterophyllum

Himalayan monkshood

Aconitum heterophyllum, commonly known as Atis, is a perennial herb native to the Himalayan regions. Highly valued in Ayurvedic and traditional medicine, this potent plant contains alkaloids used to treat fever, inflammation, and digestive issues. However, due to its high toxicity, it must be handled with extreme caution.

  • Scientific name: Aconitum heterophyllum
  • Family: Ranunculaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Aconitum heterophyllum, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, falling under the order Ranunculales. Finally, it is a member of the family Ranunculaceae and is situated within the genus Aconitum.

This plant exhibits a specific geographical range concentrated within the Indian Subcontinent. It is found growing in the mountainous terrains of Nepal, where its natural habitat is well-established. Additionally, the species is distributed across various regions of Pakistan. A significant portion of its population is also located within the West Himalaya range. These diverse locations highlight the plant's preference for the high-altitude environments found in these specific areas.

Plant Parts

This plant, Aconitum heterophyllum, possesses several distinct morphological features that are utilized in various medicinal applications. The leaf structure is typically deeply divided, providing a characteristic lush appearance to the foliage. Supporting the plant is a sturdy stem that elevates the flowers and leaves above the ground. Most importantly, the therapeutic properties are concentrated underground, where the plant develops thick, fleshy roots. Within this underground system, the development of specialized root tubers serves as a primary storage organ for nutrients and active alkaloids. Consequently, the entire root complex is often the most significant part harvested for its pharmacological potency.
