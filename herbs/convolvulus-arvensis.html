Reproduction

Reproduction in Convolvulus arvensis is primarily sexual, occurring through bisexual flowers that exhibit a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically attracting insects such as bees. The flowering period is variable, typically beginning between March and April and extending through to September. The flowers are pink in color, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 1.5 cm to a maximum of 2.5 cm. Self-fertilization is noted as absent, highlighting the importance of cross-pollination. Following fertilization, the plant produces dry, dehiscence-style capsules as its fruit type. These capsules vary in size, with a mean length of 0.675 cm (ranging from 0.5 to 0.8 cm) and a mean width of 0.7 cm (ranging from 0.5 to 0.7 cm). Each fruit is highly prolific, containing between 101 and 1000 seeds. The seeds themselves are quite small, with a mean length of 3.1 mm (ranging from 3 to 4 mm) and a mean volume of 14 mm³. Seed mass is also highly variable, with a mean of approximately 0.01356 g, though values can range from a minimum of 0.00035 g to a maximum of 0.03469 g. Dispersal of these seeds is facilitated through several syndromes, including anemochorous (wind), unspecialized, and anthropochorous (human-mediated) methods. Asexual reproduction is absent in this species.
