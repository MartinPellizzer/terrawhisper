Torreya jackii

What's the taxonomical classification of Torreya jackii?

Torreya jackii belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is situated in the subclass Pinidae and the order Pinales. Further taxonomic refinement places this plant in the family Taxaceae, which contains the genus Torreya, and it is specifically identified by the species name jackii.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Torreya jackii has linear, dark green leaves that are arranged in two ranks along the branches and possess a leathery texture with a distinctively pointed tip. The foliage grows in a dense, pyramidal shape, often appearing somewhat stiff to the touch. Its reproductive structures consist of small, inconspicuous male cones that produce pollen and larger, fleshy female cones that mature into reddish-brown seeds. These seeds are typically held within a thick, pulpy integument that helps facilitate dispersal. The bark of the tree is generally smooth and greyish, becoming more textured as the specimen matures.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Torreya jackii is utilized medicinally through its leaves, seeds, and twigs. The leaves are often processed to create extracts that may possess various bioactive properties. The seeds are highly valued for their nutritional content and potential therapeutic applications in traditional practices. Additionally, the twigs can be harvested and used in decoctions to prepare medicinal teas. These different components collectively contribute to the plant's diverse pharmacological profile.

According to a study published by "Phytochemistry", a phytochemical investigation on the MeOH extract of the leaves and twigs of Torreya jackii led to the isolation and characterization of 21 structurally diverse diterpenoids. Among these isolates, six are previously undescribed, which includes four abiettane-type torreyins A-D and two labdane-type torreyins E and F. Within the group, torreyins A-C are identified as rare 11,12-seco-abiettane type diterpenoids possessing a dilactone moiety. The compounds 7-oxo-dehydroabietic acid and 15-methoxy-7,13-abietadien-18-oic acid showed considerable inhibitory effects against ACC1 and PTP1B. These specific bioactivities were measured with IC50 values of 3.1 and 6.8 μM, respectively.
