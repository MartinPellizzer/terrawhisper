Common alder, scientifically known as Alnus glutinosa, is a deciduous tree renowned for its ecological importance and medicinal versatility. Traditionally used in herbalism, its bark and leaves possess astringent, antiseptic, and anti-inflammatory properties. It has long been utilized to treat skin irritations, gastrointestinal issues, and various inflammatory conditions.

Plant Parts

This plant, Alnus glutinosa, commonly known as the European alder, contains various components that are utilized in traditional medicine. The bark is highly valued for its astringent properties and is frequently applied topically to aid in wound healing by reducing inflammation and promoting tissue repair. In addition to the bark, the female catkins serve as a distinct morphological feature that can be harvested for specific herbal preparations. Furthermore, the aerial parts of the tree, which encompass the leaves, twigs, and branches, are often collected to create decoctions used in various therapeutic applications.
