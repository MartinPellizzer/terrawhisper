Alnus glutinosa

Common alder

Alnus glutinosa, commonly known as the European alder, is a versatile wetland tree prized for its medicinal properties. Historically used to treat skin inflammations, digestive issues, and respiratory ailments, its bark and leaves contain tannins and bioactive compounds that offer significant therapeutic potential in traditional and modern herbal medicine.

  • Scientific name: Alnus glutinosa
  • Family: Betulaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fagales, falling into the family Betulaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Alnus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Alnus glutinosa, is recognized by various common names across different languages and regions. In Bosnia, it is referred to as Crna joha, while in Poland, it is called olsza czarna. The Basque language identifies it as Haltz beltz, and in the Udmurt language, it is known as Сьӧд лулпу. Additionally, in the Vec language, it is known as Onaro moro.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several regions within Northern Europe. Specifically, its presence is well-documented in the country of Denmark. It can also be found growing naturally throughout Finland. The species is common in the landscapes of both Great Britain and Ireland. Finally, its range extends further north into the territories of Norway.

Plant Parts

This plant, Alnus glutinosa, commonly known as the European alder, contains various components that are utilized in traditional medicine. The bark is highly valued for its astringent properties and is frequently applied topically to aid in wound healing by reducing inflammation and promoting tissue repair. In addition to the bark, the female catkins serve as a distinct morphological feature that can be harvested for specific herbal preparations. Furthermore, the aerial parts of the tree, which encompass the leaves, twigs, and branches, are often collected to create decoctions used in various therapeutic applications.
