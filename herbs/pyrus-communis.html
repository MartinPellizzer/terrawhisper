Reproduction

The reproduction of Pyrus communis is characterized by a biotic pollination process, specifically utilizing an insect-based pollination syndrome. The flowering period typically commences in April and concludes in May, featuring white flowers arranged in a raceme inflorescence. These flowers vary in size, with a maximum length of 3 cm and a maximum width of 3 cm, and a minimum length of 1.2 cm. Notably, self-fertilization is absent, and there is no recorded asexual reproduction, indicating a reliance on sexual reproduction for genetic diversity. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fruit that varies in dimensions, with a mean length of 5 cm (ranging from a minimum of 1 cm to a maximum of 5 cm) and a mean width of 1.75 cm (ranging from 1.5 cm to a maximum of 2 cm). The dispersal syndrome is classified as zoochorous, specifically endozoochorous, meaning seeds are dispersed through the digestive tracts of animals.
