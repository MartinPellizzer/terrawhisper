Fat-hen, scientifically known as Chenopodium album, is a versatile annual herb found globally in disturbed soils. Often dismissed as a common weed, this resilient plant is rich in essential nutrients, including proteins, vitamins, and minerals, making it a valuable historical food source and a subject of medicinal interest.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, specifically falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Caryophyllales, it is classified under the family Amaranthaceae. It is further identified by the genus Chenopodium.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad presence across several regions in Northern Europe. In the northernmost reaches, it can be found growing in Iceland and Finland. Its distribution continues through the island of Førøya, where it adapts to the local climate. Furthermore, the species is well-established within the territories of Denmark and Great Britain. Together, these locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in various Northern European environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as lamb's quarters, is a versatile species where almost every component can be utilized. The entire whole plant is often studied for its nutritional and therapeutic properties, though the most frequently harvested part is the tender leaves, which are rich in vitamins and minerals. In traditional medicine, the plant is often prepared as a dried herb to be used in various remedies. Furthermore, the small, dark seeds are highly nutritious and can be collected for consumption, with each individual seed containing a concentrated amount of energy and essential nutrients.
