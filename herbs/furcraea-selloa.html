Scientifically known as Furcraea selloa, is a perennial succulent native to tropical regions. Commonly called agave or sisal, this hardy plant is prized for its fibrous leaves. Beyond industrial use, it holds significant medicinal value, traditionally utilized in various cultures to treat ailments ranging from skin irritations to digestive issues.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several distinct regions. In Southwestern Europe, its presence is noted within the territories of Portugal and Spain. It also thrives in the Macaronesian islands, specifically inhabiting the Canary Islands and Madeira. Furthermore, its range extends significantly to the Southern African region, particularly within the Cape Provinces. This wide dispersal demonstrates the adaptability of the species across different oceanic and continental environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, Furcraea selloa, is primarily valued for its large, succulent leaf structures, which grow in a dense rosette pattern. These long, sword-shaped leaves are fibrous and sturdy, containing various bioactive compounds that contribute to its medicinal properties. In traditional practices, the various components found within the leaf are often utilized for their therapeutic potential in treating different ailments.
