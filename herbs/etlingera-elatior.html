Etlingera elatior

Etlingera elatior, commonly known as the Torch Ginger, is a striking tropical perennial valued for both its ornamental beauty and medicinal properties. Widely used in Southeast Asian traditional medicine, its fragrant blooms and rhizomes possess antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial qualities, offering diverse therapeutic benefits for holistic wellness.

  • Scientific name: Etlingera elatior
  • Family: Zingiberaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Zingiberales, falling into the family Zingiberaceae. Specifically, it is a member of the genus Etlingera.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Etlingera elatior, is recognized by various regional names across different languages. In French, it is referred to as Rose de porcelaine, while in Acehnese, it is called Kala. Its Portuguese name is Rosa-de-porcelana, and in Javanese, it is known as Kecombrang. Additionally, in Sundanese, the plant is commonly called Honjé.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning multiple tropical regions across the globe. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found specifically around the Gulf of Guinea islands. Its presence extends into the Western Indian Ocean, where it inhabits both Comoros and Mauritius. Within the Asian continent, the species is located in South-Central China. Finally, its range reaches into Indo-China, particularly within the borders of Thailand.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the cardamom इला (Etlingera elatior), is a versatile medicinal herb where various components offer therapeutic value. The large, lanceolate leaves are often utilized for their aromatic properties and essential oils. The most striking feature is the large, showy inflorescence, which is characterized by its vibrant, waxy petals that attract pollinators. Beneath the soil, the plant develops thick, fleshy rhizomes, which are the primary parts harvested for medicinal use, often being processed alongside or referred to in conjunction with the roots for their potent bioactive compounds.
