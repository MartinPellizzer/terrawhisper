Hydnora africana

Hydnorah afriana, commonly known as the African Hydnora, is a fascinating, parasitic plant found in southern Africa. Lacking leaves and chlorophyll, it lives entirely underground, attaching to host roots. While not a traditional herb, its unique biology and pungent scent offer intriguing subjects for botanical and medicinal research.

  • Scientific name: Hydnora africana
  • Family: Aristolochiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Piperales, it is classified under the family Aristolochiaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Hydnora.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Hydnora africana, is recognized by various regional names across different languages and cultures. In South Africa, it is commonly referred to as Jakkalskoos in Afrikaans, while in Chinese, it is called 非洲白鷺花. Its names in other languages include гиднора африканская in Russian, Африкалық гиднора in Kazakh, and نبات هيدنورا أفريكانا in Arabic.
