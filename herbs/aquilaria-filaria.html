Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical range concentrated within the tropical regions of Southeast Asia and Oceania. Its presence is documented across the Malesia botanical region, specifically within the Maluku Islands. Additionally, it can be found inhabiting various parts of the Philippines. The species also extends into the Papuasia region, where it grows in New Guinea. These locations highlight its distribution within the humid, island ecosystems of the western Pacific.

Plant Parts

This plant, Aquilaria filaria, is most highly valued for the production of agarwood, a fragrant, resinous substance that forms within the heartwood. This precious material is not a natural part of the tree's healthy structure but is instead produced as a defense mechanism when the plant becomes infected by specific fungi or undergoes physical injury. As the tree reacts to these stressors, it produces a dark, aromatic resin that permeates the wood, transforming it into the highly sought-after agarwood used extensively in traditional medicines, incense, and luxury perfumes.
