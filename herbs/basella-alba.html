Basella alba

Vine spinach

Basella alba, commonly known as Malabar spinach, is a nutritious climbing vine prized in tropical cuisines. Renowned for its succulent leaves and medicinal properties, it serves as a potent antioxidant. Often used to support digestive health and combat inflammation, this versatile plant offers both culinary delight and holistic healing.

  • Scientific name: Basella alba
  • Family: Basellaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. It further falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Caryophyllales. Finally, its taxonomic hierarchy places it in the family Basellaceae and the genus Basella.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning across different climatic zones. In Middle Europe, its presence is noted within the regions of Czechia and Slovakia. Moving toward the West Tropical Africa region, the species is widely distributed. Specifically, it can be found in the nations of Benin, Burkina, and Ghana. Additionally, its habitat extends to the coastal areas of Guinea-Bissau.

Plant Parts

This plant, Basella alba, utilizes various anatomical structures for its growth and medicinal properties. The lush, succulent leaves are the most prominent feature, often used in traditional remedies for their cooling effects. These leaves grow along climbing stems that are thick and fleshy, providing the necessary support for the vine to ascend. Below the surface, the roots anchor the plant firmly into the soil to absorb essential nutrients. Whether one utilizes the tender leaf for culinary purposes or the entire vine for healing, every part of the plant plays a vital role in its biological function.
