Oyster plant, scientifically known as Acanthus mollis, is a striking perennial herb native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its elegant, white bracts and lush green foliage, it is highly valued in ornamental gardening. Beyond its beauty, this species holds significant traditional importance in various medicinal practices across tropical regions.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Acanthus mollis involves both sexual and asexual mechanisms, with the sexual process facilitated by bisexual flowers. These violet-colored flowers are organized into inflorescences that typically range from a minimum of 0.1 m to a maximum of 0.6 m in length. The flowering season generally begins in April and concludes in July, though it can vary. Pollination is driven by biotic factors, specifically through an insect pollination syndrome, and the plant lacks the ability for self-fertilization. Once fertilized, the plant produces fruit in the form of a dehiscent capsule. These capsules vary in length, with a mean of 3.5 cm, though they can be as small as 0.2 cm or as large as 0.35 cm. Each fruit is highly productive, containing between 101 and 1000 seeds. The seeds themselves have a consistent mean width and minimum width of 11.5 mm, with a mean seed mass of 0.208 g (which also serves as the minimum and maximum recorded mass). Seed dispersal is achieved through both autochorous (self-dispersal) and anthropochorous (human-mediated) syndromes.
