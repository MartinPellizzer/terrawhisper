Acanthus mollis

Oyster plant

Acanthus mollis, commonly known as the Bear's Breeches, is a striking perennial celebrated for its architectural foliage and dramatic spired flowers. Beyond its ornamental appeal in lush gardens, this hardy plant holds significant historical importance in traditional medicine, valued for its diverse therapeutic properties and unique botanical characteristics.

  • Scientific name: Acanthus mollis
  • Family: Acanthaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae in the order Lamiales, falling into the family Acanthaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Acanthus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Acanthus mollis, is referred to by various common names depending on the language. In Scottish Gaelic, it is called Acanto, while in Polish, it is known as Akant miękki. In Persian, the name is written as آکانتوس مولیس, and in French, it is commonly called Acanthe à feuilles molles. Finally, in Catalan, the plant is known as acant.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of locations across several European regions. In Northern Europe, its presence can be found in both Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward the center of the continent, it is also documented within Germany in Middle Europe. Its distribution extends to Southwestern Europe, where it inhabits the Baleares. Finally, the species is recorded in the island of Corse within the same southwestern territory.
