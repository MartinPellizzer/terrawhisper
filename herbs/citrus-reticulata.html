Citrus reticulata

Mandarine

Citrus reticulata, commonly known as the mandarin orange, is a highly valued medicinal plant celebrated for its therapeutic properties. Rich in vitamin C, flavonoids, and essential oils, it is traditionally used to enhance immunity, aid digestion, and alleviate respiratory issues, making it a cornerstone in various holistic healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Citrus reticulata
  • Family: Rutaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Sapindaales and the family Rutaceae, it is classified under the genus Citrus. Specifically, Citrus reticulata represents a significant species within this taxonomic hierarchy.

Common Names

This plant, scientifically known as Citrus reticulata, is recognized by various common names across different languages and cultures. In Poland, it is known as mandarynka, while in Bosnia and Herzegovina, it is referred to as Mandarina. In Malaysia, it is commonly called limau mandarin, and in Hungary, it is simply called mandarin. Additionally, in Estonia, it is identified as harilik mandariinipuu.

Distribution

This plant is primarily identified by its specific geographical presence in certain regions. It is native to and widely distributed throughout China. More specifically, its habitat is concentrated in the Southeast part of the country. This localized distribution plays a significant role in where the species can be harvested for medicinal purposes. Consequently, the Southeast Chinese landscape serves as a key environment for the growth of Citrus reticulata.

Plant Parts

This plant, Citrus reticulata, offers various therapeutic benefits through its different anatomical structures. The medicinal properties are often concentrated in the peel, which is the outermost layer of the fruit, and more specifically within the pericarpium, the complex fruit wall that houses the essential oils. While the entire peel is frequently used in traditional remedies, the seeds can also be harvested for specific chemical constituents. Furthermore, the root system of the tree is sometimes utilized in herbal preparations, ensuring that nearly every part of the organism contributes to its medicinal profile.
