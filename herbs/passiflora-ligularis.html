Passiflora ligularis

Sweet granadilla

Passiflora ligularis, commonly known as the tongue-leaf passionflower, is a striking climbing vine native to tropical regions. Characterized by its unique, elongated leaves and intricate, exotic blooms, this species belongs to the Passifloraceae family. It is highly valued in botanical studies for its ornamental beauty and diverse ecological roles.

  • Scientific name: Passiflora ligularis
  • Family: Passifloraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, it is classified under the family Passifloraceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Passiflora, with its species designation being Passiflora ligularis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Passiflora ligularis, is referred to by various regional names across different linguistic groups. In Simplified Chinese, it is called 甜西番果, while in Traditional Chinese, it is known as 甜百香果, and in Hong Kong, it is commonly identified as 甜熱情果. Furthermore, it is known as Tintin in the Qiang language and Konyal in Sundanese.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a remarkably broad and diverse geographical distribution across several distinct regions. In South Tropical Africa, it can be found specifically within Zimbabwe, while its presence extends to Eastern Asia in Taiwan. The species is also documented within the Indian Subcontinent, particularly in Sri Lanka. Moving toward the Malesia region, it inhabits the island of Jawa. Finally, its range reaches as far as the North-Central Pacific in Hawaii.
