Corn thistle, scientifically known as Cirsium arvense, is a resilient perennial herb belonging to the Asteraceae family. Often viewed as a weed, this plant possesses significant medicinal potential. Traditionally, its various parts have been utilized in folk medicine to address inflammatory conditions, digestive ailments, and skin irritations.

The reproduction of Cirsium arvense is characterized by a sexual process facilitated by bisexual flowers, which are capable of self-fertilization. The flowering period typically begins in July and extends through September. This plant utilizes a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects such as bees to ensure successful fertilization. Following pollination, the plant develops dry, indehiscent fruits classified as achenes. These fruits vary in size, with a mean length of 0.3625 cm (ranging from 0.3 to 0.45 cm) and a mean width of 0.115 cm (ranging from 0.1 to 0.15 cm). The resulting seeds are relatively uniform in dimension, maintaining a consistent length, width, and volume of approximately 3.5 mm, 1.3 mm, and 2 mm³, respectively. The seed mass fluctuates between a minimum of 0.0008 g and a maximum of 0.00213 g, with a mean mass of approximately 0.0015 g. Once mature, the seeds are dispersed via an anemochorous syndrome, meaning they are primarily spread by the wind. Asexual reproduction is notably absent in this species.
