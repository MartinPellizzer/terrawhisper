Afrocarpus gracilior

Afrocarpus gracilior, commonly known as a medicinal shrub within the Rubiaceae family, holds significant ethnobotanical importance. Found across various African landscapes, this plant is traditionally utilized for its therapeutic properties. Research explores its potential bioactive compounds, highlighting its role in treating diverse ailments and its value in pharmacological studies.

  • Scientific name: Afrocarpus gracilior
  • Family: Podocarpaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, placing it within the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Pinidae and the order Pinales. Finally, Afrocarpus gracilior is a member of the family Podocarpaceae and the genus Afrocarpus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Afrocarpous gracilior, is referred to by various names depending on the language and region. In German, it is called Schlanke Afrogelbholz, while in Afrikaans, it is known as Oos-Afrikaanse geelhout. Its names in other languages include アフリカマキ in Japanese, چوب‌زرد شرق آفریقا in Persian, and 细叶非洲杉 in Chinese.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern concentrated within the tropical regions of Africa. In Northeast Tropical Africa, its presence is documented across Eritrea, Ethiopia, and the territories of Sudan and South Sudan. The species also extends into East Tropical Africa, where it can be found in Kenya. Additionally, its range encompasses the landscape of Tanzania. Collectively, these locations highlight a regional focus within the eastern and northeastern parts of the continent.
