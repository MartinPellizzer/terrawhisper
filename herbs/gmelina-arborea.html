Gmelina arborea

Beechwood

Gmelina arborea, commonly known as Beechwood or Gamini, is a fast-growing deciduous tree native to South Asia. Renowned for its versatile medicinal properties, it is used in traditional Ayurveda to treat ailments ranging from digestive issues to skin disorders. Its leaves, bark, and fruit offer significant therapeutic benefits.

  • Scientific name: Gmelina arborea
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Gmelina.

Plant Parts

This plant, Gmelina arborea, possesses several parts that are utilized for their various therapeutic properties. The large, green leaves are frequently used in traditional medicine to treat ailments such as inflammation and skin issues, while the roots are valued for their ability to act as a tonic and aid in digestive health. The small, fragrant flowers often serve as indicators of the plant's seasonal vitality and contribute to its overall medicinal profile. The sturdy stems provide the structural integrity necessary for the tree to reach significant heights, and the thick stem bark is particularly prized for its medicinal extracts, often used to treat fever, coughs, and various internal discomforts.
