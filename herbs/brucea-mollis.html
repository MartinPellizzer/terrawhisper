Brucea mollis

Brucea mollis, commonly known as the soft Brucea, is a significant medicinal shrub native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, particularly bruceine, it is traditionally used to treat various ailments, including skin diseases and intestinal parasites, playing a vital role in both folk medicine and pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Brucea mollis
  • Family: Simaroubaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Sapindales and the family Simaroubaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Brucea, specifically identified by the species name Brucea mollis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Brucea mollis, is recognized by several different common names depending on the linguistic context and specific variety. In Chinese (zh), it is widely referred to as 柔毛鸦胆子, 鸦胆子, or 大果鸦胆子. Specifically, in both Mainland China (zh-cn) and Taiwan (zh-tw), the name 柔毛鸦胆子 is used to identify this medicinal species.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across several key regions in Asia. In China, it is specifically found within the South-Central and Southeast territories. Its presence extends into the Indian Subcontinent, where it inhabits the state of Assam. Furthermore, the species can be located throughout the country of Bangladesh. Finally, its range encompasses the diverse landscapes of the East Himalaya.
