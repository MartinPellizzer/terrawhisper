Dhundal tree

Xylocarpus granatum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (97 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Dhundal tree, scientifically known as Xylocarpus granatum, is a mangrove species native to the coastal regions of the Indo-Pacific. Renowned for its medicinal properties, it is used in traditional systems to treat skin diseases, inflammation, and digestive issues. Its unique chemical constituents offer significant therapeutic potential in pharmacology.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several distinct regions of the African continent. In Northeast Tropical Africa, its presence is noted specifically in Somalia. Moving further south, it is found throughout East Tropical Africa in both Kenya and Tanzania. The species also extends into South Tropical Africa, where it inhabits Mozambique. Finally, its range reaches into Southern Africa, specifically within the KwaZulu-Natal region.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Xylocarpus granatum, possesses various anatomical components that are utilized for its medicinal properties. The large, woody fruits are a defining characteristic, often appearing as heavy pods that eventually split to release numerous seeds. The lush green leaves are frequently studied for their bioactive compounds, while the small twigs and bark are also harvested for traditional remedies. Each of these plant parts, from the fleshy fruit to the internal seeds, contributes to the overall therapeutic profile of this mangrove species.
