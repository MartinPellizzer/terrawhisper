Borago officinalis

Borage

Borage officinalis, commonly known as borage, is a charismatic annual herb celebrated for its striking, star-shaped blue flowers. Renowned in traditional herbalism, it is prized for its ability to soothe inflammation and reduce stress. Its edible blossoms add a refreshing, cucumber-like flavor to salads, drinks, and various culinary creations.

  • Scientific name: Borago officinalis
  • Family: Boraginaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized into the order Boraginales and the family Boraginaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is defined by the genus Borago.

Moving toward Middle Europe, the species can be found growing in the landscapes of Austria and Belgium. Plant Parts

This plant, Borago officinalis, is highly valued for its diverse medicinal properties derived from various components. The aerial parts of the plant, which encompass the stems and flowers, are often used in herbal preparations, while the lush green leaves are frequently utilized for their soothing qualities. Most notably, the plant is a significant source of fatty acids found within the seeds. From these, borage seed oil is extracted, a highly prized substance known for its high concentration of gamma-linolenic acid. This specialized seed oil is widely used in skincare and nutritional supplements to support skin health and reduce inflammation.
