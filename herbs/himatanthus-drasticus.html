Himatanthus drasticus

Himatanthus drasticus, commonly known as Devil's Trumpet or Madagascar Periwinkle, is a tropical shrub prized in ethnobotany. Renowned for its milky latex, this plant contains diverse alkaloids used traditionally for healing wounds and treating inflammation. Its potent bioactive compounds continue to inspire modern pharmacological research into various therapeutic applications.

  • Scientific name: Himatanthus drasticus
  • Family: Apocynaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized into the order Gentianales and the family Apocynaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Himatanthus.

Common Names

This plant, Himatanthus drasticus, is known by various names depending on the region and language. In English, it is commonly referred to as the Madagascar Periwinkle or the Madagascar Oleander, while in Portuguese, it is widely called Jasmim-manga. In Spanish, the plant is known as Jazmín de Madagascar, and in French, it is identified as Pervenche de Madagascar.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern primarily focused across Northern South America. It can be found inhabiting the natural landscapes of French Guiana, Guyana, and Suriname. Furthermore, its presence extends significantly into the Brazilian territory. Within Brazil, the species is notably distributed throughout the Northeast region. Additionally, the plant is also documented to grow within the Southeast region of the country.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Himatanthus dracunculus, possesses several distinctive components that are utilized for their medicinal properties. When the stem or branches are injured, the plant exudes a thick, milky white latex that is often the primary focus of its therapeutic study. The bark, which forms a protective outer layer for the trunk, is also frequently harvested for its chemical constituents. Additionally, the broad, green leaves serve as important vegetative structures that contribute to the plant's overall biological profile.
