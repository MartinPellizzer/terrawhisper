Triticum aestivum

Common wheat

Triticum aestivum, commonly known as common wheat, is a vital cereal crop globally recognized for its nutritional and medicinal potential. Beyond its role as a dietary staple, it contains bioactive compounds, including antioxidants and polyphenols, which offer anti-inflammatory and protective properties, supporting overall health and wellness through natural nourishment.

  • Scientific name: Triticum aestivum
  • Family: Poaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Poales and the family Poaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is completed by its classification in the genus Triticum.

Distribution

This plant, known scientifically as Triticum aestivum, exhibits a specific distribution across several regions in Northern Europe. It can be found growing within the borders of Denmark. The species is also present in the northern territories of Finland. Furthermore, its range extends to the islands of Great Britain and Iceland. Finally, this medicinal plant is documented to occur in Ireland.

Plant Parts

This plant, Triticum aestivum, commonly known as wheat, is a vital cereal crop where various components are utilized for their nutritional and medicinal properties. The germ, or the embryo of the seed, is a highly concentrated source of essential vitamins, minerals, and healthy fats, making it a potent component in dietary supplements. Additionally, the wheat bran, which constitutes the hard outer layer of the kernel, is exceptionally rich in dietary fiber and antioxidants, playing a crucial role in promoting digestive health and regulating metabolic functions.
