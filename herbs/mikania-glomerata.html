Mikania glomerata

Guaco

Mikania glomerata, commonly known as climber weed or erva-de-kong, is a medicinal climbing plant native to Central and South America. Renowned for its potent anti-inflammatory and antibacterial properties, it is traditionally used to treat skin infections, respiratory issues, and wounds, serving as a vital resource in folk medicine.

  • Scientific name: Mikania glomerata
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asterales, it is classified under the family Asteraceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic designation is within the genus Mikaenia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several regions in Central and South America. In Central America, it can be found growing in the countries of El Salvador, Guatemala, and Honduras. Its presence extends significantly into Brazil, where it is established in multiple territories. Specifically, the species is documented throughout the Northeast region of Brazil. Additionally, it is also distributed across the Southeast part of the country.

Plant Parts

This plant, Mikania glomerata, commonly known as Guaco, utilizes various components for its therapeutic properties. The most significant parts used in traditional medicine are the lush green leaves, which are rich in essential oils and coumarins. While the roots are sometimes studied for their chemical composition, the most common way to consume its medicinal benefits is by preparing teas brewed from the dried or fresh foliage. These infusions are widely used to treat respiratory issues and as an effective remedy for snake bites.
