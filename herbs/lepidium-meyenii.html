Maca

Lepidium meyenii · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (619 studies) · ★★★★★

Maca, scientifically known as Lepidium meyenii, is a resilient cruciferous plant native to the high altitudes of the Peruvian Andes. Renowned for its potent adaptogenic properties, this "superfood" has been used for centuries to enhance vitality, balance hormones, and support overall energy levels and reproductive health naturally.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, scientifically known as Lepidium meyenii, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae within the order Brassicales. Furthermore, it is a member of the family Brassicaceae and falls under the genus Lepidium.

Distribution

This plant is native to the diverse landscapes of Western and Southern South America. Within the western region, its presence is specifically documented in the countries of Bolivia and Peru. Moving toward the southern part of the continent, it can be found throughout the Northwest region of Argentina. Its distribution also extends into the northern territories of Chile. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's preference for the varied terrains of the Andean area.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Maca, derives its medicinal potency primarily from its specialized underground structures. The most significant part used for therapeutic purposes is the root, specifically the fleshy, underground storage hypocotyls that accumulate nutrients. These nutrient-dense tubers serve as the reservoir for the plant's bioactive compounds, which are harvested and processed to create various tuber extracts. By focusing on these specialized roots, practitioners are able to utilize the concentrated energy stored within the plant's subterranean anatomy.
