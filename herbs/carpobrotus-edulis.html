Reproduction

The reproduction of Carpobrotus edulis involves both sexual and asexual mechanisms, with the sexual process characterized by bisexual flowers. The flowering period typically begins in March and concludes in June, though the timing can be variable. The flowers are pink in color and possess significant dimensions, ranging in length from 2 cm to 4.5 cm and in width from 8 cm to 10 cm. This species utilizes a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects such as bees to facilitate fertilization; notably, self-fertilization is absent, ensuring genetic diversity through cross-pollination. Once fertilized, the plant produces prolific quantities of seeds, often exceeding 1,000 seeds per fruit. These seeds are relatively small, with a consistent mean length of 1.15 mm and a mean mass of 3e-04 g. The dispersal of these seeds follows a zoochorous syndrome, specifically endozoochorous, meaning they are dispersed via the internal passage through animals.
