Reproduction

The reproduction of Cotoneaster integerrimus is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, primarily driven by insects such as bees. The flowering period typically begins in April and concludes in June, though the timing can be variable. This species relies on sexual reproduction, as asexual reproduction is absent and self-fertilization is also noted to be absent, necessitating cross-pollination. Following fertilization, the plant produces indehiscent fruits classified as drupes. These fruits typically measure a mean length of 0.675 cm (ranging from a minimum of 0.6 cm to a maximum of 0.7 cm) and a mean width of 0.61 cm (ranging from 0.6 cm to 0.7 cm). The seeds contained within these fruits have a mean mass of 0.03474 g, with individual seed masses ranging from a minimum of 0.00823 g to a maximum of 0.009883 g. Dispersal is achieved through a zoochorous syndrome, specifically via endozoochory, where the fruits are consumed and dispersed by animals.
