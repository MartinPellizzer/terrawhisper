Hibiscus sinosyriacus

What's the taxonomical classification of Hibiscus sinosyriacus?

Hibiscus syriacus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving through its higher taxonomic ranks, it falls under the order Malvales and the family Malvacaceae. Finally, its specific biological identity is defined by the genus Hibiscus and the species syriacus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Hibiscus syriacus has an upright, deciduous shrub habit characterized by woody stems, elliptical leaves with serrated margins, and large, trumpet-shaped flowers featuring a prominent central stamen column. The foliage typically consists of three-lobed or ovate leaves that are dark green and slightly leathery in texture. Its flowers emerge in various shades of purple, white, or pink and possess five distinct petals surrounding a long, protruding reproductive structure. The plant develops a multi-stemmed architecture that can reach heights of up to twelve feet in mature specimens. During the blooming season, the large blossoms often appear singly or in small clusters at the ends of the branches.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

Because of its hardiness, it has been widely naturalized and cultivated in many other temperate parts of the world. How is this plant cultivated?

This plant thrives in full sun and well-draining, fertile soil with a neutral to slightly acidic pH.

It requires regular watering during dry spells to maintain consistent moisture without allowing the roots to sit in standing water. Pruning is most effective in late winter or early spring to encourage vigorous new growth and prolific flowering. Once established, the shrub is quite hardy and can tolerate various soil types provided they are not overly compacted.

Periodic mulching helps retain necessary soil moisture and suppresses competing weeds around the base.
