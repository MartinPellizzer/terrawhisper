Dracocephalum integrifolium

What's the taxonomical classification of Dracocephalum integrifolium?

Dracocephalum integrifolium belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant is further organized under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement in the genus Dracocephalum, with the specific designation of the species integrifolium.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Dracocephalum integrifoliuum has square, decumbent stems that support opposite, sessile, lanceolate leaves with entire margins and a characteristic grayish-green pubescence. The plant features terminal verticillasters composed of small, two-lipped labiate flowers that typically display a purplish or bluish coloration. Its floral structure includes a prominent upper lip and a broader lower lip, often marked with lighter patterns to guide pollinators. The calyx is tubular and persistent, protecting the developing nutlets throughout the maturation process. Small, dry fruits known as nutlets are produced at the base of each flower after successful pollination.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-draining, sandy or gravelly soil and full sun exposure to thrive in a temperate climate.

It is a drought-tolerant perennial that performs best when planted in areas where moisture does not collect around the root zone. For successful growth, avoid overwatering and ensure the soil stays relatively dry between watering sessions. You can propagate it easily through stem cuttings taken during the growing season.
