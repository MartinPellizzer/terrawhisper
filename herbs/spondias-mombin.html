Scientific literature: Very Extensive (281 studies) · ★★★★★

Hog plum, scientifically known as Spondias mombin, is a versatile tropical fruit tree prized for its medicinal properties. Beyond its delicious flavor, various parts of the plant—including leaves, bark, and fruit—are utilized in traditional medicine to treat inflammation, digestive issues, and skin ailments, offering significant therapeutic potential.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across both Atlantic islands and the African mainland. In the Macaronesian region, its presence is noted in the archipelago of Cape Verde. Moving to West Tropical Africa, the species is widely distributed across several nations. Specifically, it can be found growing in the territories of Benin, Burkina, and the Gambia. Furthermore, its range extends eastward to include the country of Ghana.

The reproduction of Spondias mombin is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, primarily driven by insects, with bees playing a significant role in the transfer of pollen. The plant produces fleshy drupes that are indehiscent, meaning they do not split open at maturity to release their contents. The seeds themselves exhibit significant morphological variation: they possess a mean seed mass of approximately 0.83 g, ranging from a minimum of 0.15873 g to a maximum of 3.98 g.
