Loranthus tanakae

Loranthus tanakae, commonly known as a species of mistletoe, is a fascinating hemiparasitic plant found across East Asia. Thriving on host trees, it plays a unique role in forest ecosystems. Traditionally utilized in various medicinal practices, this plant offers intriguing potential for pharmacological research and botanical studies.

  • Scientific name: Loranthus tanakae
  • Family: Loranthaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Santalales, it is classified under the family Loranthaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Loranthus as the species Loranthus tanakae.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions of East Asia. Within China, its presence is documented across the South-Central, North-Central, and Inner Mongolia provinces. Beyond the borders of China, the species is also found throughout the Japanese archipelago. Additionally, it extends its range into the Korean Peninsula. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's prevalence throughout the eastern territories of the continent.
