Crinum bulbispermum

Crinum bulbispermum, commonly known as the Bulbous Amaryllis, is a perennial plant cherished in traditional medicine. Renowned for its potent bioactive alkaloids, it is frequently utilized to treat skin conditions, inflammation, and various ailments. This botanical specimen serves as a vital resource in ethnopharmacology for its diverse therapeutic potential.

  • Scientific name: Crinum bulbispermum
  • Family: Amaryllidaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Asparagales and is a member of the Amaryllidaceae family. Specifically, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Crinum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across several distinct regions. Within Macaronesia, it can be found growing in Madeira. Its presence is also notable throughout various parts of Southern Africa. Specifically, it is documented in the Cape Provinces, Lesotho, and KwaZulu-Natal. Additionally, the species extends its range into the Free State.
