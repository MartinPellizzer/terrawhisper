Geranium divaricatum

What's the taxonomical classification of Geranium divaricatum?

Geranium divaricatum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is situated in the subclass Magnoliidae and falls under the order Geraniales. Furthermore, it is a member of the family Geraniaceae, which contains the genus Geranium, and is specifically identified as the species divaricatum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Geranium divaricatum has branched stems that typically reach heights of 10 to 40 centimeters and bear deeply lobed, palmately divided leaves with serrated margins. The foliage often displays a hairy texture, which provides a tactile element to the plant's structure. Its small, five-petaled flowers are usually pink or light purple and emerge from the leaf axils on slender pedicels. These blooms are supported by a characteristic structure of spreading, divergent branches that give the species its specific epithet. Once pollination occurs, the plant develops specialized seed capsules that utilize mechanical tension to disperse seeds.
