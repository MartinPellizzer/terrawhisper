Artemisia eriopoda

What's the taxonomical classification of Artemisia eriopoda?

Artemisia eriopoda belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further categorized under the order Asterales. This plant is a member of the family Asteraceae and is situated within the genus Artemisia, specifically identified by its species name, eriopoda.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Artemisia eriopoda has a perennial rootstock that produces multiple upright, woody stems ranging from 10 to 60 centimeters in height, each featuring narrow, linear leaves that are silvery-gray due to dense, fine hairs. The foliage is typically characterized by a revolute leaf margin, where the edges curl downward toward the underside. These leaves are arranged alternately along the stems and possess a distinctively fine, woolly texture. During the flowering season, the plant produces small, inconspicuous flower heads in terminal or axillary clusters. The overall appearance is compact and bushy, adapted to arid environments through its specialized surface coverings.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

The species typically occupies well-drained soils across various elevations throughout its range. Many populations are concentrated in the high-desert basins where seasonal precipitation is minimal. How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, commonly known as sand sagebrush, is cultivated by sowing seeds in well-draining, sandy soil during late autumn or early spring to allow for natural stratification.

Because it is highly drought-tolerant and adapted to arid environments, it is an ideal choice for xeriscaping and restoring native prairie habitats.
