Luculia pinceana

What's the taxonomical classification of Luculia pinceana?

Luculia pinceana belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is situated in the subclass Magnoliidae and falls under the order Gentianaales. This plant is a member of the family Rubiaceae, specifically categorized within the genus Luculia, and is identified by its unique species name, pinceana.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Luculia pinceana has opposite, elliptical to oblong leaves that are dark green, leathery, and possess slightly serrated margins. The foliage is characterized by a glossy texture and prominent venation that adds depth to the canopy. Small, fragrant flowers emerge from the axils of the leaves, typically displaying a pale yellow or creamy white coloration. These blooms are often solitary or in small clusters, providing a delicate contrast to the dense greenery. The plant develops a woody structure that supports its spreading habit as it matures.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-draining, acidic soil and consistent moisture to thrive in a partially shaded environment with filtered sunlight.

You should avoid placing it in direct afternoon sun to prevent the delicate foliage from scorching. Regular watering is essential, but the soil must never remain waterlogged to prevent root rot. Providing organic mulch helps maintain the steady humidity and soil temperature this species prefers.

Once established, it can be pruned lightly after its flowering season to maintain a compact shape.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant features medicinal components derived specifically from its flowers and stems. The fragrant flowers are often harvested to create infusions or extracts used in traditional remedies. Additionally, the stems provide essential bioactive compounds when processed for therapeutic use. These various parts are carefully collected to ensure the potency of the medicine. Practitioners utilize these elements to address specific health concerns within traditional healing practices.

According to a study published by "Molecules (Basel, Switzerland)", the floral scent of Luculia pinceana contains main compounds with many medicinal properties. The study identified a total of 39 compounds at four different stages of flower development, including 26 at the bud stage, 26 at the initial-flowering stage, 32 at the full-flowering stage, and 32 at the end-flowering stage. The most abundant compound was paeonol, which accounted for 51% to 83% of the volatiles. Because of these compounds, the plant has a promising prospect for development as essential oil.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds cincholic acid 28-O-beta-D-glucopyranosyl ester, cincholic acid-3-O-beta-D-glucopyranoside, loganin, loganoside, and vogeloside. These specific chemical constituents are primary components identified within the phytochemical profile of Luculia pinceana. The presence of these iridoids and cincholic acid derivatives suggests significant pharmacological potential. Researchers often study these molecules to understand their biological activities in various therapeutic applications. Such detailed chemical analysis helps characterize the unique medicinal properties of this botanical species.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", seven compounds were isolated from the n-BuOH fraction of the ethanolic extract from the stem of Luculia pinceana. These isolated compounds include vogeloside (1), epi-vogeloside (2), loganoside (3), and loganin (4). The study also identified cincholic acid 28-O-beta-D-glucopyranosyl ester (5), cincholic acid-3-O-beta-D-glucopyranoside, 28-O-beta-D-glucopyranosyl ester (6), and cincholic acid-3-O-beta-D-glucopyranoside (7). These specific compounds 1-7 were isolated from the genus for the first time.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is used to create medicinal herbal preparations such as essential oil and ethanolic extract for various therapeutic applications. The essential oil is typically derived through steam distillation to capture the plant's concentrated aromatic compounds. Meanwhile, the ethanolic extract is produced by soaking the plant material in alcohol to pull out specific bioactive constituents. These different preparations allow for diverse methods of administration in traditional and modern herbal medicine. Each form serves to harness the unique chemical properties found within the Luculia pinceana species.

According to a study published by "Molecules (Basel, Switzerland)", Luculia pinceana has a promising prospect for development as essential oil because its main floral scent compounds have many medicinal properties. Researchers identified a total of 39 compounds at four different stages of flower development, including 26 at the bud stage, 26 at the initial-flowering stage, 32 at the full-flowering stage, and 32 at the end-flowering stage. The study suggests that these medicinal properties make the plant suitable for future development.
