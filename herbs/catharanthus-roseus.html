Madagascar periwinkle

Catharanthus roseus · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1664 studies) · ★★★★★

Madagascar periwinkle, scientifically known as Catharanthus roseus, is a versatile medicinal shrub native to Madagascar. Renowned for its vibrant pink flowers, it serves as a critical source of alkaloids, specifically vincristine and vinblastine. These potent compounds are essential in modern pharmacology for treating various types of cancer.

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several distinct regions. In Southwestern Europe, it can be found growing in Spain. Reproduction

The reproductive cycle of Catharanthus roseus is characterized by a versatile flowering period that typically commences in April and continues through October, though it can be variable throughout the year. The plant produces pink flowers that utilize biotic pollination syndromes, specifically attracting insects such as butterflies to facilitate the reproductive process. Self-fertilization is present within the species, ensuring reproductive success. Following pollination, the plant develops fruit in the form of a follicle, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 1.5 cm to a maximum of 2.5 cm. Each follicle is prolific, containing between 11 and 100 seeds. The seeds themselves are quite small, with a mean length of approximately 1.9 mm (ranging from 1.9 mm to 2 mm) and a mean mass of 0.001425 g (varying between 0.00135 g and 0.0015 g).
