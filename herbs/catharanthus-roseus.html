Madagascar periwinkle

Catharanthus roseus · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1664 studies) · ★★★★★

Madagascar periwinkle, scientifically known as Catharanthus roseus, is a versatile medicinal shrub native to Madagascar. Renowned for its vibrant pink flowers, it serves as a critical source of alkaloids, specifically vincristine and vinblastine. These potent compounds are essential in modern pharmacology for treating various types of cancer.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered under Gentianaales and belongs to the family Apocynaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identification is placed within the genus Catharanthus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several distinct regions. In Southwestern Europe, it can be found growing in Spain. Moving toward Southeastern Europe, its presence extends to Italy and the island of Kriti. The species also inhabits Northern Africa, specifically within the territory of Tunisia. Finally, its range reaches the Macaronesian islands, including the Canary Is.

Plant Parts

This plant, Catharanthus roseus, is a medicinal shrub where various components contain bioactive alkaloids. The leaves are perhaps the most studied part, as each individual leaf serves as a primary source for extracting vincristine and vinblastine. The woody stem provides the structural support for the plant's growth, while the roots are also integrated into the plant's complex chemical profile. Although the root system is essential for nutrient uptake, it is the foliage that remains the most significant focus in pharmacological research.
