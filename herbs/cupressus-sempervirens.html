Mediterranean Cypress

Cupressus sempervirens · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (53 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Mediterranean Cypress, scientifically known as Cupressus sempervirens, is a resilient evergreen native to the Mediterranean region. Renowned for its longevity and aromatic foliage, this plant offers diverse medicinal benefits. Its essential oils are traditionally utilized in aromatherapy to promote relaxation, support respiratory health, and provide potent antimicrobial properties.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Cupressus sempervirens is characterized by a monoecious sexual reproduction system, with asexual reproduction being absent. The flowering period typically begins in February and concludes in April. The flowers are relatively small, with a length ranging from 0.3 to 0.5 cm and a width reaching a maximum of 0.2 cm. Pollination is an abiotic process driven by a wind pollination syndrome. Following fertilization, the plant develops woody cones as its fruit type. These brown-colored cones vary in size, with a length ranging from a minimum of 2.5 cm to a maximum of 4 cm (averaging 3 cm) and a width between 0.15 and 0.2 cm. The seeds produced within these cones are quite light, possessing a mean mass of approximately 0.0074218 g, with values ranging from a minimum of 0.00646 g to a maximum of 0.0085 g. Finally, the dispersal of these seeds is anemochorous, relying on the wind for distribution.
