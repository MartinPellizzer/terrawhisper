Mediterranean Cypress

Cupressus sempervirens · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (53 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Mediterranean Cypress, scientifically known as Cupressus sempervirens, is a resilient evergreen native to the Mediterranean region. Renowned for its longevity and aromatic foliage, this plant offers diverse medicinal benefits. Its essential oils are traditionally utilized in aromatherapy to promote relaxation, support respiratory health, and provide potent antimicrobial properties.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Pinidae and the order Pinales, it is a member of the family Cupressaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Cupressus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several distinct regions of Europe. In Middle Europe, its presence can be specifically noted in Switzerland. Moving toward the Mediterranean, it is found throughout various parts of Southwestern Europe. This includes the islands of the Baleares and Corse. Additionally, the species is commonly distributed across the mainland territories of France and Portugal.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Mediterranean cypress, utilizes various components for its medicinal and aromatic properties. While it does not produce edible nuts in the traditional sense, its small, woody fruits, which are technically cones, contain essential oils that are highly valued in therapeutic applications. Furthermore, the aerial parts, including the foliage and twigs, are frequently harvested to extract volatile oils used for their antiseptic and calming effects.
