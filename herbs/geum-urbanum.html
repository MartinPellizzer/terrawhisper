Geum urbanum

Wood avens

Geum urbanum, commonly known as Wood Avens, is a perennial herb characterized by its delicate, yellowish flowers and distinctive hooked seeds. Historically used in traditional herbalism, this plant possesses mild astringent and anti-inflammatory properties. It thrives in shady woodlands, serving as a resilient component of temperate ecosystems.

  • Scientific name: Geum urbanum
  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Rosales and the family Rosaceae, it is classified under the genus Geum.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Geum urbanum, is recognized by various traditional names across different languages and regions. In Poland, it is called kuklik pospolity, while in France, it is commonly referred to as benoîte commune. Its presence is also noted in Russian-speaking areas as гравилат городской, and in Serbian, it is known as both zečija stopa and зечија стопа.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across several regions within Northern Europe. Specifically, it can be found growing in the natural landscapes of Denmark. Its distribution also extends to the northern territories of Finland and Norway. In the western parts of the continent, the species is well-established in both Great Britain and Ireland. Together, these locations highlight the plant's affinity for the Northern European climate.
