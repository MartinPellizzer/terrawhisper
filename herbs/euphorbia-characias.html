Euphorbia characias

Euphorbia characias, commonly known as Mediterranean spurge, is a striking perennial prized for its architectural foliage and lime-green bracts. While celebrated in landscaping for its drought tolerance and structural beauty, it contains a milky latex that requires careful handling due to its potential skin and eye irritation properties.

  • Scientific name: Euphorbia characias
  • Family: Euphorbiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, it is classified under the family Euphorbiaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Euphorbia.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Mediterranean spurge, is characterized by its striking architectural form and unique physiological features. The most notable aspect of its biology is the presence of a milky, white latex that flows from the stems and leaves when the plant is injured or broken. This sap contains various chemical compounds that serve as a defense mechanism against herbivores, though it is also known to be a significant skin irritant to humans. Beyond this distinctive fluid, the plant is defined by its structural components, including its fleshy, blue-green glaucous leaves and its large, showy yellowish-green bracts that surround the tiny, inconspicuous true flowers.
