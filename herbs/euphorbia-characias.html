Mediterranean Spurge, scientifically known as Euphorbia characias, is a striking, evergreen perennial native to the Mediterranean region. Renowned for its architectural blue-green foliage and dramatic yellowish bracts, it is a staple in xeriscaping. While visually stunning, its milky sap requires careful handling due to its skin-irritating properties.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Mediterranean spurge, is characterized by its striking architectural form and unique physiological features. The most notable aspect of its biology is the presence of a milky, white latex that flows from the stems and leaves when the plant is injured or broken. This sap contains various chemical compounds that serve as a defense mechanism against herbivores, though it is also known to be a significant skin irritant to humans. Beyond this distinctive fluid, the plant is defined by its structural components, including its fleshy, blue-green glaucous leaves and its large, showy yellowish-green bracts that surround the tiny, inconspicuous true flowers.
