Chinese wood-oil-tree

Vernicia fordii · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Moderate (135 studies) · ★★★☆☆

Chinese wood-oil-tree, scientifically known as Vernicia fordii, is a significant medicinal and industrial plant native to East Asia. Renowned for its oil-rich seeds, it serves as a vital source of tung oil. Beyond industrial use, its bioactive compounds offer therapeutic potential in traditional medicine for various healing applications.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across both African and Asian continents. Reproduction

The reproduction of Vernicia fordii is characterized by a monoecious sexual reproduction system, where both male and female reproductive structures are present on the same plant. The flowering period typically begins in May and concludes within the same month, though the timing can be variable. Pollination is a biotic process, primarily driven by insects, with bees identified as a key pollinator. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fruits that vary in width, with a minimum diameter of 4 cm, a mean width of 4.25 cm, and a maximum width reaching 6 cm. The plant is capable of self-fertilization, which aids in its reproductive success. The resulting seeds are uniform in mass, with both the minimum and maximum recorded values, as well as the mean, consistently measured at 1.136 g.
