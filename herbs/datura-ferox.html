Datura ferox

Datura ferox, commonly known as the prickly devil's snare, is a potent medicinal plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its high alkaloid content, it is utilized in traditional medicine for its sedative and analgesic properties. However, its extreme toxicity requires cautious handling due to its powerful psychoactive effects.

  • Scientific name: Datura ferox
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is placed in the order Solanales and the family Solanaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Datura.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse presence across various regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its range extends to include Great Britain. Moving into Middle Europe, it can be found within the territories of Germany as well as Czechia and Slovakia. Its distribution continues into Southwestern Europe, where it is documented in France. Additionally, the species is located in the Mediterranean region on the island of Sardegna.

Plant Parts

This plant, Datura ferox, is known for its potent medicinal and toxic properties, which are primarily concentrated in its various anatomical structures. While the leaves, flowers, and roots all contain bioactive alkaloids, the seeds are particularly significant due to their high concentration of tropane alkaloids. These seeds, which are typically contained within spiny capsules, are the most frequently utilized part in traditional pharmacological applications, though they must be handled with extreme caution because of their intense physiological effects.
