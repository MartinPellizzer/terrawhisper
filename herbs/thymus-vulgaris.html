Thymus vulgaris

Thyme

Thymus vulgaris, commonly known as thyme, is a perennial herb belonging to the Lamiaceae family. Renowned for its aromatic leaves, this medicinal plant is rich in essential oils like thymol. It offers potent antibacterial, antifungal, and antioxidant properties, making it a staple in traditional remedies for respiratory health.

  • Scientific name: Thymus vulgaris
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Thymus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Thymus vulgaris, is recognized by various traditional names across different languages. Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Thymus vulgaris, is highly valued in herbal medicine for its diverse therapeutic properties. Medicinally, the whole plants are often harvested to ensure maximum potency, though the most significant therapeutic compounds are concentrated within the aerial part of the organism. Specifically, the aromatic leaves serve as the primary source of its bioactive constituents. When processed, the plant yields potent essential oils that contain thymol, a powerful antimicrobial agent. These essential oils are frequently extracted from the vegetation to be used in various topical and aromatic applications.
