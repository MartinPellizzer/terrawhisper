Ulex parviflorus subsp. airensis

Ulex parviflorus subsp. airensis, commonly known as the Airy Gorse, is a resilient shrub native to specific Mediterranean coastal regions. Renowned for its dense, thorny structure and vibrant yellow blooms, this subspecies holds significant ethnobotanical interest, traditionally utilized in various folk remedies for its unique bioactive properties.

  • Scientific name: Ulex parviflorus subsp. airensis
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, taxonomically classified as Ulex parviflorus subsp. airensis, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Fabales and the family Fabaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Ulex.

Common Names

This plant, Ulex parviflorus subsp. airensis, is known by several different common names depending on the linguistic context. In English, it is frequently referred to as the small-flowered gorse, while in Spanish, it is commonly called retama de airensis.

This plant is a specific subspecies characterized by a very restricted range. Its natural occurrence is limited to the region of Southwestern Europe. Within this territory, the species can be found specifically in Portugal. This narrow distribution suggests that it may be an endemic species to that particular area.
