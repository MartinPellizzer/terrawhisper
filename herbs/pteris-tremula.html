Pteris tremula

Australian brake

Pteris tremula, commonly known as the trembling fern, is a delicate species native to various tropical regions. Renowned for its fine, lace-like foliage, this fern is valued in traditional medicine for its potential therapeutic properties. Understanding its botanical characteristics is essential for exploring its role in natural healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Pteris tremula
  • Family: Pteridaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Polypodiinae, it is categorized under the order Polypodiales. Finally, its taxonomic classification concludes within the family Pteridaceae and the genus Pteris.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Pteris tremula, is referred to by several different common names depending on the language and region. In Māori, it is called turawera, while in Chinese, it is known as 澳洲凤尾蕨. In English, it is frequently called the Australian brake, the shaking brake, or the tender brake.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide-ranging geographical distribution that spans across two distinct major regions. Within the Macaronesian islands, it can be found growing in the Azores, Madeira, and the Canary Islands. Moving toward the African continent, its presence extends into the southern reaches of the territory. Specifically, it is located within the Cape Provinces of Southern Africa. Additionally, the species inhabits the coastal regions of KwaZulu-Natal.
