Mongolian ephedra, scientifically known as Ephedra equisetina, is a perennial shrub native to the arid regions of East Asia. Renowned for its high concentration of ephedrine alkaloids, it has been utilized in traditional medicine for centuries to treat respiratory ailments, boost energy levels, and enhance metabolic function.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions of Northern Asia. In Siberia, its presence is documented in the Altay and Krasnoyarsk regions. It can also be found throughout the vast landscapes of West Siberia and the mountainous areas of Tuva. Moving toward the eastern reaches, the species extends into the Russian Far East. Specifically, it inhabits the unique ecosystems located within the Primorye territory.

Plant Parts

This plant, Ephedra equisetina, is categorized as a perennial medicinal herb that grows in arid environments. As an herbaceous species, it lacks a woody stem, instead developing slender, jointed, and green photosynthetic branches that function similarly to leaves. The entire herb is often utilized in traditional medicine, as its various parts contain active alkaloids that are harvested for their physiological effects.
