Copernicia prunifera, scientifically known as Copernicia prunifera, is a majestic palm native to the arid regions of northeastern Brazil. Renowned for its versatile wax, this resilient species holds significant ethnobotanical value. It serves as a vital natural resource, providing essential materials for traditional medicine, cosmetics, and industrial applications.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions of South America and the Caribbean. In the Caribbean, its presence is specifically noted within the islands of Trinidad and Tobago. Within the vast territory of Brazil, it can be found spanning multiple distinct environmental zones. The species inhabits the Northeast region of Brazil as well as the Southeast. Additionally, its range extends significantly into the North of Brazil, demonstrating its adaptability to diverse habitats.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the carnauba palm, utilizes various components for its medicinal and industrial applications. While the fruit is often noted for its role in the plant's reproductive cycle, it is the leaves that are most significant in traditional practices, as they are harvested to extract the wax and various bioactive compounds. These leaves serve as the primary source of the plant's most valuable derivatives, which are used in both topical treatments and various preparations within regional medicine.
