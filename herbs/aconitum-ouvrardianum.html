Aconitum ouvrardianum

What's the taxonomical classification of Aconitum ouvrardianum?

Aconitum ouvrardianum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. It falls under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, following the evolutionary lineage of the order Ranunculales. As a member of the family Ranunculaceae, this plant is further categorized under the genus Aconitum, with its specific identity defined by the species name ouvrardianum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Aconium ouvrardianum has a perennial herbaceous habit characterized by erect stems that reach heights of up to one meter and pinnately divided leaves with narrow, lanceolate segments. These zygomorphic blossoms feature a prominent upper sepal that forms a protective structure over the internal reproductive organs. The foliage is lush and dark green, providing a textured backdrop to the striking floral displays. What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains various diterpene alkaloids, specifically aconitine, mesaconitine, and hypaconitine, which serve as its primary medicinal compounds. These potent alkaloids function by interacting with voltage-gated sodium channels in the nervous system. When used in controlled pharmaceutical preparations, these substances can provide significant analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects. However, the therapeutic window for these compounds is extremely narrow due to their inherent toxicity. Precise dosage is critical to prevent life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias or respiratory failure.

    What side effects this plant can have?

    This plant can cause severe toxicity leading to nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, dizziness, confusion, paresthesia, paralysis, and convulsions. Ingestion triggers dangerous cardiovascular symptoms such as arrhythmia, bradycardia, and hypotension. These physiological disturbances often progress to critical respiratory failure. Ultimately, the poisoning of this species can result in death.
