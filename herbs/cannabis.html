Cannabis

Cannabis, commonly known as marijuana or hemp, is a versatile plant species with a long history of medicinal and industrial use. Rich in bioactive cannabinoids like THC and CBD, it is increasingly studied for its therapeutic potential in managing chronic pain, anxiety, epilepsy, and various other complex health conditions.

  • Scientific name: Cannabis
  • Family: Cannabaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Rosales and the family Cannabaceae. Ultimately, the medicinal species is identified by the genus Cannabis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Cannabis, is recognized by various names across different linguistic and cultural landscapes. In Chinese, it is referred to as 大麻属, while in the Quechua language, it is called Kañamu. In Scottish Gaelic, the name is expressed as Cainb, and in Tamil, it is known as கஞ்சா. Additionally, in Hungarian, the plant is commonly called kender.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a notable presence across several regions within Northern Europe. In the coastal and mainland areas of Denmark, it can be found growing in various environments. It also extends its range northward into the expansive landscapes of Finland. Moving westward, the distribution includes the island territories of Great Britain and Ireland. Finally, the plant's reach continues through the rugged terrain of Norway.

Plant Parts

This plant possesses several distinct components that are utilized for various medicinal and industrial purposes. The most recognizable parts are the serrated leaves, which are often associated with its psychoactive properties, and the dense inflorescences that contain high concentrations of cannabinoids. Within these flowering structures, the flowers serve as the primary site for chemical production, particularly where the sticky resin accumulates on the trichomes to protect the plant. Additionally, the seeds are highly valued for their nutritional profile, offering a rich source of essential fatty acids and proteins.
