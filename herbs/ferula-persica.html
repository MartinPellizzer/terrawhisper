Ferula persica

Persian asafoetida

Ferula persica, commonly known as Persian fennel, is a perennial herb belonging to the Apiaceae family. Native to the arid regions of the Middle East, this medicinal plant is highly valued in traditional medicine for its diverse therapeutic properties, including its potent anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and antimicrobial bioactive compounds.

  • Scientific name: Ferula persica
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Apiales and the family Apiaceae. Ultimately, its scientific classification is defined by the genus Ferula, specifically as the species Ferula persica.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ferula persica, is recognized by various regional and botanical common names across different languages and classifications. In Azerbaijan, it is referred to as İran ilankölgəsi, while in Persian, it is known as کمای ایرانی. In Czech, the species is called Ločidlo perské. Interestingly, within certain French linguistic contexts or botanical records, it has been associated with the names Ferula puberula and Ferula szowitziana var. kandavanensis.

Distribution

This plant is widely recognized for its specific range across certain mountainous and arid regions. Its presence is notably documented within the Caucasus, particularly throughout the Transcaucasus area. Furthermore, the species extends its reach into Western Asia, where it is found in Iran. These locations highlight the distinct environmental preferences required for its natural growth. Consequently, its distribution remains concentrated within these specific geopolitical territories.
