Cordia myxa

Cordia myxa, commonly known as laad amrud or Indian cherry, is a versatile medicinal shrub valued in traditional Ayurvedic practices. Renowned for its nutritious fruit and mucilaginous properties, it is frequently used to treat respiratory issues, digestive disorders, and skin ailments, offering significant therapeutic benefits for holistic wellness.

  • Scientific name: Cordia myxa
  • Family: Boraginaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further organized under the order Boraginales and the family Boraginaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic designation falls under the genus Cordia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution spanning across several regions in Africa. In Northern Africa, it can be found growing in the countries of Algeria, Egypt, and Libya. Moving toward the western part of the continent, its presence extends into West Tropical Africa. Specifically, it is documented to inhabit the territories of Benin and Burkina. This range highlights the plant's adaptability to different African climates.
