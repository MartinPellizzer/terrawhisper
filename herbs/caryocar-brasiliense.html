Physiology

The physiology of Caryocar brasiliense is characterized by a metabolic framework consistent with many woody species inhabiting the Brazilian Cerrado, primarily utilizing the C3 photosynthetic pathway for carbon fixation. This C3 mechanism involves the direct carboxylation of ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate by the enzyme RuBisCO, a process that requires careful regulation of stomatal conductance to balance CO2 uptake with transpirational water loss in its semi-arid habitat. Furthermore, the species does not function as a nitrogen fixer, meaning it lacks the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria necessary to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms, relying instead on the uptake of mineral nitrogen from the soil through its root system.
