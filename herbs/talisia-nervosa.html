Talisia nervosa

Talisia nervosa, commonly known as the Guayaquil walnut or Cashew tree, is a versatile tropical species native to Central and South America. Renowned for its nutritious seeds and medicinal properties, it offers significant bioactive compounds that support wellness, making it a valuable resource in traditional herbal medicine practices.

  • Scientific name: Talisia nervosa
  • Family: Sapindaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Talisia nervosa, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Sapindales. Finally, it is a member of the family Sapindaceae and is situated within the genus Talisia.

Common Names

This plant, scientifically known as Taliaisia nervosa, is known by various common names depending on the region and language. In English, it is frequently referred to as the Guayaquil nut or the tropical almond. In Spanish, it is widely recognized as guanábana de monte, guayaquil, or almendra de guayaquil, while in Portuguese, it is commonly called amêndoa-da-guiné.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution spanning across parts of Central and South America. In Central America, its range includes the countries of Costa Rica, Nicaragua, and Panamá. Moving into Northern South America, the species can be found growing in Venezuela. Additionally, its presence extends further east to include French Guiana. These diverse locations highlight the tropical habitats where this medicinal plant thrives.
