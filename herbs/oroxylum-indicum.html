Indian Trumpet Tree

Oroxylum indicum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (219 studies) · ★★★★★

Indian Trumpet Tree, scientifically known as Oroxylum indicum, is a magnificent medicinal plant native to South Asia. Renowned for its striking yellow blooms, this evergreen species is highly valued in traditional medicine. Its bark and leaves contain potent bioactive compounds used to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and various systemic infections.

The physiology of Oroxylum indicum is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, a metabolic strategy common among many woody dicotyledonous species where carbon fixation occurs primarily through the Calvin cycle within the mesophyll cells. This pathway involves the direct fixation of atmospheric carbon dioxide by the enzyme RuBisCO. In terms of nutrient cycling and symbiotic relationships, the plant does not function as a nitrogen fixer, meaning it lacks the specialized root nodules or symbiotic associations with diazotrophic bacteria required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms; instead, it relies on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium from the soil matrix to satisfy its nitrogen requirements for protein and nucleic acid synthesis.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Oroxylum indicum is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, with specific interactions involving bats. The plant produces fleshy fruits that are classified as capsules. These fruits are dehiscent, meaning they split open at maturity to release the seeds. The seeds produced by this species exhibit a consistent volume of 100 mm³, with both the minimum and maximum recorded values being 100 mm³. In terms of mass, the seeds are relatively small and uniform, with a mean seed mass of 0.0948 g, ranging from a minimum of 0.0916 g to a maximum of 0.098 g. Following the maturation of the fruit, the dispersal syndrome is anemochorous, indicating that the seeds are primarily dispersed by the wind.
