Hemionitis farinosa

Hemionitis farinosa, commonly known as the powdery hemioniis, is a distinctive perennial herb belonging to the Asteraceae family. Characterized by its unique farinaceous, or flour-like, coating on the foliage, this plant thrives in specific rocky habitats. It holds significant interest in botanical studies and traditional ecological contexts for its morphology.

  • Scientific name: Hemionitis farinosa
  • Family: Pteridaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Polypodiinae and the order Polypodiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Pteridaceae and falls under the genus Hemionitis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Hemionitis farinosa, is recognized by various regional names across different languages. In English, it is commonly referred to as the floury spleenwort, a name that alludes to its distinctively powdery appearance. In German, it is known as der mehlige Streifenfarn, and in French, it is called la capillaire farineuse. Within botanical and local contexts in other regions, it is also identified as la capillarina farinosa in Spanish.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse distribution pattern spanning across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in both Nigeria and Sierra Leone. The species also extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, where it can be found in Burundi and Cameroon. Additionally, its range includes the specific coastal area of the Gulf of Guinea Islands. These locations highlight the plant's ability to inhabit various tropical environments across the western and central zones.

Plant Parts

This plant, Hemionitis farinosa, is a type of fern characterized by its distinct structural anatomy. The primary visible component consists of its fronds, which are the large, leaf-like structures that facilitate photosynthesis. These fronds are typically divided and may exhibit a characteristic dusty or farinaceous coating on their undersides, a feature that gives the species its name. Together with the rhizome and the sori located on the fronds, these parts work in unison to support the plant's growth and reproductive cycle.
