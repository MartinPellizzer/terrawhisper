Uraria sinensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Uraria sinensis?

Uraria sinensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. At the class level, it is categorized as Equisetopsida, which falls under the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant is further organized into the order Fabales and is a member of the family Fabaceae. Within this botanical hierarchy, it is identified by the genus Uraria, with its specific designation being the species sinensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Uraria sinensis has an erect, herbaceous growth habit characterized by slender, quadrangular stems that can reach heights of up to 1 meter. The leaves are arranged in an opposite pattern and consist of several pairs of ovate to lanceolate leaflets with entire margins. Small, inconspicuous flowers are typically borne in axillary clusters, displaying a pale yellow or whitish coloration. The fruit is a small, indehiscent achene that contains a single seed. These morphological traits distinguish the species within the Fabaceae family.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

These harvested components are typically prepared as decoctions or teas to utilize their bioactive properties. Practitioners often rely on these specific structures to extract the chemical compounds necessary for therapeutic effects. This traditional application focuses on the vitality contained within the plant's visible growth.

According to a study published by "Molecules (Basel, Switzerland)", the aerial parts of Uraria sinensis were used to isolate a new sesquilignan glucoside called urariasinoside A (1). This new compound was found alongside 8 known compounds, which included 2 lignans, 1 sesquilignan, 1 dilignan, and 4 flavonoid derivatives. The structure of compound 1 was determined through extensive spectroscopic analyses and comparison with literature data. Furthermore, compound 1 was evaluated for in vitro cytotoxicity activity against 6 specific cell lines, which were HL-60, SMMC-7721, A549, MCF-7, SW480, and BEA2-2B.
