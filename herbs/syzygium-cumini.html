Java plum

Syzygium cumini · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (724 studies) · ★★★★★

Java plum, scientifically known as Syzygium cumini, is a versatile medicinal plant celebrated for its therapeutic properties. Rich in antioxidants and essential nutrients, its fruit, bark, and leaves are utilized in traditional medicine to treat digestive issues, manage diabetes, and reduce inflammation, offering significant holistic health benefits.

These flowers are arranged in inflorescences with lengths ranging from a minimum of 0.04 m to a maximum of 0.1 m. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fleshy, indehiscent berries as its fruit type. These fruits vary in size, with a mean length of 1.5 cm, a minimum of 1 cm, and a maximum of 3 cm. Each fruit typically contains between 1 and 10 seeds. The seeds themselves vary in physical dimensions, featuring a minimum length of 10 mm and a maximum length of 15 mm, with volumes ranging from a minimum of 84 mm³ to a mean of approximately 1508.89 mm³ and a maximum of 4200 mm³. In terms of mass, the seeds weigh between a minimum of 0.24 g and a maximum of 1.32 g, with an average seed mass of approximately 0.47 g. The dispersal of these seeds follows a zoochorous syndrome, specifically through endozoochory, where animals consume the fleshy fruit and disperse the seeds through their digestive tracts.
