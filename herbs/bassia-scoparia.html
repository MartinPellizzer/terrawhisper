Reproduction

The reproduction of Bassia scoparia is primarily a sexual process, as asexual reproduction is absent. The flowering period typically commences in July and continues through October. This reproductive cycle relies on abiotic pollination, specifically utilizing a wind pollination syndrome to facilitate fertilization, as self-fertilization is absent. The resulting fruit is classified as an achene and is indehiscent. The seeds produced are characterized by a highly consistent length of 1.8 mm (with both minimum and maximum values at 1.8 mm) and a mean mass of approximately 0.00104 g, though individual seed masses can range from a minimum of 0.00012 g to a maximum of 0.0058 g. For the spread of the species, Bassia scoparia employs an anemochorous dispersal syndrome, relying on the wind to transport its seeds to new locations.
