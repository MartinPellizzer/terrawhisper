Linum usitatissimum var. usitatissimum

Linum usitatissimum var. usitatissimum, commonly known as flax, is a versatile medicinal plant prized for its nutrient-dense seeds. Rich in omega-3 fatty acids, fiber, and lignans, it supports cardiovascular health, aids digestion, and offers anti-inflammatory benefits. This ancient botanical remains a cornerstone of natural wellness and nutritional therapy.

  • Scientific name: Linum usitatissimum var. usitatissimum
  • Family: Linaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malpighiales, it falls into the family Linaceae. Specifically, it is identified by the genus Linum and the variety usitatissimum.

Common Names

This plant, Linum usitatissimum var. usitatissimum, is known by a variety of common names across different languages and regions. In English, it is most widely recognized as flax or linseed, while in French, it is referred to as lin. In German, the plant is called Leinsamen, and in Spanish, it is known as lino. In Chinese, it is called 亚麻 (yàmá), and in Russian, it is called лён (lyon).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across several regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence can be identified in both Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward the center of the continent, it is also found throughout Middle Europe. Specifically, its range extends through Belgium and into the territories of Czechia and Slovakia. Finally, the geographical reach of this variety includes Germany, completing its presence in Middle Europe.
