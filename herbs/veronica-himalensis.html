Veronica himalensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Veronica himalensis?

Veronica himalenensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant further falls into the order Lamiales and is a member of the family Plantaginaceae. Specifically, it is identified by its genus, Veronica, and is distinguished as the species himalenis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Veronica himalenensis has a perennial growth habit characterized by erect, branched stems that reach heights of approximately 10 to 40 centimeters. The leaves are typically opposite, lanceolate to oblong in shape, and possess finely serrated margins with distinctively long, pointed tips. Its inflorescence consists of dense, terminal racemes that bear small, four-lobed flowers, usually ranging in color from pale blue to violet. Each individual flower features a prominent lower lobe that is significantly larger than the other three, which is a defining trait of the genus. The calyx is composed of four sepals that are often hairy, providing protection to the developing seed pods.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude regions of the Himalayas, spanning across countries such as India, Nepal, Bhutan, and parts of Tibet. It typically inhabits montane environments where it grows in rocky crevices and alpine meadows. These populations are often found at elevations ranging from 3,000 to 4,500 meters above sea level. The specific distribution is closely tied to the cool, moist climates characteristic of these mountain ranges. Consequently, its range is fragmented by the rugged topography of the Himalayan massif.
