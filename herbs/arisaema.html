Jack-in-the-pulpit

Arisaema · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (10 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Jack-in-the-pulpit, scientifically known as Arisaema, is a captivating genus of flowering plants recognized by their unique, hood-like spathes. While celebrated for their striking architectural beauty in woodland gardens, these plants contain calcium oxalate crystals, making them toxic if ingested. They serve as fascinating subjects in botanical studies.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern concentrated within the tropical regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found inhabiting the landscapes of Burundi, Rwanda, and the DR Congo. Its presence extends further east into the diverse terrains of Northeast Tropical Africa. Specifically, documented populations exist within the borders of Ethiopia and Somalia. Consequently, the geographical range of Arisaema spans from the central heart of the continent to its northeastern reaches.
