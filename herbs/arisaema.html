Arisaema

Arisaema, commonly known as jack-in-the-pulpit or cobra lily, is a fascinating genus of flowering plants in the Araceae family. Renowned for their unique, hooded inflorescences, these perennials play vital roles in forest ecosystems. While historically used in traditional medicine, many species contain calcium oxalate crystals, requiring careful handling.

  • Scientific name: Arisaema
  • Family: Araceae
  • Native range: Africa

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Alismatales, it is classified under the family Araceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Arisaema.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern concentrated within the tropical regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found inhabiting the landscapes of Burundi, Rwanda, and the DR Congo. Its presence extends further east into the diverse terrains of Northeast Tropical Africa. Specifically, documented populations exist within the borders of Ethiopia and Somalia. Consequently, the geographical range of Arisaema spans from the central heart of the continent to its northeastern reaches.
