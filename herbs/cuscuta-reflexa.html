Cuscuta reflexa

Common dodder

Cuscuta reflexa, commonly known as Dodder, is a parasitic plant characterized by its leafless, yellow, thread-like stems. Frequently used in traditional Ayurvedic medicine, it possesses significant therapeutic properties. It is widely studied for its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective potential, offering promising applications in treating various neurological and metabolic disorders.

  • Scientific name: Cuscuta reflexa
  • Family: Convolvulaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Cuscuta reflexa, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Solanales and the family Convolvulaceae. Ultimately, it is classified under the genus Cuscuta.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution spanning several distinct regions across Asia and Africa. In East Tropical Africa, it can be found growing within the borders of Kenya. Its range extends further into the Western Indian Ocean, specifically reaching the island of Mauritius. Within Western Asia, the species is documented to inhabit parts of Afghanistan. Finally, in China, it is distributed across both the South-Central and Southeast regions.
