Common Dodder

Cuscuta reflexa · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (250 studies) · ★★★★★

Common Dodder, scientifically known as Cuscuta reflexa, is a parasitic, leafless climbing plant characterized by its bright yellow, thread-like stems. Often found intertwining with host vegetation, it lacks chlorophyll and relies entirely on its host for nutrients. In traditional medicine, it is valued for its diverse therapeutic properties.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution spanning several distinct regions across Asia and Africa. In East Tropical Africa, it can be found growing within the borders of Kenya. Its range extends further into the Western Indian Ocean, specifically reaching the island of Mauritius. Within Western Asia, the species is documented to inhabit parts of Afghanistan. Finally, in China, it is distributed across both the South-Central and Southeast regions.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Cuscuta reflexa, is a parasitic climber that is often utilized in its entirety for various traditional medicinal purposes. The medicinal properties are distributed throughout the whole plant, as it lacks true leaves and relies on its specialized structures to thrive. Specifically, the stems serve as the primary functional component for nutrient absorption from host plants, while the juice extracted from the plant is frequently employed in various therapeutic preparations. Furthermore, the aerial parts are commonly collected and processed to harness the bioactive compounds essential for its medicinal efficacy.
