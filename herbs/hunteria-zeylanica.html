Hunteria, scientifically known as Hunteria zeylanica, is a significant medicinal shrub native to South Asia. Renowned for its diverse pharmacological properties, this plant is extensively utilized in traditional medicine to treat various ailments. Its bioactive compounds offer therapeutic potential, particularly in addressing neurological disorders and managing inflammation effectively.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered under Gentianaales and falls into the family Apocynaceae. Finally, it is specifically categorized under the genus Hunteria.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution that spans across several regions of Africa and Asia. In Northeast Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in Somalia. Its presence extends further through East Tropical Africa, where it inhabits both Kenya and Tanzania. Additionally, the species is located in South Tropical Africa within the country of Mozambique. Beyond the African continent, its range reaches as far as Hainan in China.

Plant Parts

This plant, Hunteria zeylanica, possesses various components that are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The medicinal value is often derived from the leaves, which are frequently studied for their bioactive compounds, as well as the twigs that can be processed for decoctions. Furthermore, both the stem bark and the thicker bark of the main trunk contain significant alkaloid concentrations, making these woody parts essential in traditional pharmacological applications.
