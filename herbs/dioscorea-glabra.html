Dioscorea glabra

What's the taxonomical classification of Dioscorea glabra?

Dioscorea glabra belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is situated in the subclass Magnoliidae and falls under the order Dioscoreales. This plant is a member of the family Dioscoreaceae, specifically categorized within the genus Dioscorea, and is identified by its unique species name, glabra.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Dioscorea glabra has climbing, herbaceous stems that originate from fleshy, tuberous rhizomes and feature cordate or sagittate leaves with smooth, glabrous surfaces. The foliage typically consists of heart-shaped blades supported by long petioles that allow the plant to scramble over surrounding vegetation. Its flowers are small and inconspicuous, often appearing in clusters that lack showy petals. The reproductive structures are adapted for wind or insect pollination depending on the specific subspecies. As the plant matures, the vine-like stems can extend several meters in length to reach sunlight.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-draining, fertile soil and partial shade to thrive during its active growing season.

Growers should provide consistent moisture while ensuring the tubers do not sit in stagnant water to prevent rot. Once the aerial parts die back in the winter, the tubers should be lifted and stored in a cool, dry environment.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Dioscorea glabra is primarily utilized for its medicinal tubers, which contain high concentrations of steroidal saponins.

The chemical compounds found within these parts are essential for producing drugs like progesterone and cortisone.
