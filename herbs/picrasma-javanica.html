Picrasma javanica

Picrasma javaica, commonly known as Java bitterwood, is a significant medicinal plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its intense bitterness, the plant contains potent bioactive compounds like quassinoids. Traditionally used in herbal medicine, it serves as an effective emetic, febrifuge, and treatment for various intestinal parasitic infections.

  • Scientific name: Picrasma javanica
  • Family: Simaroubaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindales, it is positioned in the family Simaroubaceae. Specifically, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Picrasma, within which the species Picrasma javanica is found.

Common Names

This plant, Picrasma javanica, is known by various names depending on the region and language. In English, it is frequently referred to as Java bitterwood, while in Japanese, it is commonly called nanten or kusa-nikki. Additionally, in Chinese, the plant is known as jia-ku-pi.

Distribution

This plant is native to several regions within the Indian Subcontinent. It can be found growing in the diverse landscapes of India and its neighboring country, Bangladesh. Its distribution also extends across the mountainous terrain of the East Himalaya. Specific populations are known to thrive in the state of Assam. Furthermore, the species is documented to inhabit the country of Nepal.
