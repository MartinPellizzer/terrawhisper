Qat, scientifically known as Catha edulis, is a flowering shrub native to the Horn of Africa and the Arabian Peninsula. Renowned for its stimulant properties, its leaves are chewed to release cathinone. While culturally significant in many regions, its use is strictly regulated due to its psychoactive effects.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several key regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it is notably found within the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Moving toward the Horn of Africa, its presence is established in the Northeast Tropical regions of Eritrea and Ethiopia. The species also extends into the coastal areas of Somalia. Finally, its geographical range encompasses the territories of both Sudan and South Sudan.

Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as khat, is primarily valued for its plant leaves, which contain the psychoactive alkaloids responsible for its stimulant effects. These green leaves are typically harvested from the fresh shrubs and are consumed by chewing them to release their chemical properties. While the stems and twigs are sometimes included during the chewing process, it is the tender, young plant leaves that serve as the essential medicinal and recreational component of the species.
