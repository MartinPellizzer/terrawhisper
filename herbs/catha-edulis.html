Catha edulis

Catha edulis, commonly known as khat, is a flowering shrub native to the Horn of Africa and the Arabian Peninsula. Traditionally chewed for its stimulant effects, the plant contains cathinone, which acts similarly to amphetamines. It holds significant cultural importance in East Africa, though its use remains controversial.

  • Scientific name: Catha edulis
  • Family: Celastraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Catha edulis, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Celastrales and the family Celastraceae. Its taxonomic lineage concludes with the genus Catha.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several key regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it is notably found within the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Moving toward the Horn of Africa, its presence is established in the Northeast Tropical regions of Eritrea and Ethiopia. The species also extends into the coastal areas of Somalia. Finally, its geographical range encompasses the territories of both Sudan and South Sudan.
