Piper auritum

Hoja santa

Piper auritum, commonly known as Hoja Santa or Holy Leaf, is a perennial shrub native to Central and South America. Renowned for its large, heart-shaped leaves, this aromatic plant is highly valued in traditional medicine for its digestive, anti-inflammatory, and analgesic properties, frequently used in herbal teas and culinary dishes.

  • Scientific name: Piper auritum
  • Family: Piperaceae
  • Native range: Pacific

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, specifically falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Piperales, it is classified under the family Piperaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Piper and the specific species Piper auritum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range that spans across both the Pacific Islands and parts of the Americas. Within the Pacific region, its distribution includes Samoa in the Southwestern Pacific and the Caroline Islands in the Northwestern Pacific. It can also be found in the North-Central Pacific, specifically in Hawaii. Moving toward the Americas, the species is located in Central Mexico. Finally, its range extends to the Southeastern United States, where it is present in Florida.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Hoja Santa, is characterized by its distinct morphological features, most notably its large, heart-shaped leaves that emit a strong, anise-like aroma. Each individual leaf is broad and thin with prominent venation, making it a highly recognizable component used in both culinary and medicinal applications. Supporting these expansive structures is a sturdy, upright stem that provides the necessary stability for the plant to grow in tropical environments.
