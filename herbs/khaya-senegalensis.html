African mahogany

Khaya senegalensis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (75 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

African mahogany, scientifically known as Khaya senegalensis, is a majestic evergreen tree native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its durable timber, it also holds profound medicinal value. Various parts, including the bark and leaves, are traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to malaria.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the West Tropical Africa region. Specifically, it can be found growing within the borders of Benin. Its natural range also extends into the nation of Burkina. Furthermore, the species is documented to exist in Gambia and Guinea-Bissau. Finally, the geographical presence of this medicinal plant includes the country of Guinea.

Plant Parts

This plant, Khaya senegalensis, possesses various components that are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The leaves are often harvested for decoctions, while the entire leaf structure can be used in different preparations depending on the specific medicinal need. The stem serves as the primary structural support for the tree, but it is the stem bark that is frequently sought after for its bioactive compounds. Additionally, the root bark is highly valued in traditional medicine, offering potent medicinal qualities that are distinct from those found in the upper portions of the plant.
