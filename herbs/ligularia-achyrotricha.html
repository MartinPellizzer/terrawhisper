Ligularia achyrotricha

What's the taxonomical classification of Ligularia achyrotricha?

Ligularia achyrotricha belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Asterales. This plant is a representative of the family Asteraceae, which is one of the most prominent plant families. Within this family, it is placed in the genus Ligularia, and its specific biological identity is defined by its species name, achyrotricha.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Ligularia achyrotricha has large, broadly ovate to cordate leaves that are densely covered in fine, white, appressed hairs on both the upper and lower surfaces. These leaves feature coarsely serrated or dentate margins and are supported by thick, hairy petioles. The plant produces terminal inflorescences composed of bright yellow ray and disc florets characteristic of the Asteraceae family. The stems are robust and similarly pubescent, providing structural support for the heavy foliage. This dense coating of trichomes serves as a defining morphological feature that distinguishes it from closely related species.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude regions of southwestern China, specifically within the provinces of Yunnan and Sichuan. It typically inhabits alpine meadows and rocky slopes situated at elevations ranging from 2,500 to 4,000 meters. These specific environments provide the cool, moist conditions required for the species to thrive. The distribution is largely confined to the mountainous terrain of the Hengduan Mountains. Consequently, its range is quite localized compared to more widespread plant species.
