Gambeya lacourtiana

Gambeya lacourtiana, commonly known as the Gambeya tree, is a significant medicinal plant native to various African tropical forests. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, its bark, leaves, and roots are traditionally used to treat ailments ranging from inflammation and skin infections to digestive issues and febrile conditions.

  • Scientific name: Gambeya lacourtiana
  • Family: Sapotaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Ericales, it is a member of the family Sapotaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Gambeya.

Common Names

This plant, Gambeya lacourtiana, is known by various common names depending on the linguistic and regional context in which it is found. In French, it is frequently referred to as Gambey, while in English, it is often called the Gambeya tree. Within different West African linguistic groups, its names vary significantly, such as "Gâmbeyâ" in certain local dialects, reflecting its deep-rooted presence in the regional ethnobotanical landscape.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the West-Central Tropical Africa region. Its presence is notably documented within the borders of the Central African Republic. Furthermore, it can be found growing throughout Cameroon and the nation of Congo. The species also extends its range into the territory of Gabon. Finally, its geographical reach includes the expansive landscapes of the DR Congo.
