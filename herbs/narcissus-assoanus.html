Narcissus assoanus

Narcissus assoanus, commonly known as the Assoan Daffodil, is a delicate perennial species native to the rugged terrains of the Middle East. Renowned for its elegant, pale yellow blooms, this botanical treasure holds significant ethnobotanical interest, offering unique phytochemical properties that suggest potential applications in traditional medicinal practices.

  • Scientific name: Narcissus assoanus
  • Family: Amaryllidaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asparagales, specifically within the family Amaryllidaceae. Finally, its taxonomic placement is completed at the genus level as Narcissus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Narcissus assoanus, is identified by various regional names across different languages and cultures. In French, it is referred to as Narcisse d'Asso, while in Catalan, it bears the name almesquí. Its nomenclature extends into Arabic dialects, where it is called نرجس أسوني (narjis asuni) and similarly known as نرجس اسونى in Sinai Arabic. Interestingly, in German contexts, it is sometimes associated with the name Narcissus requienii.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning several continents and distinct climatic zones. In Middle Europe, its presence can be noted within the borders of Germany. Its distribution extends into Southwestern Europe, where it is found in both France and Spain. Moving eastward, the species is also established across Western Asia, specifically in Türkiye. Interestingly, the plant has reached the Southeastern United States, with documented occurrences in Louisiana.
