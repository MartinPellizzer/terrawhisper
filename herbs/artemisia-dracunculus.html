Artemisia dracunculus

Artemisia dracunculus, commonly known as tarragon, is a perennial herb celebrated for its distinct anise-like aroma and culinary versatility. Beyond the kitchen, this aromatic plant holds significant medicinal value, traditionally utilized for its digestive, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties, making it a staple in both herbalism and gourmet cooking.

  • Scientific name: Artemisia dracunculus
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales, falling into the family Asteraceae. Finally, its taxonomic placement concludes within the genus Artemisia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution across several regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, it can be found growing in both Great Britain and Norway. Moving toward the center of the continent, the species is also established in Middle Europe. Specifically, its range includes the territories of Austria and the Czechia-Slovakia region. Finally, the plant is documented as being present within Germany.
