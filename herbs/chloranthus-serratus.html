Chloranthus serratus

Chloranthus serratus, commonly known as the serrated chloranthus, is a perennial herb native to East Asia. Valued in traditional medicine, this plant is recognized for its distinct serrated leaf margins and small, fragrant flowers. It is frequently utilized for its purported anti-inflammatory properties and its role in various herbal remedies.

  • Scientific name: Chloranthus serratus
  • Family: Chloranthaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further organized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Chloranthales, falling into the family Chloranthaceae. Finally, its taxonomic placement concludes within the genus Chloranthus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical range across several key regions of Eastern Asia. Within the Russian Far East, its presence is specifically noted in the Kuril Islands. The species is also extensively distributed throughout various parts of China, including the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan regions. Additionally, its habitat extends into the islands of Japan.
