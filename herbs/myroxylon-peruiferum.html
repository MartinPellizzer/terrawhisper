Myroxylon peruiferum

Myroxylon peruiferum, commonly known as the Peruvian Myroxylon, is a significant medicinal plant native to South American rainforests. Renowned for its aromatic resins, it possesses potent antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties. Traditionally used to treat skin ailments and respiratory issues, this botanical treasure remains vital in modern natural pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Myroxylon peruiferum
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, specifically classified within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It falls under the order Fabales and the family Fabaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement within the genus Myroxylon.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Myroxylon peruiferum, is recognized by various names across different linguistic and cultural contexts. In Quechua, it is called Pukakina, while in Portuguese, it is commonly referred to as Bálsamo-do-peru. In Chinese, the species is identified as 南美槐, and in Spanish, it is known as Quinina colorada.

This plant exhibits a diverse range of habitats throughout the southern portions of North America and Central America.
