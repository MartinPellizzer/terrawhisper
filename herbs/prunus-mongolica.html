Prunus mongolica

Prunus mongolica, commonly known as the Mongolian Cherry, is a resilient deciduous shrub native to East Asian landscapes. Valued in traditional medicine, its bark and fruits possess antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. This hardy plant serves as a vital resource for herbal remedies and contributes to regional ecological biodiversity.

  • Scientific name: Prunus mongolica
  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Rosales, it is classified under the family Rosaceae. Finally, its taxonomic designation is completed by its genus, Prunus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Prunus mongolica, is referred to by several different common names depending on the language and region. In Chinese, it is known as 蒙古扁桃 (zh), which is specifically written as 蒙古扁桃 in both mainland China (zh-cn) and Taiwan (zh-tw). Additionally, the plant is identified by the name 乌兰-布衣勒语 (zh) in its Mongolian context.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across specific regions of East Asia. In China, it can be found throughout the Inner Mongolia autonomous region. Its presence also extends into the North-Central parts of China. Furthermore, the species is widely distributed across the country of Mongolia. These locations define the core natural range for Prunus mongolica.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Prunus mongolica, is valued for its medicinal properties which are primarily derived from its seeds. The most significant component used in traditional remedies is the kernel, which is extracted from the fruit. Within each individual kernel, there is a rich concentration of therapeutic oil that is often processed for various applications. By utilizing the kernels in this manner, practitioners can access the concentrated nutrients and bioactive compounds essential for its healing effects.
