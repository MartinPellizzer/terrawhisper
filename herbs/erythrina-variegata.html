Indian Coral Tree

Erythrina variegata · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Moderate (108 studies) · ★★★☆☆

Indian Coral Tree, scientifically known as Erythrina variegata, is a striking ornamental legume renowned for its vibrant scarlet blossoms. Beyond its aesthetic appeal, this versatile plant holds significant value in traditional medicine, where its various parts are utilized to treat ailments ranging from inflammation to skin disorders and digestive issues.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fabales, it is a member of the Fabaceae family. Specifically, it is classified under the genus Erythrina.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several regions across the African continent. In Northern Africa, its presence can be noted in Egypt. Its range extends to the Macaronesian islands, specifically including Cape Verde. Within West Tropical Africa, the species is found in both Nigeria and Senegal. Finally, it reaches into West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically around the Gulf of Guinea Islands.

Plant Parts

This plant, Erythrina variegata, possesses various components that are utilized in traditional medicinal practices. The root bark is often sought for its specific therapeutic properties, while the bark found on the main trunk and the specialized stem bark are also harvested for their bioactive compounds. Additionally, the sturdy wood of the tree and its small, potent seeds contribute to the diverse range of botanical materials available from this species for various herbal applications.
