Sea Rush, scientifically known as Juncus maritimus, is a resilient perennial plant commonly found in coastal salt marshes and sandy habitats. Renowned for its ability to thrive in saline environments, it offers significant ecological value and traditional medicinal uses, particularly for its soothing properties in treating skin ailments.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Juncus maritimus is primarily sexual, characterized by bisexual flowers that do not exhibit asexual reproduction. The flowering period typically begins in July and concludes in August, featuring small, brown flowers that range in length from 0.3 cm to 0.35 cm. These flowers are arranged in inflorescences with a length between 0.04 m and 0.09 m. The plant utilizes an abiotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on the wind for pollination, and self-fertilization is notably absent. Following successful pollination, the plant develops dehiscent fruit in the form of a capsule. These capsules are relatively small, measuring between 0.25 cm and 0.35 cm in length, and are highly prolific, containing more than 1000 seeds per fruit. The seeds are extremely lightweight, with a mean mass of 3.1e-05 g (ranging from 3e-05 g to 3e-05 g), which facilitates their dispersal. The dispersal syndrome is classified as zoochorous, specifically through epizoochory, allowing the seeds to be transported by animals.
