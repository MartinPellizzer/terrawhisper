Teucrium labiosum

What's the taxonomical classification of Teucrium labiosum?

Teucrium labiosum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Following its taxonomic descent, it falls under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales, it is a member of the family Lamiaceae. Finally, the plant is categorized under the genus Teucrium, with its specific designation being the species labiosum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Teucrium labiosum has woody, perennial stems and small, lanceolate leaves that are typically grayish-green with slightly serrated margins. The foliage is often characterized by a fine, pubescent texture that helps the plant retain moisture. Its most distinctive morphological feature is the bilabiate flower, which displays a prominent upper lip and a smaller, protruding lower lip. These tubular blossoms are usually light pink or white and emerge from the axils of the leaves. The plant's overall structure is compact and shrubby, making it well-adapted to rocky or arid environments.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is endemic to the dry, rocky regions of the southwestern part of the Iberian Peninsula, specifically within certain areas of southern Spain. Its natural range is highly localized and restricted to specific Mediterranean habitats. It typically flourishes in limestone-rich soils found on steep slopes and cliffs. These specialized environmental conditions limit its presence to very small, fragmented populations. Consequently, its geographical spread is quite narrow compared to other species in its genus.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-draining, sandy soil and full sunlight to thrive in a warm, Mediterranean-style climate.

You should water it sparingly, allowing the substrate to dry out completely between irrigation cycles to prevent root rot. Because it is a drought-tolerant species, it performs best in xeriscaping projects or rock gardens where water is limited. Regular pruning helps maintain its compact, mounding shape and encourages denser foliage growth.

Avoid over-fertilizing, as excessive nutrients can lead to weak stems and leggy growth patterns.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains alpha-spinasterol, friedelinol, lupeol, stearic acid, and methyl phaeophorbid e a as its primary medicinal compounds. These specific chemical constituents contribute to the diverse pharmacological profile of the species. The presence of triterpenoids like lupeol and friedelinol often relates to anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties. Additionally, the fatty acid stearic acid and various sterols support the plant's overall biological activity. These bioactive molecules work together to define the therapeutic potential of Teucrium labiosum.

According to a study published by "Zhong yao cai = Zhongyaocai = Journal of Chinese medicinal materials", six compounds were obtained and identified from Teucrium labiosum. These constituents include stearic acid (I), alpha-spinasterol (II), Methyl phaeophorbide a (III), friedelino (IV), lupeol (V), and stigmasta-5,22-dien-3beta-ol (VI). The isolation and purification of these components were achieved through silica gel column and Sephadex LH-20 chromatography. All 6 of these compounds are obtained from this plant for the first time.
