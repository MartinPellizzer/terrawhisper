Waxflower, scientifically known as Hoya, is a captivating genus of tropical climbing plants celebrated for their stunning, star-shaped blooms. Often called porcelain flowers due to their waxy texture, these resilient succulents feature fragrant blossoms and lush, trailing foliage, making them a beloved choice for indoor gardeners worldwide.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Gentianales, it is classified under the family Apocynaceae. Ultimately, its specific taxonomic designation is placed within the genus Hoya.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution throughout various regions of East Asia. Within China, it can be found spanning from the South-Central provinces to the island of Hainan. Its presence also extends into the Southeast regions of the country and up into the high altitudes of Tibet. Beyond the Chinese mainland, the species is widely distributed across Eastern Asia. Specifically, its range includes the islands of Japan, completing its regional footprint.
