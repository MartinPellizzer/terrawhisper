Hoya

Waxflower

Hoya, commonly known as wax plants, are captivating tropical climbers celebrated for their thick, waxy, star-shaped blossoms. Beyond their stunning ornamental appeal, various species offer traditional medicinal uses, ranging from treating skin ailments to soothing digestive issues. These resilient plants blend aesthetic beauty with fascinating ethnobotanical properties.

  • Scientific name: Hoya
  • Family: Apocynaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Gentianales, it is classified under the family Apocynaceae. Ultimately, its specific taxonomic designation is placed within the genus Hoya.

Its presence also extends into the Southeast regions of the country and up into the high altitudes of Tibet. Beyond the Chinese mainland, the species is widely distributed across Eastern Asia. Specifically, its range includes the islands of Japan, completing its regional footprint.
