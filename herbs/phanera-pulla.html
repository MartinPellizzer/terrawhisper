Phanera pulla

Phanera pulla, commonly known as a significant medicinal woody climber, holds profound importance in traditional healing practices. Native to tropical regions, this plant is highly valued for its diverse therapeutic properties. Researchers increasingly explore its bioactive compounds to develop natural remedies for various ailments, bridging ancient wisdom with modern pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Phanera pulla
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Under the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered within the Fabales and placed in the family Fabaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Phanera.

Common Names

This plant, Phanera pulla, is known by various names depending on the region and language. In English, it is commonly referred to as the Black Butterfly Pea, while in Hindi, it is known as Kalipali. In Bengali, the plant is called Kalo Phali, and in Tamil, it is identified as Karuppu Phali.

Distribution

This plant is characterized by a specific and limited geographical range within the Southeast Asian region. Its natural habitat is primarily concentrated within the Indo-China peninsula. Specifically, the species can be found growing in the territories of Cambodia. Additionally, its distribution extends into the country of Thailand. These locations represent the primary areas where this medicinal species is naturally documented.
