Ficus sarmentosa

What's the taxonomical classification of Ficus sarmentosa?

Ficus sarmentosa belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its progression through the taxonomic hierarchy, it falls under the order Rosales and is a member of the family Moraceae. Ultimately, the plant is identified by its genus, Ficus, and its specific species designation, sarmentosa.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Ficus sarmentoosa has woody, climbing stems characterized by a twining habit and elliptical to ovate leaves that are typically dark green with acute tips. The foliage often features a glossy texture and prominent venation on the undersides. Small, inconspicuous flowers are produced within specialized structures known as syconia. These fleshy fruits eventually ripen to a dark color, serving as a primary feature of the plant's reproductive morphology. The overall structure allows the plant to scramble over other vegetation in its natural habitat.
