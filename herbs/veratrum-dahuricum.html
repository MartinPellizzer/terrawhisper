Veratrum dahuricum

Veratrum dahuricum, commonly known as Dahurian false hellebore, is a potent medicinal herb native to East Asia. Renowned in traditional medicine for its alkaloid-rich properties, it is primarily used to treat hypertension and various inflammatory conditions. However, due to its high toxicity, it requires expert handling and precise dosage.

  • Scientific name: Veratrum dahuricum
  • Family: Melanthiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and order Liliales, it is further categorized into the family Melanthiaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Veratrum.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several key regions in Northern Asia. Within Siberia, it can be found growing in the areas of Buryatiya, Chita, and Irkutsk. Its presence extends significantly into the Russian Far East as well. Specifically, it inhabits the Amur region and the Khabarovsk territory. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in various parts of the Siberian and Far Eastern landscapes.

Plant Parts

This plant, Veratrum dahuricum, is a perennial herb known for its medicinal properties, which are primarily concentrated in its underground structures. The most significant part used in traditional pharmacology is the rhizome, a thick, fleshy stem that grows horizontally beneath the soil surface. This part of the plant serves as a vital storage organ, accumulating various potent alkaloids that are harvested for their therapeutic effects. While the leaves and stems are also visible above ground, it is the complex chemical composition found within the rhizome that defines its medicinal value.
