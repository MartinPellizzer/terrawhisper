Bletilla striata

Chinese ground orchid

Bletilla striata, commonly known as the fringed bletilla or Chinese orchid, is a perennial herb highly valued in traditional medicine. Renowned for its medicinal tubers, it is extensively used to promote wound healing, reduce inflammation, and treat various respiratory ailments, serving as a vital component in many herbal remedies.

  • Scientific name: Bletilla striata
  • Family: Orchidaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Bletilla striata, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Asparagales. Finally, it is a member of the Orchidaceae family and is situated within the genus Bletilla.

Common Names

This plant, Bletilla striata, is widely recognized across various Chinese-speaking regions by the common name 白芨, which is used consistently in simplified and traditional scripts. Specifically, in mainland China (zh-cn), Singapore (zh-sg), and Hong Kong (zh-hk), it is known as 白芨, while in Taiwan (zh-tw) and for general traditional Chinese usage (zh-hant), the name remains 白芨.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across several key regions of East Asia. In China, its presence is documented throughout the South-Central, North-Central, and Southeast provinces. Its range extends beyond the mainland into the island nation of Japan. Additionally, the species can be found growing within the Korean Peninsula. Together, these locations highlight a widespread habitat within the eastern temperate zones.

Plant Parts

This plant, Bletilla striata, possesses a specialized underground anatomy essential for its survival and medicinal value. Its structure is characterized by thick, fleshy pseudobulbs that serve as primary storage organs, which are interconnected through a network of creeping rhizomes. These rhizomes facilitate vegetative spread, while the tubers and various fleshy roots allow the plant to store vital nutrients and moisture during dormant periods. Chemically, these parts are highly significant because they are rich in polysaccharide compounds, which are the key bioactive constituents utilized in traditional medicine for their healing properties.
