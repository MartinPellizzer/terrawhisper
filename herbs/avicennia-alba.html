Physiology

The physiology of Avicennia alba is characterized by specialized adaptations to hypersaline and anaerobic intertidal environments. In terms of carbon assimilation, the plant utilizes a C3 photosynthetic pathway, which dictates its metabolic processes and gas exchange mechanisms within the mangrove ecosystem. Furthermore, regarding its nutrient acquisition capabilities, Avicennia alba is not a nitrogen fixer, meaning it relies on the uptake of available nitrates and ammonium from the surrounding substrate rather than engaging in symbiotic nitrogen fixation. To survive in its extreme habitat, the plant also employs complex physiological mechanisms such as salt excretion through specialized glands on its leaves and specialized aerial roots, known as pneumatophores, which facilitate gas exchange in waterlogged, oxygen-deficient soils.
