sour cherry

Prunus cerasus · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (915 studies) · ★★★★★

Sour cherry, scientifically known as Prunus cerasus, is a nutrient-dense stone fruit celebrated for its medicinal properties. Rich in anthocyanins, vitamin C, and melatonin, it serves as a potent antioxidant. It is widely used to reduce inflammation, support joint health, and improve sleep quality through natural metabolic regulation.

The reproduction of Prunus cerasus is characterized by a sexual process facilitated by biotic pollination, specifically utilizing an insect pollination syndrome. The flowering period typically begins in April and concludes in May, during which the plant produces white flowers arranged in umbel inflorescences. These flowers vary in length, ranging from a minimum of 1 cm to a maximum of 2.5 cm. Notably, self-fertilization is absent in this species, and asexual reproduction is also not present, emphasizing its reliance on cross-pollination. Following successful fertilization, the plant develops fleshy, indehiscent fruits classified as drupes. These red fruits typically measure between 1.2 cm and 1.5 cm in mean length and 1.2 cm to 1.53 cm in mean width, with maximum dimensions reaching 1.5 cm in length and 2 cm in width. The dispersal of the plant follows a zoochorous strategy, specifically endozoochorous, meaning the seeds are dispersed through the digestive tracts of animals. The seeds themselves are characterized by a mean mass of approximately 0.209 g (ranging from 0.1275 g to 0.205 g), a mean volume of 180 mm³, and a maximum length of 10 mm.
