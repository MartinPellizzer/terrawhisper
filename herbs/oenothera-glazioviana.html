What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant utilizes its entire aerial structure, including the leaves, stems, and flowers, for various medicinal applications. The plant's surface is densely covered in glandular trichomes that facilitate the production and storage of bioactive secondary metabolites. These tiny hair-like structures are essential for concentrating the chemical compounds used in traditional remedies. Extracts from these parts are often studied for their potential anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. By harvesting the plant at specific growth stages, practitioners can maximize the potency of these therapeutic elements.

According to a study published by "Plant diversity", the capitae glandular trichomes of Oenothera glazioviana were collected using laser microdissection and analyzed by gas chromatography-mass spectrometry. These glandular trichomes produce a volatile compound identified as 4-hydroxy-4-methylpentan-2-one, which is also referred to as compound 1. This specific compound 1 displays antimicrobial, insecticidal, and phytotoxic activities. These results suggest that compound 1 might function as a defensive compound in the capitae glandular trichomes of Oenothera glazioviana against pathogens, insect herbivores, and competitive plants.
