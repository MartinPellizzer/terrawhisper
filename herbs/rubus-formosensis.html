Rubus formosensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Rubus formosensis?

Rubus formosensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Moving through its hierarchical structure, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its lineage through the order Rosales and the family Rosaceae, the plant is categorized under the genus Rubus, ultimately reaching its specific designation as the species formosensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Rubus formosenensis has arching, woody stems characterized by dense coverings of sharp, recurved prickles and a distinctively glandular hairy texture. The leaves are typically trifoliate or pentafoliate, featuring serrated margins and a dark green, somewhat rugose upper surface. Beneath the foliage, the undersides often exhibit a paler coloration due to a fine layer of pubescence. Small, white to pale pink flowers emerge from the terminal ends of the branches during the blooming season. These blossoms eventually give way to aggregate fruits that transition from green to a deep red or black when fully mature.

Because its range is restricted to this specific territory, it is considered a localized species within the broader Rubus genus.
