castor bean plant

Ricinus communis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (756 studies) · ★★★★★

Castor bean plant, scientifically known as Ricinus communis, is a versatile perennial shrub prized for its medicinal and industrial properties. While its seeds contain the potent toxin ricin, they are a vital source of castor oil. This oil is widely used in traditional medicine and various modern pharmaceutical applications.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malpighiales, it falls under the family Euphorbiaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Ricinus, specifically known as Ricinus communis.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse presence across several key regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its distribution can be observed within Great Britain. Moving toward the center of the continent, it is found throughout Middle Europe, specifically in Germany. The distribution extends significantly into Southwestern Europe, where it thrives in France. Finally, the plant is also well-established in the Southwestern European territories of Portugal and Spain.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Ricinus communis, utilizes various anatomical structures for its biological functions and medicinal applications. The large, palmate leaves are highly distinctive, with each individual leaf providing a significant surface area for photosynthesis. The most potent part of the plant is contained within the seeds, which are known to produce castor oil; however, each single seed must be handled with extreme caution due to the presence of ricin. Below the surface, the extensive roots anchor the plant firmly in the soil and facilitate the absorption of water and essential nutrients necessary for its rapid growth.
