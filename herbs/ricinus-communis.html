castor bean plant

Ricinus communis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (756 studies) · ★★★★★

Castor bean plant, scientifically known as Ricinus communis, is a versatile perennial shrub prized for its medicinal and industrial properties. While its seeds contain the potent toxin ricin, they are a vital source of castor oil. This oil is widely used in traditional medicine and various modern pharmaceutical applications.

The plant exhibits a non-succulent morphology and is strictly non-carnivorous, relying on standard soil-based nutrient uptake rather than specialized trapping mechanisms. Notably, it is not a nitrogen fixer, meaning it depends on available soil nitrates and ammonium for its nitrogen requirements. The foliage displays significant morphological variability; the leaves are elliptic in form, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 10 cm to a maximum of 60 cm. Reproduction

The reproduction of Ricinus communis is characterized by a sexual process that is monoecious in nature, with asexual reproduction being entirely absent. The flowering period is quite extensive, typically beginning in January and concluding in August, though it can be variable. The inflorescence is relatively small, ranging from a minimum length of 0.15 m to a maximum of 0.3 m. The flowers themselves are small, with dimensions ranging from 0.4 to 1.2 cm in length and 0.3 to 0.5 cm in width. Pollination involves both abiotic and biotic mechanisms, specifically utilizing wind and insects. Once fertilized, the plant produces dry, dehiscence-type fruits categorized as capsules. These capsules vary in size, with lengths ranging from 1 cm to a maximum of 2.5 cm (averaging 2 cm) and widths between 1.8 cm and 2 cm. The dispersal syndrome is identified as hydrochorous, meaning seeds are dispersed by water. The resulting seeds are highly productive, with counts exceeding 1000 seeds per fruit. Individual seeds possess a mean mass of approximately 0.322 g (ranging from 0.12 g to 0.73 g), a mean length of 15 mm (with a range of 7 to 12 mm), and a consistent mean volume of 360 mm³. Notably, self-fertilization is absent in this species.
