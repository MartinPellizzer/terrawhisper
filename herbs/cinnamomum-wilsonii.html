What's the taxonomical classification of Cinnamomum wilsonii?

Cinnamomum wilsonii belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is classified in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Its taxonomic progression continues through the order Laurales and the family Lauraceae, eventually reaching its specific placement within the genus Cinnamomum. Finally, the plant is identified at the species level as wilsonii.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant has leaves that are used medicinally to treat various ailments. These foliage components are often prepared as decoctions or infusions to address internal health issues. Practitioners frequently utilize the leaf extracts for their potential anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties. Beyond topical applications, the leaves serve as a key ingredient in traditional herbal remedies aimed at improving digestion. This specific use of the leaves highlights the versatile therapeutic value found within the plant's structure.

According to a study published by "Journal of natural products", twelve new megastigmane sesquiterpenoids called wilsonols A-L (1-12) were isolated from the leaves of Cinnamomum wilsonii. These compounds were identified alongside seven known analogues (13-19) through spectroscopic analyses. The absolute configurations of compounds 1-5 were established via single-crystal X-ray diffraction with Cu Kα irradiation, while 6-12 were determined by the modified Mosher's method. For medicinal evaluation, compounds 1-9 and 13-19 were tested for in vitro cytotoxicity against 5 human cancer cell lines, which were HL-60, SMMC-7721, A-549, MCF-7, and SW-480. Compound 13 specifically showed IC50 values between 2.5 and 12 μM and selectivity indices of >10 against the SMMC-7721, A-549, and MCF-7 cell lines.
