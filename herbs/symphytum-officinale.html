Comfrey, scientifically known as Symphytum officinale, is a perennial herb renowned for its potent healing properties. Historically valued in traditional medicine, it contains allantoin, which promotes cell regeneration. Often used topically for skin irritations, bruises, and bone fractures, this versatile plant remains a cornerstone of herbal wound care.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as comfrey, is highly valued in traditional herbalism for the specific properties found within its underground structures. The most therapeutically significant part is the root, which contains high concentrations of allantoin to aid in tissue repair. When harvesting, practitioners often collect the thick, fleshy roots to ensure a potent extract, as the medicinal compounds are most concentrated in this area. Whether prepared as a decoction or a topical salve, the root serves as the primary source of the plant's healing potential.
