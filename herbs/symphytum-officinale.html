Symphytum officinale

Comfrey

Symphytum officinale, commonly known as comfrey, is a perennial herb valued for centuries in traditional medicine. Rich in allantoin, it promotes tissue regeneration and wound healing. Often used topically for bruises, sprains, and inflammation, this potent plant requires careful handling due to its complex alkaloid profile and medicinal strength.

  • Scientific name: Symphytum officinale
  • Family: Boraginaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized into the order Boraginales and the family Boraginaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is established under the genus Symphytum.

Common Names

This plant, Symphytum officinale, is known by various names across different regions and languages. In Simplified Chinese, it is referred to as 聚合草 (zh-hans), and this same name is used in Mainland China (zh-cn), Singapore (zh-sg), and Hong Kong (zh-hk). Similarly, in Traditional Chinese, it is called 聚合草 (zh-hant).

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as comfrey, is highly valued in traditional herbalism for the specific properties found within its underground structures. The most therapeutically significant part is the root, which contains high concentrations of allantoin to aid in tissue repair. When harvesting, practitioners often collect the thick, fleshy roots to ensure a potent extract, as the medicinal compounds are most concentrated in this area. Whether prepared as a decoction or a topical salve, the root serves as the primary source of the plant's healing potential.
