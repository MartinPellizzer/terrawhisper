Scientifically known as Chlorophyton arundinaceum, is a resilient perennial herb celebrated in traditional medicine. Often referred to as "Devil's Claw" in certain regions, this plant is prized for its bioactive compounds. It is frequently utilized for its potential anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and adaptogenic properties to support overall holistic wellness.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution primarily centered within the Asian continent. In the Indian Subcontinent, it can be found growing in the region of Assam. Furthermore, its presence extends to the East Himalaya and throughout the country of Nepal. Beyond the Indian Subcontinent, the species is also distributed within the Indo-China region. Specifically, it has been documented growing in Myanmar.

Plant Parts

This plant, Chlorophyton amaryllifolium (often referred to in various botanical contexts as Chlorophyton arundinaceum), derives its significant medicinal properties primarily from its underground root system. The tuberous root serves as the main storage organ for the plant, containing a concentrated array of bioactive compounds used in traditional healing practices. Because the therapeutic essence is housed within this specific part, the root must be carefully harvested and processed to extract its healing benefits.
