Begonia

Begonia, commonly known as a versatile ornamental, holds significant value in traditional medicine. Beyond its striking foliage, various species possess bioactive compounds with anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant properties. Often used in folk remedies to treat skin ailments and digestive issues, this plant bridges the gap between beauty and healing.

  • Scientific name: Begonia
  • Family: Begoniaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the order Cucurbitales and is a member of the family Begoniaceae. Ultimately, it is categorized under the genus Begonia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Begonia, is recognized by various common names depending on the linguistic context. In Simplified Chinese, it is referred to as 秋海棠属 (zh-hans) and 秋海棠属 (zh-cn), while in Traditional Chinese, it is known as 秋海棠屬 (zh-hant). In the specific regional contexts of Hong Kong, it is identified as 秋海棠屬 (zh-hk), and in Singapore, it is called 秋海棠属 (zh-sg).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution that spans several distinct regions across the globe. In Southeastern Europe, it can be found specifically within the island of Sicilia. Its range also extends to the Macaronesia archipelago, where it is located on the island of Madeira. Within West Tropical Africa, the species is documented in Burkina and Ghana. Furthermore, the plant's presence is confirmed in the West Tropical African nation of Guinea-Bissau.
