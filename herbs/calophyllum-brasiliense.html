Jacareuba, scientifically known as Calophyllum brasiliense, is a versatile tropical tree valued for its robust timber and profound medicinal properties. Native to Neotropical regions, its leaves and bark contain bioactive compounds with significant anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant activities, offering promising potential for traditional healing and modern pharmacological research.

It thrives in a variety of distinct habitats, ranging from seasonally flooded environments like Floresta de Várzea and Floresta de Igapó to more stable settings such as Floresta de Terra Firme, Floresta Ombrófila (pluvial forests), and Floresta Estacional Semidecidual. Additionally, this species is found in specialized landscapes including Campinarana, Restinga, Palmeiral, and riparian corridors known as Floresta Ciliar or Galeria. The reproduction of Calophyllum brasiliense is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, relying primarily on insects to facilitate the transfer of pollen. Following successful pollination, the plant produces seeds that exhibit significant morphological variation. These seeds possess a mean length of 18 mm, with individual measurements ranging from a minimum of 15 mm to a maximum of 30 mm. In terms of mass, the seeds show a wide distributional spectrum, with a mean mass of approximately 3.95 g; however, individual seed weight can vary drastically, from a minimum of 0.51 g to a maximum of as much as 15.97 g. Once mature, the dispersal of these seeds follows a zoochorous syndrome, meaning they are distributed via animals, which plays a crucial role in the species' ability to colonize new habitats.
