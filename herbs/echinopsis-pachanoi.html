Echinopsis pachanoi

San pedro

Echinopsis pachanoi, commonly known as San Pedro cactus, is a columnar succulent native to the Andean highlands. Renowned for its long, ribbed stems, it has been used for centuries in traditional South American rituals. Beyond its cultural significance, it contains psychoactive alkaloids, making it a subject of botanical interest.

  • Scientific name: Echinopsis pachanoi
  • Family: Cactaceae
  • Native range: Europe

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Caryophyllales and the family Cactaceae. Finally, it is identified by its scientific genus, Echinopsis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Echinopsis pachanoi, is recognized by various names across different cultures and languages. In Portuguese, it is commonly called Wachuma, while in Polish, it is referred to as San pedro. In Quechua, the names Sanpidru wachuma are used, whereas in Hungarian, it is known as San Pedro-kaktusz, and in Finnish, it is called Kynttiläkaktus.
