Licorice, scientifically known as Glycyrrhiza, is a versatile medicinal plant prized for its sweet, woody roots. Long used in traditional medicine, it possesses potent anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and soothing properties. Frequently employed to alleviate digestive issues and respiratory congestion, this botanical powerhouse remains a staple in both herbalism and pharmacology.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as licorice, is highly valued in traditional medicine primarily for its underground structures. The most therapeutically active component is the root, which contains high concentrations of glycyrrhizin. In many pharmacopeias, the medicinal substance is specifically referred to as radix Glycyrrhizae. While the botanical structure consists of the primary roots, some preparations may involve the thicker, horizontal rhizomes that extend through the soil. To ensure stability and ease of storage for medicinal use, the plant is typically processed into dried roots, which are then used to create various decoctions and extracts.
