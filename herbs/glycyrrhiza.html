Glycyrrhiza

Glycyrrhiza, commonly known as licorice, is a versatile medicinal plant prized for centuries in traditional medicine. Its roots contain glycyrrhizin, offering potent anti-inflammatory, soothing, and antimicrobial properties. Widely used to treat digestive issues, respiratory ailments, and skin irritations, this botanical powerhouse remains a staple in modern herbal pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Glycyrrhiza
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales. Finally, it is a member of the family Fabaceae, specifically categorized under the genus Glycyrrhiza.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as licorice, is highly valued in traditional medicine primarily for its underground structures. The most therapeutically active component is the root, which contains high concentrations of glycyrrhizin. In many pharmacopeias, the medicinal substance is specifically referred to as radix Glycyrrhizae. While the botanical structure consists of the primary roots, some preparations may involve the thicker, horizontal rhizomes that extend through the soil. To ensure stability and ease of storage for medicinal use, the plant is typically processed into dried roots, which are then used to create various decoctions and extracts.
