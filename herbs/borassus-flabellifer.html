Reproduction

The reproduction of Borassus flabellifer is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, primarily driven by insects such as bees. The species exhibits a lack of self-fertilization, ensuring genetic diversity through cross-pollination. Following successful fertilization, the plant produces fleshy fruits that are black in color. These fruits vary in dimensions, with a mean fruit length of 16.25 cm (ranging from a minimum of 10 cm to a maximum of 15 cm) and a mean fruit width of 20 cm. The seeds produced are significant in size, maintaining a consistent seed mass of 20 g (with no variance between minimum and maximum values) and a uniform seed volume of 26,000 mm³. Dispersal is achieved through a zoochorous syndrome, where animals play a critical role in distributing the seeds to new environments.
