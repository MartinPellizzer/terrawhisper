Aristolochia contorta

Aristolochia contorta, commonly known as creeping aristolochia, is a perennial climbing herb native to East Asia. Historically utilized in traditional medicine for its anti-inflammatory and analgesic properties, modern science warns of its significant nephrotoxicity and carcinogenicity due to aristolochic acids, strictly limiting its contemporary medicinal application and safety.

  • Scientific name: Aristolochia contorta
  • Family: Aristolochiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Piperales and the family Aristolochiaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Aristolochia, and its specific species name, Aristolochia contorta.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions in East Asia. In the Russian Far East, it can be found growing within the Khabarovsk and Primorye territories. Its presence extends significantly into China, where it inhabits the region of Inner Mongolia. Additionally, the species is documented throughout the Manchuria area of China. Finally, its range reaches as far as China North-Central, demonstrating its adaptability to diverse environments.
