Chinese lobelia, scientifically known as Lobelia chinensis, is a versatile medicinal herb native to East Asia. Renowned in traditional practices, it is primarily utilized for its respiratory benefits and anti-inflammatory properties. This flowering plant offers a unique blend of therapeutic potential, making it a significant subject in ethnobotanical studies.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide distribution across several key regions in East Asia. Within China, it can be found throughout the South-Central and Southeast territories. The morphology of Lobelia chinensis is characterized by a growth form classified as a non-woody herb and specifically a forb. The physiology of Lobelia chinensis is characterized by its reliance on the C3 photosynthetic pathway, a mechanism where carbon dioxide is fixed directly into a three-carbon compound during the first stage of the Calvin cycle. This metabolic process involves the enzyme RuBisCO facilitating the carboxylation of ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate, which is typical for most temperate and subtropical herbaceous plants. Because it utilizes this C3 pathway, the plant's physiological efficiency is highly dependent on environmental conditions such as light intensity, temperature, and water availability, as it is susceptible to photorespiration when stomata close under moisture stress.
