Common St. John's wort

Hypericum perforatum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (2497 studies) · ★★★★★

Common St. John's wort, scientifically known as Hypericum perforatum, is a perennial flowering plant renowned for its medicinal properties. Primarily utilized in herbal medicine to alleviate mild-to-moderate depression, it contains bioactive compounds like hypericin. This versatile herb is widely studied for its therapeutic potential in supporting mental well-being.

This plant, known scientifically as Hypericum perforatum, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Malpighiales and the family Hypericaceae. Finally, its taxonomic classification is completed by its placement within the genus Hypericum.

Distribution

This plant, known as Hypericum perforatum, is widely distributed across various regions of Northern Europe. It can be found growing naturally throughout the landscape of Denmark. The species also thrives in the northern territories of Finland and Norway. Additionally, its presence is well-documented across the islands of Great Britain and Ireland. Together, these locations represent a significant portion of its northern geographical range.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as St. John's Wort, is a perennial herb characterized by its diverse and pharmacologically active components. The aerial parts of the specimen, which include the stems and foliage, are frequently harvested for medicinal preparation. The bright yellow flowers are perhaps the most iconic feature, containing high concentrations of hypericin, while the dark, translucent-dotted leaves also contribute significantly to the plant's chemical profile. Although much of the therapeutic focus remains on the upper growth, the roots also contain bioactive compounds that support the plant's overall medicinal potency.
