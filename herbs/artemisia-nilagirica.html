Nilgri wormwood, scientifically known as Artemisia nilagirica, is an aromatic perennial herb endemic to the Western Ghats of India. Renowned for its distinctive fragrance and potent bioactive compounds, this medicinal plant is traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat various ailments, offering significant potential for modern pharmacological research and development.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning across parts of East Asia and the Indian subcontinent. In China, its distribution is notably found within the South-Central regions as well as the high-altitude areas of Tibet. Moving toward the Indian subcontinent, the species can be located in the state of Assam. Furthermore, it is widely recorded throughout the country of India. Finally, its habitat extends to the specific ecological zones of the West Himalaya.

Plant Parts

This plant, Artemisia nilagirica, is highly valued for its various therapeutic components derived primarily from its vegetative structure. The most significant part used in medicinal applications is the leaf, which contains a high concentration of bioactive compounds. From the leaves, researchers and practitioners derive potent herbal extracts that are utilized in traditional and modern medicine to treat various ailments. Furthermore, the plant is a rich source of essential oil, which is extracted through distillation processes to capture its aromatic and pharmacological properties, making the entire chemical profile of the foliage essential for its medicinal efficacy.
