Cinchona Bark, scientifically known as Cinchona, is a legendary medicinal plant native to the tropical Andes. Renowned for containing quinine, it has historically served as a vital treatment for malaria. This potent botanical resource remains a cornerstone in pharmacology, bridging ancient traditional healing with modern life-saving antimalarial medicine.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the source of Peruvian bark, is highly valued in pharmacology for its therapeutic properties. The most significant part of the plant used for medicinal purposes is the cortex, which is harvested from the tree. Specifically, the stem bark is collected and processed to extract its most vital component, quinine, an alkaloid used to treat malaria. Therefore, the dried bark remains one of the most historically important botanical resources in medicine.
