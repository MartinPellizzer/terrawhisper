Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning across several distinct tropical regions. In the Western Indian Ocean, it can be found growing within the Seychelles. Its range extends into the Indian Subcontinent, where it is documented in both Assam and Bangladesh. Additionally, the species is present throughout parts of Indo-China. Specifically, it inhabits the landscapes of Cambodia and Laos.

Physiology

The physiology of Leea rubra is characterized by its metabolic processes tailored for tropical and subtropical environments, focusing on efficient nutrient uptake and structural growth. As a woody shrub, it exhibits robust photosynthetic capabilities to support its dense foliage and vibrant inflorescences. Regarding its nutrient acquisition strategies, the plant does not possess the specialized symbiotic relationships required for atmospheric nitrogen conversion; specifically, it is not a nitrogen fixer, as indicated by the absence of nitrogen-fixing nodules or associated rhizobia within its root system. Consequently, its physiological health and growth rates are heavily dependent on the availability of nitrates and ammonium ions within the soil substrate. Its water regulation and transpiration rates are adapted to maintain turgor pressure in humid climates, ensuring stable cellular function and the continuous transport of minerals from the roots to the expanding canopy.
