Reproduction

The reproduction of Cedrela odorata is driven by biotic pollination syndromes, primarily involving insects such as bees, which facilitate the fertilization process. Following successful pollination, the plant develops a woody capsule as its fruit type. These capsules are characterized by a mean length of 6.75 cm (ranging from a minimum of 6.5 cm to a maximum of 7 cm), a mean width of 3.75 cm (ranging from 3.5 cm to 4 cm), and a mean height of 3.75 cm (ranging from 4 cm to a maximum of 4 cm). The fruit is dehiscent, meaning it opens at maturity to release its seeds. The seeds themselves are quite small, with a mean mass of 0.0228425 g, though they can range from a minimum of 0.0096 g to a maximum of 0.036 g. In terms of dimensions, the seeds exhibit a length between 1 mm and 30 mm and a consistent volume of approximately 20 mm³. Dispersal is achieved through an anemochorous syndrome, where the seeds are primarily dispersed by the wind to colonize new areas.
