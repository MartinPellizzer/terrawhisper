Wild custard-apple, scientifically known as Annona senegalensis, is a versatile shrub native to tropical Africa. Highly valued in traditional medicine, its leaves, bark, and fruits possess diverse therapeutic properties. It is commonly used to treat ailments ranging from digestive issues to inflammation, serving as a vital resource for community healthcare.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and order Magnoliales, it is a member of the family Annonaceae. Ultimately, Annona senehalensis is categorized under the genus Annona.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several distinct regions of the African continent. In the Macaronesian region, its presence is specifically noted in Cape Verde. Moving to West Tropical Africa, it can be found growing within the borders of Benin. The species also inhabits the landscapes of Burkina and the Gambia. Finally, its range extends further along the coast to include Ghana.

Plant Parts

This plant, Annona senegalensis, possesses various components that are utilized for their therapeutic properties in traditional medicine. The leaves are frequently employed in decoctions to treat ailments such as fever or inflammation, while the stem bark and various stem barks harvested from the woody structure are often processed to address digestive issues or skin conditions. Furthermore, the seeds are sometimes utilized in specific preparations, and in many medicinal practices, the whole ground plant material is used to create potent remedies that harness the combined synergistic effects of all its botanical elements.
