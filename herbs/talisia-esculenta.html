Talisia esculenta

Talisia esculenta, commonly known as the ramailho or wild custard apple, is a tropical tree prized for its nutritious, fleshy fruits. Beyond its culinary uses, this medicinal plant offers significant therapeutic potential, traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat various ailments and promote overall wellness throughout its native regions.

  • Scientific name: Talisia esculenta
  • Family: Sapindaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Sapindales and the family Sapindaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Talisia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Talisia esculenta, is recognized by various names across different languages and cultures. In Dutch and Portuguese, it is commonly referred to as Pitomba, while in Esperanto, the name is written as Pitombo. Its unique appearance has led to descriptive names in other languages, such as گاوچشم in Persian and 牛睛果 in Chinese.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several distinct regions of the South American continent. In Northern South America, its presence is noted within the borders of Guyana. Moving toward the western part of the continent, it can also be found in Bolivia. Within Brazil, the species occupies a broad range that includes the West-Central region. Furthermore, its geographical reach extends to both the Northeast and Southeast regions of Brazil.
