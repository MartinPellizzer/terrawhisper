Rumex trisetifer

Rumex trisetifer, commonly known as the three-seeded dock, is a distinctive perennial herb within the Polygonaceae family. Characterized by its unique, winged fruits, this plant thrives in diverse habitats. While primarily recognized for its botanical characteristics, it holds significant interest in ethnobotany for its potential medicinal and nutritional properties.

  • Scientific name: Rumex trisetifer
  • Family: Polygonaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Caryophyllales. Finally, it is a member of the family Polygonaceae, specifically situated within the genus Rumex.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Rumex trisetifer, is referred to by several different common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In Chinese, it is widely recognized as 长刺酸模 (zh), 長刺酸模 (zh-tw), or 长刺酸模 (zh-cn), while the broader term 酸模 (zh) is also frequently used to categorize it within the dock family. Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions of East Asia. In China, its presence is documented throughout the South-Central and North-Central territories. It can also be found in the Southeast regions of China as well as on the island of Hainan. Moving beyond the mainland, the species is also distributed within Japan. Together, these locations highlight the plant's significant range across diverse Asian landscapes.
