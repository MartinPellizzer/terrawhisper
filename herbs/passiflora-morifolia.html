Passiflora morifolia

Blue sweet calabash

Passiflora morifolia, commonly known as the forest passionflower, is a captivating climbing vine native to tropical regions. Renowned for its intricate, exotic blooms, this medicinal plant is traditionally valued for its calming properties. It is often utilized in herbal remedies to alleviate anxiety, promote restful sleep, and support relaxation.

  • Scientific name: Passiflora morifolia
  • Family: Passifloraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and order Malpighiales, it is a member of the family Passifloraceae. Specifically, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement within the genus Passiflora.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide and diverse geographical distribution across several continents. In the Macaronesian region, it can be found specifically within the Canary Islands. Its presence extends to South Tropical Africa, where it inhabits areas such as Zimbabwe. In Western Asia, the species is documented in Palestine, while in China, it is located in the South-Central region. Finally, the plant is also distributed throughout the Southwest of Mexico.
