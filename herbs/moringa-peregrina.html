Moringa peregrina

Moringa peregrina, commonly known as the wild moringa, is a rare, slow-growing tree endemic to the Socotra Archipelago. Highly valued for its nutrient-dense seeds and medicinal properties, it serves as a vital resource for local communities, offering traditional remedies for various ailments while representing a unique botanical treasure.

  • Scientific name: Moringa peregrina
  • Family: Moringaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Brassicales and the family Moringaecae. Ultimately, it is identified by the genus Moringa, specifically as the species Moringa peregrina.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution concentrated within the arid regions of the African continent. In Northern Africa, its presence is recorded within the borders of Egypt. Moving toward the Northeast Tropical Africa region, it can be found across several distinct territories. Specifically, its habitat includes the nations of Djibouti, Eritrea, and Ethiopia. Finally, the range of this medicinal species extends further into the Horn of Africa to include Somalia.
