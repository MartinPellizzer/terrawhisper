Vernonia guineensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Vernonia guineensis?

Vernonia guineensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is organized under the order Asterales. It is a member of the family Asteraceae, which places it within the genus Vernonia, and is specifically identified by its species name, guineensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Vernonia guineensis has an erect, perennial herbaceous structure characterized by woody stems, lanceolate leaves with serrated margins, and terminal heads of small, tubular purple or blue florets. The leaves are typically arranged oppositely along the stem and possess a distinctively textured surface. Its flowering heads are composed of numerous individual disc florets that emerge from a common involucral base. The plant often develops a branched architecture as it matures, increasing its surface area for floral display. Fine pubescence may be present on the stems and leaf undersides depending on the specific environmental conditions.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Vernonia guieneensis provides medicinal value through its leaves, roots, and stem bark. The leaves are frequently decocted to treat ailments such as malaria and various digestive disorders. Extracts from the roots are often employed to manage fever and inflammatory conditions. Additionally, the stem bark contains bioactive compounds used in traditional remedies for skin infections and stomach pains. These different components allow for a diverse range of therapeutic applications in herbal medicine.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", preparations of Vernonia guineeensis Benth. are used in folk medicine in Cameroon to treat ailments including prostate cancer and malaria, and it is used as an anthelmintic, adaptogen and antidote. An anti-prostate cancer sugar ester was previously isolated from the root extract of the plant. Acetone extracts of the leaves were tested against 10 cancer cell lines, demonstrating in vitro activity ranging from IC50 4-26μg/mL. Two sesquiterpene lactones, Vernopicrin (1) and Vernomelitensin (2), were isolated from the leaf extract and demonstrated activity with IC50 values ranging from 0.35-2.04μM (P<0.05) and 0.13-1.5μM (P<0.05) respectively.

Further study of these molecules helps clarify how the plant interacts with various biological systems.

According to a study published by "Phytochemistry", two bitter stigmastane derivatives, vernoguinosterol (1) and vernoguinoside (2), have been isolated from the stem bark of Vernonia guineensis. These specific medicinal compounds, vernoguinosterol (1) and vernoguinoside (2), are identified as trypanocidal stigmastane derivatives. The structures of these compounds were elucidated using spectroscopic methods. Furthermore, these new compounds exhibit trypanocidal activity.
