Pellitory-of-the-wall, scientifically known as Parietaria judaica, is a perennial climbing herb widely recognized in traditional medicine. Often found growing on stone walls, this plant is valued for its diverse therapeutic properties, including anti-inflammatory, diuretic, and antimicrobial effects, making it a significant subject in ethnobotanical studies globally.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Parietarica judaica is characterized by a sexual process involving bisexual flowers arranged in a raceme inflorescence. The flowering period typically commences in May and extends through October. The plant relies on abiotic pollination, specifically utilizing wind as its primary pollination syndrome. Sexual reproduction is the sole method of propagation, as asexual reproduction is absent, and self-fertilization is also noted to be absent. Following successful pollination, the plant produces small, black, indehiscent fruits classified as achenes, which measure between 0.1 cm and 0.12 cm in length. These fruits are highly prolific, containing a high seed count ranging from 101 to 1000 seeds per fruit. The seeds themselves are minute, with a consistent length of 1.25 mm and a mean mass of 0.000226 g (ranging from a minimum of 0.00019 g to a maximum of 0.00036 g). Dispersal of the seeds is facilitated through an unspecialized syndrome, though it also exhibits anthropochorous characteristics, indicating human-mediated movement.
