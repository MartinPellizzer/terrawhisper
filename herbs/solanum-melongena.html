Solanum melongena

Eggplant

Solanum melongena, commonly known as eggplant or aubergine, is a versatile perennial plant belonging to the Solanaceae family. Beyond its culinary importance, it holds significant medicinal value in various traditional practices. Rich in bioactive compounds, it is frequently utilized for its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and potential anti-diabetic properties.

  • Scientific name: Solanum melongena
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Solanales and the family Solanaceae. Finally, its taxonomic placement concludes within the genus Solanum.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Solanum melongena, is recognized by several different common names depending on the region and language. In English, it is referred to as an eggplant in Canada (en-ca), whereas in Great Britain (en-gb), it is called an aubergine. This same term, aubergine, is also used in French (fr), Norwegian (nb), and German (de), where it is written as Aubergine.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning multiple continents and climate zones. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found growing within the DR Congo. Its presence extends across the Western Indian Ocean, where it inhabits the islands of Mauritius, Madagascar, and Réunion. Additionally, the species reaches into Central Asia, specifically located in Kazakhstan. This wide distribution highlights the adaptability of the species across various environments.
