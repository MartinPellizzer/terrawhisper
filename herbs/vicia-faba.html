Broad Bean

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1378 studies) · ★★★★★

Broad Bean, scientifically known as Vicia faba, is a versatile legume valued for both nutritional and medicinal properties. Rich in proteins, vitamins, and minerals, it serves as a potent dietary supplement. Traditionally, it has been utilized to support digestive health and provide essential nutrients to aid bodily recovery.

Physiology

The physiology of Vicia faba is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, a common metabolic strategy for temperate legumes that facilitates carbon fixation under standard environmental conditions. As a nitrogen fixer, the plant possesses the specialized ability to convert atmospheric nitrogen into a usable form, enhancing its nutrient uptake efficiency. Its leaf morphology is defined by an elliptic form, with leaf lengths ranging from a minimum of 2 cm to a maximum of 9.5 cm, and leaf widths spanning from 1 cm to 3.5 cm, resulting in a mean leaf size of approximately 92.22 cm². The plant exhibits succulence and maintains a mean Specific Leaf Area (SLA) of 255.85 cm²/g, reflecting its leaf density and resource allocation strategies. Furthermore, Vicia faba is a non-carnivorous species, relying entirely on its photosynthetic and nitrogen-fixing capabilities rather than the capture of prey for nutritional sustenance.

Reproduction

Reproduction in Vicia faba is primarily a sexual process, as asexual reproduction is absent. The flowering period typically begins in May and concludes in July, featuring distinctive violet-colored flowers. The plant relies on a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically utilizing insects with a strong preference for bees to facilitate pollen transfer, though self-fertilization is also present. Following successful pollination, the plant develops dry fruit in the form of a capsule. These capsules vary in size, with a length ranging from a minimum of 8 cm to a maximum of 20 cm, and a width between 1 cm and 2 cm. The resulting seeds are relatively substantial, characterized by a mean seed length of 25 mm and a mean volume of 690 mm³ (with both minimum and maximum values recorded at 690 mm³). The seed mass is variable, with a mean mass of approximately 0.9016 g, ranging from a minimum of 0.3935 g to a maximum of 1.3405 g. Dispersal of the seeds is categorized as anthropochorous, indicating that human activity plays a role in their distribution.
