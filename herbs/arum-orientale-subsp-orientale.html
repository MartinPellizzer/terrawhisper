Arum orientale subsp. orientale, is a distinctive perennial herb native to the Mediterranean region. Characterized by its striking spadix and spathe, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical interest. While containing calcium oxalate crystals requiring careful handling, it has been historically utilized in traditional medicine for various therapeutic applications.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Following its placement in the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Alismatales, it is a member of the family Araceae. Finally, it is identified under the genus Arum as the subspecies orientale.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical range that spans across parts of Middle and Southeastern Europe. In Middle Europe, its presence is documented within the territories of Austria, Hungary, and the Czechia-Slovakia region. Moving toward the Balkan Peninsula, its distribution extends into Southeastern Europe. Specifically, it can be found growing in the natural habitats of Albania and Bulgaria. Consequently, this subspecies maintains a localized but significant footprint across these diverse European landscapes.
