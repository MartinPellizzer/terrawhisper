Viburnum fordiae

What's the taxonomical classification of Viburnum fordiae?

Viburnum fordiae belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Dipsacales. The plant is further categorized into the family Viburnaceae, which contains the genus Viburnum, and is specifically identified by the species name foridae.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Viburnum fordiae has an upright, multi-stemmed growth habit characterized by opposite, ovate leaves with serrated margins and a smooth, somewhat leathery texture. The foliage typically transitions from a deep green in summer to vibrant shades of red or burgundy during the autumn months. Small, white, five-petaled flowers bloom in flat-topped cymes during the late spring or early summer. These blossoms eventually give way to small, fleshy drupes that change from green to red and finally to a dark blue or black color. The bark on older stems develops a textured, grayish appearance that adds visual interest to the shrub's structure.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant utilizes its small, red fruit medicinally to treat various ailments. These berries are often harvested when they reach peak ripeness to ensure maximum potency. People traditionally use extracts from the fruit to soothe digestive issues and reduce inflammation. Some practitioners also value the fruit for its potential to support immune health through its nutrient density. This specific application highlights the importance of the fruit within traditional herbal practices.

According to a study published by "Journal of agricultural and food chemistry", the fruits of Viburnum fordiae Hance contain nine new neolignan glycosides (1-9), two new neolignnans (10, 11), and seven known analogues (12-18). These secondary metabolites were evaluated for α-glucosidase inhibitory, ABTS•+ and DPPH• scavenging, and anti-inflammatory activities. Out of 14 metabolites found to inhibit NO production in LPS-stimulated RAW264.7 macrophage cells, compounds 2, 4, 6, 10, 11, 14, 17, and 18 were potent with IC50 values of 10.88-41.10 μM. These findings suggest that the fruits possessing these neolignan compounds may serve as a medicinal resource to prevent and treat type 2 diabetes (T2D).

What are the pharmacological activities of Viburnum fordiae?

This plant has potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and enzyme inhibitory properties, specifically demonstrating ABTS•+ scavenging, DPPH• scavenging, anti-inflammatory, inhibiting NO production, and α-glucosidase inhibitory activities. Its ability to scavenge free radicals through ABTS•+ and DPPH• mechanisms makes it a significant source of natural antioxidants. Furthermore, the plant exerts anti-inflammatory effects by effectively inhibiting NO production in cellular models. It also shows promise in metabolic health by acting as an α-glucosidase inhibitor to help manage glucose levels. These diverse pharmacological activities highlight its potential for various therapeutic applications.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause skin irritation, redness, or allergic contact dermatitis in individuals sensitive to its sap or foliage. Some people may experience swelling or discomfort if they touch the branches or leaves during pruning. There is limited clinical documentation regarding internal toxicity, but caution is advised to avoid accidental consumption. Always wash your hands thoroughly after handling the shrub to prevent any lingering irritation.
