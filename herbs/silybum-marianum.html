The reproduction of Silybum marianum is primarily sexual, characterized by bisexual flowers that facilitate efficient propagation. The flowering period typically begins in July and concludes in August, featuring red-colored flowers that vary in length from 2 cm to 3.5 cm. The plant utilizes a biotic pollination syndrome, relying specifically on insects to facilitate the transfer of pollen. Once fertilized, the plant produces indehiscent fruits classified as achenes. These fruits generally measure between 0.6 cm and 0.7 cm in length, with a width ranging from 0.3 cm to 0.35 cm. Each fruit is prolific, containing between 101 and 1000 seeds. The individual seeds possess a mean mass of approximately 0.021785909 g, with values ranging from a minimum of 0.019 g to a maximum of 0.02573 g. While self-fertilization is present, the plant does not exhibit asexual reproduction. Following maturity, the seeds are distributed via an anemochorous dispersal syndrome, utilizing wind to spread the next generation.
