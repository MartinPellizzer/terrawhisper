Eleutherococcus

Eleutherococcus, commonly known as Siberian ginseng, is a powerful adaptogen renowned for its ability to bolster the immune system and combat fatigue. Unlike true ginseng, it helps the body resist physical and emotional stress, enhancing mental clarity and energy levels while supporting overall metabolic balance and long-term vitality.

  • Scientific name: Eleutherococcus
  • Family: Araliaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further organized into the order Apiales and the family Araliaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Eleutherococcus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution primarily focused within the regions of the Russian Far East. It can be found growing across the Amur territory and throughout the Khabarovsk region. Furthermore, its presence extends to the coastal areas of Primorye and the island of Sakhalin. Beyond Russia, the species also inhabits parts of China, specifically within the South-Central region. Collectively, these locations define the natural habitat where this medicinal species thrives.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Siberian ginseng, derives its primary medicinal value from its root. The root is the most significant part used in herbal medicine, as it contains the active compounds responsible for its adaptogenic properties. Typically harvested after several years of growth, the root is often dried and processed into powders, tinctures, or extracts to help the body resist stress and improve physical endurance.
