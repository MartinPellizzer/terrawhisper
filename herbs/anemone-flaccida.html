Anemone flaccida

What's the taxonomical classification of Anemone flaccida?

Anemone flacciida belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Ranunculales. This plant is a representative of the family Ranunculaceae, specifically situated within the genus Anemone, and is identified by its unique species designation, flacciida.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Anemone flaccida has a perennial herbaceous habit characterized by a creeping rhizome and basal leaves that are deeply divided into narrow, linear segments. The foliage is typically bright green and grows in a rosette pattern near the ground. Its flowering stems are slender and upright, bearing solitary, drooping flowers that transition from a pale yellow to a more intense golden hue as they mature. Each blossom features several delicate petals and a prominent central cluster of stamens. These morphological traits allow the plant to thrive in the shaded, moist environments of forest floors.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, Anemona flaccida, is utilized in Traditional Chinese Medicine as a key herbal ingredient known as Qing Feng Teng to treat conditions such as numbness, joint pain, and convulsions. Practitioners historically prescribe the dried rhizomes to clear wind and alleviate pain caused by internal or external obstructions. It has been traditionally applied to address symptoms related to neurological issues and musculoskeletal discomfort. The plant's historical use is often integrated into complex formulas designed to improve blood circulation and calm the liver. These long-standing practices rely on the specific chemical properties of the plant to manage various inflammatory and nervous system disorders.

Part B, Resources", Anemone flaccida has long-term been used in Chinese traditional medicine for its anticancer, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and immune regulation properties. The complete chloroplast genome of this species was sequenced and has a size of 157,614 bp in length. This genome consists of a large single copy of 79,055 bp, a small single copy of 16,191 bp, and a pair of inverted repeat regions measuring 31,184 bp. There are 138 annotated genes in total, which include 90 protein-coding genes, 40 tRNA genes, and 8 rRNA genes. The GC content of the genome is 37.74%.

According to a study published by "Frontiers in plant science", the rhizome of Anemone flaccida has long been used to treat arthritis as a tradition in China. The plant contains a rich source of triterpenoid saponins. Research using Illumina HiSeq 2000 sequencing and iTRAQ technique obtained 46,962 high-quality unigenes with an average sequence length of 1,310 bp and 1,473 unique proteins from Anemone flaccida. Among the transcripts, 36,617 or 77.97% showed significant similarity to known proteins with an E-value < 1e (-5). Additionally, 14,004 isoforms/unigenes were upregulated and 1,4090 were down-regulated in the rhizomes compared to the leaves. The study identified 126 putative cytochrome P450 and 32 putative UDP glycosyltransferases as candidates for modifying these saponins.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains oleanolic acid, β-amyrin, and various triterpenoid saponins including oleanane-type triterpenoid saponins and pentacyclic triterpenoid saponins. These specific medicinal compounds are primarily classified as triterpenoids which are known for their diverse biological activities. The presence of oleanolic acid and β-amyrin provides the plant with significant pharmacological potential. Furthermore, the complex structure of the pentacyclic triterpenoid saponins contributes to the overall chemical profile of Anemona flaccida. These diverse chemical constituents work together to define the therapeutic properties of the species.

According to a study published by "Biochemical and biophysical research communications", the rhizome of the perennial herb Anemone flaccida contains pharmacologically active oleanane-type triterpenoid saponins. These medicinal components include oleanolic acid, which is a natural triterpenoid with diverse biological activities. Researchers identified 4 possible cytochrome P450 genes belonging to the CYP716A subfamily from the transcriptome of Anemone flaccida. Within this group, the specific gene CYP716A254 encodes a cytochrome P450 monooxygenase that catalyzes the conversion of β-amyrin into oleanolic acid. This process involves the oxidation of β-amyrin at the C-18 position to achieve oleanolic acid production.
