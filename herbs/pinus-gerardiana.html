Pinus gerardiana

Chilgoza pine

Pinus gerardiana, commonly known as the Blue Pine, is a majestic coniferous species native to the high-altitude regions of the Himalayas. Renowned for its nutrient-dense seeds and aromatic resin, this resilient tree holds significant ethnobotanical value, offering medicinal properties and vital ecological benefits to its unique mountain ecosystem.

  • Scientific name: Pinus gerardiana
  • Family: Pinaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Pinidae and the order Pinales, falling into the family Pinaceae. Ultimately, the species is identified by its genus, Pinus.

Common Names

This plant, Pinus gerardiana, is known by various common names depending on the language and region. In Chinese, it is referred to as 西藏白皮松 (zh-Hans), 西藏白皮松 (zh-Hant), and 西藏白皮松 (zh-HK). In Poland, it is called Sosna Gerarda (pl), while in Persian, it is known as کاج چلغوزه (fa).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning several distinct regions across Asia. In Western Asia, its presence is noted within the borders of Afghanistan. The species also extends into the high-altitude regions of China, specifically within Tibet. Moving toward the Indian Subcontinent, it can be found growing in Pakistan. Finally, its range encompasses the mountainous terrain of the West Himalaya.

Plant Parts

This plant, Pinus gerardiana (commonly known as the Chilgoza pine), is highly valued for its reproductive structures, specifically its seeds. The seeds are contained within woody, oval-shaped cones that mature over several seasons. Once the cones open, they release nutrient-dense, edible seeds that are rich in essential fatty acids and proteins, making them a vital medicinal and nutritional resource for local communities.
