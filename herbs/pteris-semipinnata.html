Physiology

The physiology of Pteris semipinnata is characterized by a fundamental reliance on the C3 photosynthetic pathway, a trait common among most pteridophytes. In this metabolic process, the initial fixation of atmospheric carbon dioxide occurs via the enzyme Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase (RuBisCO), which facilitates the incorporation of $\text{CO}_2$ into a three-carbon compound, 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA), within the mesophyll cells. Unlike C4 or CAM plants, Pteris semipinnata does not possess specialized Kranz anatomy or temporal separation mechanisms to concentrate carbon dioxide, meaning it is susceptible to photorespiration when environmental temperatures rise or stomatal conductance decreases. Consequently, its physiological efficiency is highly dependent on maintaining adequate moisture levels to facilitate gas exchange and manage the metabolic costs associated with the oxygenase activity of RuBisCO.
