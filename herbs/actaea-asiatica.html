Actaea asiatica

Actaea asiatica, commonly known as Asian bugbane, is a perennial herb native to East Asia. Renowned in traditional medicine, this botanical species contains various bioactive compounds. It is primarily utilized for its potential pharmacological properties, specifically in treating inflammatory conditions, though careful handling is required due to its toxicity.

  • Scientific name: Actaea asiatica
  • Family: Ranunculaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Actaea asiatica, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Ranunculales. It is further classified into the family Ranunculaceae and the genus Actaea.

Common Names

This plant, Actaea asiatica, is known by various common names depending on the region and language. In Chinese, it is referred to as 类叶升麻 (zh-hans), 類葉升麻 (zh-hant), and 類葉升麻 (zh-hk). In Japanese, the species is called ルイヨウショウマ (ja), while in Korean, it is known as 노루삼 (ko).

Distribution

This plant is geographically distributed across several key regions in East Asia. In the Russian Far East, its presence is noted specifically within the Khabarovsk and Primorye regions. The species also extends into China, where it can be found in the South-Central areas. Furthermore, its range includes the northern territories of Inner Mongolia. Finally, the distribution encompasses the Manchuria region of China.

Plant Parts

This plant, Actaea asiatica, utilizes its underground structures for nutrient storage and propagation, specifically through its specialized rhizomes. The roots/rhizomes of this species contain various bioactive compounds that are often the primary focus of medicinal studies. Because these thickened stems grow horizontally beneath the soil surface, they serve as a vital reservoir for the plant's chemical constituents, making the rhizomes the most significant part used in traditional applications.
