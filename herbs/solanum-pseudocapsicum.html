Jerusalem-cherry

Solanum pseudocapsicum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (13 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Jerusalem-cherry, scientifically known as Solanum pseudocapsicum, is a perennial shrub native to the Americas. Distinguished by its bright orange, berry-like fruits, it holds significant historical importance in traditional medicine. While used to treat various ailments, its alkaloids require careful handling due to potential toxicity if consumed incorrectly.

Ecology

The ecology of Solanum pseudocapsicum is characterized by its remarkable adaptability to diverse environmental conditions and varied topographical settings. This species exhibits a broad elevational distribution, capable of thriving at sea level (0 m AMSL) and extending its range up to altitudes of 2800 m AMSL. Its habitat preferences are highly eclectic, allowing it to colonize a wide spectrum of ecosystems. It is frequently found in anthropogenic areas (Área Antrópica) and open landscapes such as clean fields (Campo Limpo) and the Cerrado (latu sensu) biome. Furthermore, it integrates into complex forest structures, including Deciduous Seasonal Forests (Floresta Estacional Decidual), Semi-deciduous Seasonal Forests (Floresta Estacional Semidecidual), Ombrophilous Forests (Floresta Ombrófila/Pluvial), and Mixed Ombrophilous Forests (Floresta Ombrófila Mista). Additionally, its ecological niche extends to coastal environments such as Restinga, demonstrating a high capacity for survival across both disturbed and natural, high-moisture and seasonally dry habitats.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Solanum pseudocapsicum is characterized by a sexual process involving bisexual flowers that exhibit an orange coloration. The flowering period is extensive and variable, beginning as early as December and concluding in October. These flowers, which range in width from 1 cm to 1.5 cm, rely on biotic pollination, specifically through an insect-driven syndrome. Notably, self-fertilization is absent in this species. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fleshy, indehiscent berries that are orange in color. These fruits vary in size, with a mean length of 1.45 cm (ranging from 1 cm to 1.5 cm) and a mean width of approximately 1.42 cm (ranging from 0.8 cm to 2 cm). Within each fruit, there is a high seed yield, typically containing between 101 and 1000 seeds. The seeds themselves are relatively small, with a mean mass of approximately 0.002989 g (ranging from 0.00236 g to 0.00353 g), a mean length of 2.875 mm (with a consistent range of 2.75 mm), and a mean width of 2 mm. The seed volume remains constant at a mean of 12 mm³. Dispersal is achieved through a zoochorous syndrome, specifically via endozoochory, where seeds are spread through the digestive tracts of animals. Asexual reproduction is absent in this plant.
