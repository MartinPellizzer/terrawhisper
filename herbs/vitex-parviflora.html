Vitex parviflora

Molave

Vitex parviflora, commonly known as Black Chaste Tree, is a versatile medicinal plant native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, it is traditionally utilized to treat inflammatory conditions, skin ailments, and various infections. Modern research continues to explore its significant antioxidant and antimicrobial therapeutic potential.

  • Scientific name: Vitex parviflora
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement in the genus Vitex.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Vitex parviflora, is referred to by various names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In Pam, it is commonly called Bulaun, while in Tagalog, it is identified as both molave and Molawiin. In Chinese, the species is known as 小花牡荊, and in Arabic, it is referred to as نبات فيتكس.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several distinct oceanic and island regions. Within the Malesia botanical zone, it can be found throughout the Lesser Sunda Islands and the Maluku archipelago. Its presence extends further north into the Philippines and across the island of Sulawesi. Additionally, the species reaches into the Northwestern Pacific region. Specifically, it is documented to inhabit the Caroline Islands within this area.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Vitex parviflora, utilizes its foliage as a primary component in traditional medicinal practices. The leaves, appearing both as individual leaf structures and in dense clusters, are the most frequently harvested parts for therapeutic use. These leaves contain various bioactive compounds that are extracted to treat various ailments, making the leaf a vital element in the plant's medicinal profile.
