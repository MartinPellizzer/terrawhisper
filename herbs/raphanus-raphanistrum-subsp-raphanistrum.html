Raphanus raphanistrum subsp. raphanistrum

Nominate wild radish

Raphanus raphanistrum subsp. raphanistrum, commonly known as wild radish, is a resilient herbaceous plant found in various disturbed habitats. Renowned in traditional medicine, it possesses significant therapeutic potential. Rich in bioactive compounds, this subspecies is studied for its anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant properties, offering promising pharmacological applications.

  • Scientific name: Raphanus raphanistrum subsp. raphanistrum
  • Family: Brassicaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Brassicales and the family Brassicaceae, it is classified under the genus Raphanus. Specifically, its taxonomic identity is defined as the subspecies raphanistrum of the species Raphanus raphanistrum.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found across various regions of Northern Europe. Its presence is documented in the coastal and inland areas of Denmark. It also grows naturally within the territories of Finland. Furthermore, the species can be located throughout Great Britain and Ireland. Finally, its distribution extends to the northern landscapes of Norway.
