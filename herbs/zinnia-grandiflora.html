Zinnia grandiflora

Zinnia grandiflora, commonly known as the large-flowered zinnia, is a vibrant annual celebrated for its striking blooms and ornamental beauty. Beyond its aesthetic appeal, this resilient plant holds significant value in traditional herbal practices, valued for its potential medicinal properties and its ability to thrive in diverse environments.

  • Scientific name: Zinnia grandiflora
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales, falling into the family Asteraceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Zinnia.

Common Names

This plant, Zinnia grandiflora, is known by various names across different linguistic traditions. In Navajo, it is referred to as Nííʼiiʼnił ntsxaaígíí, while in Chinese, it is commonly called 大花百日菊 or 加拿大百日草.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse presence across several regions of the United States. In the Northwestern U.S.A., its range includes the state of Colorado. Moving toward the North-Central U.S.A., it can be found growing in both Kansas and Oklahoma. The species also extends into the Southwestern U.S.A., specifically within Arizona. Finally, its distribution reaches the South-Central U.S.A. through the state of New Mexico.

Plant Parts

This plant, Zinnia grandiflora, is primarily valued for its vibrant floral displays, but from a medicinal and botanical perspective, its therapeutic properties are concentrated within its aerial parts. These above-ground structures include the sturdy, hairy stems, the lanceolate leaves, and the colorful composite flower heads. When utilizing the aerial parts for various traditional applications, practitioners focus on the foliage and blossoms, which contain the bioactive compounds necessary for their intended use.
