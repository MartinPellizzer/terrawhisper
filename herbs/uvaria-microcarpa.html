Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution across several regions in Eastern and Southeast Asia. In China, its presence is documented in both Hainan and the Southeast regions of the country. The species can also be found on the island of Taiwan within Eastern Asia. Furthermore, its range extends into the Indo-China peninsula, specifically within Vietnam. Morphology

The morphology of Uvaria microcarpa is characterized by a predominantly woody growth habit, manifesting as a scrambling shrub or a woody climber. The stems are robust and lignified, providing the structural support necessary for its climbing nature. The leaves are typically arranged alternately along the woody branches, displaying an elliptical to oblong shape with entire margins and acute or acuminate apices. The venation is distinct, often featuring prominent secondary veins that extend toward the leaf margins. The reproductive structures consist of relatively small, inconspicuous flowers that emerge from the axils of the leaves or from older woody branches. The fruits are characteristic of the Annonaceae family, appearing as small, fleshy berries or aggregate fruits that develop from the syncarpous ovaries, often clustered together on the woody stems.
