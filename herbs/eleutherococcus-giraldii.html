Eleutherococcus giraldii

Eleutherococcus giraldii, commonly known as Siberian ginseng or Eleutherococcus, is a potent adaptogenic plant native to East Asia. Renowned for its ability to enhance physical endurance and mental focus, it helps the body resist stress. This medicinal herb is widely utilized in traditional medicine to boost immunity and vitality.

  • Scientific name: Eleutherococcus giraldii
  • Family: Araliaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Apiales, specifically being a member of the family Araliaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Eleutherococcus, with its specific species name being giraldii.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Eleutherococcus giraldii, is referred to by several different common names depending on the linguistic context and regional usage. In Chinese, it is commonly called 毛梗红毛五加, 红毛五加, or 纪氏五加. Specifically, in Mainland China, the standard name used is 毛梗红毛五加 (zh-cn), while in Taiwan, it is identified as 毛梗紅毛五加 (zh-tw).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range across several distinct regions of China. Its distribution includes the lush landscapes of China South-Central and the humid areas of China Southeast. Furthermore, it can be found growing in the more arid environments of China North-Central and Inner Mongolia. The species also extends its presence into the high-altitude terrain of Qinghai. Collectively, these locations demonstrate the wide ecological adaptability of Eleutherococcus giraldii.

Plant Parts

This plant, Eleutherococcus giraldii, is utilized in traditional medicine specifically through the collection of its branch bark. The medicinal properties are concentrated within the cortical tissues of the woody stems, which are harvested to extract the active compounds responsible for its adaptogenic effects. When preparing remedies, the bark is stripped from the branches and typically processed into dried preparations or extracts to ensure the potency of its bioactive constituents.
