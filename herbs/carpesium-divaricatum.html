This plant Carpesium divaricatum is utilized medicinally through its aerial parts, roots, shoots, and the whole plant. Medicinal practitioners also rely on the roots to provide concentrated therapeutic properties. Additionally, the shoots are incorporated into various herbal preparations to enhance healing processes. According to a study published by "Molecules (Basel, Switzerland)", Carpesium divaricatum has long been used as traditional medicine. The compositions of essential oils distilled from the roots and shoots of the plant were studied using GC-MS-FID and NMR spectroscopy. Analyses identified 112 compounds localized in the aerial parts and 80 in the roots of plants grown in the open field, totaling 145 compounds. In the shoots, the major volatiles were α-pinene at 40%, nerol at 4%, and neryl-isobutyrate at 3%. The predominant components of the root oil were 10-isobutyryloxy-8,9-epoxythymyl-isobutyrate at 29%, thymyl-isobutyrate at 6%, and 9-isobutyryloxythymyl-isobutyrate at 6%. Seventeen thymol derivatives were identified in the analyzed oils, including 8 compounds specific for the roots.

A new compound from C. divaricatum, known as the sesquiterpene lactone 2beta,5-epoxy-5,10-dihydroxy-6alpha-angeloyloxy-9beta-isobutyloxy-germacran-8alpha,12-olide (C-1), was recently identified. What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds cardvin A, cardvin B, cardvin C, cardvin D, and germacrane-type sesquiterpene lactones. These specific bioactive constituents are primary components identified within the chemical profile of Carpesium divaricatum. The cardvin series of compounds represents a distinct group of molecules isolated from its tissues. Additionally, the presence of germacrane-type sesquiterpene lactones contributes to the plant's complex pharmacological properties. These diverse chemical elements are often studied for their potential therapeutic applications in medicinal research.

According to a study published by "Journal of natural products", four new sesquiterpenes of the germacranolide type were isolated from the aerial parts of Carpesium divaricatum. These four new cytotoxic germacranolides are named cardivin A (1), B (2), C (3), and D (4). The structures of compounds 1, 2, 3, and 4 were elucidated using spectroscopic techniques. These 4 compounds demonstrated cytotoxicity toward 5 different human tumor cells. The specific cell lines affected included A-549 (nonsmall cell lung), SK-OV-3 (ovary), SK-MEL-2 (skin), XF-498 (central nervous system), and HCT-15 (colon).

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is Carpesium divaricatum, which is utilized to create medicinal herbal preparations such as ethanol extract for therapeutic purposes. This specific extract is often produced by soaking the plant material in alcohol to isolate its bioactive compounds. These preparations are frequently studied for their potential pharmacological properties and medicinal efficacy. Researchers examine the concentration of active constituents within the ethanol extract to ensure standardized dosing. Such preparations serve as a foundational component in traditional and experimental herbal medicine.

According to a study published by "Molecules (Basel, Switzerland)", eight new 11-methoxymethylgermacranolides (1-8) were isolated from the ethanol extract of the whole plant of Carpesium divaricatum. Detailed spectroscopic analysis determined the planar structures and relative configurations of these new compounds. The absolute configuration of compound 1 was established through electronic circular dichroism (ECD) spectrum and X-ray crystallographic analysis, while the stereochemistry of compounds 2-8 was determined using similar ECD data. Additionally, the absolute configurations of compounds 5 and 7 were confirmed by using quantum chemical electronic circular dichroism (ECD) calculations. Regarding biological activity, compound 4 exhibited weak cytotoxicity against MCF-7 cells, and compound 8 could potently decrease PGE2 productions in LPS-induced RAW 264.7 cells.
