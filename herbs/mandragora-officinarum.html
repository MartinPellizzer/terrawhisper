Mandragora officinarum

Mandragora officinarum, commonly known as mandrake, is a legendary medicinal plant renowned throughout antiquity. Characterized by its distinctive, human-like root, it has long been utilized for its potent sedative and anesthetic properties. While deeply embedded in folklore and mysticism, its bioactive alkaloids demand careful handling due to their toxicity.

  • Scientific name: Mandragora officinarum
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Mandragora officinarum, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Solanales and the family Solanaceae. Finally, it is classified under the genus Mandragora.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found distributed across several distinct regions of the Mediterranean basin. In Southeastern Europe, its presence is notably documented within the borders of Italy. The species also extends across the Northwestern Balkan Peninsula as part of its European range. Moving further east, its habitat encompasses parts of Western Asia. Specifically, this geographical distribution includes the areas of Lebanon and Syria.
