Punica granatum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1996 studies) · ★★★★★

Pomegranate, scientifically known as Punica granatum, is a versatile medicinal fruit celebrated for its profound health benefits. Rich in potent antioxidants like punicalagins and anthocyanins, it possesses powerful anti-inflammatory, cardioprotective, and antimicrobial properties. This ancient botanical is widely utilized in traditional medicine to support heart health and immunity.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Punica granatum is characterized by a distinct flowering period that typically begins in May and concludes in July. The plant produces vibrant red flowers that vary in size, with lengths ranging from 1.8 cm to 2.4 cm and widths between 3 cm and 4 cm. These flowers utilize a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insect pollinators to facilitate fertilization. Following successful pollination, the plant develops characteristic red fruits that can reach a maximum width of 12 cm, with a minimum width of 5 cm and a mean length of approximately 7 cm. The seeds contained within the fruit are quite small, with a mean mass of 0.06008625 g, ranging from a minimum of 0.01014 g to a maximum of 0.073 g. Dispersal of the seeds occurs through zoochorous methods, where animals play a role in spreading the plant, though it is also noted to be subject to anthropochorous dispersal through human intervention.
