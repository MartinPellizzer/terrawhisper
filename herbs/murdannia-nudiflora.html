Murdannia nudiflora

Murdannia nudiflora, commonly known as the blue dayflower, is a delicate herbaceous plant native to tropical regions. Often found in moist, shady habitats, it features charming small blue blossoms. Beyond its aesthetic appeal, this species holds significant value in traditional medicine for its diverse therapeutic and bioactive properties.

  • Scientific name: Murdannia nudiflora
  • Family: Commelinaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further categorized under the order Commelinares and the family Commelinaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Murdannia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide-ranging geographical distribution across several distinct tropical and subtropical regions. Within the Macaronesian islands, it can be found specifically in the Canary Islands and Madeira. Moving toward the African continent, its presence extends through West Tropical Africa, including Nigeria and Sierra Leone. Furthermore, the species reaches into West-Central Tropical Africa, where it is documented in the Congo. Consequently, its habitat spans from oceanic archipelagos to the diverse landscapes of the African mainland.
