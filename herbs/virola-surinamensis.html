Baboonwood, scientifically known as Virola surinamensis, is a versatile medicinal tree native to tropical rainforests. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, it is traditionally used to treat various ailments, including skin infections and inflammation. Reproduction

The reproduction of Virola surinamensis is characterized by the production of fleshy fruits that facilitate successful propagation. These fruits possess a mean length of 1.88 cm and a mean width of 1.52 cm, serving as the primary vessel for seed protection and dispersal. The plant utilizes a zoochorous dispersal syndrome, meaning its fleshy fruits are consumed and moved by animals, aiding in the distribution of its offspring. Within these fruits, the seeds vary significantly in size and weight; the seed mass ranges from a minimum of 1 g to a maximum of 2.48 g, with an average mass of approximately 1.91902 g. The seeds exhibit considerable morphological diversity, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 15 mm to a maximum of 30 mm (mean length of 17.5 mm), and widths reaching up to 14.2 mm. In terms of vertical dimension, the seed height reaches a maximum of 14.2 mm. The volumetric data for the seeds is consistent, showing a mean, minimum, and maximum seed volume of 2200 mm³, indicating a robust and substantial seed structure essential for the plant's life cycle.
