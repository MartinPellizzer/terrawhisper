Virola surinamensis

Baboonwood

Virola surinamensis, commonly known as the Suriname Virola, is a significant medicinal tree native to the Amazon rainforest. Renowned for its potent resin, it has been traditionally used by indigenous communities for its psychoactive and therapeutic properties, offering potential applications in treating skin ailments and addressing neurological conditions.

  • Scientific name: Virola surinamensis
  • Family: Myristicaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Magnoliales, it is classified under the family Myristicaceae. It is further identified by the genus Virola and the specific species name surinamensis.

Common Names

In Suriname, the local name is Babun-udu (srn), and in Chinese, it is identified as 刺莖肉荳蔻 (zh). Additionally, in Portuguese-speaking regions, it is commonly called Ucúúba (pt).

Distribution

Moving to the mainland, the species is widely distributed across the Guianas, including French Guiana, Guyana, and Suriname. Furthermore, its habitat extends into the northern territories of Venezuela. Collectively, these locations define the primary natural environment where this medicinal species can be found.
