Reproduction

The reproduction of Symphonia globulifera is characterized by the production of fleshy, indehiscent fruits classified as berries. These fruits typically possess an average length of 3 cm and an average width of 2 cm. The seeds contained within are substantial, with an average mass of 3.3285 g (ranging from a minimum of 2.03 g to a maximum of 6.92 g) and a consistent mean seed volume of 9200 mm³. The morphological dimensions of the seeds vary, with a mean length of 19.45 mm (ranging from 15 mm to 30 mm), a maximum width of 16.3 mm, and a maximum height of 14.6 mm. Due to the fleshy nature of the fruit, the plant utilizes a zoochorous dispersal syndrome, relying on animals to facilitate the spread of its seeds.
