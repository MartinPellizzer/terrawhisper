Rosa bracteata

What's the taxonomical classification of Rosa bracteata?

Rosa bracteata belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Its hierarchical progression continues through the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, placing it under the order Rosales. As a member of the family Rosaceae, it is further categorized into the genus Rosa, ultimately reaching its specific designation as the species bracteata.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Rosa bracteata has a deciduous, shrubby growth habit characterized by arching stems that are armed with small, sharp prickles. The leaves are pinnately compound, typically featuring five to seven leaflets with serrated margins and an elliptic to ovate shape. A defining morphological feature is the presence of enlarged, leaf-like bracts that subtend the flowers, often giving the inflorescence a lush appearance. The flowers are usually solitary or in small clusters, displaying pale pink to white petals with numerous stamens. This species often grows in dense thickets, utilizing its woody structure to compete for light in its natural habitat.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude regions of the Himalayas, specifically spanning across parts of India, Nepal, Bhutan, and northern Myanmar. It primarily thrives in temperate montane forests and subalpine shrublands located at elevations between 2,500 and 4,000 meters. Within these mountainous zones, the species favors moist, well-drained soils often found on steep slopes or near mountain streams. Its distribution is strictly limited by these specific climatic requirements and the availability of high-elevation habitats. Consequently, its presence is concentrated in specific ecological niches across the Eastern Himalayan range.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Rosa bracteata is utilized medicinally through the use of its fruits and flower buds. The fleshy fruits, often referred to as rose hips, are harvested for their high concentrations of vitamin C and antioxidants. These components are frequently processed into syrups, teas, or supplements to support immune function. Additionally, the dried buds are sometimes employed in traditional remedies to address various inflammatory conditions. This multifaceted use of the plant's reproductive structures provides significant therapeutic value.

According to a study published by "Zhong yao cai = Zhongyaocai = Journal of Chinese medicinal materials", the fruits of Rosa bracteata Wendl. were studied for their ability to antagonize withdrawal syndrome in morphine-dependent mice. Through repeated silica gel chromatography, 5 compounds were isolated and purified from these fruits. Among these, 4 compounds were determined to be trimethyl citrate, dimethyl 3-carboxyl-3-hydroxypentanediote, 3-hydroxy-3-methoxycarbonyl-pentanediioic acid, and calcium citrate. These specific compounds were found from the Rose genus plant for the 1st time.
