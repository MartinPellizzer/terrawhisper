Tetraclinis articulata

Sandarac gum tree

Tetraclinis articulata, commonly known as the Atlas Cedar, is a majestic coniferous evergreen native to the Mediterranean regions of North Africa. Renowned for its aromatic, resinous wood and striking structural beauty, this resilient species holds significant ecological value and has been utilized traditionally for both medicinal and ornamental purposes.

  • Scientific name: Tetraclinis articulata
  • Family: Cupressaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Following its lineage through the subclass Pinidae and order Pinales, it is a member of the family Cupressaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Tetraclinis.

Plant Parts

This plant, Tetraclinis articulata, possesses several distinct anatomical features that contribute to its biological profile. The leaves are scale-like and arranged in a dense, overlapping fashion along the branches, giving the foliage a characteristic textured appearance. Within these tissues, the plant produces a fragrant essential oil that serves as a key component for various medicinal and aromatic applications. As the plant matures, it develops woody cones that facilitate reproduction, while the structural integrity of the organism is provided by its sturdy woods. The durable quality of this wood has historically made it a valuable resource for both construction and traditional therapeutic uses.
