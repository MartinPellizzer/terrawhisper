Boswellia dalzielii

Boswellia dalzielii, commonly known as the African frankincense tree, is a significant medicinal plant native to West Africa. Renowned for its aromatic resin, it is traditionally utilized in various cultures to treat inflammatory conditions, skin ailments, and respiratory issues, offering potent bioactive compounds with diverse therapeutic potential.

  • Scientific name: Boswellia dalzielii
  • Family: Burseraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Sapindales, it is a member of the family Burseraceae. Finally, it is classified under the genus Boswellia, specifically identified as the species Boswellia dalzielii.

Distribution

This plant is primarily located within the West Tropical Africa region. Specifically, its presence is documented across several distinct nations in this part of the continent. Within this zone, the species can be found growing in Benin and Burkina. It also maintains a distribution throughout Ghana and the Ivory Coast. Finally, its geographical range extends into the country of Mali.

Plant Parts

This plant, Boswellia dalzielii, is valued for several of its components which contribute to its medicinal profile. The most significant part harvested is the oleo-gum resin, which is obtained by making incisions in the stem bark to allow the substance to exude. This exudate is often referred to as the oleoresin, a viscous substance that contains the plant's bioactive compounds. Additionally, the root bark can be utilized for various traditional purposes, while the leaves are also recognized as a functional part of the plant's anatomy.
