What's the taxonomical classification of Elsholtzia splendens?

Elsholtzia splendens belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Its evolutionary lineage continues through the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, it is situated in the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Ultimately, this plant is identified by its specific genus, Elsholtzia, and its unique species designation, splendens.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Elsholtzia splendens has a deciduous habit characterized by a spreading crown, pinnately compound leaves with 7 to 15 leaflets, and small, inconspicuous yellowish-green flowers. The tree typically develops a rugged, fissured bark that matures into a grayish-brown color. Its leaflets are elliptic to oblong in shape and feature finely serrated margins. During the fruiting season, the plant produces small, winged samaras that facilitate wind dispersal. The overall structure of the plant is designed to support a dense canopy that provides significant shade.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Elsholtzia splendens utilizes its aerial parts, including the leaves and bark, for various medicinal purposes. These harvested components are often prepared as decoctions or infusions to treat different ailments. The bark is frequently employed in traditional practices to address inflammatory conditions. Meanwhile, the foliage can be processed to extract bioactive compounds used in herbal remedies. This specific usage of the above-ground structures highlights the plant's importance in ethnobotanical studies.

According to a study published by "Archives of pharmacal research", the aerial part of Elsholtzia splendens is used in North-East Asia as a folk medicine for treating cough, headache, and inflammation. A 75% ethanol extract of this plant part showed a 44.6% inhibition of croton oil-induced ear edema in mice when administered orally at 400 mg/kg. The material also demonstrated significant inhibitory activity against subchronic inflammation induced by multiple treatments of phorbol ester for 3 days. Regarding analgesic activity, the plant provided 50% inhibition of mouse acetic acid-induced writhing at 400 mg/kg, compared to 95% inhibition by indomethacin at 5 mg/kg. Furthermore, Elsholtzia splendens at concentrations of 5-100 microg/mL significantly inhibited PGE2 production by pre-induced cyclooxygenase-2 of lipopolysaccharide-treated RAW 264.7 cells.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains a variety of bioactive medicinal compounds including flavonoids, tannins, and various alkaloids. These flavonoids serve as potent antioxidants that help neutralize free radicals within the body. Additionally, the presence of tannins provides significant astringent and antimicrobial properties to the botanical extracts. Researchers study these specific chemical constituents to understand their potential roles in treating inflammatory conditions. Such chemical complexity makes the plant a subject of interest for pharmacological development.

According to a study published by "Environmental toxicology and pharmacology", Elsholtzia splendens is rich in flavonoids and used as folk medicine to benefit cardiovascular systems. The total flavonoids of Elsholtzia splendens (TFES) at concentrations of 5-200mg/L caused a concentration-dependent vasorelaxation in endothelium-intact rings. The endothelium-dependent vasorelaxation of TFES was significantly blocked by the NOS inhibitor N(ω)-nitro-l-arginine methyl ester at 100μM and the guanylate cyclase inhibitor 1H-[1,2,4]oxadiazolo[4,2-α]quinoxalin-1-one at 30μM. Additionally, the indo-methacin at 10μM did not affect the vasorelaxation induced by TFES. The endothelium-independent vasorelaxation was significantly attenuated by the KATP channel blocker glibenclamide.
