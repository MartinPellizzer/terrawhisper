Ficus insipida

Ficus insipida, commonly known as the weeping fig or various local names, is a versatile medicinal plant valued in traditional healing. Often found in tropical regions, its leaves and bark possess significant bioactive properties, traditionally used to treat inflammatory conditions, digestive issues, and skin ailments through diverse herbal applications.

  • Scientific name: Ficus insipida
  • Family: Moraceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Rosales and the family Moraceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Ficus, specifically as the species Ficus insipida.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ficus insipida, is recognized by various local names across different linguistic traditions. In Nahuatl, it is referred to as Āmacuahuitl, while in Quechua, the name used is Tankama. In Russian, the plant is called Аматe, and in Spanish, it is commonly known as cabeza de piyo. Additionally, in Simplified Chinese, it is identified as 无味榕.

Plant Parts

This plant, Ficus insipida, is characterized by the presence of a milky, white substance known as latex, which flows from the stems and leaves when the plant tissue is damaged. This latex serves as a vital component of the plant's biological makeup and is the primary part utilized in various traditional medicinal applications. In many cultures, this sticky secretion is harvested for its specific therapeutic properties, making the latex a central element in the plant's medicinal profile.
