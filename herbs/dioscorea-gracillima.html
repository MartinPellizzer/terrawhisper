Dioscorea gracillima

What's the taxonomical classification of Dioscorea gracillima?

Dioscorea gracillima belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is situated in the subclass Magnoliidae. The taxonomic hierarchy continues through the order Dioscoreales and the family Dioscoreaceae, eventually leading to its placement in the genus Dioscorea. Ultimately, its specific identity is defined by its species name, gracillima.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Dioscorea gracillima has climbing, tuberous stems that produce heart-shaped leaves with prominent venation and small, greenish-yellow flowers. The plant utilizes slender, twining vines to ascend surrounding vegetation in search of light. Its foliage is typically characterized by a lush, climbing habit that covers significant surface area. Beneath the soil, it develops fleshy tubers that serve as primary storage organs for nutrients. These morphological adaptations allow the species to thrive in tropical forest environments.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Dioscorea gracillima is primarily valued for its medicinal rhizome, which serves as the main source of therapeutic compounds. This underground stem contains concentrated bioactive substances used in various traditional healing practices. Practitioners often process the rhizome to treat inflammatory conditions and other internal ailments. The extraction of these components allows for the creation of specialized herbal remedies. Such applications highlight the significant pharmacological importance of this specific plant part.

According to a study published by "Zhong yao cai = Zhongyaocai = Journal of Chinese medicinal materials", the rhizome of Dioscorea gracillima is identified as the main forgery of Rhizoma Dioscoreae Hypoglaucae. The study reports results concerning the characteristics of its appearance and the microscopical characteristics of its cross section and powder. Additionally, the research utilizes a scanning electron microscope to examine the characteristics of the inner wall of the trachea. These specific results allow the rhizome of Dioscorea gracillima to be distinguished from Rhizoma Dioscoreae Hypoglaucae.
