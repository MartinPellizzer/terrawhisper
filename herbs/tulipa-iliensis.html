Tulipa iliensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Tulipa iliensis?

Tulipa iliensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Moving through its hierarchical lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. It falls under the order Liliales and is a member of the family Liliaceae. Finally, the plant is defined by its genus Tulipa and its specific species designation, iliensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Tulipa iliensis has a bulbous base, erect stems reaching up to 20 centimeters, and lanceolate leaves that are typically green with a slightly glaucous coating. The flowers are characterized by their cup-shaped tepals that range in color from pale yellow to creamy white. Each bloom is supported by a single sturdy stem that emerges directly from the basal foliage. The leaf arrangement is often basal, providing a structural foundation for the flowering stalk. These morphological traits distinguish it within its specific regional habitat.

Its distribution is limited to these particular high-altitude habitats in Central Asia. The species occupies a very narrow ecological niche within this specific geographic range. How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-draining, sandy loam soil and a period of deep winter dormancy to trigger successful spring flowering.

Cultivators should plant the bulbs at a depth of approximately six inches to protect them from temperature fluctuations. It thrives best in full sun exposure where the soil remains moist but never waterlogged. Regular mulching helps maintain consistent moisture levels and regulates the root zone temperature.

Once the foliage yellows in late spring, the leaves should be removed to prevent fungal issues.
