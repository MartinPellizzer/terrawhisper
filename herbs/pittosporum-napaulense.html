Pittosporum napaulense

What's the taxonomical classification of Pittosporum napaulense?

Pittosporum napaulense belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving further down the taxonomic hierarchy, the plant is placed in the order Apiales and belongs to the family Pittosporaceae. Finally, it is identified within the genus Pittosporum, specifically as the species napaulense.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Pittosporum napaulense has elliptical to lanceolate leaves that are leathery in texture, glossy dark green on the upper surface, and often feature a lighter underside. The plant typically develops as a small tree or large shrub with spreading branches and a dense canopy. Its flowers are small, cream-colored, and produced in clusters that emit a distinct fragrance to attract pollinators. After flowering, the plant produces woody capsules that split open to reveal bright orange or red seeds. These seeds are often surrounded by a fleshy aril which serves as a food source for local fauna.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant utilizes its bark as the primary medicinal component for treating various ailments. Indigenous communities traditionally harvest this layer to prepare infusions and decoctions. These preparations are frequently applied to manage skin conditions or to soothe internal discomfort. The chemical compounds found within the bark provide the essential therapeutic properties. Using this specific part of the plant allows for the concentrated extraction of its healing elements.

According to a study published by "Natural product research", Pittosporum napaulense (DC.) Rehder & E. H. Wilson is an important medicinal plant with widespread pharmacological importance. The ethanolic extract of the plant showed high phenolic and flavonoid contents responsible for antioxidant activity with an IC50 of 5.58 µg/ml for DPPH and 5.28 µg/ml for ABTS radical scavenging. The biological activities of the bark of Pittosporum napaulense (DC.) were attributed to phytoconstituent presence of Malabaricone C, Borapetoside B, and Kanzonol R. as shown by UPLC-Q-TOF-MS analysis. These extracts also demonstrated antibacterial and antibiofilm properties against multidrug-resistant bacterial strains. This plant could be explored for developing potential therapeutic drug candidates against severe bacterial diseases.
