Artemisia frigida

Artemisia frigida, commonly known as frigid wormwood, is a resilient perennial herb native to Arctic and subarctic tundra ecosystems. Characterized by its silvery, aromatic foliage, this plant plays a vital role in high-latitude biodiversity, offering potential medicinal properties and ecological significance within harsh, cold-climate environments across the northern hemisphere.

  • Scientific name: Artemisia frigida
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Asterales and is a member of the Asteraceae family. Finally, it is specifically identified by its genus, Artemisia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Artemisia frigida, is referred to by various common names across different languages and cultures. In the Chyaskia language, it is called He'évánó'êstse, while in Navajo, it is known as Tóikałí. Its Chinese name is 冷蒿 (lěng hāo), and in Persian, it is called علف جگن دشتی. Additionally, in Bulgarian, the plant is commonly referred to as студенолюбив пелин.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions of Northern Eurasia. In Eastern Europe, its presence is specifically noted within East European Russia. Moving eastward, it becomes widely distributed throughout various territories in Siberia. Within the Siberian region, it can be found in the Altay and Buryatiya areas. Furthermore, the species is documented in the Chita and Irkutsk regions of Siberia.
