Physiology

The physiology of Aloe nyeriensis is characterized by its specialized Crassulacean Acid Metabolism (CAM), a photosynthetic adaptation that allows the plant to conserve water by fixing carbon dioxide at night and performing the Calvin cycle during the day. This metabolic pathway is essential for survival in arid environments, as it minimizes transpirational water loss through stomatal closure during peak heat. The plant's internal structures are optimized for succulent water storage within large, parenchymatous cells that maintain turgor pressure and facilitate nutrient transport. Regarding its nutrient cycling capabilities, Aloe nyeriensis does not function as a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria, such as Rhizobium, necessary to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms, meaning it must rely on organic matter and soil minerals for its nitrogen requirements.
