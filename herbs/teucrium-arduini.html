The life history of Teucrium arduini is characterized by its classification as a chamaephyte, a growth form where the permanent shoots are located close to the ground, typically staying within a few centimeters of the soil surface. This life strategy allows the plant to withstand environmental stressors by keeping its vital organs protected near the substrate. Within the broader spectrum of botanical life forms, it is specifically identified as a chamaephyte, distinguishing it from phanerophytes, hemicryptophytes, or therophytes. By maintaining a low-growing stature, the plant optimizes its ability to survive in its specific ecological niche, utilizing its proximity to the earth to manage resource acquisition and seasonal changes effectively.
