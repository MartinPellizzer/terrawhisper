Morning-glory, scientifically known as Ipomoea tricolor, is a striking plant renowned for its vibrant, trumpet-shaped blooms. While celebrated in gardens for its aesthetic beauty, it holds significant historical importance in traditional medicine. Its seeds contain potent alkaloids, requiring careful study for their complex pharmacological and psychoactive properties.

The physiology of Ipomoea tricolor is characterized by its utilization of the C3 photosynthetic pathway, a mechanism common to most temperate and tropical herbaceous plants where carbon fixation occurs primarily through the enzyme RuBisCO within the mesophyll cells. This metabolic process involves the direct fixation of atmospheric CO2 into three-carbon organic compounds during the Calvin cycle. In terms of nutrient cycling and symbiotic relationships, the plant is not a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the specialized root nodules required to host nitrogen-fixing bacteria, meaning it relies on the uptake of available nitrates and ammonium from the soil to meet its nitrogenous nutritional requirements.
