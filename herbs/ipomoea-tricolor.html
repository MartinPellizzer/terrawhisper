Ipomoea tricolor

Ipomoea tricolor, commonly known as the tricolor morning glory, is a striking ornamental plant celebrated for its vibrant, multi-colored blooms. Beyond its aesthetic appeal, this species holds significance in traditional herbal practices. Exploring its unique botanical characteristics reveals much about its role in both garden landscapes and ethnobotany.

  • Scientific name: Ipomoea tricolor
  • Family: Convolvulaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Solanales and the family Convolvulaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Ipomoea.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ipomoea tricolor, is recognized by several regional common names across different linguistic contexts. In Simplified Chinese, it is referred to as 三色牵牛, a name that is also consistently used in mainland China (zh-cn), Singapore (zh-sg), and Hong Kong (zh-hk). Additionally, in Traditional Chinese, the plant is documented as 三色牽牛.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution that spans across multiple continents. In Middle Europe, its presence can be noted in Germany. Moving toward Southeastern Europe, the species is found within Romania. Its range extends significantly into West Tropical Africa, specifically in Ivory Coast and Nigeria. Finally, the distribution reaches into West-Central Tropical Africa, where it is located in Burundi.
