Heptapleurum actinophyllum

Umbrella tree

Heptapleurum actinophyllum, commonly known as the "Sea Poison Tree," is a resilient coastal shrub valued in traditional medicine. Renowned for its diverse phytochemical properties, it is utilized to treat various ailments, including skin infections and inflammation. This plant serves as a vital natural resource in tropical maritime ecosystems.

  • Scientific name: Heptapleurum actinophyllum
  • Family: Araliaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Apiales and the family Araliaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Heptapleurum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several distinct oceanic and continental regions. Within the Macaronesia archipelago, it can be found growing in both the Canary Islands and Madeira. Its presence extends into the Western Indian Ocean, specifically inhabiting the Comoros. Furthermore, the species is recorded in the Indian Subcontinent, with documented sightings in Bangladesh. Finally, its range reaches into Indo-China, where it is located on the Nicobar Islands.
