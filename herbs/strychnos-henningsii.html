Strychnos henningsii

Strychnos henningii, commonly known as a medicinal climber, is a significant species within the Loganiaceae family. Indigenous to various African regions, it holds deep cultural importance. Traditionally, various parts of the plant are utilized in herbal remedies to treat diverse ailments, showcasing its profound ethnobotanical value in local healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Strychnos henningsii
  • Family: Loganiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It further falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Gentianaales, specifically within the family Loganiaceae. Finally, it is identified under the genus Strychnos.

Common Names

This plant, Strychnos henningii, is known by various names depending on the region and language. In English, it is referred to as Henning's Strychnine, while in German, it is commonly called Hennings Strychnos. Local linguistic variations also provide unique identifiers, such as the name Mshonzi in isiZulu and the term Sifofane in isiNdebele.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several key regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found within the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Its presence extends into Northeast Tropical Africa, where it inhabits Ethiopia, Somalia, and the areas encompassing Sudan and South Sudan. Additionally, the species is documented in East Tropical Africa, specifically within Kenya. Collectively, these locations highlight its varied ecological range across the tropical belt.
