Canary grass, scientifically known as Phalaris canariensis, is a resilient perennial species native to the Canary Islands. While primarily recognized in botanical studies and agriculture, it holds significant ethnobotanical interest. This plant is valued for its unique chemical properties and potential medicinal applications within traditional holistic healing practices.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Phalaris canariensis is characterized by a sexual process, as asexual reproduction is absent. The plant produces green flowers that are relatively small, ranging in length from 0.4 cm to 0.55 cm, which are arranged in a paniculate inflorescence. This inflorescence is compact, with a length varying between 0.02 m and 0.04 m. The pollination syndrome is classified as abiotic, specifically utilizing wind for the transfer of pollen. Following successful pollination, the plant develops dry fruits containing seeds with highly consistent dimensions; the seeds maintain a mean length of 4 mm, a mean width of 1.4 mm, and a mean volume of 5 mm³. The seed mass is quite light, with a mean of 0.005633 g, though it ranges from a minimum of 0.0021 g to a maximum of 0.00687 g. The flowering period typically begins in June and concludes in September. Finally, the dispersal of these seeds is identified as anthropochorous, meaning they are spread via human activity.
