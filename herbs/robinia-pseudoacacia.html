Reproduction

The reproduction of Robinia pseudoacacia is characterized by a sexual process through bisexual flowers, with asexual reproduction being absent. The flowering period typically begins in May and concludes in June, featuring white flowers arranged in a raceme inflorescence that can reach a maximum length of 0.2 meters. The pollination syndrome is biotic, specifically relying on insects and bees to facilitate the process, while self-fertilization is absent. Following pollination, the plant develops dry, dehiscence-type fruit in the form of a pod. These pods vary in size, with a mean length of 5.8 cm (ranging from 3.5 to 10 cm) and a width ranging from 1 to 2 cm. The fruit is highly prolific, containing more than 1000 seeds per fruit. The individual seeds have a mean mass of approximately 0.01965 g (ranging from 0.01584 to 0.02645 g) and a consistent mean volume of 20 mm³. Dispersal is achieved through an autochorous syndrome, where the seeds are released via the dehiscence of the dry pods.
