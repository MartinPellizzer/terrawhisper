The physiology of Senecio glaucus is characterized by specialized adaptations for water conservation and efficient resource management in arid environments. A defining physiological feature is its high degree of succulence, which allows the plant to store significant quantities of water within its fleshy tissues to mitigate the effects of drought and high evaporation rates. This water-storage capacity is supported by a specialized cellular structure designed to maintain turgor pressure even during extended dry periods. Complementing this moisture retention strategy is the plant's morphological configuration, specifically its linear leaf form, which minimizes the surface-area-to-volume ratio. This reduced surface area is a critical physiological adaptation that limits the area available for transpiration, thereby significantly reducing water loss through the stomata. Together, these traits facilitate a highly efficient metabolic state that enables the plant to thrive in habitats where water availability is sporadic.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Senecio glaucus is characterized by its distinct flowering and fruiting stages, featuring bright yellow flowers that are arranged in a raceme inflorescence. These flowers vary in width, ranging from a minimum of 1.5 cm to a maximum of 3 cm. Following pollination, the plant produces fruit in the form of an achene.
