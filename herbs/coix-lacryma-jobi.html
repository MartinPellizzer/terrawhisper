Coix lacryma-jobi

Job's tears

Coix lacryma-jobi, commonly known as Job's Tears, is a versatile medicinal plant prized in traditional herbalism. Renowned for its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, this grain is used to support kidney health, reduce swelling, and improve skin conditions. It serves as both a nutritional staple and a powerful therapeutic agent.

  • Scientific name: Coix lacryma-jobi
  • Family: Poaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further organized into the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Poales, falling under the family Poaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Coix.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Coix lacryma-jobi, is recognized by various traditional and local names across different regions and languages. In Chinese contexts, including Simplified Chinese (zh-hans), Traditional Chinese (zh-hant), and the Hong Kong dialect (zh-hk), it is commonly referred to as 薏苡. In the Pamati language (pam), it is called Balantakan, while in Javanese (jv), it is known as Jali.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse distribution across several regions of the European continent. In Middle Europe, it can be found growing within the borders of both Austria and Germany. Moving toward the coast, its presence extends into Southwestern Europe, specifically throughout France and Spain. Additionally, the species reaches into Southeastern Europe, where it is documented in Greece. Consequently, its range spans a significant longitudinal stretch from the western Mediterranean to the eastern Balkan Peninsula.
