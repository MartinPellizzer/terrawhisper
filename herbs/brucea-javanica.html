Java brucea

Brucea javanica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (209 studies) · ★★★★★

Java brucea, scientifically known as Brucea javaica, is a medicinal shrub native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, particularly bruceines, it is traditionally utilized in various cultures to treat ailments ranging from intestinal parasites to skin infections, offering significant potential for modern pharmacological research and development.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across several major regions. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found specifically within the DR Congo. Its presence extends into Central Asia, where it is documented in both Tajikistan and Uzbekistan. Within China, the species is located in the South-Central regions as well as on the island of Hainan. These varied locations highlight the broad environmental adaptability of the species.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Brucea javanica is characterized by a specific seasonal and biological cycle involving biotic interactions. The flowering period is concentrated around August, serving as both the start and end of the flowering season. The flowers are relatively minute, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 0.15 cm to a maximum of 0.2 cm. In terms of pollination, the plant utilizes a biotic syndrome, specifically relying on insects for the transfer of pollen. Successful fertilization is dependent on these external agents, as self-fertilization is noted to be absent. Following pollination, the plant develops fruits that vary in size, typically measuring between 0.6 cm and 0.8 cm in length, with an average length of 0.7 cm. The width of these fruits ranges from a minimum of 0.4 cm to a maximum of 0.6 cm, with a mean width of 0.5 cm. Once the fruit is mature, the dispersal syndrome is zoochorous, meaning the seeds are dispersed via animals.
