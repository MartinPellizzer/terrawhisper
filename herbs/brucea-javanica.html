Brucea javanica

Brucea javanica, commonly known as the Brucea tree, is a significant medicinal plant native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, particularly bruceines, it is extensively used in traditional medicine to treat malaria, inflammation, and various skin conditions, offering promising pharmacological potential for modern therapeutic development.

  • Scientific name: Brucea javanica
  • Family: Simaroubaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Further taxonomic descent places it in the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindales. It is a member of the family Simaroubaceae and is specifically identified under the genus Brucea.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Brucea javanica, is referred to by various common names depending on the region and language. In Chinese, it is called 鸦胆子 (zh) or 鴉膽子 (zh-tw), while in Vietnamese, it is known as Xoan rừng (vi). In Thailand, the plant is called ราชดัด (th), and in Japan, it is commonly referred to as アタンシ (ja).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across several major regions. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found specifically within the DR Congo. Its presence extends into Central Asia, where it is documented in both Tajikistan and Uzbekistan. Within China, the species is located in the South-Central regions as well as on the island of Hainan. These varied locations highlight the broad environmental adaptability of the species.
