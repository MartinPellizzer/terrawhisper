Java brucea

Brucea javanica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (209 studies) · ★★★★★

Java brucea, scientifically known as Brucea javaica, is a medicinal shrub native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, particularly bruceines, it is traditionally utilized in various cultures to treat ailments ranging from intestinal parasites to skin infections, offering significant potential for modern pharmacological research and development.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Further taxonomic descent places it in the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindales. It is a member of the family Simaroubaceae and is specifically identified under the genus Brucea.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across several major regions. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found specifically within the DR Congo. Its presence extends into Central Asia, where it is documented in both Tajikistan and Uzbekistan. Within China, the species is located in the South-Central regions as well as on the island of Hainan. These varied locations highlight the broad environmental adaptability of the species.

Plant Parts

This plant, Brucea javaica, is a medicinal shrub where various components are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The leaves are frequently studied for their bioactive compounds, while the root is often processed for traditional medicinal preparations. The reproductive structures are particularly notable, as the fruit develops into a distinct form that eventually houses the seeds. Specifically, the mature fruits are known to contain potent alkaloids, making the entire botanical structure a significant subject in pharmacological research.
