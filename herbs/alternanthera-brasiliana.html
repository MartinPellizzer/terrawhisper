Alternanthera brasiliana

Achyranthes brasiliana

Alternanthera brasiliana, commonly known as Brazilian Joyweed, is a striking ornamental plant valued for its deep burgundy foliage. Beyond its aesthetic appeal, this species holds significant potential in ethnobotanical research. Exploring its phytochemical properties may reveal therapeutic applications, offering valuable insights into its medicinal contributions to traditional wellness practices.

  • Scientific name: Alternanthera brasiliana
  • Family: Amaranthaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Caryophyllales and the family Amaranthaceae. Finally, it is identified by its scientific genus, Alternanthera.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning several distinct regions across Africa and the Indian Ocean. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in the nations of Benin, Gambia, and Guinea. The species also extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically within the borders of Cameroon. Furthermore, its range reaches the Western Indian Ocean, where it can be found in the Comoros. Consequently, these locations highlight the diverse tropical environments where the plant thrives.

Because the medicinal value is concentrated in these visible components, the aerial parts are typically harvested for decoctions, infusions, or topical preparations in traditional herbal practices.
