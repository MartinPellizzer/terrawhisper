Lepidium sativum

Garden cress

Lepidium sativum, commonly known as garden cress, is a nutrient-dense annual herb prized for its peppery flavor and medicinal properties. Rich in vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants, it has been traditionally used to support digestion, hormonal balance, and bone health, making it a powerful functional food in various cultures.

  • Scientific name: Lepidium sativum
  • Family: Brassicaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Lepidium satiivum, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Brassicales and the family Brassicaceae. Its specific taxonomic placement concludes within the genus Lepidium.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Lepidium sativum, is referred to by various names across different languages and cultures. In Chinese, it is called 水芹 (shuǐqín), while in Polish, it is known as pieprzyca siewna. The Quechua language uses the name Willk'uyuyu, and in French, it is identified as Cresson alénois. Additionally, it is sometimes referred to by the Spanish name Lepido.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide distribution across several regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence is documented in both Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward Middle Europe, the species can be found growing in Austria. It also maintains a significant presence within the borders of Belgium. Finally, its geographical range extends through the regions of Czechia and Slovakia.

Plant Parts
