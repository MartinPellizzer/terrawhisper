Chinese parsnip

Saposhnikovia divaricata · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (223 studies) · ★★★★★

Chinese parsnip, scientifically known as Saposhnikovia divaricata, is a perennial herb deeply rooted in Traditional Chinese Medicine. Renowned for its therapeutic properties, it is primarily used to dispel wind and eliminate dampness. This potent botanical is frequently employed to treat ailments like skin itching, joint pain, and seasonal colds.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions of Northern and Central Asia. In the vast territory of Siberia, it can be found specifically within the areas of Buryatiya and Chita. Its range extends further east into the Russian Far East, spanning the Amur and Primorye regions. Additionally, the species is documented in Central Asia, specifically within Kazakhstan. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in varying continental climates.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Saposhnikovia divaricata, is highly valued in traditional medicine primarily for its underground structures. The most significant part utilized for therapeutic purposes is the roots, which are harvested and processed to create the medicinal dried root used in various formulations. While the primary medicinal component is the root, the botanical structure includes complex underground systems such as rhizomes that facilitate growth and nutrient storage. Furthermore, the internal anatomy of the plant's woody components contains the xylem, which plays a crucial role in transporting water and minerals upward to support the plant's vitality.
