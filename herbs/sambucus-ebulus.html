Danewort, scientifically known as Sambucus ebulus, is a deciduous shrub native to temperate regions. Distinguished by its creamy-white flower clusters and dark berries, it belongs to the Adoxaceae family. While often used in traditional herbalism, careful identification is essential due to its potent properties and potential toxicity.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide-ranging distribution across several key regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, it can be found growing throughout Denmark and Sweden. Its presence is also well-documented across the British Isles, specifically within Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward the center of the continent, the species extends into Middle Europe. Notably, this geographical reach includes the mountainous landscapes of Austria.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Sambucus ebulus or the European elderberry, contains various components that have been studied for their biological properties. The leaves and the woody roots are often examined for their chemical constituents, while the rhizome serves as a structural base for the plant's growth. Historically, much of the medicinal interest has centered on the flowers, which are often used in traditional preparations, and the fruit extracts, which are valued for their concentrated bioactive compounds.
