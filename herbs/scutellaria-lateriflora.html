Scutellaria lateriflora

Blue skullcap

Scutellaria lateriflora, commonly known as skullcap, is a perennial herb native to North America. Traditionally valued in herbal medicine, it is primarily utilized for its calming and sedative properties. Often used to alleviate anxiety, insomnia, and tension, this botanical offers a gentle approach to supporting emotional and neurological balance.

  • Scientific name: Scutellaria lateriflora
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Scutellaria.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several provinces in Canada. In Western Canada, its presence is noted in both British Columbia and Manitoba. Moving toward the Atlantic coast, the species is also found within Eastern Canada. Specifically, it inhabits the provinces of New Brunswick and Nova Scotia. Finally, its range extends to the island of Newfoundland.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Scutellaria lateriflora, offers various components that are utilized in medicinal applications. While the roots are an integral part of its biological structure, much of the traditional herbal preparation relies on the aerial parts of the organism. In commercial and pharmaceutical contexts, the herb is often processed into dried mad-dog skullcap to preserve its bioactive compounds. Furthermore, modern biotechnological advancements have enabled the development of hairy root cultures, which allow for the controlled production of specific secondary metabolites within a laboratory setting, often using the plant's leaves as a reference for studying its chemical profile.
