Iris

Irises

Iris, commonly known as the flag flower, is a versatile medicinal plant cherished for centuries. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, various species are utilized to treat skin ailments, respiratory issues, and digestive concerns. Beyond its striking beauty, the iris offers significant pharmacological potential in traditional and modern herbal medicine.

  • Scientific name: Iris
  • Family: Iridaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asparagales and the family Iridaceae, it is classified under the genus Iris.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific presence across various regions within Northern Europe. Its geographical range includes the coastal and inland areas of Denmark. It can also be found growing in the landscapes of Finland. Furthermore, the species is distributed throughout the Faroe Islands, known locally as Føroyar. Finally, its distribution extends to the islands of Great Britain and Ireland.

Plant Parts

This plant is primarily valued for its medicinal properties, which are concentrated within its underground rhizomes. The thick, fleshy rhizome of the Iris serves as the main source for extracting essential oils and active compounds used in traditional remedies. Because these rhizoomes store nutrients and energy for the plant, they contain the highest density of the botanical constituents sought after for therapeutic applications.
