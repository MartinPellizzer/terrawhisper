Schreber's water-shield, scientifically known as Brasenia schreberi, is a distinctive aquatic perennial found in slow-moving freshwater habitats. Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of locations across the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is specifically noted in Nigeria. Moving toward the center of the continent, it can be found throughout West-Central Tropical Africa, including Burundi, Cameroon, and the DR Congo. Additionally, the species extends its reach into Northeast Tropical Africa within Ethiopia. Together, these regions define the primary geographical distribution of Brasenia schreberi.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Bryonia or watershield, is characterized by its unique aquatic morphology, particularly in its specialized leaves. These floating foliage structures are heart-shaped and provide a broad surface area for photosynthesis while submerged in shallow waters. Within the tissues of the plant, a significant amount of mucilage is produced, a thick, gelatinous substance that serves to maintain hydration and may hold various bioactive properties used in traditional medicinal applications.
