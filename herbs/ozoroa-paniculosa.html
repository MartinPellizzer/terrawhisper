Ozoroa paniculosa

Bushveld ozoroa

Ozoroa paniculosa, commonly known as the Ozoroa or false custard apple, is a versatile medicinal plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its bioactive compounds, it is traditionally utilized to treat various ailments, including skin infections and digestive issues, offering significant therapeutic potential in ethnobotanical research and herbal medicine.

  • Scientific name: Ozoroa paniculosa
  • Family: Anacardiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Ozoroa paniculosa, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, falling under the order Sapindales. Finally, it is a member of the family Anacardiaceae and the genus Ozoroa.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ozoroa paniculosa, is referred to by various common names depending on the language. In English, it is called the Bushveld ozoroa, while in Afrikaans, it is known as the Bosveldharpuisboom, and in isiNdebele, it is called Mudumbula.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions of the African continent. Within South Tropical Africa, its presence is specifically noted in Mozambique. Moving into the Southern African region, it can be found throughout Botswana and Namibia. The species also has a significant range within the Cape Provinces of South Africa. Additionally, it is widely distributed within the KwaZulu-Natal province.
