Pyrus boissieriana

Pyrus boissieriana, commonly known as the Boissier's pear, is a rare medicinal plant indigenous to specific highland regions. Valued in traditional ethnobotanical practices, it possesses unique bioactive compounds. Research suggests its potential applications in treating inflammatory conditions, making it a significant subject for pharmacological study and biodiversity conservation.

  • Scientific name: Pyrus boissieriana
  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Rosales and the family Rosaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Pyrus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across several key regions of Western and Central Asia. Its presence can be documented within the Transcaucasus region, spanning the Caucasus mountains. In Western Asia, the species is widely distributed throughout the territories of both Iran and Türkiye. Furthermore, its range extends into Central Asia, specifically within the borders of Turkmenistan. Together, these locations define the natural habitat where Pyrus boissieriana can be found growing.
