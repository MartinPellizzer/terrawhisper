Reproduction

The reproduction of Anchusa azurea is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects for successful fertilization, as self-fertilization is notably absent. The plant produces vibrant blue flowers that typically measure between 0.65 cm and 0.8 cm in length, with a width ranging from 1 cm to 1.6 cm. The flowering season begins in April and extends through August. Following pollination, the plant develops brown, indehiscent fruits categorized as achenes. These fruits typically have a mean length of 0.6 cm (ranging up to a maximum of 0.7 cm) and a maximum width of 0.3 cm. The resulting seeds exhibit a mean mass of approximately 0.0174 g, with individual seed masses varying from a minimum of 0.0022 g to a maximum of 0.02846 g. Dispersal of the seeds is primarily zoochorous, involving animal vectors, with specific evidence of myrmecocory, where ants assist in the dispersal process.
