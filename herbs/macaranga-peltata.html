Macaranga peltata

Macaranga peltata, commonly known as "pau-de-pau" in Brazil, is a versatile medicinal shrub valued in traditional ethnobotany. Rich in bioactive compounds, it is primarily used to treat inflammatory conditions, skin ailments, and digestive issues. Research continues to explore its potential pharmacological properties for developing new therapeutic treatments.

  • Scientific name: Macaranga peltata
  • Family: Euphorbiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Macaranga peltata, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Malpighiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Euphorbiaceae and the genus Macaranga.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad presence across various regions within the Indian Subcontinent. It is widely documented in India and can also be found throughout the East Himalaya range. Furthermore, its distribution extends to the island nation of Sri Lanka and the country of Bangladesh. Beyond the mainland, the species is also located within the Andaman Islands. This diverse range highlights its significant presence throughout the Indo-China and South Asian landscapes.

Plant Parts

This plant, Macaranga peltata, utilizes its large, distinctive leaves as a primary component in various traditional medicinal applications. These leaves are characterized by their peltate structure, where the petiole attaches to the center of the leaf blade rather than the margin, creating a wide, shield-like surface area. In many herbal practices, the leaves are harvested and prepared as poultices or decoctions to treat skin inflammations and other topical ailments, making them the most vital part of the plant for therapeutic use.
