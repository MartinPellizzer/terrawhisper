Lotus

Nelumbo nucifera · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1256 studies) · ★★★★★

Lotus, scientifically known as Nelumbo nucifera, is a revered aquatic perennial celebrated for its profound medicinal and spiritual significance. Rich in bioactive compounds, this sacred plant offers potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective properties. Traditionally used in Ayurvedic medicine, it supports cardiovascular health, skin vitality, and overall holistic well-being.

Ecology

The ecology of Nelumbo nucifera is defined by its preference for stagnant or slow-moving freshwater ecosystems, where it thrives in shallow, nutrient-rich muddy substrates. This aquatic perennial occupies a highly specific niche within its habitat, characterized by a specialized elevational range that remains constant at 1400 m AMSL, representing both its minimum and maximum altitudinal limits. It functions as a foundation species in wetland environments, utilizing large, floating leaves to create shade that influences the light availability and thermal regulation of the water column below. The plant's presence significantly impacts local biodiversity by providing structural complexity for aquatic fauna and stabilizing benthic sediments through its extensive rhizome systems.

Life history

The life history of Nelumbo nucifera is characterized by its status as a perennial species, allowing it to persist through multiple growing seasons via its robust underground rhizomes. As a specialized hydrophyte, it is evolutionarily adapted to aquatic environments, thriving in shallow, still, or slow-moving waters where its roots remain submerged in the substrate while its foliage reaches toward the surface. Its structural classification as a cryptophyte further defines its survival strategy, as the vital regenerative organs are protected beneath the water or soil surface, ensuring the plant can endure seasonal fluctuations and unfavorable environmental conditions.

Physiology

The physiology of Nelumbo nucifera is characterized by a specialized metabolic framework adapted to its aquatic environment. The plant utilizes a C3 photosynthetic pathway, which is the primary mechanism for carbon fixation during the light-independent reactions, allowing it to convert atmospheric carbon dioxide into organic compounds through the Calvin cycle. Furthermore, Nelumbo nucifera does not function as a nitrogen fixer, meaning it lacks the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms and must instead rely on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium from its surrounding aquatic substrate.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Nelumbo nucifera is driven by a biotic pollination syndrome, primarily involving insects such as beetles to facilitate the transfer of pollen. The flowering period for this species is highly variable, with the flowering time spanning across almost every month of the year, including December, January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, and November. The flowers themselves are substantial in size, characterized by a length that ranges from a minimum of 10 cm to a maximum of 20 cm. Following successful pollination, the plant develops distinctive fruits; these fruits typically maintain a mean length of 2.15 cm, with individual dimensions ranging from a minimum of 1.8 cm to a maximum of 2.5 cm.
