Lotus

Nelumbo nucifera · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1256 studies) · ★★★★★

Lotus, scientifically known as Nelumbo nucifera, is a revered aquatic perennial celebrated for its profound medicinal and spiritual significance. Rich in bioactive compounds, this sacred plant offers potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective properties. Traditionally used in Ayurvedic medicine, it supports cardiovascular health, skin vitality, and overall holistic well-being.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Proteales, it is classified under the family Nelumbonaceae. Finally, Nelumbo nucifera is identified by its genus, Nelumbo.

Plant Parts

This plant, Nelumbo nucifera, commonly known as the sacred lotus, is a remarkable medicinal species where almost every component offers therapeutic value. The large, floating leaves are often used in traditional medicine to treat various ailments due to their cooling properties. Beneath the water, the thick, nutrient-dense rhizomes serve as a vital source of nutrition and are frequently utilized to treat digestive issues and inflammation. While the rhizome is a primary medicinal focus, the entire aquatic structure supports a beautiful flowering process involving delicate petals that protect the reproductive center. Within the heart of the blossom, the stamens play a crucial role in the plant's life cycle, and even these fine structures can be harvested for specific herbal preparations.
