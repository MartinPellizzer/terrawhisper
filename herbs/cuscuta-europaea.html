Physiology

The physiology of Cuscuta europaea is defined by its specialized parasitic lifestyle, characterized by a complete lack of functional roots and leaves, which precludes the ability to perform independent photosynthesis. Unlike true carnivorous plants that capture and digest insects to supplement nutrient uptake, this species is strictly non-carnivorous; it instead relies on a sophisticated mechanism of heterotrophic parasitism. The plant functions through the development of specialized organ structures called haustoria, which penetrate the vascular tissues of a host plant. Once established, the haustoria facilitate the physiological hijacking of the host's xylem and phloem, allowing the parasite to absorb water, essential mineral ions, and organic nutrients, such as sucrose, directly from the host's circulatory system. This metabolic dependency means that the physiological processes of Cuscuta europaea are entirely integrated with the nutrient flow of its host, bypassing the need for traditional soil-based absorption or insectivory.
