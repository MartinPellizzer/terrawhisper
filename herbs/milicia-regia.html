Milicia regia

Miliicia regia, commonly known as the African teak, is a majestic deciduous tree native to the African savannahs. Valued for its durable, high-quality timber, it also holds significant ethnobotanical importance. Various parts of the tree are utilized in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to inflammation.

  • Scientific name: Milicia regia
  • Family: Moraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further organized under the order Rosales and the family Moraceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement in the genus Milicia.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed throughout the West Tropical Africa region. Within this broad area, it can be specifically found growing in Gambia. Its presence extends to the coastal and inland areas of Ghana as well. Furthermore, the species is known to inhabit parts of Guinea-Bissau and Guinea. Finally, its geographical range includes the diverse landscapes of the Ivory Coast.
