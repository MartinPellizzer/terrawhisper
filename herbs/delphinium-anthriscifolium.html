Delphinium anthriscifolium

What's the taxonomical classification of Delphinium anthriscifolium?

Delphinium anthriscifolium belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Its taxonomic hierarchy continues through the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, placing it under the order Ranunculales. Within this order, the plant is a member of the family Ranunculaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Delphinium, with the specific designation of the species anthriscifolium.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Delphinium anthriscifolium has deeply divided, ternately compound leaves that resemble those of the angelica species, often displaying a dark green color and a serrated margin. The plant typically reaches heights of up to 1.5 meters and features sturdy, erect stems that support its floral structure. Its inflorescence consists of dense, terminal racemes composed of numerous zygomorphic flowers. These flowers are characterized by a prominent spurred nectar guide and often present in shades of blue, violet, or white. The overall vegetative structure is robust, facilitating its growth in moist, mountainous habitats.

According to a study published by "Journal of Asian natural products research", the whole plant of Delphinium anthriscifolium var. Majus was used to isolate two new norditerpenoid alkaloids with a lycoctoniine skeleton. These specific alkaloids are identified as anthriscifolcones A (1) and B (2). The structural establishment of these compounds involved the use of IR, MS, (1)H NMR, (13)C NMR, and 2D NMR methods. These 2D NMR methods included HSQC, (1)H-(1)H COSY, HMBC, and NOESY experiments.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains a complex mixture of toxic alkaloids, most notably delphinine, methyllycaconitine, and isopelletierine. These nitrogenous compounds act as potent neurotoxins by interfering with nicotinic acetylcholine receptors in the nervous system. In addition to these primary alkaloids, the plant features various diterpenoid structures that contribute to its pharmacological profile. The concentration of these chemical constituents can vary significantly based on the plant's growth stage and environmental conditions. Consequently, these medicinal compounds are studied for their potential physiological effects despite their high level of toxicity.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe poisoning characterized by nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal pain. As the toxins affect the central nervous system, individuals may experience dizziness and violent convulsions. The physiological impact can escalate to dangerous respiratory depression, which hinders normal breathing. Furthermore, ingestion may lead to life-threatening cardiac arrhythmia by disrupting the heart's rhythm. These intense symptoms highlight the extreme toxicity of the species.
