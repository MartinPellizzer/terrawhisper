Alisma

Alisma, commonly known as water plantain, is a versatile perennial herb found in wetland habitats. Valued in traditional medicine, its rhizomes possess diuretic, anti-inflammatory, and detoxifying properties. Used to treat urinary ailments and edema, this aquatic plant remains a significant botanical resource for both herbal remedies and ecological studies.

  • Scientific name: Alisma
  • Family: Alismataceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized into the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Alismatales. Finally, it falls under the family Alismataceae and is identified by the genus Alisma.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Alisma orientale, is valued in traditional medicine for its therapeutic properties derived specifically from its underground structures. The primary medicinal component is the rhizome, which serves as the storage organ for the plant's active compounds. In pharmaceutical applications, these are typically processed into dried rhizomes to ensure stability and potency for decoctions or powders. In formal botanical and medicinal nomenclature, this specific preparation is referred to as Rhizoma alismatis, representing the standardized part used to treat various ailments related to water metabolism.
