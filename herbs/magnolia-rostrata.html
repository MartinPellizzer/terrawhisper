Magnolia rostrata

What's the taxonomical classification of Magnolia rostrata?

Magnolia rostrata belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant follows the evolutionary lineage of the order Magnoliales. It is a distinguished representative of the family Magnoliaceae, falling under the genus Magnolia, and is specifically identified by the species name rostrata.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Magnolia rostrata has large, leathery, elliptic-shaped leaves that measure up to 30 centimeters in length and are characterized by a distinctively drooping or pendulous habit. The foliage is deep green on the upper surface and often features a paler underside, creating a textured appearance in the canopy. Its most striking morphological feature is the long, beak-like style that extends significantly from the center of its creamy white flowers. These blossoms are typically solitary and emerge from terminal buds, hanging downward due to the weight of the elongated reproductive structures. The tree also develops a spreading crown with branches that arch gracefully, contributing to its unique architectural silhouette.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is endemic to the mountains of Veracruz and Puebla in central Mexico. It is primarily found growing in high-altitude cloud forests and temperate montane forests. These specific habitats provide the cool, moist conditions necessary for its survival. Populations are often restricted to narrow elevational bands within these volcanic mountain ranges. Because its range is so limited, it is considered a specialized species with a very specific environmental niche.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-drained, acidic soil and a sheltered location with partial shade to thrive in temperate climates.

You should maintain consistent moisture in the soil while ensuring the roots never sit in standing water.
