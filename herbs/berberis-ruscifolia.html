Berberis ruscifolia

Berberis ruscifolia, commonly known as the Rusciifolia Barberry, is a significant medicinal shrub valued in traditional herbalism. Native to specific mountainous regions, it contains potent alkaloids like berberine. These bioactive compounds offer powerful anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant properties, making it a vital resource for treating various metabolic ailments.

  • Scientific name: Berberis ruscifolia
  • Family: Berberidaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Berberis ruscifolia, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae within the order Ranunculales. Finally, it is a member of the family Berberidaceae and the genus Berberis.

Common Names

This plant, Berberis ruscifolia, is known by various common names depending on the region and language. In English, it is frequently referred to as the ruscus-leaved barberry, while in Spanish, it is commonly called barberry de hoja de rusco or berberis de hoja de rusco. In Portuguese, the plant is known as barberry de folha de rusco, and in French, it is identified as berbéris à feuilles de ruscus.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed throughout the southern regions of South America. Within Argentina, it can be found spanning across the Northeast, South, and Northwest territories. Its presence also extends into the neighboring country of Uruguay. This wide range demonstrates its ability to inhabit diverse environmental conditions across these specific latitudes.
