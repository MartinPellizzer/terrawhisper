Life history

The life history of Marsilea vestita is characterized by a perennial lifecycle, allowing the plant to persist through multiple growing seasons. As a water-fern, its development involves a complex alternation of generations, transitioning between a dominant sporophyte stage and a microscopic gametophyte stage. The plant spreads through the expansion of its creeping rhizomes, which anchor it within aquatic or semi-aquatic substrates. During favorable environmental conditions, the sporophyte produces specialized sporocarps—hard, protective structures that house the spores. These structures are highly resilient, enabling the plant to endure periods of desiccation or unfavorable climates by remaining dormant until moisture levels become optimal for germination. Once triggered by suitable hydration, the spores germinate to produce gametophytes, which eventually facilitate sexual reproduction to sustain the continuous, long-term presence of the species in its habitat.
