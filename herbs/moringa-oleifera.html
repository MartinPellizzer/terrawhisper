Moringa, scientifically known as Moringa oleifera, is a nutrient-dense powerhouse often referred to as the "miracle tree." Native to parts of India, this versatile plant offers exceptional health benefits. Its leaves, seeds, and pods are packed with essential vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants, making it a vital medicinal resource.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Moringa oleifera, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, falling within the order Brassicales. Finally, it is a member of the family Moringaceae and is situated in the genus Moringa.

Distribution

This plant, Moringa oleifera, exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several warm regions. In Northern Africa, it can be found growing in the country of Libya. Its presence extends to the Macaronesian islands, specifically within Cape Verde. In West Tropical Africa, the species is established in Benin and Burkina. Additionally, the plant is widely distributed throughout the Gambia.

Plant Parts

This plant, Moringa oleifera, is highly valued because almost every part of it offers significant health benefits. The nutrient-dense leaves are perhaps the most commonly consumed part, often dried into a powder to boost the dietary intake of vitamins and minerals. Whether used fresh or dried, each individual leaf serves as a potent source of antioxidants and protein. Furthermore, the seeds are utilized for their antimicrobial properties and ability to purify water. Each small seed can also be pressed to produce a high-quality oil used in both culinary and medicinal applications. Ultimately, the abundance of medicinal Leaves and other components makes this species a true superfood.
