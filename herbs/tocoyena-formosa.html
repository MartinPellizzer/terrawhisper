Tocoyena formosa

Tocoyena formosa, commonly known as the "Miracle Fruit" or Synsepalum dulcificum, is a remarkable medicinal plant celebrated for its unique properties. It contains miraculin, a glycoprotein that temporarily transforms sour flavors into intense sweetness, offering transformative potential for managing taste disorders and enhancing dietary enjoyment for many patients.

  • Scientific name: Tocoyena formosa
  • Family: Rubiaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Gentianaales, it is classified under the family Rubiaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic designation is placed within the genus Tocoyena.

Common Names

This plant, Tocoyena formosa, is known by various regional names depending on the linguistic context. In English, it is commonly referred to as the golden berry or golden berry plant, while in Spanish, it is known as uchuva or aguaymanto. In Quechua, the plant is called aguaymanto, reflecting its deep roots in Andean culture.
