Gentiana waltonii

What's the taxonomical classification of Gentiana waltonii?

Gentiana waltonii belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae and falls within the order Gentianales. As a member of the family Gentianaceae, it is placed in the genus Gentiana, with its specific identification being the species waltonii.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Gentiana waltonii has a low-growing perennial habit characterized by small, opposite, lanceolate leaves and solitary, funnel-shaped flowers that typically range from pale blue to deep violet. The foliage is often somewhat fleshy and remains close to the ground in its alpine habitat. Each flower features a distinct corolla with five fused petals forming a tube that flares into lobes. Delicate dark spotting or markings are frequently visible within the throat of the bloom. The plant's overall structure is compact, allowing it to withstand the harsh conditions of its high-altitude environment.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is endemic to the high-altitude alpine regions of the southern Rocky Mountains, specifically located within the mountains of Colorado. It grows primarily in subalpine and alpine tundra environments where rocky, well-drained soils are present. Individual populations are often scattered across specific mountain peaks and ridges rather than forming continuous blankets of growth. The species thrives in these cool, moist habitats that are characteristic of high elevations. Because of this specialized habitat requirement, its range remains highly fragmented and localized.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires a substrate of acidic, well-draining peat and perlite kept consistently moist but never waterlogged.

Cultivation is most successful in cool, shaded environments that mimic its natural alpine or subalpine habitat. Growers should maintain cool root temperatures by using porous containers and avoiding direct afternoon sunlight. Providing high humidity through misting or pebble trays helps prevent leaf desiccation in indoor settings.

Careful monitoring of moisture levels is essential to prevent fungal rot in the delicate root system.
