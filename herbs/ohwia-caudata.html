Ohwia caudata

Ohwia caudata, commonly known as Siberian ginseng, is a powerful perennial herb valued in traditional medicine for its adaptogenic properties. Renowned for enhancing vitality and reducing stress, this plant supports immune function and cognitive health. It serves as a natural tonic, promoting overall longevity and physical resilience through its bioactive compounds.

  • Scientific name: Ohwia caudata
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further organized into the order Fabales and the family Fabaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is defined by the genus Ohwia, specifically the species Ohwia caudata.

Common Names

This plant, Ohwia caudata, is known by several different common names depending on the region and language. In Chinese-speaking areas, it is referred to as 小槐花 (zh-tw), while in mainland China, it is commonly called 草鞋板 (zh-hans and zh-cn), a name also used in other Chinese dialects (zh). In Indonesia, the plant is known as Buah cau (id).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range across several key regions in East Asia. In China, its presence is documented throughout the South-Central and Southeast territories. Additionally, it can be found growing in the high-altitude regions of Tibet. Beyond mainland China, its distribution extends into the neighboring countries of Japan and Korea. Together, these locations define the specific ecological footprint of Ophiopogon japonicus (Ohwia caudata).

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Eleuthero or Siberian Ginseng, is valued in traditional medicine for its diverse therapeutic properties derived from various components. While the whole plants can be studied for their complete biological profile, most medicinal preparations focus on specific structures to maximize efficacy. The roots are perhaps the most significant part used, often harvested and dried to create adaptogenic tonics. Additionally, the stems and leaves are frequently utilized in herbal studies, with the leaf often being processed for its unique chemical constituents.
