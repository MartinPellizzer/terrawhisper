Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range across several key regions in East Asia. In China, its presence is documented throughout the South-Central and Southeast territories. Additionally, it can be found growing in the high-altitude regions of Tibet. Beyond mainland China, its distribution extends into the neighboring countries of Japan and Korea. Together, these locations define the specific ecological footprint of Ophiopogon japonicus (Ohwia caudata).

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Eleuthero or Siberian Ginseng, is valued in traditional medicine for its diverse therapeutic properties derived from various components. While the whole plants can be studied for their complete biological profile, most medicinal preparations focus on specific structures to maximize efficacy. The roots are perhaps the most significant part used, often harvested and dried to create adaptogenic tonics. Additionally, the stems and leaves are frequently utilized in herbal studies, with the leaf often being processed for its unique chemical constituents. Even the leaves themselves are sometimes incorporated into specialized extracts to capture the full spectrum of the plant's healing potential.
