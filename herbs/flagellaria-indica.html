Flagellaria indica

Supplejack

Flagellaria indica, commonly known as the water lily or flagellaria, is a unique aquatic plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its floating leaves and distinct medicinal properties, it is utilized in traditional medicine to treat various ailments, including skin infections and inflammatory conditions, showcasing its significant therapeutic potential.

  • Scientific name: Flagellaria indica
  • Family: Flagellariaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Flagellaria indica, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Poales. Finally, it is classified under the family Flagellariaceae and the genus Flagellaria.

Common Names

In Vietnamese (vi), the plant is known as Mây nước, and in general Chinese (zh), it is commonly identified as 须叶藤.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of locations throughout the tropical regions of the African continent and surrounding waters. In East Tropical Africa, its presence is noted within the borders of Tanzania. Moving toward the south, the species can also be found in Mozambique, which is part of South Tropical Africa. Furthermore, the plant is widely distributed across the Western Indian Ocean. Specifically, its habitat includes the islands of Comoros, Mauritius, and Madagascar.
