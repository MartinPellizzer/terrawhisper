Litsea glutinosa

What's the taxonomical classification of Litsea glutinosa?

Litsea glutinosa belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is further categorized into the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Laurales. This plant is a member of the family Lauraceae, falling under the genus Litsea, and is specifically identified by its species name, glutinosa.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Litsea glutinosa has an evergreen tree habit characterized by spreading crowns, simple alternate leaves with entire margins, and small yellowish-green flowers arranged in axillary panicles. The leaves are typically coriaceous, elliptical to oblong in shape, and possess a distinctively shiny upper surface. Its bark is generally smooth or slightly fissured, providing a sturdy structure for the developing canopy. The plant produces small drupes that transition in color as they mature, serving as the primary reproductive fruit. These morphological features collectively distinguish the species within its specific ecological niche.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Litsea glutinosa provides medicinal benefits through the utilization of its bark and leaves. The bark is frequently processed to create decoctions used for treating various internal ailments and skin conditions. Meanwhile, the leaves are often crushed or brewed to serve as a remedy for respiratory issues and digestive discomfort. These specific components contain bioactive compounds that contribute to the plant's overall therapeutic properties. Integrating both parts into traditional practices allows for a wide range of healing applications.

According to a study published by "Phytomedicine : international journal of phytotherapy and phytopharmacology", the bark of Litsea glutinosa contains an alkaloid-rich extract that demonstrates potent antidiabetic effects. Laurolitsiine is identified as the richest alkaloid found in this bark extract. In experiments using the db/db mouse model, this substance showed hypoglycemic activity and promoted weight loss. The extract works by regulating the hepatic LKB1-AMPK pathway and modulating the gut microbial community. Specifically, it decreases the abundances of Mucispirillum schaedleri and Anaerotruncus_sp_G3_2012 to assist in its antidiabetic effects.

What are the pharmacological activities of Litsea glutinosa?

This plant has demonstrated significant pharmacological activities including analgesic, antibacterial, antimicrobial, antioxidant, and antipyretic properties. Its bioactive compounds function to alleviate pain and reduce fever in biological systems. The plant also exhibits a strong capacity to inhibit the growth of various harmful microorganisms through its antimicrobial and antibacterial mechanisms. Furthermore, its antioxidant capabilities help protect cells from oxidative stress and damage caused by free radicals. These diverse therapeutic effects suggest that Litsea glutinosa holds substantial potential for medicinal applications.

According to a study published by "PloS one", Litsea glutinosa leaves demonstrate significant analgesic, antipyretic, and antibacterial activities. In the hot plate method, a 500 mg/kg dose of the crude extract produced a pain inhibitory activity of 3.37 ± 0.31 sec, which differed significantly (P < 0.01 and P < 0.001) from morphine's 6.47 ± 0.23 sec. The 500 mg/kg dose also achieved a 70% suppression of writhing in mice (p < 0.001) compared to the 77.5% inhibition from morphine. Regarding antipyretic activity, the crude extract at 300 mg/kg-body weight reduced body temperature to 36.17 ± 0.32 °C after 3 h, while the standard at 100 mg/kg-body weight resulted in 36.32 ± 0.67 °C. Furthermore, a 400 μg dose of each extract inhibited bacterial growth within zones of 11 to 22 mm.

According to a study published by "Molecules (Basel, Switzerland)", Litsea glutinosa is considered an evidence-based medicinal plant for the treatment of cancer. The methanolic extract of the plant, known as LGBME, demonstrated antioxidant properties with an IC50 value of 5.51 µg/mL. In cytotoxicity studies, the LD50 of the LGBME was recorded at 24.93 µg/mL. When used in vivo for Ehrlich's ascites carcinoma-induced Swiss albino mice, the LGBME at a dose of 150 mg/kg/bw showed significant cell-growth inhibition. Specifically, the highest cell-growth inhibition observed at the 150 mg/kg/bw dose was 85.76%. This dose also resulted in decreased tumor weight and an increased mean survival rate.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is used to create various medicinal preparations including an alkaloid-rich extract, aqueous extracted samples, a carbon tetrachloride fraction, and cold methanol extracts. Researchers utilize these specific methods to isolate bioactive compounds for pharmacological study. The aqueous extracted samples provide water-soluble components, while cold methanol extracts help capture specific organic constituents. Additionally, the carbon tetrachloride fraction allows for the separation of non-polar substances within the plant material. These diverse preparations collectively help scientists understand the full therapeutic potential of Litsea glutinosa.

According to a study published by "Phytomedicine : international journal of phytotherapy and phytopharmacology", the alkaloid-rich extract from Litsea glutinosa bark (CG) has potent antidiabetic effects. Laurolitsiine (LL) is the richest alkaloid found in the CG extract. In experiments using HL-7702 hepatocytes in vitro, LL stimulated glucose consumption and activated AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) without inducing lactic acid production or cytotoxicity. In vivo studies using db/db mice showed that LL provided hypoglycemic activity, improved insulin resistance, glucose tolerance, and lipid metabolism. Furthermore, LL promoted weight loss and protected liver, renal, and pancreatic functions in the db/db mice model.
