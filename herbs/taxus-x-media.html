Morphology

The morphology of Taxus × media is characterized by a robust, woody growth habit, reflecting its nature as a perennial hybrid. The plant exhibits a sturdy, woody stem structure that provides significant mechanical support for its spreading or upright canopy. Its foliage consists of dense, dark green, linear needles that are arranged spirally around the branches, though they often appear two-ranked due to the flattening of the shoot. The leaves are typically leathery in texture, tapering to a sharp point at the apex. As a woody taxon, the plant develops a complex branching system and a substantial root architecture capable of anchoring its mass in various soil types. The reproductive structures are typically characterized by small, inconspicuous female cones that eventually develop into fleshy, cup-shaped arils surrounding the seeds.
