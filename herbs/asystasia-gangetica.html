Asystasia gangetica

What's the taxonomical classification of Asystasia gangetica?

Asystasia gangetica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant is further categorized under the order Lamiales and is a member of the family Acanthaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Asystasia, and its unique species name, gangetica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Asystasia gangeetica has erect, herbaceous stems that typically reach heights of 30 to 60 centimeters and feature opposite, ovate leaves with entire or slightly wavy margins. The foliage is characterized by a bright green color and a soft, sometimes pubescent texture on the leaf surfaces. Its inflorescence consists of funnel-shaped flowers that are predominantly white or pale lilac with distinct yellowish markings on the throat. These tubular blossoms are borne in axillary clusters and emerge from the leaf axils along the stem. The plant's reproductive structures include a small capsule that contains numerous tiny seeds for dispersal.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the Himalayan mountain range, specifically concentrated in the regions of India, Nepal, and Bhutan. It thrives primarily in high-altitude environments where the terrain is often rocky or mountainous. The species prefers the moist, cool climates characteristic of these specific subtropical and temperate highland zones. In traditional practices, the leaf extracts are also consumed to address respiratory issues like coughs and congestion. Consequently, the leaves serve as a versatile component in herbal remedies for both internal and external use.

According to a study published by "Journal of intercultural ethnopharmacology", the leaf extracts of Asystasia gangetica are used medicinally. The study ascertained the capacities of crude aqueous and ethanolic leaf extracts of Asystasia gangetica to ameliorate hyperglycemia in Type I diabetic rats. Individual hyperglycemic rats received separate doses of 20 mg/kg bw/24 h of the plant for 14 days. Both aqueous extracts and ethanolic extracts were prepared according to standard methods to measure fasting blood glucose concentration. The study showed that ethanolic extracts of the herbal samples were comparatively more potent than the corresponding aqueous extracts as agents of glycemic control and for the management of hyperglycemia.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Asystasia gangetica is used in Cameroonian traditional medicine for the treatment of bone diseases and fracture repair. The study aimed to evaluate the effect of ethanolic extracts of the plant at doses of 250, 500 and 750mg/kg orally. These doses were administered to adult female Sprague-Dawley rats that had a 0.8mm drill hole injury in the femur diaphysis. Following 12 days of treatment, the femur bones were collected for evaluation.

According to a study published by "Data in brief", the methanolic extract of Asystasia gangetica is studied for its inhibition properties towards the proteolytic subunit of the caseinolytic protease system of Plasmodium knowlesi (Pk-ClpP). This system is a target for antimalarial drug development to eradicate P. knowlesi malaria infection in Malaysia and the Southeast Asian region. The extract of Asystasia gangetica contains phenolic and flavonoid compounds in various concentrations. Data from the inhibition assay revealed that Asystasia gangetica demonstrated remarkable inhibition activity with an IC50 value of 39.06 ± 1.98. While it showed activity, this inhibition was significantly lower than the serine protease inhibitor of phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoridenone (PMSF).
