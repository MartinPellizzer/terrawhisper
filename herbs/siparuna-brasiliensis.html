Siparuna brasiliensis

Siparuna brasiliensis, commonly known as "reina clavo," is a versatile medicinal shrub native to South American rainforests. Renowned for its aromatic foliage, it is traditionally used in folk medicine to treat skin infections, respiratory ailments, and inflammation. Its potent bioactive compounds offer significant potential for modern pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Siparuna brasiliensis
  • Family: Siparunaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Laurales, it is classified under the family Siparunaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Siparuna and the specific species Siparuna brasiliensis.
