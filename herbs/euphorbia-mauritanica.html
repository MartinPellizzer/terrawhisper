The physiology of Euphorbia mauritanica is characterized by highly specialized metabolic adaptations designed to optimize water-use efficiency in arid environments. A defining physiological feature of this species is its photosynthetic pathway, which utilizes Crassulacean Acid Metabolism (CAM). Unlike standard C3 or C4 plants, Euphorbia mauritanica employs this CAM pathway to decouple carbon dioxide uptake from light-dependent reactions, allowing the plant to open its stomata primarily at night to minimize transpirational water loss. During the nocturnal phase, atmospheric $CO_2$ is fixed into organic acids, which are then decarboxylated during the day to provide a concentrated stream of carbon for the Calvin cycle while the stomata remain closed. This physiological strategy, combined with its succulent morphology, enables the plant to maintain positive carbon balance and survive prolonged periods of drought and high thermal stress.
