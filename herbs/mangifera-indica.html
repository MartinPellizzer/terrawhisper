Mangifera indica

Mango tree

Mangifera indica, commonly known as the mango, is a tropical evergreen tree celebrated for its nutrient-rich fruit. Beyond its culinary appeal, it holds significant medicinal value in traditional systems like Ayurveda. Its leaves, bark, and seeds contain bioactive compounds offering antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties essential for holistic healing.

  • Scientific name: Mangifera indica
  • Family: Anacardiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Mangifera indica, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Sapindales and the family Anacardiaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Mangifera.

Distribution

This plant, known scientifically as Mangifera indica, exhibits a specific presence across several regions of West Tropical Africa. Within this geographic zone, it can be found growing in the nation of Burkina. Its distribution continues through the coastal and inland areas of Gambia and Guinea-Bissau. Furthermore, the species is documented as being present within the borders of Guinea. Finally, its range extends to include the country of Senegal.

Plant Parts

This plant, Mangifera indica, commonly known as the mango tree, possesses various components that are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The leaves, or more specifically the individual leaf structure, are highly valued in traditional medicine for their antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects. Scientists often study leaf extracts to isolate bioactive compounds that can aid in managing metabolic conditions. Additionally, the bark and the inner stem bark contain tannins and polyphenols, which are frequently employed in various herbal preparations to treat skin ailments and digestive issues.
