mango tree

Mangifera indica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1072 studies) · ★★★★★

Mango tree, scientifically known as Mangifera indica, is a versatile tropical evergreen celebrated for its nutritional and medicinal properties. Beyond its delicious fruit, various parts—including leaves, bark, and seeds—contain bioactive compounds like mangiferin. These elements offer antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial benefits, making it vital in traditional medicine.

Distribution

This plant, known scientifically as Mangifera indica, exhibits a specific presence across several regions of West Tropical Africa. Within this geographic zone, it can be found growing in the nation of Burkina. Its distribution continues through the coastal and inland areas of Gambia and Guinea-Bissau. Furthermore, the species is documented as being present within the borders of Guinea. Finally, its range extends to include the country of Senegal.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Mangifera indica begins with the development of inflorescences that range in length from a minimum of 0.2 m to a maximum of 0.35 m, featuring small green flowers. The pollination process is primarily biotic, specifically involving ants as a key pollination syndrome. Once fertilized, the plant exhibits self-fertilization capabilities. The resulting fruit is a fleshy, indehiscent drupe. These fruits vary in size, with a mean length of 5.875 cm (ranging from 3.5 cm to 15 cm) and a mean width of 3.125 cm (ranging from 2.5 cm to 8 cm). Dispersal of the species follows a zoochorous and anthropochorous syndrome, relying on animals and humans for distribution. Within the fleshy fruit lies the seed, which is characterized by a mean mass of 16.4659 g (ranging from 4.5 g to 25 g) and a seed length between 50 mm and 70 mm. The seed volume is substantial, with a mean of 23,500 mm³, ranging from a minimum of 22,000 mm³ to a maximum of 25,000 mm³.
