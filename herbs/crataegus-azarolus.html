Azarole

Azarole, scientifically known as Crataegus azarolus, is a resilient deciduous shrub native to the Mediterranean region. Renowned for its small, edible fruits and striking blossoms, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. It is traditionally utilized in folk medicine for its potential antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and cardioprotective properties across various cultures.

Plant Parts

This plant, Crataegus azarolus, is a deciduous shrub or small tree characterized by a woody stem that often features thorns. The leaves are typically simple, ovate, and arranged alternately along the branches, providing a lush green canopy during the growing season. During the spring, the plant produces delicate, profuse flowers that are usually white or pale pink, attracting various pollinators. As the season progresses, these blossoms develop into a fleshy, globose fruit, often referred to as a haw, which serves as a significant part of the plant's medicinal and nutritional profile.
