Marsilea mutica

Marsilea mutica, commonly known as the great water fern, is a distinctive aquatic plant characterized by its four-leaflet foliage. Often found in wetlands and slow-moving waters, this resilient species plays a vital role in aquatic ecosystems, providing shelter for fauna and contributing to the natural purification of water bodies.

  • Scientific name: Marsilea mutica
  • Family: Marsileaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Further taxonomic details place it in the subclass Polypodiinae and the order Salviniales, specifically within the family Marsileaceae. It is identified under the genus Marsilea, which encompasses this specific medicinal species.
