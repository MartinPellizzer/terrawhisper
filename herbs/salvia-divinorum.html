This plant, Salvia divinorum, belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Lamiales, falling into the family Lamiaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement within the genus Salvia.

Distribution

This plant, known as Salvia divinoorum, possesses a very specific and limited natural range. Its primary geographical distribution is centered within the nation of Mexico. More specifically, the species is found inhabiting the various landscapes of Southwest Mexico. Because of this localized presence, it is considered an endemic species to that particular region. Consequently, researchers looking for this medicinal plant must focus their efforts on the southwestern territories of the country.

Plant Parts

This plant, Salvia divinorum, is utilized in various medicinal and ritualistic practices through several of its components. The leaves are the most prominent part of the plant, often valued for their aromatic properties. Both fresh leaves and dried leaves are commonly prepared for use, with the latter being more stable for long-term storage and consistent dosing. Ultimately, in many traditional preparations, the whole herb is employed to ensure a complete profile of its bioactive compounds.
