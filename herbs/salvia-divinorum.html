Morphology

The morphology of Salvia divinorum is characterized by a decumbent to ascending growth habit, where the stems are distinctly non-woody, maintaining a herbaceous and succulent texture throughout their development. The plant typically features long, square-shaped stems that are quadrangular in cross-section, a common trait among the Lamiaceae family, and they often exhibit a light green to purplish coloration. The leaves are large, opposite, and ovate to oblong in shape, possessing a rugose or wrinkled surface texture with serrated margins and prominent venation. These leaves are arranged in decussate pairs along the flexible, non-woody stems. The inflorescence is composed of verticillasters, which are whorl-like clusters of flowers situated in the axils of the upper leaves, typically presenting small, tubular, two-lipped flowers that vary in color from white to pale blue or purple. The overall architecture of the plant is lush and spreading, relying on its herbaceous, non-woody structure to expand across its habitat.
