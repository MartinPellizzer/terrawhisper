Chacruna, scientifically known as Psychotria viridis, is a perennial shrub native to the Amazon rainforest. Renowned for its high concentration of N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT), it serves as a vital component in traditional Ayahuasca brews. This sacred plant holds profound spiritual and medicinal significance within indigenous Amazonian shamanic practices.

Physiology

The physiology of Psychotria viridis is characterized by complex metabolic processes centered on the biosynthesis of tryptamine alkaloids, most notably N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT), through the decarboxylation and subsequent N-methylation of L-tryptophan. The plant utilizes specialized enzymatic pathways within its vegetative and leaf tissues to synthesize these secondary metabolites, which serve as chemical defense mechanisms against herbivory. Regarding its nutrient acquisition and metabolic interactions, Psychotria viridis does not function as a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria, such as Rhizobium, required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms. Consequently, its physiological growth and nitrogen assimilation are entirely dependent on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium from the soil through its root system. The plant's photosynthetic efficiency and overall vigor are closely tied to the availability of these soil nutrients and the management of its internal carbon-to-nitrogen ratios to support both primary growth and the energetically costly production of its alkaloid constituents.
