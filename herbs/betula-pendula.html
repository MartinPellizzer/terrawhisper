Betula pendula · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Moderate (105 studies) · ★★★☆☆

Silver birch, scientifically known as Betula pendula, is a versatile medicinal plant valued for its therapeutic properties. Its bark, leaves, and sap contain bioactive compounds like betulin and flavonoids. Traditionally used to support kidney health, reduce inflammation, and promote skin healing, it remains a cornerstone in herbal medicine worldwide.

Distribution

This plant, known as Betula pendula, is widely distributed across several regions in Northern Europe. It can be found growing naturally throughout the landscape of Denmark. Its presence is also well-established within the territories of Finland and Norway. Furthermore, the species is commonly distributed across the islands of Great Britain and Ireland. Together, these locations represent a significant portion of its northern geographical range.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as the silver birch, possesses various components that are utilized for their medicinal properties. The distinctive white bark is perhaps its most recognizable feature, though the stem's inner bark also contains valuable compounds. The foliage consists of numerous small, triangular leaves, where each individual leaf provides a source of tannins and other bioactive substances. Beneath the soil, the system is supported by hairy roots, which contribute to the overall chemical profile of the species.
