Lamium maculatum

Lamium maculatum, commonly known as spotted dead-nettle, is a perennial herb celebrated for its striking silver-patterned foliage. While primarily ornamental, this member of the mint family holds traditional significance in herbalism. It is often studied for its potential calming properties and its ability to support skin health and vitality.

  • Scientific name: Lamium maculatum
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales, it is classified under the family Lamiaceae and is specifically identified by the genus Lamium.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several regions of Europe. In Northern Europe, its presence is well-documented in both Great Britain and Ireland. Moving into Middle Europe, the species can be found growing in the landscapes of Austria. It also inhabits parts of Belgium within this central European territory. Finally, the plant is distributed across the Czechia-Slovakia region, completing its wide range.
