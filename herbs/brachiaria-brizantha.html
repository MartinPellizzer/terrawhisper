What's the taxonomical classification of Brachiaria brizantha?

Brachiaris briantza belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this group, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Its taxonomic progression continues through the order Poales and the family Poaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by its specific genus, Brachiaris, and its species name, briantza.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Brachiaria brizantha has an erect to decumbent growth habit characterized by densely tufted, perennial culms that typically reach heights of 30 to 150 centimeters. The leaves are linear to lanceolate with a prominent midrib and can possess either glabrous or pubescent surfaces depending on the variety. Inflorescences consist of several racemes arranged in a panicle, often appearing digitately or somewhat branched. The spikelets are small and arranged in two rows along the rachis, frequently featuring a spreading or reflexed lemma. This grass species also develops an extensive fibrous root system that aids in soil stabilization.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe photosensitization in livestock by inducing liver damage that prevents the excretion of chlorophyll derivatives. When animals ingest the plant, the resulting toxins accumulate in the bloodstream and react with sunlight to damage skin tissues. This reaction often manifests as painful swelling, redness, or ulcerations on unpigmented areas of the body. In many cases, the physiological stress can lead to significant weight loss or even death if the exposure is prolonged. Managing grazing areas is therefore critical to prevent these debilitating health complications.
