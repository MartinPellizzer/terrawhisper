Plum-yew, scientifically known as Cephalotaxus, is an ancient evergreen conifer prized for its ornamental beauty and medicinal potential. Beyond its lush, needle-like foliage, certain species are significant sources of taxanes, complex alkaloids used in advanced cancer therapies. This versatile plant remains a vital subject in both botany and pharmacology.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Pinidae and the order Pinales, it is further categorized into the family Cephalotaxaceae. Ultimately, the medicinal species is identified by the genus Cephalotaxus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range that spans across multiple significant regions. In Europe, its presence is noted specifically within Romania. The genus shows a widespread distribution throughout China, covering the North-Central areas. Furthermore, it can be found thriving in the Southeast and South-Central portions of the country. Finally, its habitat extends to the tropical island province of Hainan.
