Carex montana

Mountain sedge

Carex montana, commonly known as the mountain sedge, is a resilient perennial herb native to various temperate regions. Characterized by its distinctive tufted growth and slender leaves, this plant thrives in rocky, well-drained soils. While primarily valued for erosion control, its unique ecological role supports diverse local microhabitats.

  • Scientific name: Carex montana
  • Family: Cyperaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known as Carex montana, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Poales and the family Cyperaceae. It is ultimately categorized under the genus Carex.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Carex montana, is referred to by various common names depending on the language and region. In Estonia, it is called mägitarn, while in German-speaking areas, it is known as Berg-Segge. In Czech, the species is identified as ostřice horská, and in Russian, it is called Осока горная. Additionally, in the Chuvash language, it is referred to as Ту хăяхĕ.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide distribution across several key regions in the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence is well-documented in Denmark, Finland, and Sweden. Additionally, the species can be found inhabiting the landscape of Great Britain. Moving toward the center of the continent, its range extends into Middle Europe. Specifically, this medicinal plant is also known to grow within the borders of Austria.
