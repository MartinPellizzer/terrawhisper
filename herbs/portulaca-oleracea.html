Common purslane

Portulaca oleracea · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1001 studies) · ★★★★★

Common purslane, scientifically known as Portulaca oleracea, is a versatile succulent widely recognized for its profound medicinal properties. Rich in omega-3 fatty acids, antioxidants, and essential vitamins, this resilient plant offers significant anti-inflammatory and skin-healing benefits. It serves as both a nutritious edible green and a potent therapeutic remedy.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Caryophyllales, it is classified under the family Portulacaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Portulaca, with the specific species name oleracea.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several parts of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its presence is well-documented in countries such as Denmark and Finland. It can also be found growing throughout the regions of Sweden and Great Britain. Moving toward the center of the continent, the species extends its reach into Middle Europe. Specifically, this medicinal plant has been identified as being native to Austria.

Plant Parts

This plant, Portulaica oleracea, is a succulent herb characterized by its diverse morphological features. The shoot system consists of prostrate, fleshy stems that spread across the ground, providing a base for the various organs. The leaves are thick, cylindrical, and water-retaining, with each individual leaf serving as a vital reservoir for moisture. Below the surface, the root system is typically shallow but effective at anchoring the plant in sandy or disturbed soils. Finally, the plant ensures its survival and dispersal through the production of numerous tiny, dark seeds that can remain viable in the soil for extended periods.
