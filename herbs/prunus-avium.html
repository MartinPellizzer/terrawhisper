Reproduction

The reproduction of Prunus avium is driven by biotic processes, specifically utilizing an insect-based pollination syndrome. The flowering period typically begins in April and concludes in May, during which the plant relies on cross-pollination as self-fertilization is absent and asexual reproduction is also not present. Following successful pollination, the plant develops a fleshy, indehiscent fruit classified as a drupe. These fruits vary in size, with a mean length of 2 cm (ranging from 1.5 to 2.5 cm) and a mean width of approximately 1.57 cm (ranging from 1.5 to 2.5 cm). The dispersal of the plant is zoochorous, meaning it relies on animals for seed distribution. Inside the drupe, the seeds possess a mean length of 10 mm and a mean mass of approximately 0.238 g, with individual seed masses ranging from a minimum of 0.1182 g to a maximum of 0.2475 g. The seed volume is consistently recorded at 180 mm³.
