, scientifically known as Huperzia fargesii, is a perennial clubmoss native to East Asia. Renowned in traditional medicine, it contains huperzine A, a potent acetylcholinesterase inhibitor. This bioactive compound is widely studied for its potential to enhance cognitive function, improve memory, and protect against neurodegenerative diseases like Alzheimer's.

Morphology

The morphology of Huperzia fargesii is characterized by its herbaceous, non-woody growth habit, as it lacks any woody structure throughout its life cycle. The plant typically presents as a perennial lycopod with an erect or ascending stem that emerges from a creeping, branched rhizome. The leaves, or microphylls, are small, lanceolate to linear-lanceolate in shape, and are arranged spirally around the stem, often appearing densely imbricated. The reproductive structures consist of strobili (cones) located at the termini of the branches, which contain the sporangia responsible for spore production. The overall architecture is characterized by a slender, flexible stem and a lush, green appearance typical of forest-dwelling clubmosses.
