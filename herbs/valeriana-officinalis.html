Common valerian, scientifically known as Valeriana officinalis, is a perennial flowering plant renowned for its potent sedative and calming properties. Historically used to treat insomnia and anxiety, its aromatic rhizomes contain compounds that soothe the nervous system, offering a natural herbal remedy for promoting restful sleep and relaxation.

Distribution

This plant, Valeriana officinalis, exhibits a specific presence across several regions in Northern Europe. In the northern reaches of the continent, it can be found growing in the natural landscapes of Finland. The species is also well-distributed throughout the Scandinavian peninsula, including Norway and Sweden. Moving westward, the plant is documented within the borders of Great Britain. Finally, its geographical range extends to the coastal and inland areas of Denmark.

Plant Parts

This plant, Valeriana officinalis, is highly valued in herbal medicine primarily for its underground structures. The medicinal efficacy is concentrated within the roots and the thickened rhizomes, which are often harvested together to produce what is commonly known as valerian root. Within these specialized parts, the plant stores various bioactive compounds, including volatile essential oils, which contribute to its characteristic aroma and therapeutic sedative properties. Whether referred to as the single root or the collective mass of the subterranean system, it is this specific anatomy that serves as the primary source for pharmaceutical applications.
