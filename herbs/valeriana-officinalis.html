Valeriana officinalis

Valeriana officinalis, commonly known as valerian, is a perennial flowering plant prized for its sedative and calming properties. Historically used to treat insomnia, anxiety, and nervous tension, its potent rhizomes contain compounds that interact with the GABA system, offering a natural approach to promoting relaxation and restful sleep.

  • Scientific name: Valeriana officinalis
  • Family: Caprifoliaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Valeriana officinalis, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae within the order Dipsacales. Finally, it falls under the family Caprifoliaceae and the genus Valeriana.

Distribution

This plant, Valeriana officinalis, exhibits a specific presence across several regions in Northern Europe. In the northern reaches of the continent, it can be found growing in the natural landscapes of Finland. The species is also well-distributed throughout the Scandinavian peninsula, including Norway and Sweden. Moving westward, the plant is documented within the borders of Great Britain. Finally, its geographical range extends to the coastal and inland areas of Denmark.
