Salvia fruticosa

Salvia fruticosa, commonly known as Greek sage, is a perennial shrub native to the Mediterranean region. Renowned for its aromatic, silvery-green foliage and blue flowers, this hardy plant is prized in traditional medicine. It possesses antioxidant and antimicrobial properties, frequently used to support digestive health and soothe inflammation.

  • Scientific name: Salvia fruticosa
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales, it is classified under the family Lamiaceae and the genus Salvia.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several distinct regions of the Mediterranean. In Southwestern Europe, its presence is documented in both Portugal and Spain. Moving toward the eastern part of the continent, it can be found throughout Southeastern Europe. Specifically, this species inhabits the landscapes of Albania and Greece. Additionally, its geographical range extends into the Southeastern European territory of Italy.
