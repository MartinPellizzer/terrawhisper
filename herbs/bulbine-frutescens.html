Stalked bulbine, scientifically known as Bulbine frutescens, is a resilient, succulent perennial native to South Africa. Renowned for its fleshy, yellowish-green leaves and delicate white flowers, this hardy plant is widely used in traditional medicine to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and various internal conditions through its bioactive properties.

Distribution

This plant is native to various regions throughout Southern Africa. Its primary distribution can be found across the diverse landscapes of the Cape Provinces. It also grows within the mountainous terrain of Lesotho. Additionally, the species is located in parts of Namibia. Finally, its range extends to the KwaZulu-Natal and Free State provinces.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Bulbine, features a unique morphological structure that contributes to its medicinal properties. The organism consists of a sturdy shoot that emerges from the ground, supporting a fleshy architecture. Beneath the surface, the root system provides stability and nutrient uptake, while the roots themselves are often succulent in nature. Most notably, the leaves are thick and fleshy, housing specialized leaf gels that are frequently harvested for their soothing and healing applications.
