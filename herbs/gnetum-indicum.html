Gnetum indicum

Gnetum indicum, commonly known as Gnetum or Gnetum plant, is a significant medicinal shrub native to tropical regions. Valued in traditional medicine, its leaves and stems possess potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Often used to treat skin ailments and fever, it remains a vital resource in ethnobotanical studies.

  • Scientific name: Gnetum indicum
  • Family: Gnetaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Gnetidae, falling within the order Gnetales. Furthermore, it is a member of the family Gnetaceae and is identified by the genus Gnetum.

Common Names

This plant, Gnetum indicum, is known by a variety of common names depending on the region and language. In English, it is frequently referred to as Malabar nut or simply as Gnetum. In the local languages of South Asia, it is commonly called Kanchana in Kannada and Kanchana in Malayalam, while in Tamil, it is known as Kanchana. Additionally, it is referred to as Gnetum indicum in scientific contexts across various regions.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several key regions in East and Southeast Asia. In China, its presence is noted in the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan areas. Moving into the Indo-China peninsula, the species can also be found within the borders of Laos. Furthermore, its geographical range extends into the territory of Vietnam. Collectively, these locations define the specific ecological footprint of Gnetum indicum.

This plant, Gnetum indicum, is characterized by its unique structural morphology, which allows it to thrive in tropical forest environments. It grows primarily as woody lianas, utilizing its climbing ability to reach sunlight through the forest canopy.
