Gentiana lhassica

What's the taxonomical classification of Gentiana lhassica?

Gentiana lhassica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is further categorized into the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant follows the order Gentianales and is a member of the family Gentianaceae. Its taxonomic hierarchy is completed by its placement in the genus Gentiana, with its specific designation being the species lhassica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Gentiana lhassica has upright, perennial stems that typically reach heights of 10 to 30 centimeters and bear opposite, lanceolate to ovate leaves with entire margins. The foliage is characterized by a bright green color and a smooth texture that lacks significant pubescence. Its flowers are trumpet-shaped and exhibit a deep blue to violet hue, often featuring subtle longitudinal stripes within the corolla. These blossoms emerge from the leaf axils and are supported by short, sturdy pedicels. The plant's overall structure is adapted to the rocky, high-altitude environments of the Himalayan region.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude regions of the Himalayas, specifically distributed across parts of India, Nepal, Bhutan, and China. It typically thrives in alpine or subalpine zones where the terrain is characterized by rocky slopes and meadows. These populations are often found at elevations ranging from 3,000 to 4,500 meters above sea level. The species favors moist environments found near mountain streams or in damp, grassy areas. Due to its specific niche, its presence is strictly limited to these cold, high-elevation ecosystems.

The transcriptome analysis involved the construction of databases for the roots, stems, leaves, and flowers. Through qRT-PCR, the relative expression levels of 11 genes, including AACT, DXS, MCS, HDS, IDI, GPPS, GES, G10H, 7-DLNGT, 7-DLGT, and SLS, were validated. The results showed that the relative expression levels of these 11 genes were higher in the aboveground parts, which consist of the stem, leaf, and flower, than in the underground part, the root. Additionally, the total contents of gentiopicroside and loganic acid were higher in the stem, leaf, and flower than in the root.
