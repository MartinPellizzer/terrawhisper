Ficus formosana

What's the taxonomical classification of Ficus formosana?

Ficus formosana belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant further falls under the order Rosales and is a member of the family Moraceae. Ultimately, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Ficus and the species formosana.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Ficus formosana has a woody, perennial structure characterized by a spreading canopy, elliptical to ovate leaves with acute tips, and a complex root system that often develops aerial roots. The leaves are typically dark green, leathery in texture, and possess smooth margins with prominent venation. Its stems are robust and can become quite thick as the plant matures into a small tree or shrub. Small, inconspicuous figs serve as the reproductive structures, appearing directly from the branches or leaf axils. These botanical features allow the species to thrive in various subtropical environments.
