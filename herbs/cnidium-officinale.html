Cnidium officinale

What's the taxonomical classification of Cnidium officinale?

Cnidium officinale belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Apiales. This plant is a representative of the family Apiaceae, which is characterized by its distinctive flowering structures. Within this family, it is placed in the genus Cnidium, and its specific biological identity is defined by the species officinale.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Cnidium officinale has an erect, perennial herbaceous structure characterized by hollow, furrowed stems that can reach heights of up to 1.5 meters. The leaves are highly dissected and pinnately compound, exhibiting a lace-like appearance with serrated margins. Small, white flowers are arranged in dense, compound umbels that emerge from the terminal ends of the branches. The plant produces small, ribbed schizocarps that serve as its characteristic seeds. Its root system consists of a thick, fleshy rhizome that is often used in medicinal preparations.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to a vast region encompassing much of central and southern Europe, parts of western and central Asia, and northern Africa. It thrives primarily in temperate climates where it can find suitable habitats in dry, sunny environments. Many populations are commonly found growing in rocky crevices or along the edges of dry grasslands and steppes. Its distribution follows specific ecological niches that favor well-drained soils and open landscapes. Due to its medicinal properties, it has also been introduced to various other regions through human cultivation and trade.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant Cnidium officiinale thrives in moist, well-drained soils within temperate climates and requires consistent moisture to support its perennial growth.

Cultivators typically plant the rhizomes in the spring to ensure a strong establishment before the summer heat arrives. It prefers partial shade or dappled sunlight, which helps prevent the foliage from drying out excessively. Regular weeding and careful monitoring for fungal diseases are essential steps to maintain a healthy crop.

Once established, the plant can be harvested for its medicinal roots after several years of growth.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Cnidium officiinale is utilized medicinally through its rhizome, leaf, rhizomatous buds, root, and shoots. The dried rhizome is the most frequently employed part in traditional formulations due to its concentrated active compounds. Practitioners also utilize the roots and rhizomatous buds to prepare various therapeutic decoctions. Additionally, the leaves and shoots can be incorporated into specific medicinal applications. Each of these components contributes unique properties to the plant's overall healing profile.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, Cnidium officinale, has been utilized extensively in Asian traditional medicine and the Japanese Kampo system to treat various inflammatory and gynecological conditions. Practitioners historically employed its dried umbels to regulate menstruation and alleviate menstrual pain. In many Eastern traditions, the plant is valued for its ability to improve blood circulation and dispel wind-dampness. Its therapeutic applications often focus on treating skin ailments and joint discomfort through its perceived warming properties. This long history of medicinal use reflects its significant role in classical herbal formulations across the region.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains several bioactive medicinal compounds including osthole, various phthalides, Z-ligustilide, ligustilide, and butylidenephthalide. These specific chemical constituents are primarily responsible for the plant's diverse pharmacological effects on the human body. The phthalides group serves as a core structural component within the plant's chemical profile. Among these, ligustilide and its specific isomer Z-ligustilide are noted for their significant biological activities. Additionally, butylidenephthalide and osthole contribute to the overall medicinal potency of the species.

According to a study published by "Biology", osthole is a natural coumarin-like compound isolated from the Fructus cnidii. This plant-derived product and its derivatives have aroused much attention for interesting biological activities including anticancer, anti-inflammatory, neuroprotective, and insecticidal effects. The review summarizes recent progress on the biological activities of osthole and its derivatives from 2018 to early 2025. The research focuses on total synthesis, structural modifications, and mechanisms of action. Additionally, structure-activity relationships of osthole derivatives are presented to serve as a reference for medicinal and agricultural applications.

In a study involving 111 singleton pregnant women, Cnidium root was indicated for controlling cough and cold in 54.1% of patients. What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause significant health side effects including acting as a potent blood pressure depressant that may lead to hypotension. Consuming excessive amounts might result in dizziness, lightheadedness, or fainting due to this reduction in circulatory pressure. Some individuals also report gastrointestinal distress such as nausea or stomach discomfort after ingestion. There is also a potential for skin irritation or allergic reactions when the plant comes into direct contact with the body. It is essential to consult a medical professional before using this herb to avoid dangerous interactions with existing medications.

According to a study published by "Cancer epidemiology", the dried rhizomes of Cnidium officinale are used as herbal drugs to treat blood pressure depressant, a deficiency disease of antivitamin, and inhibition of small intestine sympathetic nerve, as well as cosmetics for skin care. Twenty constituents were identified in the essential oil, which showed good antioxidant properties. The IC50 value in DPPH and ABTS was 6.79 and 7.33 microg/ml for Cnidium officinale. These essential oils inhibited the migration of damaged DNA induced by UV-B. They also decreased p21 expression and increased cyclin D1 expression as apoptosis-regulatory genes.
