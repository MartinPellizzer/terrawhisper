Glasswort, scientifically known as Salicornia, is a succulent halophyte thriving in salty coastal marshes and saline soils. Renowned for its unique fleshy, jointed stems, this resilient plant plays a vital role in coastal ecosystems. It is increasingly valued for its nutritional potential and applications in biotechnology and bioremediation.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as glasswort or samphire, is characterized primarily by its succulent and fleshy shoots. These specialized stems serve as the main structural component of the plant, storing water and essential nutrients to help it thrive in saline, coastal environments. Because the shoots are thick and fleshy, they are often the most utilized part of the plant, prized both for their unique texture and their medicinal and culinary properties.
