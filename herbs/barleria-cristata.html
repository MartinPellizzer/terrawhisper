Philippine violet

Philippine violet, scientifically known as Barleria cristata, is a versatile medicinal shrub prized for its vibrant purple blooms. Widely used in traditional medicine, its leaves and roots possess potent anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and diuretic properties. It serves as a natural remedy for various ailments, including skin infections and respiratory issues.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Barleria cristata is characterized by a flowering period that typically commences in May and extends through October, though the timing can be variable. During the blooming phase, the plant produces flowers that exhibit a pink coloration, with individual flower lengths ranging from a minimum of 0.8 cm to a maximum of 1.5 cm. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fruit in the form of a capsule. These capsules are relatively small, maintaining a mean length of 1.5 cm, with individual fruit dimensions ranging from a minimum of 1.2 cm to a maximum of 1.8 cm. The fruit is classified as dehiscence, meaning it opens at maturity to release its contents. The dispersal of the seeds is identified as anthropochorous, suggesting that human activity plays a role in the movement and spread of the species.
