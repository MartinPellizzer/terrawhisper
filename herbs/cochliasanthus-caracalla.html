Cochliasanthus caracalla

Cochliasanthus caracalla, commonly known as the "cara calla," is a significant medicinal plant native to specific tropical regions. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, it is traditionally utilized in various folk remedies to treat inflammatory conditions and skin ailments, offering a rich source of bioactive compounds for pharmacological study.

  • Scientific name: Cochliasanthus caracalla
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the order Fabales and is a member of the Fabaceae family. Specifically, it is identified by its genus, Cochliasanthus, as part of the species Cochliasanthus caracalla.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad and diverse geographical distribution across several distinct regions. Within the Indian Subcontinent, it can be found growing in both India and Sri Lanka. Its range extends into the Malesian region, specifically documented in Jawa. In the southern hemisphere, the species is also present in Queensland, Australia. Finally, its distribution reaches as far west as the Northeast region of Mexico.
