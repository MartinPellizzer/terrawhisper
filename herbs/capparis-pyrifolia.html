Capparis pyrifolia

Capparis pyrifolia, commonly known as Caper bush, is a resilient, evergreen shrub native to arid regions. Renowned for its edible flower buds and medicinal properties, it is widely used in traditional medicine to treat inflammation, digestive issues, and skin ailments, offering significant therapeutic potential in ethnobotanical studies.

  • Scientific name: Capparis pyrifolia
  • Family: Not available
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Distribution

This plant, known as Capparis pyrifolia, exhibits a significant presence across several regions in Asia. Within the Indian Subcontinent, it is notably distributed throughout India. Its range also extends extensively into the Indo-China region. Specifically, it can be found growing in the countries of Cambodia, Laos, and Thailand. Additionally, the species is well-established within the borders of Vietnam.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Capparis pyrifolia and commonly referred to as the hog plum or karonda, utilizes its leaves as a significant component in various traditional medicinal practices. The leaves are typically characterized by their elliptical shape and are often employed in herbal formulations to address different ailments. Due to their specific phytochemical properties, these leaves are frequently used in decoctions or topical applications to aid in healing and promoting overall wellness.
