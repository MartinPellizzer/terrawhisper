Life history

The life history of Plocama calabrica is characterized by its specialized survival strategy as a chamaphyte, a classification that reflects its growth habit and ability to endure environmental fluctuations. In terms of its specific life form categorization, it is identified as a chamaphyte, meaning its perennating buds are located close to the soil surface, often protected by the surrounding substrate or low-lying vegetation. This life form strategy allows the plant to maintain a low profile, which is essential for navigating its specific ecological niche. Furthermore, when analyzed through a broader structural lens, its life form is consistently defined as a chamaphyte, distinguishing it from taller phanerophytes or ephemeral therophytes. This physiological positioning enables Plocama calabrica to persist through seasonal changes by keeping its vital reproductive and vegetative structures near the ground level, ensuring resilience against wind and temperature extremes.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Plocama calabrica is characterized by the production of drupe-type fruits that are fundamentally indehiscent, meaning they do not split open at maturity to release their contents. These fruits possess average dimensions of 0.54 cm in length and 0.37 cm in width. Within these fruits, the seeds exhibit a specialized mass profile, with a mean seed mass of approximately 0.00554 g, ranging from a minimum of 0.00546 g to a maximum of 0.01047 g. The dispersal of the species follows a zoochorous syndrome, indicating that the plant relies on animals to facilitate the movement and distribution of its reproductive units.
