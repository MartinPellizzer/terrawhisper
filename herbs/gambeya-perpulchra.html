Gambeya perpulchra

Gambeya perpulchra, commonly known as the Gambeya tree, is a significant medicinal plant native to African tropical forests. Valued in traditional medicine, its various parts—including bark and leaves—are utilized to treat ailments such as fever, skin infections, and digestive issues, offering promising potential for modern pharmacological research and development.

  • Scientific name: Gambeya perpulchra
  • Family: Sapotaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further organized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Ericales. Ultimately, Gambeya perpulchra is a member of the family Sapotaceae within the genus Gambeya.

Common Names

This plant, Gambeya perpulchra, is known by several different names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In English, it is commonly referred to as the Gambeya tree, while in French, it is known as Gambeya. Within specific local languages, it carries unique designations such as "Dandawu" in Hausa and "N'zassa" in Baoulé.

Distribution

This plant, Gambeya perpulchra, is primarily distributed across the West Tropical Africa region. Within this area, it can be found growing in the country of Ghana. Its range also extends to the nation of Guinea. Furthermore, the species is present in both Ivory Coast and Liberia. Finally, its geographical presence is recorded within Nigeria.

Plant Parts

This plant, Gambeya perpulchra, is often utilized in traditional medicine where the dried plant bark serves as a primary therapeutic component. This specific part of the plant is harvested, processed, and dried to preserve its bioactive compounds before being prepared for various medicinal applications. Depending on the traditional practice, the dried plant bark may be decocted or powdered to treat a range of ailments, making it a vital element in the ethnobotanical use of this species.
