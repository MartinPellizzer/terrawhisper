Angelica nitida

What's the taxonomical classification of Angelica nitida?

Angelica nitida belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, the plant is placed in the order Apiales and the family Apiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Angelica and the specific species epithet nitida.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Angelica nitida has a robust, perennial structure characterized by hollow, ridged stems that can reach significant heights and large, compound leaves with deeply serrated leaflets. The foliage is typically dark green and displays a distinctively coarse texture. Its inflorescence consists of large, flat-topped umbels composed of numerous tiny, creamy-white flowers. These floral clusters are supported by thick peduncles that emerge from the leaf axils. The root system is sturdy and fleshy, providing the plant with essential stability and nutrient storage.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Angelica nitiida utilizes its roots as the primary medicinal component for various therapeutic applications. These underground structures contain specific bioactive compounds that are extracted to treat different ailments. Practitioners often prepare decoctions or tinctures from the harvested roots to harness their healing properties. The chemical profile within the root system provides the essential medicinal strength required for effective use. By focusing on this specific part, herbalists can access the most concentrated essence of the species.

According to a study published by "Zhong yao cai = Zhongyaocai = Journal of Chinese medicinal materials", the roots of Angelica nitida are used for investigating chemical constituents. Nine compounds were isolated from the roots using silica gel and identified through spectral analysis. These 9 identified compounds include isoimperatorin(I), imperatorin(II), cnidilin(III), beta-sitosterol(IV), isopimpinellin(V), phellopterin(VI), neobyakangelicol(VII), (3S)-2,2-dimethyl-3,5-dihydroxy-8-hydroxylmethyl-3,4-dihydro-2H,6H-benzo[1,2-b: 5,4-b'] dipyran-6-one(VIII), and byakangelicin(IX). All of these 9 compounds were isolated from the plant for the first time.
