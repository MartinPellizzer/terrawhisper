R. rubiginosa

Rosa rubiginosa · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (234 studies) · ★★★★★

R. rubiginosa, scientifically known as Rosa rubiginosa, is a resilient, thorny shrub native to the mountainous regions of Europe and Asia. Valued for its vibrant pink blossoms and nutrient-rich hips, this species is widely utilized in traditional medicine for its potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immune-boosting therapeutic properties.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Rosa rubiginosa involves both sexual and asexual processes, as asexual reproduction is present in this species. The flowering period typically begins in June and concludes in August. During the flowering stage, the plant utilizes a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects, with bees being a primary pollinator. While the plant is capable of self-fertilization, its reproductive success is heavily supported by these insect interactions. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fleshy fruits that have a mean length of 1.7 cm and a mean width of 1.4 cm. These fruits facilitate seed dispersal through a zoochorous syndrome, specifically via endozoochory, where seeds are dispersed after being consumed by animals. The seeds themselves are characterized by a mean mass of approximately 0.01499 g (ranging from a minimum of 0.01129 g to a maximum of 0.01959 g) and a consistent seed volume of 14 mm³.
