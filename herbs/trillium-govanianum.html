Trillium govanianum

Trillium govanianum, commonly known as the Govan's Trillium, is a rare, perennial wildflower native to specific coastal rainforest habitats. This medicinal plant is valued for its unique botanical properties. Understanding its ecological significance and traditional uses is essential for conservation efforts and exploring its potential within herbal pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Trillium govanianum
  • Family: Melanthiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Liliales and falls into the family Melanthiaceae. Its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Trillium.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several key mountainous regions in Asia. In Western Asia, its presence has been documented within the borders of Afghanistan. It can also be found in the high-altitude regions of Tibet in China. Within the Indian Subcontinent, the species thrives in the East Himalaya and throughout Nepal. Additionally, its geographical range extends into the rugged terrains of Pakistan.
