Strychnos potatorum

Strychnos potatorum, commonly known as the potato vine, is a climbing woody shrub native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its potent alkaloids, particularly strychnine, this plant holds significant pharmacological importance. While toxic if ingested, its bioactive compounds are extensively studied for their potential applications in modern medicinal chemistry.

  • Scientific name: Strychnos potatorum
  • Family: Loganiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Gentianales, it is classified under the family Loganiaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Strychnos as the species Strychnos potatorum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found inhabiting the landscapes of Burundi, Rwanda, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Moving toward the eastern part of the continent, its presence extends into East Tropical Africa, specifically within Tanzania. Additionally, the species reaches into South Tropical Africa, where it is documented in Malawi. Together, these locations highlight the wide range of environments this medicinal species occupies.
