Physiology

The physiology of Kleinia longiflora is fundamentally characterized by its adaptation to arid environments through a specialized photosynthetic pathway known as Crassulacean Acid Metabolism (CAM). Unlike typical C3 or C4 plants, this species utilizes the CAM pathway to maximize water-use efficiency by decoupling the processes of carbon dioxide uptake and fixation. During the night, the plant opens its stomata to absorb CO2, which is then chemically fixed and stored as malic acid within the vacuoles of its succulent cells. During the daylight hours, the stomata remain tightly closed to prevent transpirational water loss, while the stored malate is decarboxylated to release CO2 internally for fixation by the enzyme RuBisCO through the Calvin cycle. This metabolic strategy allows Kleinia longiflora to maintain photosynthetic activity while minimizing the evaporative stress common in its natural habitat.
