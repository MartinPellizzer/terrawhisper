Solanum khasianum

What's the taxonomical classification of Solanum khasianum?

Solanum khasianum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this botanical hierarchy, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic progression, the plant is organized under the order Solanales and the family Solanaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Solanum, and its unique species designation, khasianum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Solanum khasianum has a perennial or subshrubby growth habit characterized by woody stems, large ovate to lanceolate leaves with wavy margins, and small, star-shaped white flowers. The foliage is typically covered in a dense layer of stellate hairs that provide a velvety texture to the leaf surfaces. Its inflorescences consist of small clusters of flowers featuring prominent yellow anthers and five distinct petals. The resulting fruit is a small, globose berry that transitions from green to a ripe color upon maturity. These morphological traits are essential for distinguishing this species from other members of the Solanaceae family.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the northeastern regions of India, specifically within the states of Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram, and Nagaland. It thrives primarily in the subtropical and tropical moist broadleaf forests found in these hilly terrains. The species is often discovered growing in shaded areas or along forest edges where humidity remains high. Its distribution is closely linked to the specific climatic conditions and soil types characteristic of the Indo-Burma biodiversity hotspot. According to a study published by "Planta medica", the fruits of Solanum khasianum are used for their solasodine content. Under mild field conditions, the yield per plant remains the same with and without irrigation, and the solasodine content is not affected. Severe water stress affects both fruit production and yield, even though an increase in solasodine content per dry weight is observed. However, the solasodine yield per plant becomes lower due to the loss in fruit yield under these severe conditions. Under extreme water stress, the plant develops more negative water and osmotic potentials while maintaining turgor in the leaves.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds solamargine, solasodine, and solasodine glycoside. These specific steroidal alkaloids are primary components found within the chemical profile of Solanum khasianum. The presence of solasodine, a key steroidal sapogenin, suggests significant biological potential for the species. Furthermore, the various solasodine glycoside forms contribute to the complex pharmacological properties of the plant. Research into these bioactive molecules aims to uncover their potential therapeutic applications in medicine.

According to a study published by "Current drug discovery technologies", Solanum khasianum contains solasodine glycosides. The study utilized a scFv gene to enhance the accumulation of these solasodine glycosides in hairy root cultures. In the transgenic hairy roots, solasodine glycoside levels were 2.3-fold higher than those found in the wild-type hairy root based on binding activities and soluble protein levels. The expressed anti-solamargine scFv protein can be used in an ELISA assay with a full linear range extended from 1.5-10 µg/ml to determine the total solasodine glycoside content. This methodology allows for the production of a high yield of these secondary metabolites.
