Mashua

Tropaeolum tuberosum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (232 studies) · ★★★★★

Mashua, scientifically known as Tropaeoluum tuberosum, is a resilient tuberous plant native to the high Andes. Valued for centuries in traditional Andean medicine, this pungent root offers significant therapeutic potential. It is frequently utilized for its antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties, serving as a vital nutritional and medicinal resource.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Tropaeolum tuberosum, is highly valued for its edible and medicinal properties, which are primarily concentrated in its underground storage organs. The most significant parts of the plant are the tubers, which develop as fleshy, starchy swellings that store nutrients for the plant's survival. Each individual tuber serves as a vital source of energy, and in many traditional culinary and medicinal practices, the harvested tuber is prepared to harness its specific health benefits.
