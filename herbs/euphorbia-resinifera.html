Euphorbia resinifera

Euphorbia resinifera, commonly known as the resin spurge, is a medicinal plant native to Morocco. It is most renowned for producing resiniferatoxin, a potent compound used in scientific research. This plant holds significant pharmacological importance due to its unique chemical properties and its potential applications in pain management studies.

  • Scientific name: Euphorbia resinifera
  • Family: Euphorbiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, it is classified under the family Euphorbiaceae and specifically within the genus Euphorbia.

Additionally, the species can be located across the sea in Northern Africa. Within this African territory, it is documented to grow in Morocco. Consequently, its natural habitat is limited to these two distinct geographical areas.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as resinifer, is primarily valued for the production of resiniferatoxin, a potent compound derived from its specialized secretory structures. The most significant medicinal component is the milky latex that flows from the plant's tissues when the stems or leaves are damaged. This viscous substance contains high concentrations of diterpene esters, which are harvested and processed for their intense physiological effects, particularly in the study and treatment of pain signaling.
