Morphology

The morphology of Lycopodium japonicum is characterized by a creeping, rhizomatous growth habit where the plant is strictly non-woody. The plant develops via slender, horizontal, branched rhizomes that spread along the soil surface or through the upper layers of substrate. From these rhizomes, upright, dichotomously branched aerial stems emerge, which are relatively thin and flexible, lacking any significant lignification. The leaves are small, needle-like, and lanceolate, arranged spirally around the stems in a dense, overlapping manner. The reproductive structures consist of strobili (cones) located at the terminal ends of the branches, which are composed of densely packed sporophylls. The overall architecture is herbaceous and delicate, consistent with its non-woody classification.
