Oxycoccus palustris, scientifically known as Vaccinium oxycoccos, is a perennial evergreen shrub native to acidic peat bogs. Commonly referred to as the small cranberry, this plant produces tart, red berries rich in antioxidants, vitamin C, and organic acids, making it highly valued for its diverse therapeutic and nutritional properties.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the small cranberry, utilizes various components for its medicinal and nutritional value. The most prominent part is the fruits, which are small, tart berries harvested for their high antioxidant content. Within these berries, the juicy pulp is often extracted to create juices and supplements, while the outer peels contain a concentrated amount of beneficial polyphenols and pigments. Additionally, the tiny seeds found inside the fruit are also rich in essential fatty acids, making the entire berry a complex source of health-promoting compounds.
