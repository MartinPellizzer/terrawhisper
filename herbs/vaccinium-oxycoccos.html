Oxycoccus palustris, scientifically known as Vaccinium oxycoccos, is a perennial evergreen shrub native to acidic peat bogs. Commonly referred to as the small cranberry, this plant produces tart, red berries rich in antioxidants, vitamin C, and organic acids, making it highly valued for its diverse therapeutic and nutritional properties.

Ecology

The ecology of Vaccinium oxycoccos is defined by its specialized adaptation to nutrient-poor, acidic environments, where it is most notably observed forming sizeable stands in sphagnum bogs. These plants thrive in peatland ecosystems characterized by waterlogged soils, high moisture availability, and low pH levels, which are typical of ombrotrophic habitats. By colonizing these moss-dominated landscapes, the species plays a role in the structural complexity of the bog community, often spreading via creeping rhizomes to establish dense mats. Its ability to withstand the physiological stresses of anaerobic soil conditions and limited nitrogen availability allows it to dominate specific niches within the mire, where it integrates closely with the surrounding bryophyte layer to maintain the stability of its specialized habitat.
