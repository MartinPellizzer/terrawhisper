Ophiopogon japonicus

Dwarf lilyturf

Ophiopogon japonicus, commonly known as Mondo Grass or Dwarf Lilyturf, is a resilient evergreen perennial valued in both landscaping and traditional medicine. Renowned for its dark green, grass-like foliage, this hardy plant is frequently utilized in herbal remedies to nourish the lungs and support overall kidney and heart health.

  • Scientific name: Ophiopogon japonicus
  • Family: Asparagaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asparagales, it is classified under the family Asparagaceae and is identified by the genus Ophiopogon.

Common Names

This plant, Ophiopogon japonicus, is recognized by various names across different languages and cultures. In Azerbaijani, it is known as Yapon ofiopoqonu, while in French, it is referred to as muguet du Japon. Its Japanese common name is ジャノヒゲ (janohige), and in Vietnamese, it is called mạch môn. Furthermore, in Chinese, the plant is commonly known as 麦冬 (mài dōng).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad natural range across several key regions in East Asia. Within China, its presence is well-established in the South-Central, North-Central, and Southeast territories. The species also extends its reach into the island nation of Japan. Furthermore, its geographical footprint includes the Korean Peninsula. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's significant distribution throughout Eastern Asia.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Mondo Grass, is valued in traditional medicine primarily for its underground storage organs. The most significant medicinal component consists of the fleshy tuberous roots, which are harvested for their therapeutic properties. These thickened roots develop deep within the soil, forming a robust network of tubers that store essential nutrients. Whether referred to as a single tuber or a collection of many, the medicinal potency is concentrated within this specific root structure.
