Umbilicus rupestris

Navelwort

Umbilicus rupestris, commonly known as Navelwort, is a succulent perennial native to rocky cliffs and coastal regions. Characterized by its distinctive rosette of fleshy, kidney-shaped leaves, this resilient plant thrives in damp crevices. Traditionally, it has been valued in folk medicine for its cooling and soothing properties.

  • Scientific name: Umbilicus rupestris
  • Family: Crassulaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Umbilicus rupestris, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Saxifrages and the family Crassulaceae. Ultimately, it is identified by its specific genus, Umbilicus.

Common Names

In Portuguese, it is known as bacelos, whereas in Breton, it goes by the name Krampouezh-mouezig, and in Manx, it is called Wooishlagh.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several different regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, it can be found growing within the territories of Great Britain and Ireland. Its presence extends into Middle Europe, specifically documented in Germany. Moving toward the Mediterranean, the species is also located in Southwestern Europe. Within this southern region, it inhabits the Baleares and Corse.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Umbilicus rupestris or Navelwort, utilizes its succulent leaves as its most distinctive and medicinal feature. The leaves are fleshy, kidney-shaped, and arranged in a basal rosette, characterized by a deep, central indentation that resembles a navel. In traditional herbal practices, these leaves are often used for their cooling properties and are sometimes applied topically to soothe skin irritations or utilized in infusions to support various internal health functions.
