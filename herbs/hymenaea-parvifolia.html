Hymenaea parvifolia

Hymenaea parvifolia, commonly known as the Guayacán, is a majestic tropical tree prized for its dense, durable timber and medicinal properties. Traditionally, its resin and bark are utilized in folk medicine to treat inflammation and skin ailments, offering a rich source of bioactive compounds for holistic healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Hymenaea parvifolia
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Fabales and is a member of the Fabaceae family. Specifically, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Hymenaea, of which the species Hymenaea parvifolia is a part.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Hymenaea parvifolia, is recognized by various regional common names depending on the linguistic context. In English, it is frequently referred to as the small-leaved jambol or simply as a species of jambol. In Spanish, it is commonly known as guayacán or guayacán de monte, reflecting its place within the broader family of important timber and medicinal trees. Additionally, in Portuguese, it is identified as guayacán, a name that highlights its shared botanical heritage across the tropical regions of the Americas.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse distribution across several regions of the South American continent. In Northern South America, its presence is documented within the borders of Venezuela. Moving toward the western side of the continent, it can be found in Colombia, Peru, and Bolivia. Additionally, the species extends into the Brazil West-Central region.
