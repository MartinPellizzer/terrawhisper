Common reed, scientifically known as Phragmites australis, is a versatile perennial grass found in wetlands globally. Beyond its role in ecosystem stabilization, it possesses significant medicinal properties. Traditionally used in various cultures, its extracts offer potential antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant benefits, making it a subject of botanical interest.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the common reed, possesses a robust structure where both the shoot and the roots play significant roles in its biology. The shoot consists of tall, slender, and hollow stems that rise prominently above the water or soil, often topped with feathery inflorescences. Beneath the surface, the plant utilizes an extensive system of rhizomatous roots, which are thick, creeping underground stems that allow the plant to spread rapidly and anchor itself firmly in wetland environments.
