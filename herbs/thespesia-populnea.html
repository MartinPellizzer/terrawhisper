Thespesia populnea

Portia tree

Thespesia populnea, commonly known as the Portia tree or Pacific rosewood, is a versatile evergreen shrub native to tropical coasts. Renowned for its beautiful golden flowers and medicinal properties, it is utilized in traditional medicine to treat skin ailments, inflammation, and digestive issues, offering significant therapeutic potential.

  • Scientific name: Thespesia populnea
  • Family: Malvaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Thespesia populnea, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Malvales and the family Malvaceae. Finally, it is classified under the genus Thespesia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several distinct tropical and subtropical regions. In the Atlantic, it can be found within the Macaronesia region, specifically on the islands of Cape Verde. Its presence extends significantly into West Tropical Africa, where it inhabits various landscapes. Within this African zone, the species is documented in countries such as Benin, Burkina, and Gambia. Furthermore, its range continues through the coastal and inland areas of Ghana.

Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as Portia tree, utilizes various anatomical structures for its medicinal and ecological purposes. The large, heart-shaped leaf is often used in traditional practices to treat skin ailments and inflammation, while the fleshy, oval fruits contain the essential components of the plant's reproductive cycle. Within these fruits, the small, dark seeds serve as the primary means of propagation. The sturdy wood of the tree is highly valued for its durability in construction, and the stem bark is frequently harvested for its bioactive compounds, which are often employed in herbal remedies to address various ailments.
