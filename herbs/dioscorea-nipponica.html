Fan-leaf mountain yam

Dioscorea nipponica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (59 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Fan-leaf mountain yam, scientifically known as Dioscorea nipponica, is a perennial climbing herb native to East Asia. Valued in traditional medicine, this plant is frequently used to treat inflammation, digestive issues, and respiratory ailments. Its nutrient-rich tubers offer significant therapeutic potential, making it a vital subject in ethnopharmacology.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Dioscoreales, it is classified under the family Dioscoreaceae. Ultimately, it is identified by the genus Dioscorea as the species Dioscorea nipponica.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning across parts of East Asia. In the Russian Far East, its presence is documented within the Amur, Khabarovsk, and Primorye regions. Its range extends significantly into China, where it can be found in the South-Central territories. Additionally, the species is located as far north as Inner Mongolia. Together, these areas define the specific natural habitats where the species thrives.

Plant Parts

This plant, Dioscorea nipponica, is primarily valued for its underground storage organs which contain its therapeutic compounds. The most significant part used in traditional medicine is the fleshy rhizome, which serves as the plant's main energy reservoir. While some botanical descriptions may refer to the thickened structures as rhizomes in a general sense, the medicinal potency is concentrated within the specific rhizome harvested from the plant. Although it may appear similar to a simple root in structure, these specialized rhizomes are the essential components utilized for their bioactive properties.
