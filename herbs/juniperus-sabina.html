Savin Juniper

Juniperus sabina · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (226 studies) · ★★★★★

Savin Juniper, scientifically known as Juniperus sabiquina, is a distinctive evergreen shrub native to Europe and North Africa. Characterized by its scale-like leaves and aromatic berries, it has a long history in traditional medicine. However, due to its potent toxicity, it must be handled with extreme caution and expertise.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Pinidae and the order Pinales, falling into the family Cupressaceae. Ultimately, it is identified by the genus Juniperus as Juniperus sabina.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad presence across various regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its range extends into the country of Sweden. Moving toward the central part of the continent, it is widely distributed throughout Middle Europe. Specifically, it can be found in Germany as well as across the Czechia-Slovakia border regions. Finally, its Middle European distribution is further confirmed by its occurrence in Poland.

Plant Parts

This plant, Juniperus sabina, utilizes various components for its medicinal and aromatic properties, most notably its leaves and fruits. The scale-like leaves contain essential oils that are often harvested for their potent scent and therapeutic applications. Additionally, the fleshy, berry-like fruits, which are technically modified cones, serve as a significant source of bioactive compounds used in traditional remedies.
