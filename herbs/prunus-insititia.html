What's the taxonomical classification of Prunus insititia?

Prunus insititia belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Rosales. It is further categorized into the family Rosaceae, which is a diverse group of flowering plants, and is specifically identified under the genus Prunus. Ultimately, the taxonomic identity of this plant is completed by its specific epithet, resulting in the scientific name Prunus insititia.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Prunus insititia has small, elliptical to ovate leaves with serrated margins, five-petaled white or pink flowers, and small, fleshy drupes that transition from green to yellow or red when ripe. The plant typically exhibits a multi-stemmed, shrubby growth habit with woody branches that often feature short spurs. Its foliage is characterized by a smooth texture and prominent venation that becomes more visible as the leaves mature. The flowers emerge in clusters during the spring, often appearing before or alongside the new leaf growth. These botanical features collectively define the structural appearance of this species within the Rosaceae family.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and diarrhea if any part of it is ingested in significant quantities. These adverse reactions typically stem from the presence of cyanogenic glycosides found within the seeds and pits. If consumed in large amounts, these compounds may release cyanide into the bloodstream, potentially leading to more severe toxicity. Symptoms of cyanide poisoning can include dizziness, headaches, and even respiratory failure in extreme cases. Therefore, caution is necessary to avoid accidental ingestion of the plant's reproductive components.
