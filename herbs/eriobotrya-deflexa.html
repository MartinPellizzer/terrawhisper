What's the taxonomical classification of Eriobotrya deflexa?

Eriobotrya deflexa belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Following its taxonomic hierarchy, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant falls under the order Rosales and is a member of the family Rosaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Eriobotrya, with its specific designation being the species deflexa.

Eriobotrya deflexa has large, leathery, evergreen leaves that are oblong-lanceolate in shape, featuring serrated margins and a distinctive dense covering of brownish-white tomentum on the undersides. These leaves are arranged alternately along woody stems that become increasingly textured with age. During the flowering season, the plant produces clusters of white, fragrant flowers that eventually develop into large, fleshy pome fruits. The overall structure is that of a dense, spreading shrub or small tree with a robust canopy.

According to a study published by "Journal of natural products", a phytochemical study on a methanolic extract of leaves of Eriobotrya deflexa led to the isolation and characterization of nine terpenoid compounds. Four of these are new chemical entities, which include two monoterpene glycosides labeled as (1) and (2). These two glycosides are (3S)-O-alpha-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1-->3)-[4-O-(E)-coumaroyl]-alpha-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1-->6)-beta-D-glucopyranosyl-linalool and (3S)-O-alpha-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1-->3)-[4-O-(Z)-coumaroyl]-alpha-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1-->6)-beta-D-glucopyranosyl-linalool. Additionally, two triterpene acids were identified, specifically 1beta,2alpha,19alpha-trihydroxy-3-oxo-12-ursen-28-oic acid (3) and 2alpha,3alpha,19alpha-trihydroxy-12-oleanen-28-oic acid (4). The activities of these isolates were determined through an in vitro antiproliferation test.

According to a study published by "Molecules (Basel, Switzerland)", Eriobotrya deflex is used for its potential to serve as an inhibitor of the ageing process in the skin. The plant was among eight native Taiwanese materials screened for phenolic contents and their ability to measure matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9) activity. The extract from Eriobotrya deflex was shown to inhibit MMP-9 activity of WS-1 cell after ultraviolet B irradiation.
