Rubus chingii

Rubus chingii, commonly known as Chojung, is a significant medicinal plant in traditional Chinese medicine. Native to East Asia, its berries and roots are highly valued for their antioxidant properties. It is frequently used to support cardiovascular health, reduce inflammation, and promote overall longevity through its nutrient-rich compounds.

  • Scientific name: Rubus chingii
  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Rosales, falling into the family Rosaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement in the genus Rubus.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Chinquapin raspberry, is a medicinal species where various components hold significance. The most notable parts used in traditional medicine are the fruits, which undergo a distinct transformation during maturation. Initially, the plant produces green fruit that remains firm and acidic, but as they develop, they transition into the highly valued ripe fruit, which is celebrated for its nutritional and therapeutic properties. In addition to the edible components, the morphology of the plant includes characteristic prickly stems that protect the climbing vines, while the harvested fruit serves as the primary source for many medicinal preparations.
