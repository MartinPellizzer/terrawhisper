What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Aster maackii has a perennial growth habit characterized by erect stems that typically reach heights of 20 to 50 centimeters and feature numerous linear to lanceolate leaves. These leaves are mostly basal or arranged alternately along the stem, possessing serrated margins and a somewhat hairy texture. The inflorescence consists of solitary flower heads that bloom in shades of purple or lavender with distinct yellow central disc florets. Each flower head is supported by a series of involucral bracts that are often overlapping and slightly pubescent. This species is morphologically distinguished from similar asters by its specific leaf shape and the fine pubescence found on its stems and bracts.
