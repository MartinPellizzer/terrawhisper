Marshlilies, scientifically known as Crinum, is a genus of perennial bulbous plants celebrated for their striking, trumpet-shaped flowers. Beyond their ornamental beauty, various species hold significant medicinal importance, containing bioactive alkaloids used in traditional practices to treat ailments ranging from inflammation to skin conditions, marking them as vital botanical resources.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asparagales, it is classified under the family Amaryllidaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Crinum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across different oceanic and continental regions. Within the Macaronesian islands, it can be found growing in the Azores and Madeira. Moving toward the African continent, its range extends into West Tropical Africa. Specifically, it is documented in countries such as Benin and Burkina. Additionally, the species is also known to inhabit the Gambia region.
