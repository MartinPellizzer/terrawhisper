Carpinus

Hornbeam

Carpinus, commonly known as hornbeam, is a versatile genus of deciduous trees valued for their incredibly dense, durable wood. While primarily ornamental and structural, various species possess ethnobotanical significance, traditionally used in folk medicine for their astringent properties and ability to support skin health and soothe minor inflammations.

  • Scientific name: Carpinus
  • Family: Betulaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fagales and the family Betulaceae, it is classified under the genus Carpinus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Carpinus, is referred to by various common names depending on the region and language. In Simplified Chinese, it is identified as 鹅耳枥属 (zh-hans) or 鹅耳枥属 (zh-cn), while in Traditional Chinese contexts, it is called 鵝耳櫪屬 (zh-hant). Specifically in Hong Kong, the name used is 鵝耳櫪屬 (zh-hk), and in Singapore, it is recognized as 鹅耳枥属 (zh-sg).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical presence across several regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its range extends through the landscapes of Denmark and Great Britain. It can also be found inhabiting the natural environments of Norway and Sweden. Moving toward the center of the continent, the species is present within Middle Europe. Specifically, its distribution includes the mountainous and forested areas of Austria.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as hornbeam, is recognized for its distinctive botanical features, particularly its leaves. The leaves of Carpinus are characterized by their serrated margins and prominent, parallel venation that gives them a textured appearance. In many medicinal and ecological contexts, these leaves are studied for their specific chemical compositions and their role in the plant's overall biological functions.
