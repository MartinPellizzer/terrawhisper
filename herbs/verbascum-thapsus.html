Reproduction

The reproduction of Verbascum thapsus is primarily sexual, characterized by bisexual flowers that facilitate efficient reproductive cycles. The flowering period is extensive, typically beginning in April and continuing through October. These flowers are distinctively yellow in color, with widths ranging from a minimum of 1.5 cm to a maximum of 2.3 cm. The plant utilizes a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects such as butterflies to aid in the transfer of pollen, though self-fertilization is also present. As a monocarp, the plant's reproductive structure culminates in the production of dry, dehiscence-type capsules. These capsules, which have an average length of 0.9 cm, are highly prolific, yielding more than 1,000 seeds per fruit. The individual seeds are minute, with a consistent mean length of 0.8 mm and a mean mass of approximately 0.000101975 g (ranging from 0.00005 g to 0.00015 g) and a mean volume of 0.074 mm³. Dispersal is achieved through an anemochorous syndrome, meaning the seeds are primarily spread by the wind. Notably, asexual reproduction is absent in this species.
