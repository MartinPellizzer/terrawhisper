Wood-apple, scientifically known as Limonia acidissima, is a versatile deciduous tree native to South Asia. Renowned for its hard, woody shell and tangy pulp, it serves as both a nutritious food source and a potent medicinal herb, offering significant therapeutic benefits for digestive health and immune support.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across the vast landscape of the Indian Subcontinent. Within this region, it can be found growing in the diverse environments of India and Pakistan. Its presence is also well-documented throughout the territory of Bangladesh. Furthermore, the species extends its range to the lush terrains of Assam. Finally, the geographical reach of this medicinal plant includes the island nation of Sri Lanka.

Plant Parts

This plant, Limonia acidissima, commonly known as the wood apple, possesses various therapeutic components where the whole plant is frequently utilized in traditional medicine. The stem serves as the structural foundation for the tree, while the stem bark is often harvested for its specific medicinal properties. The leaves are highly valued for their bioactive compounds, and the roots are sometimes employed in decoctions to treat various ailments. Ultimately, the versatility of the species ensures that almost every part, from the sturdy stem to the delicate leaves, contributes to its pharmacological profile.
