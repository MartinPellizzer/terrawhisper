Limonia acidissima

What's the taxonomical classification of Limonia acidissima?

Limonia acidissima belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it falls under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Its taxonomic progression continues through the order Sapindales and the family Rutaecaeae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Limonia, with the specific designation of the species acidissima.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Limonia acidissima has a medium-sized tree characterized by a spreading crown, thorny branches, and pinnate leaves that typically consist of five to nine leaflets. The bark is greyish and often becomes fissured or scaly as the tree reaches maturity. Its leaves are leathery in texture with an elliptical shape and a dull green color on the upper surface. Small, fragrant white flowers emerge from the axils of the branches during the flowering season. The fruit is a woody, globose drupe with a hard, thick rind and a pulpy, acidic interior.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to South and Southeast Asia, specifically spanning across India, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, and parts of the Malay Peninsula. It thrives in various tropical and subtropical climates where seasonal rainfall is abundant. The species is frequently found in dry deciduous forests and coastal regions throughout its primary range. Due to human cultivation, it has also been introduced to other tropical zones in Africa and parts of the Caribbean. Its ability to withstand heat makes it well-suited for the diverse landscapes of the Indian subcontinent.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant Limonia acidissima thrives in tropical and subtropical regions when grown in well-drained, sandy or loamy soils under full sunlight and moderate temperatures.

It is a hardy, deciduous tree that requires minimal irrigation once the root system becomes established in the ground. Propagation is most commonly achieved through seeds, though grafting can be used to ensure specific fruit qualities. Growers must ensure the soil does not remain waterlogged to prevent root rot during the rainy season.

Because of its drought tolerance, it is often used in areas with limited water availability.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Limonia acidissima utilizes its fruit, leaves, roots, stem, and inflorescence for various medicinal purposes. The pulp of the fruit is frequently used to treat digestive issues and diarrhea, while the leaves serve as a remedy for skin diseases and fever. Root extracts are often employed to manage inflammation and abdominal pains. The stem bark provides therapeutic benefits for treating dysentery and other gastrointestinal ailments. Additionally, the inflorescence can be utilized in traditional practices to address specific systemic ailments.

According to a study published by "Heliyon", the leaves and fruits of Limonia acidissima are used medicinally for their antiviral activity against the avian influenza H5N1 virus. The antiviral potency follows an ascending arrangement where Limonia acidissima leaves (LAL) are greater than Limonia acidissima fruits (LAF) at 160 μg. Metabolomic profiling of LAL revealed the detection of 74 chromatographic peaks. Furthermore, a nano formulation of LAF has demonstrated the most splendid antiviral upshot. Researchers propose using the prepared crude methanol extracts of the plant as an antiviral agent.

Some individuals might also experience digestive disturbances such as stomach aches or nausea following consumption. It is important to monitor intake to avoid potential toxicity or adverse physiological reactions. Always consult a healthcare professional before using this plant for therapeutic purposes.
