Garcinia

Garcinia, commonly known as mangosteen or gamboge, is a genus of tropical plants celebrated for their medicinal potency. Renowned for containing bioactive xanthones, these plants offer powerful antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties. Traditionally used in holistic healing, Garcinia species are vital resources in modern pharmaceutical and nutritional research.

  • Scientific name: Garcinia
  • Family: Clusiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, it is classified under the family Clusiaceae. Its taxonomic identity is further defined by its placement in the genus Garcinia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern concentrated within the West Tropical Africa region. It is found natively in the country of Benin, where it thrives in local ecosystems. The species also extends its range into Burkina Faso, contributing to the regional biodiversity. Furthermore, its presence is documented throughout Guinea-Bissau and the neighboring nation of Guinea. Consequently, its geographical footprint is primarily defined by these specific West African territories.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Garcinia, offers various components that are utilized for their medicinal and nutritional properties. The most widely recognized part is the fruit, which is often consumed for its unique flavor and bioactive compounds. Within the fruits, the seeds are also present, though they are typically separated from the fleshy pulp. Beyond the fruit, the stem barks are frequently harvested for their concentrated chemical constituents used in traditional remedies. Additionally, the roots of the plant are sometimes employed in medicinal preparations, ensuring that almost every part of the organism serves a specific purpose in holistic health.
