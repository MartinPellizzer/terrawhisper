Tea plant, scientifically known as Camellia sinensis, is a versatile evergreen shrub celebrated globally for its medicinal and therapeutic properties. Rich in polyphenols and antioxidants, it serves as the primary source of tea. Beyond its calming ritual, it offers cognitive support, metabolic benefits, and essential cardiovascular protection for health.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Ericales, it is a member of the family Theaceae and is classified under the genus Camellia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions in Africa and the Indian Ocean. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found within the borders of Guinea. Its range extends to West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically around the Gulf of Guinea Islands. Moving toward the northeast, the species is also located in Ethiopia. Finally, it inhabits parts of the Western Indian Ocean, including the islands of Mauritius and Réunion.

Plant Parts

This plant, Camellia sinensis, is primarily valued for its foliage, as the most significant medicinal and commercial component is the leaf. Specifically, the young leaves and terminal buds are harvested to produce high-quality tea leaves, which contain a wealth of antioxidants and bioactive compounds. While the entire leaf can be utilized in various preparations, the tea leaves themselves are the most common medium for infusion. Beyond the foliage, the roots of the plant also contain specific chemical constituents that contribute to its overall pharmacological profile.
