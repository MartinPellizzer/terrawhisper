Camellia sinensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Camellia sinensis?

Camellia sinensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the taxonomic hierarchy further, it is placed in the order Ericales and the family Theaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by its genus, Camellia, and its specific species name, sinensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Camellia sinensis has an evergreen woody structure characterized by elliptical, serrated leaves that are dark green and leathery in texture. The plant grows as a shrub or small tree with a spreading canopy and a complex branching system. Its flowers are solitary and white, featuring delicate petals and prominent yellow stamens that emerge from the leaf axils. The leaf margins are finely toothed, while the undersides often display a slightly lighter shade of green. Small, round drupe-like fruits develop following pollination to complete its reproductive cycle.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the subtropical and tropical regions of East Asia, specifically centered in the mountainous areas of China, India, and Myanmar. It thrives in high-altitude environments where the climate remains humid and temperatures are moderately warm. While its origins are localized to these specific Asian territories, extensive cultivation has expanded to diverse locations worldwide. Major production hubs now include countries such as Kenya, Sri Lanka, and Vietnam due to their favorable soil and weather conditions. These global plantations allow the species to flourish far beyond its original ecological boundaries.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, Camellia sinensis, requires well-drained, acidic soil and a subtropical climate with consistent rainfall to thrive.

Growers typically plant these evergreen shrubs in regions that experience warm temperatures and high humidity levels. Regular pruning is essential to maintain a low height, which facilitates easier leaf harvesting. Once established, the bushes can be harvested multiple times a year to produce various types of tea.

Proper soil management and protection from frost ensure a productive and sustainable crop.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Camellia sinensis utilizes its buds, flowers, leaves, roots, and stems for various medicinal purposes. The young buds and leaves are most commonly harvested to create tea rich in antioxidants that support metabolic health. Floral extracts from the flowers are often used in traditional remedies to provide calming effects. The roots can be processed to address specific ailments like inflammation or digestive issues. Additionally, the stem provides structural components that can be utilized in specialized botanical preparations.

According to a study published by "Plants (Basel, Switzerland)", Camellia sinensis is an important non-alcoholic medicinal and fragrancy plant that synthesizes different secondary metabolites, including flavonoids. In the tea cultivar Longjing 43 (LJ43), eight samples were performed into three groups for transcriptome analysis under three biological replications. RNA sequencing data showed that the expression of the gene TEA031065 was highest in buds and young leaves. These specific tissues are rich in catechins and other flavonoids.

According to a study published by "The Journal of biological chemistry", herbs have been used for medicinal purposes, including the treatment of diabetes, for centuries. Plants containing flavonoids are used to treat diabetes in Indian medicine. The green tea flavonoid, epigallocatechin gallate (EGCC), is reported to have glucose-lowering effects in animals. This constituent of green tea can increase tyrosine phosphorylation of the insulin receptor and insulin receptor substrate-1 (IRS-1). EGCG also increases phosphoinositiide 3-kinase, mitogen-activated protein kinase, and p70(s6k) activity.

What are the pharmacological activities of Camellia sinensis?

This plant has diverse pharmacological activities including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-cancer, anti-diabetic, and anti-hyperlipidemic properties. The high concentration of polyphenols and catechins allows it to neutralize free radicals and reduce systemic inflammation. These bioactive compounds also play a role in inhibiting tumor growth and regulating cellular processes to prevent cancer progression. Furthermore, regular consumption helps manage blood glucose levels and improves lipid profiles by lowering cholesterol. Such multi-faceted benefits contribute to its significant role in preventative medicine and metabolic health.

According to a study published by "Journal of dietary supplements", Camellia sinensis has bioactive components with neuroprotective, antioxidant, anti-proteinopathies, neural-vasodilation, anti-inflammatory, and iron chelating potential. These components may treat Parkinson's disease at the cellular level by decreasing microglia activation and attenuating damage from radical oxygen species. They can also support correct protein folding, chelate iron, and increase the substantia nigra blood flow. Furthermore, Camellia sinensis may promote dopaminergic cell growth.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains a diverse array of medicinal compounds including the potent antioxidant (-)‐Epigalocatechin‐3‐gallate, the amino acid theanine, the stimulant caffeine, and various types of catechin. Among these, epigallocatechin gallate (EGCC) is widely studied for its significant role in metabolic health and cellular protection. The presence of theanine helps to promote relaxation and mitigate the jittery effects often associated with stimulant intake. Caffeine acts as a central nervous system stimulant that enhances cognitive alertness and physical performance. Additionally, the general category of catechin provides essential polyphenolic properties that support overall cardiovascular function.

According to a study published by "Food & function", the green tea polyphenol (-) -Epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG) is studied for its biological activities in the prevention of chronic diseases. The study focuses on EGCG oxidation-derived polymers, specifically those identified as >10 kDa EGCG polymers. These >10 kDa EGCG polymers gained an advantage over native EGCG in increasing insulin sensitivity in type 2 diabetic mice. When these >10 kDa EGCG polymers were incubated with CaCo2 colon cancer cells, they coated the cell surface and regulated multiple components of the RAS. While the >10 kDa EGCG polymers exhibited compromised activities in scavenging or initiating reactive oxygen species compared to EGCG, they gained a higher reactivity toward sulfhydryl groups.

The polyphenols found in its leaves help reduce inflammation and oxidative stress associated with neurodegenerative decline. Regular consumption can improve insulin sensitivity and blood glucose regulation in patients with metabolic disorders. Its antioxidants also support heart health by managing cholesterol levels and blood pressure. Furthermore, the compounds assist in regulating metabolic rates and protecting hepatic cells from damage.

According to a study published by "Antioxidants (Basel, Switzerland)", Camellia sinensis is used for medicinal purposes. This plant contains polyphenolic compounds that may contribute to anti-amyloidogenic properties. These compounds can attenuate amyloid-β toxicity and disrupt the aggregation of toxic amyloid-β protein fibrils. Such processes are linked to the onset and progression of Alzheimer's Disease. The study analyzed the antioxidant effects of these extracts using a 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) assay.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause a range of adverse reactions including nausea, insomnia, anxiety, jitters, stomach upset, diuretic effect, dizziness, headache, tachycardia, irritability, iron malabsorption, and vomiting. Excessive consumption of its caffeine content often leads to neurological symptoms such as jitters, anxiety, irritability, and insomnia. Digestive issues like nausea, vomiting, and general stomach upset may occur, particularly if consumed on an empty stomach. The chemical composition can also trigger physiological responses like tachycardia, dizziness, and a diuretic effect. Furthermore, the tannins in the leaves are known to interfere with nutrition by causing iron malabsorption.
