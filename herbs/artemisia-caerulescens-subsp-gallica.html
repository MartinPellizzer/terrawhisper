Artemisia caerulescens subsp. gallica

Artemisia caerulescens subsp. gallica, commonly known as Gallic wormwood, is a distinctive perennial herb native to Mediterranean ecosystems. Renowned for its aromatic foliage and silvery-green leaves, this subspecies possesses significant ethnobotanical value. It is frequently studied for its bioactive compounds, offering potential therapeutic properties in traditional medicinal practices.

  • Scientific name: Artemisia caerulescens subsp. gallica
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales. Finally, it is a member of the family Asteraceae within the genus Artemisia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across several distinct regions of the Mediterranean basin. In Southwestern Europe, its distribution includes the islands of the Baleares, as well as mainland areas of France and Spain. Moving eastward, the species can also be found throughout Southeastern Europe, specifically within Italy. Furthermore, its range extends beyond the European continent into Northern Africa, where it is located in Morocco. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive across various Mediterranean climates.
