European gromwell

Lithospermum officinale · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (27 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

European gromwell, scientifically known as Lithospermum officiinale, is a perennial herb native to Europe. Renowned for its vibrant blue berries and reddish roots, it has a long history in traditional medicine. Its roots contain naphthodianthrones, which are traditionally used for skin ailments, though modern clinical applications remain limited.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as gromwell, possesses distinct anatomical features used in traditional medicine. The most significant part is the root, which is thick, woody, and contains the characteristic red pigments often utilized for their medicinal properties. Supporting the structure is a sturdy, often somewhat hairy stem that rises from the ground to hold the plant upright. Sprouting from the stem are the leaf structures, which are typically simple, lanceolate in shape, and arranged alternately along the length of the plant.
