Filipendula ulmaria

Mead wort

Filipendula ulmaria, commonly known as meadowsweet, is a perennial herb renowned for its fragrant, creamy blossoms. Historically significant, it contains salicylic acid derivatives, serving as a natural precursor to aspirin. This versatile plant is widely used in traditional medicine to soothe digestive issues and alleviate inflammation and pain.

  • Scientific name: Filipendula ulmaria
  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Rosales, it is a member of the Rosaceae family. Ultimately, it is identified by the genus Filipendula.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as meadowsweet, is characterized by its distinct botanical features that contribute to its medicinal value. The leaves are typically long, serrated, and lanceolate in shape, providing a lush green base for the plant's structure. Rising above the foliage are the dense, creamy-white flowers, which emit a sweet, almond-like fragrance. These blossoms are arranged in large, branched inflorescences that form pyramidal clusters at the tips of the stems. Consequently, when harvesting for medicinal purposes, it is often the entire aerial parts of the plant that are collected and used in traditional remedies.
