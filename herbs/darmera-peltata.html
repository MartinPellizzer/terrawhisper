Peltiphyllum peltatum, scientifically known as Darmera peltata, is a striking perennial herb celebrated for its large, umbrella-shaped leaves. Often called Marsh Marigold, this moisture-loving plant thrives in shady, boggy environments. Beyond its ornamental beauty, it holds traditional medicinal significance in various cultures for its diverse bioactive properties.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Marsh Marigold or Umbrella Plant, is most notably characterized by its large, impressive leaves. These leaves are heart-shaped or kidney-shaped with distinctively scalloped edges and a leathery texture, often growing in lush, spreading clusters that cover the ground. In medicinal contexts, the leaves are the primary part of the plant studied, as they contain various bioactive compounds, though they must be handled with care due to their potential toxicity if consumed incorrectly.
