Darmera peltata

Peltiphyllum peltatum

Darmera peltata, commonly known as Marsh Marigold or Umbrella Plant, is a perennial herb prized for its large, kidney-shaped leaves. While primarily used as an ornamental shade plant, it holds traditional medicinal value in various cultures for treating skin ailments and inflammation due to its unique bioactive properties.

  • Scientific name: Darmera peltata
  • Family: Saxifragaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered under Saxifragales and placed in the family Saxifragaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Darmera.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several regions of Europe. In Northern Europe, it can be found growing in the Faroe Islands, Great Britain, and Ireland. Moving toward the center of the continent, the species is also present in Middle Europe. Specifically, it inhabits parts of Germany within this central region. Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Marsh Marigold or Umbrella Plant, is most notably characterized by its large, impressive leaves. These leaves are heart-shaped or kidney-shaped with distinctively scalloped edges and a leathery texture, often growing in lush, spreading clusters that cover the ground. In medicinal contexts, the leaves are the primary part of the plant studied, as they contain various bioactive compounds, though they must be handled with care due to their potential toxicity if consumed incorrectly.
