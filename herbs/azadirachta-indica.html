Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1607 studies) · ★★★★★

Neem, scientifically known as Azadirachta indica, is a versatile medicinal powerhouse native to the Indian subcontinent. Often called the "village pharmacy," this hardy evergreen tree offers profound therapeutic benefits. Renowned for its potent antibacterial, antifungal, and anti-inflammatory properties, neem serves as a cornerstone in traditional Ayurvedic medicine.

Distribution

This plant, known scientifically as Azadirachta indica, exhibits a specific presence across various regions of West Tropical Africa. Within this part of the continent, its distribution includes the nation of Burkina. It is also found in the coastal and inland areas of the Gambia. Furthermore, the species spans across Ghana and the territory of Guinea-Bissau. Finally, its geographical range extends into the country of Guinea.

Ecology

The ecology of Azadirachta indica is characterized by its adaptability to various terrains, typically thriving within an elevational range of 200 to 1300 m AMSL. This species exhibits a strong capacity for integration into human-modified landscapes, as it is frequently grown in and around villages. Due to its robust nature, the plant has demonstrated significant ecological resilience, having escaped cultivation and regenerated plentifully in specific locations, such as the Mkwaja Ranch headquarters.

The reproduction of Azadirachta indica is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, primarily driven by animal vectors rather than abiotic factors. Specifically, the plant relies on insect pollination, with bees playing a significant role in the transfer of pollen. The species exhibits a reproductive strategy where self-fertilization is absent, necessitating cross-pollination to ensure genetic diversity.
