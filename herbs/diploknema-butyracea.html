Indian-buttertree, scientifically known as Diploknema butyracea, is a versatile medicinal tree native to South Asia. Renowned for its nutrient-rich seeds and bark, it offers significant therapeutic potential. Traditionally used to treat skin ailments, inflammation, and digestive issues, this plant serves as a vital resource in Ayurvedic healing practices.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across several key regions in Asia. In China, its presence is specifically noted within the high-altitude terrain of Tibet. Moving into the Indian Subcontinent, it can be found in the lush landscapes of Assam. It is also widely distributed throughout the East Himalaya and the West Himalaya regions. Finally, its geographical range extends into the mountainous territory of Nepal.

Plant Parts

This plant, Diploknemma butyracea, is a significant medicinal and economic resource, particularly noted for the high lipid content found within its specialized structures. While various parts of the tree may be utilized, the seeds are the most vital component, as they contain a large amount of vegetable fat often referred to as "vegetable butter." These seeds are harvested and processed to extract oil, which serves not only as a base for medicinal ointments and topical applications but also as a valuable source of nutrition and industrial raw material.
