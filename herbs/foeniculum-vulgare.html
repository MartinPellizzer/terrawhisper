Fennel

Foeniculum vulgare · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1675 studies) · ★★★★★

Fennel, scientifically known as Foeniculum vulgare, is a versatile aromatic herb cherished for both culinary and medicinal purposes. Renowned for its digestive benefits, it effectively relieves bloating and indigestion. Rich in essential oils and antioxidants, this perennial plant serves as a natural remedy for respiratory health and hormonal balance.

The reproduction of Foeniculum vulgare is primarily sexual, characterized by bisexual flowers arranged in an umbel inflorescence. These small, yellow flowers typically measure between 0.13 cm and 0.16 cm in length and undergo a flowering period that begins in July and concludes in August. The plant relies on biotic pollination, specifically utilizing an insect pollination syndrome to facilitate fertilization, as self-fertilization is noted to be absent. These fruits are highly prolific, containing more than 1,000 seeds per fruit. The seeds themselves have a consistent length of approximately 4.5 mm and a mean mass of 0.0144815125 g, though individual seed mass can range from a minimum of 0.00049 g to a maximum of 0.049 g. For dispersal, the species employs an unspecialized strategy with an epizoochorous syndrome, allowing seeds to be transported via animals. Asexual reproduction is absent in this species.
