Physiology

The physiology of Uncaria tomentosa is characterized by its specialized metabolic processes as a woody, perennial climbing vine, primarily driven by its complex secondary metabolism. The plant utilizes photosynthesis to synthesize various bioactive alkaloids, most notably oxindole alkaloids, which are strategically produced and stored within its tissues to serve defensive roles. Its nutrient acquisition strategy relies on an extensive root system designed for moisture and mineral uptake from the tropical soil, though it functions as a non-nitrogen fixer; it does not possess the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria required for atmospheric nitrogen fixation, meaning its nitrogen requirements are met entirely through soil absorption. The plant's vascular physiology is adapted for liana growth, facilitating the efficient long-distance transport of water, minerals, and synthesized solutes through specialized xylem and phloem vessels to support its climbing habit and large leaf surface area.
