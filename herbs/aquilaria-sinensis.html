Incense tree, scientifically known as Aquilaria sinensis, is a prized medicinal plant celebrated for its aromatic resin. Deeply rooted in traditional Eastern medicine, its precious agarwood is utilized to treat various ailments, including digestive issues and respiratory distress, while also serving as a cornerstone in luxury perfumery and aromatherapy.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across specific regions within East and Southeast Asia. In China, its presence is noted in the tropical island province of Hainan. It can also be found throughout the diverse landscapes of China Southeast. Beyond Chinese borders, the species extends into the Indo-China peninsula. Specifically, it is well-documented as growing within the country of Vietnam.

Plant Parts

This plant, Aquilaria sinensis, is highly valued for its complex anatomical structures that contribute to its medicinal and aromatic properties. While the leaves and stems are integral parts of the plant's biological growth, the most significant components are found within the woody tissue. When the tree becomes infected, it produces a dark, aromatic substance known as agarwood, which is primarily concentrated in the resinous heartwood. This precious Agarwood is the result of the plant's defense mechanism, making the dense, resin-infused center of the trunk the most sought-after part for therapeutic and cultural uses.
