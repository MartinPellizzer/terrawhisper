Aquilaria sinensis

Incense tree

Aquilaria sinensis, commonly known as Chinese agarwood, is a prestigious medicinal plant prized for its fragrant, resinous heartwood. Renowned in traditional medicine, it is used to calm the spirit, relieve pain, and regulate Qi. This precious species remains a cornerstone of botanical healing and luxury perfumery worldwide.

  • Scientific name: Aquilaria sinensis
  • Family: Thymelaeaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malvales, it is classified under the family Thymelaeaceae and is identified by the genus Aquilaria.

Common Names

This plant, Aquilaria sineensis, is known by various names across different languages and regions. In Simplified Chinese, it is referred to as 土沉香 (zh-hans), while in Traditional Chinese (zh-hant) and specifically in Hong Kong (zh-hk), it is commonly called 牙香樹. In Vietnam, the species is known as Bạch mộc hương (vi), and in Lithuania, it is called Kininis agarmedis (lt).

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across specific regions within East and Southeast Asia. In China, its presence is noted in the tropical island province of Hainan. It can also be found throughout the diverse landscapes of China Southeast. Beyond Chinese borders, the species extends into the Indo-China peninsula. Specifically, it is well-documented as growing within the country of Vietnam.

Plant Parts

This plant, Aquilaria sinensis, is highly valued for its complex anatomical structures that contribute to its medicinal and aromatic properties. While the leaves and stems are integral parts of the plant's biological growth, the most significant components are found within the woody tissue. When the tree becomes infected, it produces a dark, aromatic substance known as agarwood, which is primarily concentrated in the resinous heartwood. This precious Agarwood is the result of the plant's defense mechanism, making the dense, resin-infused center of the trunk the most sought-after part for therapeutic and cultural uses.
