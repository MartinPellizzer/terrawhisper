Cola lateritia

What's the taxonomical classification of Cola lateritia?

Cola lateritia belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic descent through the order Malvales, it is placed within the family Malvaceae. Ultimately, this plant is identified by the genus Cola and is specifically designated as the species lateritia.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Cola lateritia has a woody structure characterized by upright stems, elliptical to oblong leaves with entire or slightly wavy margins, and bright red, ovoid fruits. These plants are known for their distinctive reddish-colored seeds which are housed within a fleshy capsule. What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, known as the African kola nut, has been used for centuries across West Africa as a vital symbol of hospitality, social bonding, and ritualistic offering in traditional ceremonies.

In many ethnic groups, the nut serves as a primary medium for negotiating marriages, settling communal disputes, and welcoming honored guests. Its stimulating caffeine and theobromine content led to its widespread use as a natural appetite suppressant and energy booster among travelers and laborers. Historically, it also played a central role in spiritual practices, where it was presented to ancestors or deities to facilitate communication with the divine.
