Gymnema, scientifically known as Gymnema sylvestre, is a powerful medicinal shrub native to India and Africa. Renowned in Ayurveda as "Gurmar," or "sugar destroyer," it is widely studied for its ability to reduce sugar absorption and curb sweet cravings, offering significant potential for managing blood glucose levels naturally.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution across the West Tropical Africa region. It is documented to grow within the borders of Benin. Furthermore, its presence is noted in the territories of Burkina and Ghana. The species can also be found in Guinea-Bissau. Finally, the geographical range extends into the country of Guinea.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Gymnema or Gymnema sylvestre, utilizes its leaf as the primary part used in traditional medicinal practices. The foliage contains unique active compounds, such as gymnemic acids, which are known for their ability to suppress the taste of sweetness and help regulate blood sugar levels. Because the therapeutic properties are concentrated within the green tissue, the leaves are typically harvested, dried, and processed into powders, teas, or extracts for health purposes.
