Brunfelsia uniflora

Brunfelsia uniflora, commonly known as yesterday-today-and-tomorrow, is a captivating ornamental shrub prized for its color-changing flowers. Beyond its beauty, this plant holds significant historical importance in traditional medicine. However, caution is essential, as its potent alkaloids can be toxic if mishandled or ingested improperly during medicinal use.

  • Scientific name: Brunfelsia uniflora
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Solanales and the family Solanaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Brunfelsia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Brunfelsia uniflora, is referred to by various names across different languages and cultures. In Portuguese, it is commonly called Manacá-de-jardim, while in Esperanto, it is known as Manakao. In Swedish, the name used is Manacabrunfelsia, whereas in Vietnamese, it is called Nhài hai màu. Additionally, in Chinese, the plant is referred to as 變色茉莉.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across several key regions in Africa and the Indian Ocean. Within East Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda. Its presence also extends into the Western Indian Ocean islands. Specifically, it is documented in the territory of Mauritius. Furthermore, this medicinal species is located on the island of Réunion.
