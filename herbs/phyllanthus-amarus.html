, scientifically known as Phyllanthus amarus, is a small, herbaceous plant widely recognized in traditional Ayurvedic medicine. Often called "Stonebreaker," it is celebrated for its potent hepatoprotective properties. This medicinal herb is primarily utilized to support liver health, assist in kidney stone management, and provide powerful antioxidant benefits.

Distribution

This plant, Phyllanthus amarus, exhibits a specific presence across several regions within West Tropical Africa. In the coastal and inland areas of Benin, it can be found growing naturally. The species is also documented throughout the territories of Burkina and Gambia. Furthermore, its distribution extends into the nation of Ghana. Following pollination, the plant develops fruit in the form of capsules, which are dehiscent in nature. The seeds contained within these fruits are equally tiny, with lengths ranging from 0.9 mm to 1 mm and widths between 0.7 mm and 0.8 mm. The fruiting cycle is highly flexible, with the fruiting period starting in March and potentially occurring throughout various months of the year, including January, February, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November, and December. Dispersal of the seeds is primarily zoochorous, meaning the plant relies on animals to facilitate the movement of its offspring.
