, scientifically known as Phyllanthus amarus, is a small, herbaceous plant widely recognized in traditional Ayurvedic medicine. Often called "Stonebreaker," it is celebrated for its potent hepatoprotective properties. This medicinal herb is primarily utilized to support liver health, assist in kidney stone management, and provide powerful antioxidant benefits.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Malpighiales and the family Phyllanthaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is completed by its placement in the genus Phyllanthus.

Distribution

This plant, Phyllanthus amarus, exhibits a specific presence across several regions within West Tropical Africa. In the coastal and inland areas of Benin, it can be found growing naturally. The species is also documented throughout the territories of Burkina and Gambia. Furthermore, its distribution extends into the nation of Ghana. Finally, this medicinal plant is also known to inhabit the landscape of Guinea-Bissau.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Phyllanthus amarus, is highly valued in traditional medicine, where almost every part can be utilized for therapeutic purposes. The whole plant is frequently employed in decoctions to treat various ailments, including liver disorders and jaundice. Specifically, the small, green leaves are often dried or crushed for medicinal use, and even a single leaf can be studied for its specific chemical properties in pharmacological research. Additionally, the stem is incorporated into herbal formulations, while the root is also used to prepare extracts that help manage inflammation and digestive issues.
