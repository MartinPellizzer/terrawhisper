Tanacetum vulgare

Tansy

Tanacetum vulgare, commonly known as Tansy, is a perennial herb valued for its aromatic foliage and bright yellow button-like flowers. Historically used in traditional medicine to treat digestive issues and parasitic infections, it must be handled with care due to its potent essential oils and potential toxicity if misused.

  • Scientific name: Tanacetum vulgare
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asterales, it is classified under the family Asteraceae. Its specific taxonomic designation is within the genus Tanacetum.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Tansy, is a perennial herb characterized by its distinct morphological features used in traditional medicine. The entire herb is often utilized, though specific therapeutic properties are concentrated in different structures. The leaves are deeply divided and aromatic, serving as a primary source of its bioactive compounds. At the flowering stage, the plant produces bright, button-like inflorescences that sit atop sturdy stems. While the roots can be processed for certain applications, much of the plant's medicinal potency is derived from its essential oil, which is extracted from the foliage and flowers to provide its characteristic pungent scent and pharmacological effects.
