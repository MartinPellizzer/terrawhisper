Gentiana purdomii

What's the taxonomical classification of Gentiana purdomii?

Gentiana purdomii belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the taxonomic hierarchy further, it resides in the order Gentianales and the family Gentianaecae. Finally, the plant is identified by its specific genus, Gentiana, and its unique species designation, purdomii.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Gentiana purdomii has upright, perennial stems that typically reach heights of 5 to 15 centimeters and bear opposite, lanceolate leaves with entire margins. The foliage is characterized by a deep green color and a somewhat fleshy texture. Its flowers are funnel-shaped and possess five distinct, fused petals that exhibit a striking pale blue to violet hue. Small, subtle veins often trace the surface of the corolla, adding fine detail to the floral structure. The plant's reproductive organs are centered within the blossom, supported by a base that integrates seamlessly with the stem.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is endemic to the high-altitude regions of the southern Andes within the central Peruvian highlands. It grows specifically in the alpine tundra zones of the Cordillera Vilcanota and surrounding mountain ranges. The species is typically restricted to moist, rocky crevices and grassy slopes found at elevations exceeding 4,000 meters. Its range is quite narrow, making it a localized specialist of these specific montane ecosystems. Due to this limited distribution, the plant is highly dependent on the unique climatic conditions of the high Andes.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires a specialized cultivation environment consisting of acidic, well-drained, humus-rich soil and constant moisture to thrive in temperate alpine conditions.

Growers must provide dappled sunlight or partial shade to prevent the delicate foliage from scorching under direct exposure. Maintaining a cool root zone through regular watering and organic mulching is essential for its long-term survival. Because it is a slow-growing species, patience is necessary as the plant establishes itself in its new habitat.
