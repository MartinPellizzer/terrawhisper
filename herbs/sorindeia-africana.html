Sorindeia africana

Sorindeia africana, commonly known as the African sandbush, is a resilient shrub native to various African landscapes. Renowned in traditional medicine, its leaves and bark are frequently utilized to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to digestive issues, showcasing the profound ethnobotanical importance of this versatile medicinal plant.

  • Scientific name: Sorindeia africana
  • Family: Anacardiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is placed in the order Sapindales and the family Anacardiaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement in the genus Sorindeia.

Common Names

This plant, Sorindeia africana, is known by a variety of different common names depending on the region and local language. In English, it is frequently referred to as the African nettle bush, while in isiZulu, it is known as umhlonyane. Moving through different linguistic landscapes, it is called ukhanyezela in isiXhosa and mnyatla in Sesotho. Additionally, the plant is recognized by the name saba in Shona.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found growing within the borders of Nigeria. Moving into West-Central Tropical Africa, its range extends into the Central African Republic. The species is also documented throughout Cameroon and the Congo. Finally, its geographical presence is confirmed in the nation of Gabon.
