Visnaga daucoides · Accepted scientific name

Khella, scientifically known as Visnaga daucoides, is a medicinal plant native to the Himalayas. Renowned in Ayurvedic medicine, it contains bioactive alkaloids like visnagin. Traditionally used to treat respiratory ailments and promote uterine contractions, modern research explores its potential cardiovascular benefits and its efficacy in regulating various physiological functions.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Apiales, it is further categorized into the family Apiaceae. Its specific taxonomic designation is within the genus Visnaga, identified by the species name daucoides.

Plant Parts

This plant, Visnaga daucoides, is a member of the Apiaceae family characterized by its distinctive flowering structure and reproductive organs. The plant produces delicate umbels, which are clusters of flowers arranged in a way that resembles the ribs of an umbrella. Once pollination occurs, the plant develops characteristic fruits that contain the essential medicinal components. Within these structures, the individual seed serves as the unit of propagation, and the collective mass of seeds is often harvested for therapeutic use. In traditional medicine, the entire harvested material or specific dried components are often processed into a fine powdered plants form to facilitate easier administration and extraction of its active compounds.
