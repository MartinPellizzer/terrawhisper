Bermuda Buttercup, scientifically known as Oxalis pes-caprae, is a perennial herb widely recognized for its bright yellow blooms. While often viewed as a weed, it holds significant ethnobotanical value. Historically, various cultures have utilized its leaves and tubers in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from inflammation to digestive issues.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Oxalis pes-caprae involves both sexual and asexual mechanisms, ensuring its ability to colonize diverse environments. Sexually, the plant produces bisexual flowers characterized by a bright yellow color, which are arranged in an umbel inflorescence. These flowers typically range in length from 1.3 cm to 2.6 cm. The pollination process is biotic, primarily driven by insects, and notably, self-fertilization is absent in this species. The flowering period generally begins around March and concludes in April, though it can vary throughout the year. Following successful pollination, the plant develops dehiscent fruit in the form of a capsule. These capsules are relatively small, with lengths ranging between 0.55 cm and 0.8 cm, and they contain a significant number of seeds, typically between 11 and 100 per fruit. Each seed has a mean mass of approximately 2e-04 g. Dispersal is achieved through an unspecialized syndrome as well as myrmecocory (dispersal by ants), which aids in the distribution of the species. Complementing this sexual cycle, asexual reproduction is also present, providing a robust secondary method for propagation.
