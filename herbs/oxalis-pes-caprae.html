What's the taxonomical classification of Oxalis pes-caprae?

Oxalis pes-caprae belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. It falls under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, following the evolutionary lineage of the order Oxalidales. At the family level, it is a member of the Oxalidaceae, which contains various species characterized by their distinctive leaf structures. Within this family, the plant is categorized under the genus Oxalis, and its specific biological identity is defined by the species pes-caprae.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Oxalis pes-caprae has a perennial growth habit characterized by underground bulbils, trifoliate leaves with obcordate leaflets, and bright yellow actinomorphic flowers. The leaves consist of three heart-shaped leaflets that exhibit nyctinastic movements by closing at night. Its flowering stems emerge from the soil to support small, five-petaled blossoms that typically appear in clusters. The plant spreads aggressively through the production of small, dark bulbils located at the base of the stem or near the roots. These morphological features allow the species to thrive in various disturbed environments.

The study evaluated both aqueous and methanolic extracts of these flowers to assess antioxidant, antiglycation, and enzyme inhibitory activities. What are the pharmacological activities of Oxalis pes-caprae?

This plant has demonstrated significant antiabetic, antioxidant, hypoglycemic, alpha-amylase inhibitory, and alpha-glucosidase inhibitory activities. Its antioxidant properties help neutralize free radicals and reduce oxidative stress within biological systems. The plant also exhibits potent hypoglycemic effects that assist in lowering elevated blood glucose levels. Specifically, it regulates glucose metabolism through its ability to inhibit alpha-amylase and alpha-glucosidase enzymes. These combined pharmacological actions suggest that Oxalis pes-caprae holds great potential for managing metabolic disorders like diabetes.

According to a study published by "Biomedicine & pharmacotherapy = Biomedecine & pharmacotherapie", Oxalis pes-caprae flowers extract demonstrates antioxidant, enzyme inhibitory, and antiglycation activities. The methanolic extract was found to be the richest in phenolic compounds, which relates to its remarkable biological effects. Oral administration of two doses, 150 mg/kg and 250 mg/kg daily for 3 weeks, resulted in a hypoglycemic effect compared to the reference drug glibenclamide at 10 mg/kg. These in-vitro and in-vivo studies confirm that Oxalis pes-caprae effectively exhibits hypoglycemic and antidiabetic effects against hyperglycemia and oxidative damage.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause oxalate poisoning in humans and livestock due to the high concentration of oxalic acid contained within its tissues. When ingested in significant quantities, these oxalates bind to calcium in the bloodstream and form insoluble calcium oxalate crystals. These crystals can accumulate in the kidneys, potentially leading to severe renal failure or permanent kidney damage. Symptoms of toxicity often include gastrointestinal distress, such as vomiting and abdominal pain, alongside neurological issues. Because of these serious health risks, the plant should be strictly avoided by both pets and people.

According to a study published by "Archives of toxicology. Supplement. = Archiv fur Toxikologie. Supplement", Oxalis pes-caprae, also known as Bermuda buttercup, causes large-scale oxalate poisoning in sheep. This plant is avidly eaten by children.
