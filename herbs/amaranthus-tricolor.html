Physiology

The physiology of Amaranthus tricolor is characterized by a highly efficient carbon assimilation process, as it utilizes the C4 photosynthetic pathway. This specialized metabolic mechanism allows the plant to concentrate carbon dioxide around the enzyme Rubisco, significantly reducing photorespiration and enabling superior water-use efficiency and rapid biomass accumulation, especially in high-temperature and high-light environments. Furthermore, regarding its nutrient acquisition strategies, Amaranthus tricolor is not a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms, meaning it relies entirely on the uptake of nitrates or ammonium present in the soil substrate to support its growth and protein synthesis.

The reproduction of Amaranthus tricolor is characterized by an abiotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on wind as the primary mechanism for pollen transfer.
