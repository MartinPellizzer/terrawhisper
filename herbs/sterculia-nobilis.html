Sterculia nobilis

What's the taxonomical classification of Sterculia nobilis?

Sterculia nobilis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is further categorized into the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malvales. The plant is a member of the family Malvaceae, falling under the genus Sterculia, and is specifically identified by its species name, nobilis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Sterculia nobilis has a large, spreading canopy supported by a thick, woody trunk with greyish-brown bark and trifoliate leaves consisting of three large, leathery, oval-shaped leaflets. The tree often reaches significant heights and develops a dense crown that provides extensive shade. Its flowers are typically small, yellowish, or greenish, and they emerge from the axils of the leaves. These blossoms eventually develop into woody, capsule-like fruits that split open to reveal bright red or orange seeds. The overall structure is robust, reflecting its status as a long-lived forest species.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical regions of Southeast Asia, specifically spanning across parts of India, Myanmar, Thailand, Malaysia, and Indonesia. It thrives primarily in the moist, lowland forests and seasonal monsoon climates characteristic of these equatorial territories. Within these habitats, the species is often found in well-drained soils ranging from riverbanks to hilly forest edges. Its range is largely dictated by the availability of consistent rainfall and warm temperatures necessary for its growth. Because it prefers tropical environments, it is rarely found outside of these specific humid latitudes.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-draining, fertile soil and full sunlight to thrive in warm, tropical to subtropical climates.

Growers should provide consistent moisture during the establishment phase, though the mature tree is relatively drought-tolerant once its root system is deep. Because it grows into a large, spreading tree, it needs significant space to prevent its canopy from interfering with nearby structures. Regular mulching helps retain soil moisture and suppresses competing weeds around the base of the trunk.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", the seed of Sterculia nobilis is identified as the plant called luohuangzi in ancient times. This research concludes that the fruit referred to as luohuangzi should be the seed of Sterculia nobilis rather than Tamarindus indica. The study suggests that luohuangzi should no longer be used as another name for T. indica to eliminate confusion about the name and material object.
