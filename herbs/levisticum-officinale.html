Levisticum officinale

Lovage

Levisticum officinale, commonly known as lovage, is a perennial herb valued for its intense, celery-like aroma. Beyond its culinary uses, this medicinal plant has a rich history in traditional herbalism. It is primarily utilized to support digestive health, act as a diuretic, and provide soothing relief for various ailments.

  • Scientific name: Levisticum officinale
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Apiales, falling into the family Apiaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Levisticum.

Plant Parts

This plant, Levisticum officinale, commonly known as lovage, is a versatile medicinal herb where various components offer therapeutic benefits. The thick, aromatic roots are frequently utilized in traditional medicine for their digestive and diuretic properties. The large, serrated green leaves are not only edible but also contain bioactive compounds used to support kidney function. Additionally, the plant produces small, ribbed fruits that can be harvested for their concentrated potency. Most notably, the essential oil extracted from the plant is highly valued for its intense aroma and its ability to serve as a powerful antimicrobial and carminative agent in herbal preparations.
