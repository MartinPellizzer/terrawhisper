Malus asiatica

What's the taxonomical classification of Malus asiatica?

Malus asiatica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the hierarchical structure of botanical classification, it falls under the order Rosales and the family Rosaceae. Ultimately, the plant is identified by its genus, Malus, and its specific species designation, asiatica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Malus asiatica has a deciduous, small-to-medium sized tree characterized by oval, finely serrated leaves and clusters of pink to white five-petaled flowers. The foliage typically features dark green upper surfaces and lighter undersides, often becoming reddish in autumn. Its woody stems possess a brownish-gray bark that may become fissured with age. The plant produces small, globose pomes that transition from green to a yellowish or reddish hue as they ripen. These fruits are supported by persistent calyxes and are often smaller than those found in many commercial apple varieties.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the mountainous regions of central and western China, specifically within provinces such as Sichuan, Yunnan, and Gansu. It thrives in high-altitude habitats where cool temperatures and well-drained soils are prevalent. These wild populations are often found in temperate forests and scrublands situated along steep slopes. Due to its unique ornamental qualities, the species has been widely introduced to temperate gardens across Europe and North America. Consequently, its range now extends far beyond its original East Asian territories.
