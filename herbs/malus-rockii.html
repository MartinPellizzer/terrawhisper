Malus rockii

What's the taxonomical classification of Malus rockii?

Malus rockii belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it falls under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the taxonomic hierarchy further, it is placed in the order Rosales and the family Rosaceae. Ultimately, the plant is identified by its genus, Malus, and its specific species designation, rockii.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Malus rockii has a deciduous small tree or shrub habit characterized by spreading branches, serrated ovate leaves, and pinkish-white five-petaled flowers that emerge in spring. The foliage is typically dark green with finely toothed margins and a slightly lustrous texture. Its blossoms are often densely packed on short spurs, creating a profuse floral display during the blooming season. Following pollination, the plant produces small, globose pomes that transition from green to a bright red or yellowish-red color when ripe. These fruits are often used as ornamental features in temperate landscapes due to their striking appearance.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the mountainous regions of central and western China, specifically within the provinces of Gansu, Hubei, Shaanxi, Sichuan, and Yunnan. It typically thrives in high-altitude environments found along rocky slopes and forest edges. These habitats provide the specific drainage and cool temperatures necessary for its growth. The species is often found at elevations ranging from 1,500 to 3,000 meters above sea level. Because of these specific environmental requirements, its natural range remains relatively restricted to these temperate mountain zones.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant uses its leaves for medicinal purposes to treat various ailments. These leaves are often collected and processed to create infusions or decoctions. They are primarily utilized in traditional practices to alleviate symptoms of fever and inflammation. Some herbalists also employ the foliage to support respiratory health through topical or oral applications. Consistent use of these specific parts helps manage systemic wellness in traditional medicine.

According to a study published by "Planta medica", phloridzin highly accumulates in Malus rockii Rehder. The dry leaves contain the highest percentage of phloridzin among wild plants at 15.54%. This compound exhibited significant hypolipidemic activity in golden Syrian hamsters at doses of 50 mg/kg and 200 mg/kg. Phloridzin also showed moderate antimalarial activity with an inhibition ratio of 31.3 ± 10.9% at a dose of 25 mg/kg/day. Furthermore, it demonstrated moderate analgesic activity with 28.0% inhibition at a dose of 50 mg/kg.
