Annona emarginata

Annona emarginata, commonly known as the custard apple or custard apple shrub, is a medicinal plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, it is frequently utilized in traditional medicine to treat skin ailments, digestive issues, and inflammation, offering significant pharmacological potential for modern healthcare.

  • Scientific name: Annona emarginata
  • Family: Annonaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, specifically classified within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Following the order Magnoliales, it is a member of the family Annonaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Annona.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the diverse landscapes of Western South America. Within this region, its presence is specifically documented in both Bolivia and Peru. In addition to these western territories, the species extends significantly into the vast country of Brazil. It can be found inhabiting the West-Central, Northeast, and Southeast regions of Brazil. Consequently, its range spans a wide variety of ecological zones across the South American continent.
