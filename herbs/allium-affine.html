Allium affine

Allium affine, commonly known as a wild relative of garlic, is a versatile medicinal plant found in diverse ecological niches. Renowned for its potent sulfur compounds, it possesses significant antimicrobial and antioxidant properties. This species is traditionally utilized in various cultures to support cardiovascular health and bolster immune functions.

  • Scientific name: Allium affine
  • Family: Amaryllidaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asparagales, it is classified under the family Amaryllidaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Allium.

Distribution

This plant is distributed across several distinct regions within the Caucasus and Western Asia. In the Caucasus, its range extends through the North Caucasus and into the Transcaucasus. Moving into Western Asia, it can be found growing within the borders of Iran. The species also inhabits parts of Iraq, further expanding its presence in the region. Finally, its geographical reach includes the areas of Lebanon and Syria.
