Ophioglossum petiolatum

Adder's tongue

Ophioglossum petiolatum, commonly known as the tongue fern, is a significant medicinal plant found in tropical regions. Valued in traditional medicine, it is frequently used to treat various ailments, including respiratory issues and inflammatory conditions. Its unique chemical properties make it a subject of growing interest in pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Ophioglossum petiolatum
  • Family: Ophioglossaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized into the subclass Ophioglossidae and the order Ophioglossales. Finally, it is a member of the family Ophioglossaceae within the genus Ophioglossum.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ophioglossum petiolatum, is referred to by various common names depending on the language and region. In Chinese, it is known by several names including 锐头瓶尔小草, 柄叶瓶尔小草, and 鋭頭瓶爾小草 (specifically in mainland China as 鋭頭瓶爾小草), while in Taiwan, it is called 銳頭瓶爾小草. Additionally, in Japanese, the species is commonly known as コヒロハハナヤスリ.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical range across several key regions of East Asia. In China, its presence is documented throughout the South-Central and Southeast areas. It also inhabits the island of Hainan and the high-altitude regions of Tibet. Moving further east, the species can be found within the borders of Japan. Collectively, these locations highlight its diverse distribution throughout the eastern part of the continent.
