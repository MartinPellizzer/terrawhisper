Solanum lycocarpum

Wolf apple

Solanum lycocarpum, commonly known as wolf apple or lódão, is a medicinal shrub native to the Brazilian Cerrado. Renowned for its antioxidant properties, this plant is widely used in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from digestive issues to hypertension, offering significant therapeutic potential in modern pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Solanum lycocarpum
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It further falls under the subclass Magnoliidae in the order Solanales, specifically within the family Solanaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Solanum.

Common Names

This plant, Solanum lycocarpum, is known by various names depending on the region and language. In Portuguese, it is commonly referred to as either Guarambá or Fruta-de-lobo, while in Japanese, it is called ウルフアップル. In Chinese, the plant is known as 狼果, and in Esperanto, it is identified as Lupofrukto.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across several distinct regions of Brazil. It can be found throughout the West-Central, Northeast, and Southeast portions of the country. Additionally, its presence extends into the southern reaches of Brazil. Beyond Brazilian borders, the species is also documented in Paraguay within Southern South America. Together, these locations highlight a wide range of habitats for the species across the continent.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as lobeira or wolf apple, is highly valued for its medicinal properties derived from various components. The most significant part used in traditional medicine is the fruit, which undergoes distinct changes in composition as it matures. The unripe fruits are often used in specific preparations, while the ripe fruits are widely consumed or processed due to their nutritional and therapeutic potential. Ultimately, whether utilizing the unripe fruits or the ripe fruits, the entire fruit serves as a vital resource in folk remedies.
