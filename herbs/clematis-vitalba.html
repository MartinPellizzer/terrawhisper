Clematis vitalba

Clematis vitalba · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Extensive (180 studies) · ★★★★☆

Clematis vitalba, scientifically known as Clematis vitalba, is a vigorous, climbing deciduous shrub commonly found across Europe and Asia. Often called Old Man's Beard, this plant is characterized by its feathery seed heads. While historically used in folk medicine, it requires careful handling due to its potential toxicity.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Clematis vitalba is primarily driven by sexual processes, as asexual reproduction is notably absent. The flowering period typically begins in June and concludes in July. During this time, the plant utilizes a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects such as bees to facilitate fertilization; notably, self-fertilization is absent, ensuring genetic diversity through cross-pollination. Following successful pollination, the plant produces dry, indehiscent fruits classified as achenes, which have a mean length of 0.2 cm. The resulting seeds are characterized by a consistent length of 2.3 mm and a mean volume of 2.1 mm³. The seed mass varies, with a minimum of 0.00127 g and a maximum of 0.0026 g (with a mean of approximately 0.002607 g). For dispersal, the species employs an anemochorous syndrome, meaning the seeds are dispersed by the wind.
