Terminalia tetraphylla

Terminalia tetraphylla, commonly known as the Four-leaf Terminalia, is a distinctive medicinal tree valued in traditional herbal medicine. Renowned for its unique leaf structure, this species offers diverse therapeutic properties. It is frequently utilized in various cultures to treat ailments ranging from skin conditions to digestive and inflammatory issues.

  • Scientific name: Terminalia tetraphylla
  • Family: Combretaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Myrtales and the family Combretaceae. It is specifically identified as a member of the genus Terminalia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific range of occurrence across the tropical regions of Central America and the Caribbean. Within the Central American corridor, its presence is documented in both Costa Rica and Panamá. Moving toward the Caribbean islands, the species can be found inhabiting the landscapes of Cuba. Additionally, its distribution extends to the island of Hispaniola, covering both the Dominican Republic and Haiti. Together, these locations define the primary geographical footprint of Terminalia tetraphylla.
