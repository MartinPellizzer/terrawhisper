Tectaria decurrens

What's the taxonomical classification of Tectaria decurrens?

Tectaria decurrens belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida, it is further organized under the subclass Polypodiinae and the order Polypodiales. This plant is a representative of the family Polypodiaceae, falling under the genus Tectaria, and is specifically identified by its species name, decurrens.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Tectaria decurrens has a creeping rhizomatous habit with long, slender, pinnately compound leaves that can reach lengths of up to 1.5 meters. The leaflets are typically lanceolate or oblong in shape and feature serrated or entire margins depending on the specific specimen. Small, inconspicuous flowers are produced in axillary panicles that emerge from the leaf bases. The plant often forms dense mats as the creeping stems spread across the forest floor or cling to damp substrates. Its root system is shallow but extensive, allowing it to colonize moist, shaded environments effectively.
