Pulsatilla cernua

Pulsatilla cerulea, commonly known as pasqueflower, is a resilient perennial celebrated in traditional herbalism. Characterized by its striking, bell-shaped blooms, this botanical treasure is prized for its calming properties. Often used to soothe nervous tension and skin irritations, it remains a significant subject in both ethnobotany and pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Pulsatilla cernua
  • Family: Ranunculaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Ranunculales and the family Ranunculaceae. Ultimately, it is identified by the genus Pulsatilla.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several key regions in East Asia. Within the Russian Far East, its presence is documented in the Amur, Khabarovsk, and Primorye territories. Moving into China, the species can be found growing in the Inner Mongolia region. Furthermore, its natural range extends into the Manchuria area of China.
