This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Ranunculales and the family Ranunculaceae. Ultimately, it is identified by the genus Pulsatilla.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several key regions in East Asia. Within the Russian Far East, its presence is documented in the Amur, Khabarovsk, and Primorye territories. Moving into China, the species can be found growing in the Inner Mongolia region. Furthermore, its natural range extends into the Manchuria area of China. Together, these locations define the specific geographical footprint of Pulsatilla cernea.

Plant Parts

This plant, Pulsatilla chinensis (often associated with the Pulsatilla genus including species like Pulsatilla tenuifolia), utilizes its underground roots as a primary source of medicinal value. The roots are the most significant part used in traditional pharmacopeia, as they contain the active compounds required for therapeutic effects. When preparing remedies, the dried root is typically harvested and processed to extract its potent properties.
