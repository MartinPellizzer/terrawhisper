Poinciana

Caesalpinia pulcherrima · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (355 studies) · ★★★★★

Poinciana, scientifically known as Caesalpinia pulcherrimia, is a vibrant ornamental shrub celebrated for its fiery red blooms. Beyond its aesthetic appeal, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Various cultures utilize its leaves and flowers in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from inflammation to digestive issues and skin infections.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and order Fabales, it is specifically situated in the Fabaceae family. Its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Caesalpinia, within which the species Caesalpinia pulcherrimna is categorized.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across several key regions. In Northern Africa, it can be found growing within the borders of Egypt. Its range extends to the Macaronesian islands, specifically including Cape Verde. Furthermore, the species is well-established throughout West Tropical Africa. This widespread presence is evidenced by its occurrence in Gambia, Ghana, and the Ivory Coast.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Pride of Barbados, possesses various morphological features that contribute to its medicinal profile. The leaves are bipinnate and delicate, providing a lush canopy for the vibrant blooms, while the woody stems offer a sturdy structural framework for the shrub. The reproductive cycle is marked by the development of elongated, flattened pods of Caesalpiniapulcherrimma, which eventually split open to release the dark, hard seeds contained within. Beneath the soil, the extensive roots serve to anchor the plant and facilitate the absorption of nutrients essential for its growth and therapeutic potential.
