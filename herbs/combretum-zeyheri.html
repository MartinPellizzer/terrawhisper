Large-fruited bushwillow, scientifically known as Combretum zeyheri, is a resilient woody shrub native to Southern Africa. Renowned for its medicinal properties, various parts of this plant are traditionally utilized to treat ailments such as inflammation, digestive issues, and skin infections, offering significant ethnobotanical value within indigenous healing practices.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several distinct regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found growing within the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Its presence extends into East Tropical Africa, specifically within the territories of Kenya and Tanzania. Moving toward the southern part of the continent, the species is also located in South Tropical Africa. This southern distribution encompasses the countries of both Angola and Malawi.
