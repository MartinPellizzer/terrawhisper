Ardisia japonica

Ardisia japonica, commonly known as Japanese coralberry, is a charming evergreen perennial native to East Asia. Renowned for its glossy foliage and vibrant red berries, this plant is valued in traditional medicine for its diverse bioactive compounds, which are studied for their potential anti-inflammatory and antioxidant therapeutic properties.

  • Scientific name: Ardisia japonica
  • Family: Primulaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Ericales, falling specifically into the family Primulaceae. Finally, its taxonomic designation is completed within the genus Ardisia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ardisia japonica, is referred to by several different common names depending on the region and language. In Japan, it is called ヤブコウジ (ja), while in Korea, it is known as 자금우 (ko). The plant is also commonly called 紫金牛 (zh) in mainland China and 紫金牛 (zh-tw) in Taiwan. In Estonia, its common name is Jaapani ardiisia (et).

Distribution

This plant is native to several distinct regions across Eastern Asia. Within China, its presence is recorded in the South-Central, North-Central, and Southeast territories. Moving further east, the species is also found naturally occurring in Japan. Additionally, its range extends into the Korean peninsula. These diverse locations highlight the broad geographical distribution of Ardisia japonica throughout the region.
