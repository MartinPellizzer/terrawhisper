Tree Cotton, scientifically known as Bombax ceiaba, is a majestic deciduous tree renowned for its vibrant red flowers and medicinal versatility. Widely used in traditional Ayurveda, various parts—including bark, flowers, and roots—possess potent anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and healing properties, making it a vital resource in natural pharmacology.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Bombax ceiba is characterized by a distinct seasonal flowering period that typically begins in February and concludes in March. The pollination process is strictly biotic, specifically employing a bat pollination syndrome to ensure successful fertilization, as self-fertilization is notably absent in this species. Following pollination, the plant produces fleshy fruits that eventually yield seeds with highly specific physical dimensions; the seeds maintain a consistent volume of 17 mm³ and exhibit a mean mass of approximately 0.04729 g, though individual seed mass can range from a minimum of 0.00364 g to a maximum of 0.1289 g. Once mature, the seeds are dispersed via an anemochorous syndrome, relying on wind currents to distribute them throughout the environment.
