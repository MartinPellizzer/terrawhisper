Tree Cotton, scientifically known as Bombax ceiaba, is a majestic deciduous tree renowned for its vibrant red flowers and medicinal versatility. Widely used in traditional Ayurveda, various parts—including bark, flowers, and roots—possess potent anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and healing properties, making it a vital resource in natural pharmacology.

This plant, known scientifically as Bombax ceiba, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across several key regions in Asia. In China, it can be found throughout the South-Central and Southeast territories. Its distribution also extends to the tropical island of Hainan within Chinese borders. Moving further east, the species is naturally occurring in Taiwan. Finally, its range reaches into the Indian Subcontinent, specifically within the Assam region.

Plant Parts

This plant, Bombax ceiba, possesses various anatomical structures that contribute to its medicinal properties. The large, palmate leaves are characteristic of its foliage, while the sturdy stem provides the structural support necessary for its towering height. The stem barks are frequently utilized in traditional medicine due to their specific chemical constituents. During the flowering stage, the plant produces striking blossoms protected by distinct sepals, where each individual sepal plays a crucial role in shielding the developing reproductive organs of the flower.
