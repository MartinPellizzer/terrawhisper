Indian saffron

Curcuma longa · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (2683 studies) · ★★★★★

Indian saffron, scientifically known as Curcuma longa, is a perennial herb renowned for its vibrant golden rhizomes. Deeply embedded in Ayurvedic tradition, it contains curcumin, a potent bioactive compound. Celebrated for its powerful anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, this versatile plant serves as both a culinary staple and medicine.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Zingiberales, it is classified under the family Zingiberaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Curcuma, specifically the species Curcuma longa.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several tropical regions. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in Guinea-Bissau and the Ivory Coast. Its presence extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically within the Congo and the Gulf of Guinea Islands. Additionally, the species is located within the Western Indian Ocean, notably in the Comoros. These various locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in different humid, tropical environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Curcuma longa, is a perennial herb where the most therapeutically significant part is the underground rhizome. While these structures are often colloquially referred to as the root, they are technically modified stems used for nutrient storage. The plant features large, vibrant green leaves that emerge from the base to facilitate photosynthesis. Although the primary medicinal value lies in the fleshy rhizomes, the entire underground system, including the fine fibrous roots, supports the plant's growth and survival in its natural habitat.
