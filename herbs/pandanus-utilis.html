Pandanus utilis

Common screwpine

Pandanus utilis, commonly known as the screw pine, is a versatile tropical plant cherished for its multifaceted uses. Beyond its striking architectural foliage, it holds significant medicinal value in traditional healing. Its leaves and fruits are utilized to treat various ailments, making it a vital resource in many cultures.

  • Scientific name: Pandanus utilis
  • Family: Pandanaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Pandanales and the family Pandanaceae, it is classified under the genus Pandanus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several remote island territories. In the Middle Atlantic Ocean, its presence can be documented on the island of Ascension. The species is also widely spread throughout the Western Indian Ocean. Specifically, it is found inhabiting the islands of Comoros, Mauritius, and Réunion. Additionally, the plant extends its range to the island of Rodrigues within the same oceanic region.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Pandanus, utilizes its various anatomical structures for both survival and medicinal application. The roots are typically stilt-like or prop roots that emerge from the lower stem to provide essential structural stability in sandy or swampy soils. The leaves are long, narrow, and arranged in a spiral pattern around the stem, often featuring sharp, serrated edges that can be used in traditional practices. Both the roots and leaves contain various bioactive compounds that are frequently studied for their therapeutic potential in herbal medicine.
