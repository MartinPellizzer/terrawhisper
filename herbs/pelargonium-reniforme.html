Pelargonium reniforme

Kidney-leaved pelargoni

Pelargonium reniforme, commonly known as the Kidney-leaved Geranium, is a unique medicinal plant native to the rocky cliffs of South Africa. Renowned for its distinctive kidney-shaped foliage, this resilient species is traditionally utilized in herbal remedies to support digestive health and possess potent antioxidant properties for holistic wellness.

  • Scientific name: Pelargonium reniforme
  • Family: Geraniaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is positioned in the order Geraniales and the family Geraniaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Pelargonium.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Pelargonium reniforme, is referred to by various common names depending on the language. In English, it is called the kidney-leaved pelaergoni, while in Afrikaans, it is known as both Rabassam and Rooirabas.

Distribution

This plant is characterized by a very specific and localized geographical range. It is native to the southern portion of the African continent. Within this region, its presence is strictly confined to the Cape Provinces. This narrow distribution suggests that it thrives only under the unique climatic conditions found there. Consequently, its habitat remains limited to these particular coastal and inland areas of South Africa.

Plant Parts

This plant, Pelargonium reniforme, is primarily valued for its medicinal properties, which are derived from its aerial parts. These above-ground structures include the characteristic kidney-shaped leaves, stems, and flowers, all of which contain the essential oils and bioactive compounds utilized in herbal remedies. By harvesting the aerial parts, practitioners can access the plant's therapeutic potency while allowing the root system to remain intact for continued growth.
