Nervilia fordii

Nervilia fordii, commonly known as the Jewel Orchid, is a captivating terrestrial plant prized for its striking, vein-patterned foliage. Native to tropical regions, this geophyte thrives in shaded, humid environments. Beyond its ornamental appeal, it holds significant ethnobotanical interest, occasionally appearing in traditional medicinal practices across its native range.

  • Scientific name: Nervilia fordii
  • Family: Orchidaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asparagales, it is classified under the family Orchidaceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Nervilia and the species Nervilia fordii.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Nervilia fordii, is referred to by several different common names depending on the linguistic context. In Chinese, it is known by the names 毛唇芋兰, 青天葵, and 福氏芋兰. More specifically, the nomenclature varies slightly across regions, appearing as 毛唇芋兰 in mainland China (zh-cn) and as 毛唇芋蘭 in Taiwan (zh-tw).

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across several key regions in East and Southeast Asia. In China, its presence is noted specifically within the South-Central and Southeast territories. Moving into the Indo-China peninsula, the species can be found growing in the landscape of Laos. It also maintains a significant distribution throughout the various provinces of Thailand. Finally, the geographical range of this medicinal plant extends into the country of Vietnam.
