The life history of Acorus gramineus is characterized by its specialized survival strategies, functioning primarily as a geophyte and a cryptophyte. As a geophyte, the plant relies on underground storage organs, such as rhizomes, to endure unfavorable environmental conditions and facilitate regrowth during favorable seasons. Its classification as a cryptophyte indicates that its perennating buds are located below the soil surface or at the soil level, protecting them from external stressors like frost or desiccation. This life cycle allows the species to maintain a persistent presence in its habitat, utilizing its subterranean structures to navigate seasonal fluctuations while emerging to perform photosynthetic activities when conditions are optimal.
