Acorus gramineus

Japanese rush

Acorus gramineus, commonly known as the Grassy Sweet Flag, is a perennial aquatic plant cherished for its elegant, grass-like foliage. Thriving in wetlands, this versatile species is valued in traditional medicine and ornamental landscaping alike. Its aromatic properties and ability to stabilize soil make it a botanical treasure.

  • Scientific name: Acorus gramineus
  • Family: Acoraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Acorus gramineus, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Acorales and the family Acoraceae. Ultimately, it is identified by its genus, Acorus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Acorus gramineus, is referred to by various common names depending on the region and language. In Chinese contexts, it is known as 金钱菖蒲 (zh-hans), while in traditional or regional variations such as in Taiwan or Hong Kong, it is identified as 石菖蒲 (zh-hant and zh-hk). In Japan, it is called セキショウ (ja), and in Poland, it is commonly known as Tatarak trawiasty (pl).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range that extends across several distinct regions. In Northern Europe, its presence is notably recorded in Great Britain. Moving eastward, it can be found within the Russian Far East, specifically in the Primorye region. The species is also widely distributed throughout China, spanning from Inner Mongolia to the South-Central provinces. Additionally, its habitat reaches as far south as the island of Hainan.
