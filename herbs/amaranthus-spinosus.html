Spiny amaranth, scientifically known as Amaranthus spinosus, is a resilient, prickly herbaceous plant found globally in tropical regions. Renowned for its therapeutic versatility, it is widely utilized in traditional medicine to treat inflammation, digestive issues, and skin ailments. This hardy species serves as a vital botanical resource in ethnopharmacology.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Amaranthus spinosus is characterized by a sexual process through bisexual flowers, with asexual reproduction being absent. The flowering period typically begins in July and concludes in October. Pollination is primarily an abiotic process, specifically utilizing wind as the main pollination syndrome. The resulting fruits are dry and indehiscent, ranging in length from a minimum of 0.1 cm to a maximum of 0.15 cm, with a mean length of 0.11 cm. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing more than 1,000 seeds. The seeds themselves are small, with a mean length of 0.8 mm (ranging from 0.8 mm to 1 mm) and a consistent seed volume of 0.18 mm³. In terms of mass, the seeds are lightweight, with a mean mass of approximately 0.000306875 g, ranging from a minimum of 0.00015 g to a maximum of 0.0003 g. Dispersal of these seeds occurs through both hydrochorous (water) and anthropochorous (human) syndromes. Notably, self-fertilization is documented as absent in this species.
