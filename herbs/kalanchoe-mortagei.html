Physiology

The physiology of Kalanchoe mortagei is defined by its specialized adaptations for water conservation and survival in arid environments, most notably through its pronounced succulence. This plant exhibits true succulence, characterized by the development of thickened, fleshy leaves and stems that serve as primary reservoirs for water storage. To manage moisture effectively, the plant utilizes Crassulacean Acid Metabolism (CAM) photosynthesis, a physiological pathway where stomata remain closed during the heat of the day to minimize transpiration and open at night to collect carbon dioxide. This nocturnal gas exchange allows for the temporal separation of CO2 uptake and photosynthetic processing, significantly increasing water-use efficiency. The cellular structure is optimized to maintain high turgor pressure within specialized large vacuoles, allowing the plant to endure prolonged periods of drought by drawing upon its internal aqueous reserves.
