Bitter-gourd-shaped passion fruit, scientifically known as Passiflora, is a versatile climbing vine celebrated for its exotic fruit and therapeutic properties. Renowned in herbal medicine for its calming effects, it is frequently used to alleviate anxiety, promote restful sleep, and soothe the nervous system through its potent bioactive compounds.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malpighiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Passifloraceae and is identified by the genus Passiflora.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as passionflower, offers various components that are utilized for their unique properties. The leaves, often referred to as Passiflora leaf, are frequently used in herbal preparations to promote relaxation and sleep. Additionally, the fruit provides distinct edible and medicinal elements, such as the flavorful, pulpy aril that surrounds the seeds and the protective outer rinds that compose the fruit's skin.
