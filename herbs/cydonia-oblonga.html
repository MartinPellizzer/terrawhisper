What's the taxonomical classification of Cydonia oblonga?

Cydonia oblonga belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant follows the taxonomic order Rosales and is a member of the family Rosaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Cydonia and the specific species epithet oblonga.

According to a study published by "Molecules (Basel, Switzerland)", Cydonia oblonga is a medicinal plant used to treat various health complications through its bark. The methanolic extract of the bark contains a total phenolic content of 70% and a total flavonoid content of 69%. In antioxidant evaluations against DPPH, the methanolic extract showed 87.41 ± 0.54% inhibition, while purified fractions showed inhibition ranging from 48.45 ± 0.62% in F7 to 571.29 ± 0.49% in F5. At a maximum concentration of 1000 µg/mL, the α-glucosidase inhibition for the methanolic extract was 78.21 ± 0.67% and the α-amylase inhibition was 77.98 ± 0.57%. Additionally, at 1000 µg/mL, the methanolic extract demonstrated α-glucosidase inhibition of 78.21 ± 0.67% and α-amylase inhibition of 77.98 ± 0.57%.

Practitioners within this system frequently prescribe its fruit to balance bodily humors and soothe inflammation in the gastrointestinal tract. According to a study published by "Current drug delivery", Cydonia oblonga possesses anti-inflammatory, anti-ulcer, and skin-protecting properties that are primarily attributed to phenolic compounds. The total phenol concentration of the dried extract was determined to be 13.34 ± 0.90 mg gallic acid equivalents per gram. A fruit extract using 70% methanol was utilized to prepare the niosomal hydrogel for evaluating these bioactive compounds. On the 12th day of treatment, histopathological findings showed that the niosomal gel promoted angiogenesis, fibroblast maturation, collagen deposition, keratinization, and epidermal layer formation. This treatment also markedly reduced inflammation in the studied full-thickness wounds.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains a diverse range of bioactive flavonoids, specifically dihydrokaempherol, kaempferol, naringenin, and quercetin. These polyphenolic compounds are primarily concentrated within the fruit's pulp and peel. Scientists have studied these specific molecules for their potential antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties in human health. The presence of naringenin and quercetin suggests that the plant may offer significant protective benefits against oxidative stress. Together, these medicinal elements contribute to the overall therapeutic profile of the species.

According to a study published by "Antioxidants (Basel, Switzerland)", the plant Cydonia oblonga contains four major groups of antioxidants consisting of flavonoids, phenolic acids and aldehydes, catechins, and procyanidins. The study examined two specific genotypes for Cydonia oblonga, which are O-LE-14 and O-LE-21, specifically using sample BA 29. Within the screened plant roots, the flavonoid dihydrokaemphol had an average concentration of 0.43 ± 0.01 μg·g-1. The least represented p-Coumaric acid exhibited an average concentration of 0.34 ± 0.01 μg·g-1 in the plant roots. Additionally, the catechins included epigallocatechin, which had an average concentration of 0.95 ± 0.08 μg·g-1, while the procyanidin compound procyanidin a2 represented an average concentration of 40.35 ± 1.61 μg·g-1.

According to a study published by "Spectrochimica acta. These Cydonia oblonga derived macromolecules allow the Au NPs to stabilize through encapsulation or capping. The resulting Au NPs/Cydonia oblonga bio-nanocomposite demonstrates significant anti-oral cancer properties against HSC-3, HSC-2, and Ca9-22 cancer cell lines in a time and dose-dependent manner. The determined IC50 values for these cell lines were 201, 192, and 246 µg/mL respectively. Furthermore, the material shows very good antioxidant potential as measured by the DPPB radical scavenging method.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause adverse health effects such as diarrhea, dysuria, and gastroesophageal reflux disease symptoms. Individuals consuming this species may experience significant gastrointestinal distress and frequent loose stools. Some users also report painful or difficult urination during the course of consumption. Additionally, the plant can trigger acid reflux, leading to uncomfortable symptoms associated with gastroesophageal reflux disease. These physiological reactions highlight the potential risks associated with the ingestion of Cydonia oblonga.
