Silk Floss Tree, scientifically known as Ceiba speciosa, is a striking tropical tree celebrated for its sculptural trunk and vibrant, bell-shaped flowers. Beyond its ornamental beauty, this species holds significant ethnobotanical value, with various parts used in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from inflammation to digestive issues.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Ceiba speciosa is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, primarily involving insects and butterflies to facilitate the transfer of pollen. The plant exhibits a reproductive strategy where self-fertilization is absent, ensuring genetic diversity through cross-pollination. Once fertilization occurs, the plant produces dry fruits that facilitate an anemochorous dispersal syndrome, allowing seeds to be spread by the wind. The seeds themselves are relatively uniform in size, with a consistent seed length of 7.821 mm and a seed volume of 150 mm³. In terms of weight, the seed mass varies from a minimum of 0.07326 g to a maximum of 0.2091 g, with an average seed mass of 0.12709 g.
