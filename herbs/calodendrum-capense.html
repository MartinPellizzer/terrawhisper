Calodendrum capense

Cape chestnut

Calodendrum capense, commonly known as the Cape Chestnut, is a striking evergreen tree native to Southern Africa. Renowned for its vibrant orange flowers, it holds significant ethnobotanical importance. Various parts of the plant are utilized in traditional medicine to treat ailments such as skin conditions, inflammation, and digestive issues.

  • Scientific name: Calodendrum capense
  • Family: Rutaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae, the order Sapindales, and the family Rutaecae. Ultimately, it is identified by its genus, Calodendrum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across several regions of the African continent. In East Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda. Its range extends further south into the region of South Tropical Africa. Specifically, the species is documented to inhabit Malawi and Zimbabwe. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive within various East and South Tropical African ecosystems.
