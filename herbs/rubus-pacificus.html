Rubus pacificus

What's the taxonomical classification of Rubus pacificus?

Rubus pacificus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this group, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant falls under the order Rosales and is a member of the family Rosaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is completed by its genus, Rubus, and its specific species designation, pacificus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Rubus pacificus has arching, woody stems known as canes that are densely covered in sharp, recurved prickles and feature small, glandular hairs. The leaves are typically palmately compound with three to five distinct leaflets that possess serrated margins and a dark green upper surface. These leaflets are often characterized by a finely textured, pubescent underside that provides a subtle contrast to the top. Small, white to pale pink flowers emerge from the terminal ends of the branches, consisting of five delicate petals surrounding a central cluster of stamens. As the plant matures, it produces aggregate fruits that transition from green to a deep, fleshy red color.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the western coast of North America, ranging from southern Alaska through British Columbia and down into California. Within these habitats, it thrives in areas with significant annual rainfall and cool temperatures. The species is most commonly found in the understory of coniferous forests where light is filtered.
