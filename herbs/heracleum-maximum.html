Cow parsnip, scientifically known as Heracleum maximum, is a robust perennial herb native to North America. Distinguished by its large, umbellifer flowers and broad leaves, this plant holds significant historical medicinal value. However, it requires careful handling due to phototoxic sap that can cause severe skin irritation and burns.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical range concentrated within the regions of the Russian Far East and Eastern Asia. It can be found growing across the Kamchatka Peninsula and throughout the Khabarovsk region. Furthermore, its distribution extends to the island territories of Sakhalin and the Kuril Islands. Moving into Eastern Asia, the species is also well-established in Japan. These locations highlight the plant's preference for the temperate climates of the North Pacific rim.
