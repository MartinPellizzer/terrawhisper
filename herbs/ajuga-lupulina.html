Ajuga lupulina

Ajuga lupulina, commonly known as black bugle, is a perennial herb native to wetlands and moist habitats across Eurasia. Characterized by its whorls of small, tubular flowers, this plant plays a vital role in supporting pollinators. Historically, it has been utilized in traditional medicine for its diverse therapeutic properties.

  • Scientific name: Ajuga lupulina
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales and family Lamiaceae, it is classified under the genus Ajuga.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions in Asia. In China, it can be found throughout the South-Central and North-Central territories. Its presence also extends to the high-altitude areas of Qinghai and Tibet. Furthermore, the species is documented within the Indian Subcontinent, specifically in Assam. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to inhabit varied environmental landscapes.
