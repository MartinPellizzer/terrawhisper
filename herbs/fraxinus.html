Fraxinus

Ashes

Fraxinus, commonly known as ash, is a genus of deciduous trees valued in traditional medicine for its diverse therapeutic properties. Often utilized in herbal remedies, various parts of the plant, including bark and leaves, are studied for their anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and analgesic effects, supporting holistic wellness and botanical health.

  • Scientific name: Fraxinus
  • Family: Oleaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Lamiales. Finally, it resides in the family Oleaceae under the genus Fraxinus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Fraxinus, is referred to by various names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In simplified Chinese, it is commonly identified as 梣属 (zh-hans) or 梣屬 (zh-cn), while in traditional Chinese contexts, it is known as 梣樹 (zh-hant) and 梣樹 (zh-hk). Additionally, in Singapore, the common name used is 梣树 (zh-sg).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific presence across several regions within Northern Europe. It can be found growing naturally in Denmark, spanning much of the Danish landscape. The species is also well-distributed throughout Great Britain and Ireland. Furthermore, its range extends into the northernmost territories of Norway. Finally, the plant is a documented part of the flora in Sweden.

The foliage is characterized by compound leaves, where each individual leaflet features a prominent midrib that provides structural support and aids in nutrient transport.
