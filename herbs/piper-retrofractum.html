Javanese long pepper

Piper retrofractum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (219 studies) · ★★★★★

Javanese long pepper, scientifically known as Piper retrofractum, is a perennial climbing vine native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its pungent, aromatic spikes, this medicinal plant has long been utilized in traditional Ayurvedic and Jamu practices to enhance digestion, stimulate circulation, and provide potent anti-inflammatory and antioxidant therapeutic benefits.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Piperales, it is classified under the family Piperaceae. Ultimately, it is categorized within the genus Piper as the species Piper retrofractum.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Piper retrofractum, is a climbing vine where various components are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The medicinal value is most prominently found in the fruits, which are typically harvested when mature to extract their bioactive compounds. Each individual fruit contains the essential oils and alkaloids necessary for its healing effects. Additionally, the stem provides the structural support for the vine and can be used in certain traditional preparations. While the primary focus is often on the reproductive structures, the roots of the plant are also recognized for their pharmacological potential, and sometimes a specific root extract is processed to treat various ailments.
