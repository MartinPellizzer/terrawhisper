Alangium salviifolium

What's the taxonomical classification of Alangium salviifolium?

Aliangium salviifolium belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the taxonomic hierarchy further, it is placed in the order Cornales and belongs to the family Cornaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by its specific genus, Aliangium, and its unique species designation, salviifolium.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Alangium salviifolium has lanceolate, sage-like leaves that are characterized by a textured, slightly pubescent surface and a distinctively aromatic scent. The foliage grows in an opposite arrangement along the stem, providing a dense and lush appearance to the plant. Its woody stems support small, inconspicuous flowers that typically emerge in clusters. The leaf margins are generally entire or slightly serrated, depending on the specific specimen's maturity. This morphological structure allows the plant to thrive in various environmental conditions while maintaining its unique visual profile.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude regions of the Himalayas, specifically spanning parts of northern India, Nepal, Bhutan, and southern Tibet. It thrives primarily in subalpine zones and rocky slopes at elevations ranging from 3,000 to 4,500 meters. The species is most frequently located in moist, mountainous environments where it can access cool temperatures and seasonal snowmelt. Its distribution follows the rugged terrain of the mountain ranges across the eastern and central Himalayan belt. What are the pharmacological activities of Alangium salviifolium?

This plant has demonstrated significant pharmacological activities including anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and antidiabetic properties. Its anti-inflammatory effects help reduce swelling and pain by modulating various biochemical pathways in the body. The antibacterial components of the plant effectively inhibit the growth of several pathogenic microorganisms. Additionally, its antidiabetic potential is evidenced by its ability to help regulate blood glucose levels. These diverse medicinal qualities make the species a subject of interest for various therapeutic applications.

According to a study published by "Bioorganic & medicinal chemistry letters", the leaves of Alangium salviifolium (L.f.) Wangerin contain three new phenolic glycosides identified as salviifosides A-C (13). These leaves also yield three known compounds, which are salicin (4), kaempferol (5), and kaempferol 3-O-beta-d-glucopyranoside (6). The anti-inflammatory activities of these new compounds (1-3) were investigated using the lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced murine macrophage cells line, RAW 264.7. Specifically, salviifoside B (2) shows the potential to inhibit the production of nitric oxide (NO), prostaglandin E(2) (PGE(2)), and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-alpha).
