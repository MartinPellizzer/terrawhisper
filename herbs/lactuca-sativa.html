Physiology

The physiology of Lactuca sativa is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, which dictates its carbon fixation processes and environmental adaptations. The plant exhibits a non-succulent morphology and is strictly non-carnivorous, relying on traditional nutrient uptake through its root system. Its vegetative architecture is defined by a rosette leaf arrangement, where the leaves emerge in a circular pattern from a central growing point. The leaves themselves are ovate in form, with dimensions that vary significantly depending on growth stage and environmental conditions; typical leaf lengths range from a minimum of 6 cm to a mean of 10.5 cm, reaching a maximum of 15 cm. Similarly, leaf width fluctuates from a minimum of 1.5 cm to a mean of 4 cm, with a maximum width of 6.5 cm. These structural characteristics are closely linked to the plant's resource acquisition efficiency, as evidenced by a mean Specific Leaf Area (SLA) of 205.2 cm²/g, reflecting its leaf thickness and its strategy for light interception and gas exchange.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Lactuca sativa is characterized by a sexual process, as asexual reproduction is notably absent, and the plant follows a monocarpic life cycle. The flowering phase typically occurs during the summer months, specifically starting in July and concluding in August. During this period, the plant produces violet-colored flowers arranged in a panicle inflorescence, with individual flowers measuring between 1.5 cm and 2 cm in length. The pollination process is biotic, relying on insects to facilitate fertilization, though self-fertilization is also present. Following successful pollination, the plant develops dry, indehiscent fruits known as achenes. These fruits are quite small, with a mean length of 0.35 cm (ranging from 0.3 to 0.4 cm) and a mean width of 0.13 cm (ranging from 0.08 to 0.13 cm). The resulting seeds are minute, possessing a mean mass of approximately 0.0010841665 g (ranging from 0.0006 to 0.00115 g) and a consistent seed volume of 0.9 mm³. For dispersal, the plant utilizes an anemochorous syndrome, meaning the seeds are primarily spread by the wind.
