Solanum quitoense

Naranjilla

Solanum quitoense, commonly known as the Quito nightshade, is a medicinal plant native to the Andean highlands. Valued in traditional ethnobotany, it possesses diverse bioactive compounds used to treat various ailments. Research continues to explore its potential pharmacological properties, highlighting its significance in both cultural heritage and modern medicine.

  • Scientific name: Solanum quitoense
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Solanales and the family Solanaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Solanum, specifically as the species Solanum quitoense.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Solanum quitoense, is referred to by various common names depending on the language: in Quechua (pam) it is called Naranjilla, while in Ukrainian (udm), French (fr), Russian (ru), and Luxembourgish (lbe), it is known as Наранхилла or Narangille.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning several distinct tropical regions. Within the Malesia zone, it can be found specifically on the island of Jawa. Its presence extends into Central America, where it is documented in Costa Rica, Nicaragua, and Panamá. Additionally, the species reaches the Caribbean region, specifically inhabiting Cuba. Together, these locations highlight the widespread nature of its natural habitat.
