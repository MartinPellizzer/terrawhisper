Glehnia littoralis

Glehnia littoralis, commonly known as the coastal glehnia, is a resilient herbaceous perennial native to East Asian maritime environments. Often found clinging to rocky shorelines, this plant is valued in traditional practices for its unique phytochemical properties, offering potential therapeutic benefits through its specialized adaptation to harsh, saline habitats.

  • Scientific name: Glehnia littoralis
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Apiales and the family Apiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Glehnia, specifically as the species Glehnia littoralis.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution concentrated within East Asia. It can be found across several key regions of the Russian Far East, including Khabarovsk, Primorye, and Sakhalin. Additionally, its range extends to the Kuril Islands within the same Russian territories. Beyond Russia, the species is also distributed throughout the Manchuria region of China. Together, these areas define the primary natural habitat for Glehnia littoralis.
