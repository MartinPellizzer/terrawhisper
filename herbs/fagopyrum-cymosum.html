Reproduction

The reproduction of Fagopyrum cymosum involves both sexual and asexual mechanisms, with the sexual process characterized by bisexual flowers. The flowering period typically begins in June and extends through October. During this cycle, the plant relies on a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically utilizing insects to facilitate fertilization, as self-fertilization is noted to be absent. Following successful pollination, the plant produces fruits that generally range in length from a minimum of 0.6 cm to a maximum of 0.8 cm, with an average length of 0.7 cm. These fruits are prolific, containing a high seed count that ranges between 101 and 1000 seeds per fruit. Each individual seed possesses a mean mass of 0.02 g. Regarding seed dissemination, the plant exhibits an unspecialized dispersal syndrome, though it is also categorized as anthropochorous, indicating that human activity plays a role in its dispersal.
