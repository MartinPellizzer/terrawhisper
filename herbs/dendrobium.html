Dendrobium

Dendrobium, commonly known as stone orchids, is a diverse genus of epiphytic plants celebrated in traditional medicine, particularly in Asia. Renowned for its restorative properties, various species are used to nourish the body, reduce inflammation, and support respiratory health, making them a vital component of herbal healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Dendrobium
  • Family: Orchidaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified within the order Asparagales and the family Orchidaceae. Specifically, it is identified by the genus Dendrobium.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several distinct regions. In the Western Indian Ocean, its presence is noted specifically in the Seychelles. Within China, it can be found throughout the South-Central areas. The species also inhabits the tropical landscapes of Hainan. Finally, its range extends to the North-Central and Southeast regions of China.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Dendrobium, utilizes various components for medicinal purposes, primarily focusing on its specialized growth structures. The medicinal properties are often concentrated in the roots, which are thick and fleshy, and the stems that support the plant's epiphytic lifestyle. Specifically, the upper part of the stem may be harvested, though much of the therapeutic value is found within the middle and lower parts of stems. By utilizing the entire architecture from the grounding roots to the segmented stem, practitioners can extract the essential compounds necessary for traditional healing.
