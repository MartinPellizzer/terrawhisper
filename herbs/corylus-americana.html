Physiology

The physiology of Corylus americana is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, a mechanism typical of most deciduous woody species in the temperate zone. This pathway involves the direct fixation of atmospheric carbon dioxide by the enzyme RuBisCO during the Calvin cycle, occurring primarily within the mesophyll cells. As a C3 plant, it relies on sufficient moisture availability to maintain stomatal conductance, as the lack of specialized carbon-concentrating mechanisms—such as those found in C4 or CAM plants—makes it more susceptible to photorespiration under conditions of high temperature and water stress. The metabolic processes of the plant are closely synchronized with seasonal temperature fluctuations, driving a cycle of vigorous biomass production during the spring and summer months, followed by a physiological transition into dormancy during the winter to conserve energy and survive freezing temperatures.
