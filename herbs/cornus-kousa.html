Cornus kousa

Kousa

Cornus kousa, commonly known as the Kousa dogwood, is a stunning ornamental tree prized for its elegant white bracts and architectural form. Beyond its aesthetic beauty, this species offers ecological value, providing nutritious berries for wildlife and serving as a resilient addition to temperate gardens and medicinal landscapes.

  • Scientific name: Cornus kousa
  • Family: Cornaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is placed in the order Cornales and the family Cornaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Cornus, specifically known as Cornus kousa.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Cornus kousa, is recognized by various regional names across different languages. Its names in other languages include dřín japonský in Czech, Asiatischer Blüten-Hartriegel in German, and 산딸나무 in Korean.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range across several distinct regions of East Asia. Within China, its distribution is quite extensive, stretching from the North-Central areas to the Southeast. It can also be found growing in the South-Central portions of the country. Furthermore, the species reaches into the northern reaches of Inner Mongolia. Beyond mainland China, its habitat extends eastward to include the islands of Japan.
