Silkrubber

Funtumia elastica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (26 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Silkrubber, scientifically known as Funtumia elastica, is a versatile evergreen tree native to tropical Africa. Renowned primarily for its high-quality latex used in rubber production, this medicinal plant also holds significant ethnobotanical value. Various parts are utilized in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to inflammation.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the lush landscapes of West Tropical Africa. Within this specific region, it can be found growing in the diverse ecosystems of Benin. Its natural range also extends through the territories of Ghana and Guinea. Furthermore, the species is well-established within the tropical forests of Ivory Coast. Finally, its geographical presence includes the coastal and inland areas of Liberia.

As a phanerophyte, its regenerative buds are located high above the ground on woody stems, a structural adaptation that defines its growth habit and survival strategy. The physiology of Funtumia elastica is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, meaning it utilizes the standard Calvin cycle to fix carbon dioxide through the enzyme RuBisCO, which is typical for many woody tropical species. In terms of nutrient acquisition and symbiotic relationships, the plant is not a nitrogen fixer, as it lacks the specialized root nodules required to host nitrogen-fixing bacteria for the conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Funtumia elasticia is characterized by a highly flexible phenological cycle, as both its flowering and fruiting periods are variable and can occur across any month of the year, from January through December. This temporal variability suggests that the plant can adapt its reproductive stages to shifting environmental conditions rather than following a strict seasonal pattern. The resulting seeds are remarkably uniform in size, maintaining a consistent seed mass with a mean, maximum, and minimum value of 0.034 g. Regarding the movement of its progeny, the species utilizes an anemochorous dispersal syndrome, relying on wind currents to transport its seeds away from the parent plant to colonize new areas.
