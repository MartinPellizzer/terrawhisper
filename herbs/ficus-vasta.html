Ficus vasta

Ficus vasta, commonly known as the Great Fig, is a majestic evergreen tree native to various African landscapes. Renowned for its expansive canopy and robust architecture, this species holds significant ecological value. Beyond its shade, it is increasingly recognized in ethnobotany for its diverse medicinal properties and traditional uses.

  • Scientific name: Ficus vasta
  • Family: Moraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Rosales, it is classified under the family Moraceae and the genus Ficus. Together, these taxonomic ranks identify Ficus vasta as a specific member of the fig family.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found within the Northeast Tropical Africa region. Its specific distribution includes the nations of Djibouti, Eritrea, and Ethiopia. Additionally, the species can be located on the island of Socotra. The plant is also documented to grow within the borders of Somalia. Together, these areas define the core geographical range for Ficus vasta.
