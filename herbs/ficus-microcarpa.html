Reproduction

The reproduction of Ficus microcarpa is characterized by a monoecious sexual system, where reproductive structures are organized within a specialized enclosed inflorescence. The flowering period typically begins in May and concludes in June, though timing can be variable. The pollination process is strictly biotic, relying on an insect pollination syndrome to facilitate fertilization. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fleshy fruits that are red in color. These fruits serve as the primary vessel for seed dispersal, utilizing a zoochorous syndrome where animals aid in spreading the progeny. The individual fruits vary in size, with widths ranging from a minimum of 0.6 cm to a maximum of 8 cm. The seeds contained within are remarkably small, with a mean mass of approximately 0.00033 g (ranging from 0.00032 g to 0.00034 g) and a mean volume of 0.344 mm³, with volumes fluctuating between 0.26 mm³ and 0.47 mm³.
