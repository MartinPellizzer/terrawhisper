Brassica cretica subsp. cretica

Brassica cretica subsp. cretica, commonly known as Cretan mustard, is a resilient biennial herb native to the Mediterranean region. Characterized by its distinctive cruciferous flowers, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Historically utilized in traditional medicine, it is valued for its unique bioactive compounds and nutritional potential in regional diets.

  • Scientific name: Brassica cretica subsp. cretica
  • Family: Brassicaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Brassicales and family Brassicaceae, it is classified under the genus Brassica. Specifically, it is identified as the subspecies Brassica cretica subsp. cretica.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide distribution across several key regions of Southeastern and Eastern Europe and Western Asia. In Southeastern Europe, its presence is noted specifically in Greece and on the island of Kriti. Its range extends into Eastern Europe, where it can be found in Krym. Moving toward Western Asia, the species is distributed throughout the East Aegean Is. region. Finally, the geographical reach of this subspecies includes the areas of Lebanon and Syria.
