Blakea parasitica

Blakea parasitica, commonly known as the parasitic flamevine, is a striking tropical climber native to Central and South America. Characterized by its vibrant orange flowers, this hemiparasitic plant survives by attaching to host roots. Beyond its ornamental beauty, it holds significant ethnobotanical value in traditional medicinal practices across regions.

  • Scientific name: Blakea parasitica
  • Family: Melastomataceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered as Myrtalees and placed in the family Melastomataceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Blakea.

Common Names

This plant, Blakea parasiticica, is known by various regional names depending on the linguistic context of its habitat. In English, it is commonly referred to as the parasitic blakea, while in Spanish, it is known as muerdagallo or muerdagallo de monte. In Portuguese, the species is identified as muerdagallo, and in certain local dialects, it may also be called blakea.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution across parts of Central and South America. Within Central America, its range includes the countries of Costa Rica and Panamá.
