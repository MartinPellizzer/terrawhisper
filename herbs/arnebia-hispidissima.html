Arabian primrose

Arnebia hispidissima · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (18 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Arabian primrose, scientifically known as Arnebia hispidula, is a perennial medicinal herb native to East Asia. Renowned for its vibrant blue flowers, it is highly valued in traditional medicine. The plant's roots contain potent naphthoquinones, offering significant anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and skin-healing properties used to treat wounds and infections.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning several distinct regions across the African continent. In Northern Africa, its presence is recorded within the borders of Egypt. Moving southward, the species is distributed throughout West Tropical Africa, specifically found in Nigeria and Niger. The distribution extends further into West-Central Tropical Africa, where it can be located in Cameroon. Finally, its range reaches into Northeast Tropical Africa within the country of Chad.

Plant Parts

This plant, Arnebia hispidula, is highly valued in traditional medicine primarily for the therapeutic properties found within its root. The most significant bioactive components are concentrated in the root system, which is often processed into an ethanolic extract to enhance the bioavailability of its medicinal compounds. This ethanolic extract of the root is widely utilized in pharmacological studies and traditional remedies due to its rich content of lithospermic acid and other naphthoquinones, which contribute to its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects.
