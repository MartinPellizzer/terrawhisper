Papaver armeniacum subsp. armeniacum

Papaver armeniacum subsp. armeniacum, commonly known as the Armenian poppy, is a resilient perennial native to the mountainous regions of Armenia. Renowned for its striking blooms, this medicinal plant holds significant ethnobotanical value, traditionally utilized in folk remedies for its potent alkaloids and various therapeutic properties in regional medicine.

  • Scientific name: Papaver armeniacum subsp. armeniacum
  • Family: Papaveraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified within the order Ranunculales and the family Papaveraceae. Specifically, it is a member of the genus Papaver, categorized as the subspecies armeniacum.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across several key regions within the Caucasus and Western Asia. In the Caucasus region, its presence is noted throughout the North Caucasus and the Transcaucasus. Moving into Western Asia, it can be found spanning across the territories of Türkiye and Iraq. Additionally, the species has a significant distribution within the country of Iran. Together, these locations define the specific geographical range of this medicinal subspecies.

Plant Parts

This plant, Papaver armeniacum subsp. armeniacum, is primarily utilized in traditional medicinal practices through its aerial parts. These above-ground structures, which include the stems, leaves, and flowers, contain the bioactive compounds necessary for its therapeutic applications. By harvesting the aerial parts, practitioners can access the plant's chemical constituents that are concentrated in the vegetative and reproductive tissues.
