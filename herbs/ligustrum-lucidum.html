Broad-leaf-privet, scientifically known as Ligustrum lucidum, is a versatile evergreen shrub valued in traditional medicine. Renowned for its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, its leaves and fruits are utilized to treat various ailments, including respiratory issues and skin conditions. This resilient plant remains a significant subject in ethnobotanical studies.

The reproduction of Ligustrum lucidum is characterized by a biotic pollination process, primarily driven by insects. These flowers are arranged in inflorescences with lengths varying between 0.08 m and 0.2 m. Following pollination, the plant produces indehiscent fruit classified as a berry. These black fruits vary in size, with a mean length of 0.725 cm (ranging from 0.7 cm to 1 cm) and a mean width of 0.5 cm (ranging from 0.4 cm to 0.95 cm). The dispersal syndrome is zoochorous, specifically endozoochorous, meaning seeds are dispersed through the digestive tracts of animals. The seeds themselves have a consistent length of 5 mm and exhibit a mean mass of approximately 0.01689473 g, with individual seed masses ranging from a minimum of 0.003 g to a maximum of 0.0362873 g. Notably, asexual reproduction is absent in this species.
