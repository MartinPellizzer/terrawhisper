The life history of Iris persica is characterized by its specialized survival strategies as a perennial herb, specifically classified under the life forms of geophyte and cryptophyte. As a geophyte, the plant relies on underground storage organs, such as rhizomes, to endure unfavorable environmental conditions and facilitate regrowth during favorable seasons. This cryptophytic nature ensures that its vital regenerative structures remain protected beneath the soil surface, shielding them from extreme temperatures and herbivory. Throughout its life cycle, the plant emerges from these subterranean organs to produce foliage and seasonal blooms, utilizing its stored energy to complete its reproductive phases before retreating back into dormancy.
