Bryonia aspera

Bryonia aspera, commonly known as the rough bryony, is a climbing herbaceous vine native to various subtropical regions. Characterized by its sandpaper-like foliage and distinctive white flowers, this plant holds significant historical importance in traditional medicine, though its potent bioactive compounds require careful handling due to inherent toxicity.

  • Scientific name: Bryonia aspera
  • Family: Cucurbitaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Cucurbitaleales, it is situated in the family Cucurbitaceae. It is further identified by the genus Bryonia, specifically under the species Bryonia aspera.

Common Names

This plant, Bryonia aspera, is known by several different common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In English, it is frequently referred to as white bryony, while in German, it is known as Weißer Einzelsitz. In French, the plant is called bryone blanche, and in Spanish, it is commonly called bryonia blanca.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across several key regions of Asia. In Central Asia, its presence is documented within the borders of Turkmenistan. Moving toward the Caucasus, it can be found throughout both the North Caucasus and the Transcaucasus regions. Its range extends further into Western Asia, where it inhabits parts of Afghanistan. Additionally, the species is well-established within the territory of Iran.
