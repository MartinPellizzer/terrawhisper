Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the diverse landscapes of Papuasia, specifically within New Guinea. Additionally, this medicinal species inhabits the Gilbert Islands located in the same oceanic area. The geographical range further encompasses the islands of New Caledonia. Physiology

The physiology of Polyscias filiciifolia is characterized by its specialized metabolic processes adapted to tropical and subtropical environments, focusing primarily on efficient photosynthetic pathways and nutrient acquisition. As a member of the Araliaceae family, the plant exhibits robust vegetative growth driven by a complex system of carbon assimilation and water regulation within its pinnate foliage. Regarding its interaction with soil nutrients, the species does not function as a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the specialized symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria, such as Rhizobium or Frankia, required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms. Consequently, its physiological reliance on nitrogen is strictly dependent on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium from the surrounding soil substrate through its root system. This nutritional dependency dictates its growth patterns, as the plant must optimize root exudates and mycorrhizal associations to enhance the bioavailability of soil nitrogen to support its structural development and enzymatic functions.
