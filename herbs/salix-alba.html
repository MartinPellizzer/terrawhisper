Salix alba

White willow

Salix alba, commonly known as white willow, is a medicinal powerhouse renowned for its high concentration of salicin. Historically used to alleviate pain and reduce fever, this bark serves as the natural precursor to modern aspirin. It remains a vital botanical resource in traditional herbalism for managing inflammation and discomfort.

  • Scientific name: Salix alba
  • Family: Salicaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and order Malpighiales, it is further categorized under the family Salicaceae. Finally, its scientific identity is defined by the genus Salix.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Salix alba, is most highly valued for its therapeutic properties found within its outer layers. The primary medicinal component is the white willow bark, which is harvested from the tree to extract salicin, a precursor to aspirin. Specifically, the bark itself contains the necessary compounds to help alleviate pain and reduce inflammation. Whether referred to as White Willow Bark in herbal preparations or simply as white willow bark in botanical studies, this specific part of the plant remains a cornerstone of traditional and modern natural medicine.
