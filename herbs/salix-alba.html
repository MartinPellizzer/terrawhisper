Scientific literature: Very Extensive (651 studies) · ★★★★★

White willow, scientifically known as Salix alba, is a deciduous tree renowned for its therapeutic properties. Historically used to alleviate pain and fever, its bark contains salicin, a natural precursor to modern aspirin. This versatile plant remains a cornerstone in traditional medicine and contemporary pharmacological studies for inflammatory relief.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Salix alba is characterized by a sexual process that is strictly dioecious, meaning individual plants are either male or female, and self-fertilization is noted as absent. The flowering period typically commences in April and concludes in May. During this phase, the plant utilizes a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects for the transfer of pollen. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fruit in the form of a capsule. These capsules are dehiscent, meaning they split open at maturity to release the seeds. The seeds are remarkably lightweight, with a consistent mean, minimum, and maximum mass of 0.00012 g. For dispersal, the species employs an anemochorous syndrome, utilizing the wind to transport its tiny seeds to new locations. Notably, asexual reproduction is absent in this species.
