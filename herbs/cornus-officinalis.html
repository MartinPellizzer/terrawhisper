Asiatic dogwood

Cornus officinalis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (473 studies) · ★★★★★

Asiatic dogwood, scientifically known as Cornus officinalis, is a versatile medicinal shrub prized in traditional herbalism. Renowned for its restorative properties, its bark and fruit are utilized to support kidney health, nourish the liver, and strengthen the essence. This potent botanical serves as a vital remedy in holistic wellness.

The life history of Cornus officinalis is characterized by its perennial nature and its classification under specific life forms, primarily functioning as a phanerophyte. As a phanerophyte, the plant maintains its buds well above the ground level, allowing it to thrive as a woody shrub or small tree in its natural habitats. While it is predominantly categorized as a phanerophyte, its structural growth patterns and environmental adaptations can occasionally align with the characteristics of a nanophanerophyte depending on the specific microhabitat and the height of its woody stems. Throughout its life cycle, the plant undergoes seasonal transitions, developing robust woody structures that support its reproductive processes, eventually producing small, fragrant flowers followed by drupes that ensure the continuation of its lineage through seed dispersal.
