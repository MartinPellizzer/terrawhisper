Asiatic dogwood

Cornus officinalis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (473 studies) · ★★★★★

Asiatic dogwood, scientifically known as Cornus officinalis, is a versatile medicinal shrub prized in traditional herbalism. Renowned for its restorative properties, its bark and fruit are utilized to support kidney health, nourish the liver, and strengthen the essence. This potent botanical serves as a vital remedy in holistic wellness.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Cornales, falling into the family Cornaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Cornus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across various regions of Eastern Asia. In China, its presence is noted in both the North-Central and Southeast territories. Furthermore, the species can be found naturally throughout the Japanese archipelago. It is also widely distributed across the Korean Peninsula. Together, these locations define its primary geographical range within the continent.

Plant Parts

This plant, Cornus officinalis, is highly valued in traditional medicine for its various therapeutic components. The most significant part used is the fruit, which is often harvested when ripe to ensure maximum potency. Specifically, the dried pulp of these fruits is frequently prepared as a medicinal decoction or syrup to treat various ailments. Each individual leaf and the overall structure of the fruit contribute to the botanical profile of the species, but it is the processed fruits that serve as the primary source for pharmacological applications.
