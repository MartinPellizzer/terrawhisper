Piper betle

Betel

Piper betle, commonly known as betel leaf, is a perennial vine celebrated in traditional medicine across Asia. Rich in bioactive compounds, it possesses potent antimicrobial, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory properties. Often chewed with areca nut, this versatile plant is widely utilized to treat digestive issues, respiratory ailments, and skin infections.

  • Scientific name: Piper betle
  • Family: Piperaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Piperales and the family Piperaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Piper, specifically the species Piper betle.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Piper betle, is recognized by several common names across different Chinese linguistic variations. In Simplified Chinese, it is referred to as 蒌叶 (zh-hans) and 蒌叶 (zh-cn), while in Traditional Chinese, it is written as 蔞葉 (zh-hant). This same Traditional Chinese spelling, 蔞葉, is also used in Hong Kong (zh-hk), and the Simplified version, 蒌叶, is the standard designation used in Singapore (zh-sg).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several distinct regions. Within the Indian Subcontinent, it is notably found in Assam. Its presence also extends significantly across the Western Indian Ocean. Specifically, it can be located in the island nations of Comoros and Madagascar. Furthermore, the species is distributed throughout Mauritius and Réunion.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as betel, is a climbing vine characterized by several distinct morphological features. The most significant medicinal component is the leaf, specifically the aromatic betel leaf, which is widely used in traditional medicine for its antiseptic and stimulant properties. While the leaves are the primary part utilized by humans, the plant also produces a specialized inflorescence that serves its reproductive function. Supporting the vine's growth are the woody stems, which allow the plant to climb through surrounding vegetation.
