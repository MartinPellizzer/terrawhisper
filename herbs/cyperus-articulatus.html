Cyperus articulatus

Jointed flatsedge

Cyperus articulatus, commonly known as Nut Grass, is a perennial sedge cherished in traditional medicine. Renowned for its aromatic rhizomes, it possesses potent anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antimicrobial properties. Widely utilized in Ayurvedic and folk practices, this resilient plant offers significant therapeutic potential for treating various digestive and gynecological ailments.

  • Scientific name: Cyperus articulatus
  • Family: Cyperaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Poales, specifically as a member of the family Cyperaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Cyperus and the species articulatus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Cyperus articulatus, is recognized by various regional names across different cultures and languages. In Portuguese, it is commonly referred to as Priprioca, while in Bengali, it is known as হন্ধা. Within certain indigenous contexts, it is called Yiriginli in the Dag language and Maaja in the Bodo language. Additionally, in Chinese, the plant is identified as 节莎草.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions of the African continent. In Northern Africa, its presence is specifically noted in Algeria and Egypt. Moving toward the Atlantic, it can be found within the Macaronesian islands of Cape Verde. The species also extends into West Tropical Africa, where it is documented in Benin. Furthermore, its range includes the landlocked West Tropical African nation of Burkina.

Plant Parts

This plant, Cyperus articulatus, is a medicinal herb characterized by its distinct structural components. The most therapeutically significant parts are the rhizomes, which are often referred to as the rhizome mass used in traditional remedies. These underground structures are accompanied by a network of fibrous roots that help anchor the plant. Above the ground, the plant features elongated, slender leaves that are typical of the sedge family. Furthermore, the plant is known to contain various EOs (essential oils), which contribute to its aromatic properties and medicinal potency.
