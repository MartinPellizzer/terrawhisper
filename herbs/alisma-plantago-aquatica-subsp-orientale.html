Alisma plantago-aquatica orientale, scientifically known as Alisma plantago-aquatica subsp. orientale, is a versatile aquatic perennial valued in traditional medicine. Native to various wetland habitats, this subspecies is widely recognized for its diuretic, anti-inflammatory, and detoxifying properties, offering significant therapeutic potential in treating kidney and bladder-related ailments.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across several key regions of Northern Asia. Within the vast territory of Siberia, it can be found inhabiting the areas of Buryatiya and Chita. Moving eastward, the species is also well-established throughout the Russian Far East. Specifically, its presence is documented in the Amur and Khabarovsk regions. Plant Parts

This plant, Alisma plantago-aquatica subsp. orientale, is characterized by a distinct underground structure used for storage and propagation. It grows via creeping rhizomes that extend through the aquatic substrate, allowing the plant to spread effectively. While some species in this family may develop distinct storage organs, the medicinal value of this specific taxon is often associated with the rootstock. In traditional preparations, the rhizome is harvested and processed, sometimes involving the use of dried tubers or similar starchy swellings found in related aquatic species. Although some botanical descriptions might mistakenly refer to its storage organs as a tuber or tuber, it is the interconnected network of the rhizome and its associated tubers that constitutes the primary medicinal part of the plant.
