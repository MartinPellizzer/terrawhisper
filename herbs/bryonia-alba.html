White bryony, scientifically known as Bryonia alba, is a climbing deciduous vine native to Europe and parts of Asia. Traditionally used in homeopathic medicine, this plant contains potent alkaloids. While historically valued for treating respiratory and inflammatory conditions, its toxicity requires extreme caution, as all parts are highly poisonous.

Distribution

This plant, known as Bryonia alba, exhibits a specific distribution across the northern regions of Europe. In the westernmost part of this range, it can be found growing in the landscape of Ireland. Moving toward Scandinavia, the species is well-established throughout Denmark. It also maintains a presence across the vast territories of Sweden and Norway. Finally, the geographical reach of this medicinal plant extends further east into Finland.

Plant Parts

This plant, Bryonia alba, is characterized by its distinct morphological structures, though it is often studied in conjunction with its relatives in the Cucurbitaceae family. While the therapeutic properties are often associated with the entire organism, specific focus is frequently placed on the roots. In many botanical discussions, comparisons are made to the roots of Bryonia dioica to distinguish between species, as both possess potent bioactive compounds. Ultimately, the medicinal efficacy of the species is heavily dependent on the chemical composition found within the root, which serves as a primary source for its pharmacological applications.
