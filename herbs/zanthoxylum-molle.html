Zanthoxylum molle

What's the taxonomical classification of Zanthoxylum molle?

Zanthoxylum molle belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is further categorized into the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindales. Its taxonomic lineage continues through the family Rutaceae, placing it within the genus Zanthoxylum, ultimately identifying it as the specific species Zanthoxylum molle.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Zanthoxylum molle has pinnately compound leaves with 7 to 15 leaflets that are elliptical or lanceolate in shape, serrated margins, and an alternate arrangement on woody stems. The tree typically reaches heights of 15 to 25 meters and features a grayish, fissured bark as it matures. Small, inconspicuous flowers bloom in panicles, eventually developing into fleshy, globose drupes that turn red or black when ripe. The wood is characterized by a dense structure and a distinct aromatic quality. Its overall growth habit is upright and spreading, providing a dense canopy of foliage.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant provides medicinal benefits through its fruit pericarp, bark, and roots. The pericarp of the fruit is frequently harvested to prepare decoctions used in traditional remedies. Its bark contains various bioactive compounds that assist in treating inflammatory conditions. Additionally, the roots are utilized in herbal formulations to address digestive issues and fever. These diverse components make the entire plant a significant resource in ethnobotanical practices.

According to a study published by "Yao xue xue bao = Acta pharmaceuctica Sinica", the morphological and histological characters of Zanthoxylum molle Rehd. were described among 8 species. The study identified important features for identification, including the external characters of the pericarp and the occurrence of hairs on the fruit stalk. Other key characteristics include the presence and location of pigment and crystals of hesperiidin, the thickness of the cell walls of the endocarp, and the presence and shape of nonglandular hairs on the fruit stalk. The research investigated drug resources across 8 provinces or autonomous regions, specifically Sichuan, Guangxi, Henan, Liaoing, Zhejiang, Anhui, Jiangsu, and Shandong. This study found that more than 18 spp. of Zanthoxylum are used as drugs in China.
