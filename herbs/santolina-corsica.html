Santolina corsica

Santolina corsica, commonly known as Corsican santolina, is a resilient evergreen shrub native to the Mediterranean. Renowned for its aromatic, silvery-gray foliage and bright yellow button-like flowers, it is prized in herbalism for its antiseptic properties and in landscaping for its drought tolerance and striking ornamental beauty.

  • Scientific name: Santolina corsica
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asterales, it is classified under the family Asteraceae and the genus Santolina.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Santolina corsica, is primarily utilized in herbal applications through its aerial parts. These above-ground structures include the aromatic, silvery-gray foliage and the small, yellow flowering heads that characterize the species. Because the essential oils and therapeutic compounds are concentrated within the leaves and blooms, the aerial parts are typically harvested for drying and subsequent use in traditional remedies.
