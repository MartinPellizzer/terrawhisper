Camellia sinensis var. assamica

Camellia sinensis var. assamica, commonly known as Assam tea, is a robust evergreen shrub native to the tropical regions of Northeast India. Renowned for its bold, malty flavor, this variety thrives in high humidity. Beyond its culinary use, it possesses potent antioxidant properties vital for holistic wellness and health.

  • Scientific name: Camellia sinensis var. assamica
  • Family: Theaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Ericales and the family Theaceae, it is classified under the genus Camellia. Specifically, it is identified as the variety assamica within the species Camellia sinensis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Camellia sinensis var. assamica, is widely recognized by several common names across different linguistic contexts. In Chinese, it is referred to as 大叶茶 (zh), 大叶茶 (zh-cn), 大葉茶 (zh-tw), and 大叶茶 (zh-hans), all of which translate to "large-leaf tea." Additionally, it is famously associated with the name 普洱茶 (zh), referring to the type of tea often produced from this variety.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions in Asia. In China, its presence is noted in the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan areas. Moving towards the Indian Subcontinent, the species is prominently found in Assam. Additionally, it is distributed within the borders of Bangladesh. Together, these locations define the primary natural habitat for this specific variety.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Assam variety of the tea plant, is primarily cultivated for its leaves, but its reproductive structures also play a biological role. The seeds of Camellia sinensis var. assamica are contained within small capsules that develop after flowering. While the seeds are not the focus of commercial tea production, they are botanically significant as they contain the embryo necessary for propagation and hold a genetic blueprint that distinguishes this robust, large-leafed variety from its Chinese counterparts.
