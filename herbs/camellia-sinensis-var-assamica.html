Camellia sinensis var. assamica

Camellia sinensis var. assamica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (21 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Scientifically known as Camellia sinensis var. assamica, is a robust evergreen shrub native to the Assam region of India. Renowned for its large leaves, this variety serves as the primary source of strong, malty black teas. Beyond its culinary use, it offers significant antioxidant properties and various therapeutic benefits.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Ericales and the family Theaceae, it is classified under the genus Camellia. Specifically, it is identified as the variety assamica within the species Camellia sinensis.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions in Asia. In China, its presence is noted in the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan areas. Moving towards the Indian Subcontinent, the species is prominently found in Assam. Additionally, it is distributed within the borders of Bangladesh. Together, these locations define the primary natural habitat for this specific variety.
