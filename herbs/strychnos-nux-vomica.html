strychnine tree

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (349 studies) · ★★★★★

Strychnine tree, scientifically known as Strychnos nux-vomica, is a tropical evergreen tree native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its potent alkaloids, particularly strychnine, it has a complex history in medicine. While historically used in minute doses for neurological stimulation, its extreme toxicity requires careful handling and strictly regulated application.

Physiology

The physiology of Strychnos nux-vomica is characterized by a standard C3 photosynthetic pathway, which involves the direct fixation of carbon dioxide through the Calvin cycle within the mesophyll cells. As a C3 plant, it relies on the enzyme RuBisCO to catalyze the initial carboxylation step, a process that is efficient under temperate to tropical conditions but can be susceptible to photorespiration in high-temperature environments. Regarding its nutrient acquisition strategies, the plant is not a nitrogen fixer, meaning it lacks the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria (such as Rhizobium) required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms; instead, it relies entirely on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium from the soil through its root system to support its metabolic processes and the synthesis of its complex indole alkaloids.
