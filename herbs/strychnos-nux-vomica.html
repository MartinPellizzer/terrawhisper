Strychnos nux-vomica

Strychnine tree

Strychnos nux-vomica, commonly known as the Strychnine tree, is a medicinal plant native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its potent alkaloids, particularly strychnine and brucine, it has historically been used in small doses for neurological stimulation. However, its extreme toxicity requires precise handling and careful pharmacological consideration.

  • Scientific name: Strychnos nux-vomica
  • Family: Loganiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Gentianales, falling into the family Loganiaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Strychnos.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across several key regions in Asia. In China, it is found throughout the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan areas. Its presence also extends to the island of Taiwan within Eastern Asia. Furthermore, the species can be located within the Indian Subcontinent, specifically in Bangladesh. Together, these locations highlight the tropical and subtropical habitats preferred by the species.

Plant Parts

This plant, Strychnos nux-vomica, is a medicinal source where various components hold significant pharmacological value. The most potent parts are the ripened seeds, which are harvested once they have matured to contain high concentrations of alkaloids. These seeds are often processed to extract strychnine and brucine, though the raw seeds themselves are highly toxic. Beyond the seeds, the leaves are frequently utilized in traditional practices, while the stem bark and root bark are also valued for their specific chemical constituents. Each of these parts, from the woody textures of the bark to the dense interior of the seeds, contributes to the complex medicinal profile of this species.
