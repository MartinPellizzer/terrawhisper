Leea guineensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Leea guineensis?

Leea guineensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is further categorized into the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant follows the order Vitales and is a member of the family Vitaceae. Taxonomically, it is placed within the genus Leea, with its specific identification being the species guineensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Leea guineensis has opposite, simple, or trifoliolate leaves with elliptical to ovate shapes and entire margins that grow from woody, perennial stems. The foliage typically exhibits a deep green color and a smooth texture on the upper surface. Its inflorescences consist of small, delicate flowers arranged in complex panicles that emerge from the leaf axils. These flowers eventually develop into small, fleshy berries that serve as a key diagnostic feature of the species. The plant's overall structure is characterized by a shrubby growth habit suitable for forest understories.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical rainforest regions of West and Central Africa, specifically spanning from Guinea through Nigeria to the Congo Basin. It thrives primarily in humid, low-altitude environments where heavy rainfall is consistent throughout the year. The species is frequently found in the understory of dense forest canopies where shade and moisture are abundant. Its range is closely tied to the Guineo-Congolian phytogeographical region. Localized populations are often concentrated near riverbanks and damp forest edges.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant thrives when grown in moist, well-draining soil under bright, indirect sunlight and temperatures between 65 and 80 degrees Fahrenheit.

It requires consistent watering to maintain high humidity levels, which prevents the foliage from drying out. Providing regular fertilization during the growing season helps support its vibrant red flower clusters. Because it is native to tropical regions, it should be protected from frost and harsh, direct midday sun.
