Picea koraiensis

Korean spruce

Picea koraienensis, commonly known as the Korayen spruce, is a rare coniferous species native to high-altitude mountainous regions. While primarily valued for its ecological significance and timber, it holds traditional medicinal potential. Researchers explore its unique phytochemical properties, investigating its bioactive compounds for potential therapeutic applications in modern pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Picea koraiensis
  • Family: Pinaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Pinidae, falling within the order Pinales and family Pinaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is established within the genus Picea.

Common Names

This plant, Picea koraienensis, is known by several different common names depending on the language and region. In Vepsian, it is referred to as Koreine kuz', while in English, it is called Korea kuusk. Its names in Chinese vary slightly by script and region, being known as 红皮云杉 in Simplified Chinese (zh-hans), 紅皮雲杉 in Traditional Chinese (zh-hant), and 紅皮雲杉 in Hong Kong Cantonese (zh-hk).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical range concentrated within the temperate regions of East Asia. In the Russian Far East, its presence is specifically noted within the Primorye territory. Moving into China, the species can be found spanning across Inner Mongolia and the Manchuria region. Furthermore, the distribution extends southward to include various parts of Korea. Together, these locations define the natural habitat for this particular medicinal species.

Plant Parts

This plant, Picea koraienensis, is often studied for its unique medicinal properties which are found within its specific growths, most notably the insect gall. These galls are specialized structures formed on the plant's tissues in response to the presence of certain insects, and they serve as the primary source for collecting the bioactive compounds used in traditional remedies. By utilizing the insect gall, researchers can isolate concentrated secondary metabolites that are characteristic of this particular species.
