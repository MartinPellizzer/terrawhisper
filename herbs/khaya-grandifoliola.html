Khaya grandifoliola

Khaya grandifolia, commonly known as the African Mahogany, is a majestic evergreen tree native to tropical Africa. Highly valued for its durable timber, this medicinal plant also holds significant ethnobotanical importance. Various parts of the tree are utilized in traditional remedies to treat ailments like malaria and skin infections.

  • Scientific name: Khaya grandifoliola
  • Family: Meliaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Sapindales, it is classified under the family Meliaceae. Its specific taxonomic designation is within the genus Khaya, where it is known as Khaya grandifolia.

Plant Parts

This plant, Khaya grandifoliola, is a large forest tree that is highly valued in traditional medicine, particularly for the therapeutic properties found within its stem bark. The stem bark is the primary part utilized for medicinal purposes, often being stripped from the trunk to be prepared as decoctions or infusions. In various traditional healing practices, this bark is employed to treat a wide range of ailments, including malaria, digestive issues, and inflammatory conditions, due to the presence of bioactive compounds contained within its fibrous layers.
