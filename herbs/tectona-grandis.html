Tectona grandis

Teak tree

Tectona grandis, commonly known as Teak, is a majestic tropical hardwood renowned for its exceptional durability and strength. Prized globally in construction and woodworking, this botanical powerhouse also holds significant medicinal value. Its leaves and bark are traditionally utilized in various cultures to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to inflammation.

  • Scientific name: Tectona grandis
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae, the order Lamiales, and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, the medicinal species is identified by its genus, Tectona.

Common Names

This plant, Tectona grandis, is known by various names across different Chinese linguistic regions, though the nomenclature remains consistent. In simplified Chinese (zh-hans), mainland Chinese (zh-cn), and Singaporean (zh-sg) contexts, it is referred to as 柚木. This same name, 柚木, is also utilized in traditional Chinese characters for Taiwan (zh-hant) and Hong Kong (zh-hk).

Distribution

This plant, Tectona grandis, exhibits a specific distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in the nations of Benin, Gambia, and Togo. Its presence extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically within the territory of Cabinda. Additionally, the species is documented inhabiting the Gulf of Guinea Islands. These locations highlight its preference for various tropical environments within these distinct geographical zones.
