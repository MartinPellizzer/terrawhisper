Teak tree, scientifically known as Tectona grandis, is a majestic deciduous species celebrated for its exceptional durability and medicinal properties. Renowned for its high-quality timber, this plant also offers therapeutic benefits, including anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial actions, making it a vital resource in both traditional healing and sustainable forestry.

Distribution

This plant, Tectona grandis, exhibits a specific distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in the nations of Benin, Gambia, and Togo. Its presence extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically within the territory of Cabinda. Additionally, the species is documented inhabiting the Gulf of Guinea Islands. These locations highlight its preference for various tropical environments within these distinct geographical zones.

Plant Parts

This plant, Tecoma stans (often referred to in certain contexts as Tecona grandis), offers various components that are utilized for their therapeutic potential. The vibrant yellow flowers are a distinguishing feature of the species, while the green leaves are frequently harvested for traditional applications. From these foliage components, a potent leaf extract can be prepared to harness specific bioactive compounds for medicinal use. Additionally, the plant produces distinct fruits that represent its reproductive stage. For specialized topical or internal traditional remedies, carbonized Tecona grandis may also be used, utilizing the charred remains of the plant matter to provide unique medicinal properties.
