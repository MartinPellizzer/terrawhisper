Artemisia judaica

Artemisia judaica, commonly known as Judean wormwood, is a resilient perennial herb native to the Mediterranean and Middle Eastern regions. Valued in traditional ethnobotany, this aromatic plant contains bioactive compounds used for their antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties, offering significant potential in modern pharmacological research and natural medicine.

  • Scientific name: Artemisia judaica
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Further taxonomic progression places it in the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales. Finally, it is a member of the family Asteraceae within the genus Artemisia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution spanning across several distinct regions. In Northern Africa, it can be found growing in the territories of Algeria, Egypt, and Libya. Its range also extends into Northeast Tropical Africa, specifically within the borders of Chad. Furthermore, the species is documented in Western Asia, particularly in Palestine. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in various environments across two continents.
