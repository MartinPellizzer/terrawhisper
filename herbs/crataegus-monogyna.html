Crataegus chlorocarpa

Crataegus monogyna · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1112 studies) · ★★★★★

Crataegus chlorocarpa, scientifically known as Crataegus monogyna, is a resilient deciduous shrub widely recognized as the common hawthorn. Renowned for its therapeutic properties, this medicinal plant is traditionally used to support cardiovascular health, improve circulation, and alleviate symptoms of anxiety, making it a cornerstone in herbal medicine practices.

Plant Parts

This plant, Crataegus monogyna, commonly known as common hawthorn, possesses a diverse range of vegetative and reproductive organs that contribute to its medicinal profile. The stems are typically woody and often feature sharp thorns, providing a sturdy structural framework for the plant's growth. The leaves are distinctive, often deeply lobed, and serve as primary sites for photosynthesis. During its flowering season, the plant produces clusters of delicate white flowers that act as the reproductive centers for seed production. Following pollination, these flowers develop into fleshy, nutrient-dense fruits, known as haws, which contain the seeds necessary for the plant's life cycle and are frequently utilized in traditional herbal remedies.
