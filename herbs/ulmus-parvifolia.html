Ulmus parvifolia

What's the taxonomical classification of Ulmus parvifolia?

Ulmus parvifolia belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the evolutionary hierarchy, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the order Rosales, the plant is placed in the family Ulmaceae. It is further categorized under the genus Ulmus, and its specific biological designation is completed by the species name parvifolia.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Ulmus parvifolia has small, alternate, ovate to lanceolate leaves measuring 1 to 3 inches long with doubly serrated margins and asymmetric bases. The bark of the mature tree is distinctive, featuring a mottled texture where it exfoliates in patches to reveal shades of gray, orange, and brown. Its branching structure is typically spreading, supporting a canopy that can be rounded or irregular depending on its environment. The flowers are small and inconspicuous, appearing in early spring before the leaves fully expand. This species produces small, winged samaras that facilitate wind dispersal of its seeds.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to East Asia, spanning a vast territory that includes China, Korea, Japan, and Taiwan. Within these regions, it thrives in diverse environments ranging from coastal lowlands to mountainous forests. Because of its exceptional adaptability, it has been widely introduced to other parts of the world, including North America and Europe. It is frequently used in urban landscaping across temperate zones due to its ability to tolerate varied soil types and pollution. How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, Ulmus parvifolia, thrives when planted in well-draining soil within full sun to partial shade environments and requires regular watering during its establishment phase.

It is highly valued in landscaping for its ability to tolerate various soil types, including clay and sandy compositions. Once mature, the tree becomes quite drought-tolerant and can withstand urban pollution and compacted ground. Pruning should be performed in late winter to maintain its shape and encourage vigorous growth.

Careful monitoring for fungal diseases ensures the long-term health of this versatile deciduous species.

According to a study published by "Plant signaling & behavior", the bark and leaves of Ulmus parvifolia are used in the treatment of diseases such as inflammation, diarrhea and fever. Through an untargeted metabolomics approach, a total of 2,578 and 2,207 metabolites were identified in the seeds and bark, respectively. The study found that 503 and 132 unique metabolites were identified in the seeds and bark. Additionally, 574 differential metabolites were detected in the two different organs and were grouped into 52 classes. These differential metabolites contained bioactive compounds with antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-cancer activities.

What are the pharmacological activities of Ulmus parvifolia?

This plant has diverse pharmacological activities including anti-cancer, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, wound healing, and epidermal skin rejuvenation properties. Its high concentration of bioactive compounds helps neutralize free radicals to protect cells from oxidative stress. These constituents also inhibit inflammatory pathways and promote the rapid closure of tissue injuries. Furthermore, the extract stimulates cellular turnover to enhance skin elasticity and appearance. Research suggests that these multifaceted effects make it a valuable candidate for both medicinal and dermatological applications.

According to a study published by "Plant signaling & behavior", the bark and leaves of Ulmus parvifolia are utilized in the treatment of diseases including inflammation, diarrhea, and fever. Through untargeted metabolomics, 2,578 metabolites were identified in the seeds and 2,207 metabolites in the bark. The analysis detected 574 differential metabolites (DEMs) grouped into 52 classes, with the flavonoid class having the highest relative content. These DEMs contain bioactive compounds such as flavonoids, terpene glycosides, triterpenoids, and sesquiterpenoids that possess antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-cancer activities. While most flavonoids and sesquiterpenes were up-regulated in the seeds, more varieties of terpene glycosides and triterpenoids were up-regulated in the bark.

According to a study published by "Journal of clinical medicine", Ulmus parvifolia root bark powder is a medicinal plant used traditionally for the treatment of wounds. In a mouse wound healing model, wound lesions decreased when using 200 mesh Ulmus parvifolia root bark powder. In vitro experiments showed that Ulmus parvifolia extract promoted the migration of human skin keratinocytes. The treatment with Ulmus parvifolia powder upregulated the expression of matrix metalloproteinase-2 and -9 protein and significantly increased transforming growth factor-beta levels. Topical administration of the bark powder exerted a significant effect on skin wound healing by upregulating the expression of MMP and transforming growth factor-beta.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause allergic skin reactions, respiratory irritation, and digestive discomfort in sensitive individuals. Inhaling pollen or fine particles from the plant can lead to sneezing, coughing, or aggravated asthma symptoms. Ingesting parts of the plant may cause nausea or stomach upset if not prepared correctly. While many use it in traditional practices, these potential side effects necessitate caution during use.
