Bauhinia aurea

What's the taxonomical classification of Bauhinia aurea?

Bauhinia aurea belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Fabales. This plant is a member of the family Fabaceae, falling into the genus Bauhinia, and is specifically identified by its species name, aurea.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Bauhinia aurea has bipinnate leaves that are composed of multiple small, oval leaflets and bright yellow flowers that grow in clusters. Each leaf structure is distinct, often featuring a fine texture that distinguishes it from other members of its genus. The flowers are characterized by five prominent petals and central stamens that attract various pollinators. Its bark is generally smooth and greyish, providing a sturdy framework for the dense foliage.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds astilbin, isoastilbin, neoastilbin, neoisoastilbin, and lupeol. These specific flavonoids and triterpenoids are responsible for much of the plant's therapeutic potential. Research indicates that these bioactive constituents may contribute to various antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects. The presence of these diverse chemical structures makes Bauhinia aurea a subject of interest in pharmacological studies. Scientists continue to examine how these individual components interact to provide health benefits.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", eight compounds were isolated from the ethyl acetate soluble fraction of the ethanolic extract of Bauhinia aurea. These structures were elucidated as isoengeletin (1), astilbin (2), neoastilbin (3), isoastilbin (4), neoisoastilbin (5), (+)-catechin (6), (-)-epicatechin (7) and (-)-epicatechin 3-O-gallate (8). The isolation process involved column chromatography over silica gel, reversed-phase RP-18, and Sephadex LH-20. Five compounds were isolated from this genus for the first time, with the exception of 2, 6 and 8. MS and NMR spectroscopic methods were utilized to determine the structures of the purified compounds.
