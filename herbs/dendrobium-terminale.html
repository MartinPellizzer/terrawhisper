Dendrobium terminale

What's the taxonomical classification of Dendrobium terminale?

Dendrobium terminale belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this group, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Its systematic lineage continues through the order Asparagales and the family Orchidaceae, which is the diverse group of orchids. Finally, it is placed within the genus Dendrobium, specifically identified as the species terminale.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Dendrobium terminale has sympodial pseudobulbs that are elongated, compressed, and typically reach lengths of up to 50 centimeters. These fleshy stems serve as primary water storage organs and are often densely clustered to form thick clumps. The leaves are arranged distichously along the upper portion of the pseudobulbs and feature a leathery, lanceolate shape. Inflorescences emerge from the apex of the stems, bearing numerous small, fragrant flowers that are typically white or pale cream in color. The floral structure consists of three sepals and three petals, with a distinct, slightly larger labellum.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires bright, filtered sunlight and high humidity levels between 60 and 80 percent to thrive in a tropical or subtropical environment.

You should grow it in a well-draining medium such as orchid bark or moss to prevent root rot. Regular watering is necessary to keep the substrate moist, but you must ensure the roots dry out slightly between applications. Providing consistent warmth and adequate air circulation will help prevent fungal diseases on its foliage.

These specific conditions encourage the development of its characteristic vibrant pink flowers.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

The comprehensive use of the whole plant ensures that all available chemical constituents are harnessed for medicinal use.

According to a study published by "Natural product research", two previously undescribed dihydrophenanthrene derivatives, labeled as 1 and 2, were isolated from the whole plant of Dendrobium terminale. These two new compounds were identified alongside 12 known analogues from the same plant. The isolated compounds underwent evaluation for cytotoxicity against 3 kinds of tumor cell lines, specifically sw1990, HCT-116, and HepG2. Among the substances tested, compounds 11 and 14 showed stronger antitumor effects. The study also included a revision of the NMR data for known phenanthrene derivatives 7 and 9.
