Erythroxylum coca

Coca

Erythroxylum coca, commonly known as the coca plant, holds profound significance in Andean cultures. Historically utilized for ritualistic and medicinal purposes, its leaves provide sustained energy and alleviate altitude sickness. While its alkaloid content requires careful regulation, the plant remains a vital element of traditional South American ethnobotany.

  • Scientific name: Erythroxylum coca
  • Family: Erythroxylaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is categorized within the order Malpighiales and the family Erythroxylaceae. Ultimately, its specific taxonomic designation is found within the genus Erythroxylum.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Erythroxylum coca, is primarily valued for the chemical properties found within its foliage. The most significant part of the species is the coca leaf, which has been used for centuries in traditional Andean medicine to alleviate fatigue and altitude sickness. Specifically, the medicinal alkaloids are concentrated in the green leaves of the shrub. When processed or chewed, these coca leaves serve as a natural stimulant and anesthetic. Whether referred to in a botanical context or a cultural one, the use of Coca leaves remains a central element of indigenous healing practices in South America.
