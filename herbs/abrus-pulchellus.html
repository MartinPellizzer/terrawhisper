Abrus pulchellus

What's the taxonomical classification of Abrus pulchellus?

Abrus pulchellus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant further falls under the order Fabales and is a member of the family Fabaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is completed within the genus Abrus, specifically as the species pulchellus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Abrus pulchellus has pinnately compound leaves consisting of several pairs of small, oval leaflets and bright red, papilionaceous flowers that bloom in clusters. The plant typically grows as a climbing shrub or vine with slender, twining stems that allow it to scale surrounding vegetation. Its foliage is characterized by a delicate texture and a deep green color that provides a backdrop for its vibrant floral displays. The flowers are notable for their striking contrast between deep crimson petals and lighter colored centers. What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe systemic toxicity if the highly poisonous protein abrin is present in its seeds. Ingestion of toxic parts often leads to immediate gastrointestinal distress, specifically nausea and vomiting. Patients may also experience significant abdominal pain and persistent diarrhea following exposure. Beyond digestive issues, the toxins can impact the neurological system, resulting in symptoms such as dizziness. Monitoring for these diverse side effects is crucial due to the dangerous nature of the plant's chemical constituents.

  • Nausea
  • Vomiting
  • Abdominal pain
  • Diarrhea
