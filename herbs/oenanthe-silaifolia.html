Oenanthe silaifolia

Oenanthe silaifolia, commonly known as the silverside water-dropwort, is a distinctive aquatic perennial found in wetland habitats. Valued in traditional herbal practices for its unique phytochemical profile, this plant offers intriguing therapeutic potential. Understanding its botanical characteristics and medicinal properties is essential for exploring its role in ethnopharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Oenanthe silaifolia
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Apiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Apiaceae and is situated within the genus Oenanthe.

Common Names

This plant, Oenanthe silaifolia, is known by various names depending on the region and language. In French, it is referred to as Œnanthe à feuilles de silaüs, while in Dutch, it is called Weidekervel-torkruid. Its local names also include Cegiden feinddail in Welsh, halucha koromáčolistá in Czech, and Գինեծաղիկ գազարատերև in Armenian.
