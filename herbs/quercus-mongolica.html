Mongolian Oak, scientifically known as Quercus mongolica, is a resilient deciduous tree native to East Asia. While primarily valued for its robust timber and ecological importance, it holds significant medicinal potential. Traditional practices utilize its bark and leaves, offering bioactive compounds with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties for various therapeutic applications.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fagales, it is a member of the family Fagaceae and is specifically classified under the genus Quercus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide-ranging distribution across several distinct regions of Northern Asia. In Siberia, its presence is specifically noted in the Chita region. The species is also well-established within the Russian Far East, particularly in the Amur area. Furthermore, it can be found throughout the Khabarovsk region and along the Kamchatka Peninsula. Finally, its geographical reach extends to the Kuril Islands, completing its northern maritime distribution.
