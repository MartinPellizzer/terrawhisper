Quercus mongolica

Mongolian oak

Quercus mongolica, commonly known as the Mongolian oak, is a resilient deciduous tree native to East Asia. Beyond its ecological importance in forest ecosystems, this species holds significant medicinal value. Its bark and leaves are traditionally utilized in various cultures for their bioactive compounds and diverse therapeutic properties.

  • Scientific name: Quercus mongolica
  • Family: Fagaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fagales, it is a member of the family Fagaceae and is specifically classified under the genus Quercus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Quercus mongolica, is referred to by various common names depending on the language and region. In Simplified Chinese (zh-hans), it is called 蒙古栎, while in Traditional Chinese (zh-hant) and the Cantonese usage of Hong Kong (zh-hk), it is known as 蒙古櫟. In Japanese (ja), the species is called モンゴリナラ, and in Czech (cs), it is identified as dub mongolský.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide-ranging distribution across several distinct regions of Northern Asia. In Siberia, its presence is specifically noted in the Chita region. The species is also well-established within the Russian Far East, particularly in the Amur area. Furthermore, it can be found throughout the Khabarovsk region and along the Kamchatka Peninsula. Finally, its geographical reach extends to the Kuril Islands, completing its northern maritime distribution.
