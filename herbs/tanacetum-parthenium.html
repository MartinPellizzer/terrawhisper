Feverfew, scientifically known as Tanacetum parthenium, is a perennial flowering herb renowned for its potent medicinal properties. Traditionally used in herbalism, it is most widely recognized for its ability to prevent migraines and reduce inflammation. This versatile plant remains a cornerstone in natural remedies for supporting vascular health.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as feverfew, possesses distinct morphological features that contribute to its medicinal profile. The vibrant, daisy-like flowers serve as the most striking visual element, typically characterized by white ray florets surrounding a yellow center. These blooms are supported by slender, green stalks that extend upward to facilitate pollination. The foliage consists of deeply lobed, aromatic leaves that contain the plant's bioactive compounds. Once the flowering cycle is complete, the plant produces numerous small seeds, which are often dispersed as individual seeds to ensure the continuation of the species in the wild.
