Reed canary grass, scientifically known as Phalaris arundinacea, is a perennial grass widely distributed across temperate regions. While primarily recognized as a robust forage plant and a potential invasive species, it holds significant ethnobotanical importance. Research explores its bioactive compounds for potential therapeutic properties and diverse medicinal applications.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Poales and family Poaceae, it is further classified under the genus Phalaris. This taxonomic hierarchy defines its biological position as a member of the grass family.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found across several regions within Northern Europe. Its distribution includes the coastal and inland areas of Denmark and Finland. It is also documented growing in the Faroe Islands, which are known as Føroyar. Furthermore, the species can be found throughout the landscapes of Great Britain. Finally, its geographical reach extends to the unique terrain of Iceland.
