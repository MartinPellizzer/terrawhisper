Distribution

This plant is primarily found across several regions within Northern Europe. Its distribution includes the coastal and inland areas of Denmark and Finland. Reproduction

The reproduction of Phalaris arundaenacea is characterized by a primarily sexual process, as asexual reproduction is noted to be absent. The plant features bisexual flowers, and its pollination syndrome is classified as abiotic, specifically relying on wind for the transfer of pollen. The flowering period typically occurs during the summer months, beginning in June and concluding in July. Following successful pollination, the plant produces seeds with a mean length of 2 mm (ranging from a minimum of 2 mm to a maximum of 2 mm) and a mean width of 1 mm (with minimum and maximum values both remaining at 1 mm). These seeds are quite light, possessing a mean mass of approximately 0.000715 g, with individual seed masses varying between a minimum of 0.00031 g and a maximum of 0.0011 g. Regarding the spread of the species, Phalaris arundaenacea utilizes an anemochorous dispersal syndrome, meaning the seeds are dispersed by the wind. Furthermore, the plant does not exhibit self-fertilization, necessitating cross-pollination to complete its reproductive cycle.
