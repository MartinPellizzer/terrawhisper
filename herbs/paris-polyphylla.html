Paris polyphylla

Paris polypylla, commonly known as Shengdi Huang, is a precious medicinal herb native to East Asia. Renowned in traditional Chinese medicine, this perennial plant features striking white flowers and dark berries. It is highly valued for its ability to clear heat, detoxify the body, and support cardiovascular health.

  • Scientific name: Paris polyphylla
  • Family: Melanthiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is positioned in the order Liliales and is a member of the family Melanthiaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identification is placed within the genus Paris.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions of Asia. In China, it can be found spanning from the South-Central provinces to the North-Central territories. Its range further extends into the high-altitude areas of Qinghai and Tibet. Beyond China, the species is also documented within the Indian Subcontinent, specifically in Assam. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in various mountainous and subtropical environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, Paris polyphylla, is a perennial herb where the most pharmacologically significant components are located underground. The medicinal value is primarily concentrated in its fleshy rhizomes, which often develop into thickened, storage-like tubers. These modified rhizomes contain the essential saponins sought after in traditional medicine. While the roots are also present, it is the specialized structure of the rhizome that serves as the primary source for extraction. In addition to these subterranean structures, the aerial parts of the plant, including its leaves and flowers, complete its morphological profile, though they are less frequently used for medicinal purposes than the underground organs.
