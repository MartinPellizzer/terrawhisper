Blumea megacephala

What's the taxonomical classification of Blumea megacephala?

Blumea megacephala belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Asterales. This plant is a member of the family Asteraceae, which places it within the genus Blumea, and it is specifically identified by the species name megacephala.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Blumea megacephala has an erect, perennial herb reaching up to 2 meters in height, characterized by large, ovate, serrated leaves and dense, discoid flower heads covered in white, woolly tomentum. The foliage is typically thick and soft to the touch due to a layer of fine hairs on both the upper and lower surfaces. Its stems are woody at the base and often exhibit a spreading growth habit. The inflorescence consists of numerous small, tubular florets tightly packed into globose heads that are heavily obscured by white pubescence. These floral structures are arranged in terminal clusters, providing a distinctively fuzzy appearance to the plant.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant Blumea megacephala is used in Zhuang medicine and other traditional Southeast Asian practices to treat ailments such as fever, indigestion, and skin infections. Practitioners often prepare decoctions from the leaves to induce perspiration and alleviate respiratory symptoms. The aromatic essential oils within the foliage are also utilized in topical applications to soothe inflammatory conditions. Historically, different parts of the plant have been integrated into folk remedies to regulate digestive functions. This widespread medicinal application reflects its long-standing importance in regional ethnobotanical knowledge.

For protocatechuic acid, the linear relationship was in the range of 1.7-17 mg x L(-1) with an average recovery of 103.4%. Chloroic acid showed a range of 15.6-156 mg x L(-1) with an average recovery of 102.2%. Caffeic acid concentrations ranged from 3.96-39.6 mg x L(-1) with an average recovery of 98.5%.
