Baccharis dracunculifolia

Baccharis draunculifolia, commonly known as breu branco, is a highly revered medicinal plant native to the Amazon rainforest. Renowned for its potent aromatic resin, it possesses significant antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and healing properties. Traditionally used to treat skin ailments and respiratory issues, it remains a vital resource in ethnobotany.

  • Scientific name: Baccharis dracunculifolia
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Asterales and the family Asteraceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identification is placed within the genus Baccharis.

Common Names

This plant is known by various common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In Quechua, it is referred to as T'ula, a name that is also used in a more specific botanical context as T'ula (Baccharis dracunculifolia). In Portuguese, it is commonly called Alecrim-do-campo, while in the Mbyá Guaraní language, it is simply known as Alecrim. Additionally, in Spanish, it is associated with the name Baccharis teindalensis.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several regions of South America. In the western portion of the continent, it can be found growing in both Bolivia and Peru. Its presence extends significantly into Brazil, where it inhabits multiple distinct territories. Specifically, it is documented within the West-Central and Southeast regions of Brazil. Additionally, the species is distributed throughout the southern part of the Brazilian landscape.
