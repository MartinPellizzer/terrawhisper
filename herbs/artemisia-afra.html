Artemisia afra

Artemisia afra, commonly known as African wormwood, is a highly versatile medicinal shrub native to Southern Africa. Renowned for its potent aromatic properties, this plant is widely utilized in traditional healing to treat respiratory ailments, fevers, and digestive issues, serving as a vital cornerstone in many indigenous pharmacopeias.

  • Scientific name: Artemisia afra
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales. Finally, Artemisia afra is a member of the family Asteraceae and the genus Artemisia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution that spans multiple continents and climatic zones. In Middle Europe, its presence can be identified within the borders of Germany. Moving toward the northeast, it is also found across the tropical landscapes of Ethiopia. The species extends significantly into South Tropical Africa, where it grows in Angola and Zambia. Finally, its range reaches further into the southern regions of the continent within Zimbabwe.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Artemisia afra, is primarily valued for its therapeutic properties found within its aerial parts. The most significant medicinal components are concentrated in the leaves, which are often harvested to prepare traditional remedies. Each individual leaf contains essential oils that contribute to the plant's aromatic profile. In addition to the plant leaves, the stems also form part of the harvested material, though the foliage remains the most potent element of the species.
