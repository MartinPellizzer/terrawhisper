The yew genus, scientifically known as Taxus, is a group of slow-growing evergreen conifers renowned for their longevity and striking appearance. While historically revered in folklore, these plants are pharmacologically significant; they contain potent alkaloids, most notably paclitaxel, which serves as a critical foundation for modern cancer therapies.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Yew, possesses several distinct anatomical features that are studied for their medicinal properties. The most recognizable parts are the dark green needles, which are actually specialized leaves that grow in a dense, linear fashion along the branches. The structural integrity of the plant is provided by its woody stems, which support the weight of the foliage. For various pharmacological applications, researchers often examine the bark and the stem barks, as these areas contain potent alkaloids like paclitaxel, which are essential in cancer treatments.
