Ipomoea asarifolia

Ipomoea asarifolia, commonly known as the morning glory variety, is a versatile medicinal plant valued in traditional healing practices. Characterized by its climbing vines and delicate blooms, it possesses significant bioactive properties. Often utilized for its anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial potential, this botanical offers promising therapeutic benefits for holistic wellness.

  • Scientific name: Ipomoea asarifolia
  • Family: Convolvulaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, specifically classified under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Solanales, it is a member of the family Convolvulaceae. Its scientific classification concludes with the genus Ipomoea, identifying it as Ipomoea asarifolia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a notable geographical distribution spanning both Atlantic islands and the African mainland. Within the Macaronesian region, it can be found growing in Cape Verde. Its presence extends significantly into West Tropical Africa across several different nations. Specifically, the species is documented in Burkina, Gambia, and Ghana. Additionally, its range includes the coastal areas of Guinea.

Plant Parts

This plant, Ipomoea asarifolia, is a climbing herbaceous species where the leaves serve as a primary component for medicinal application. The leaves are typically characterized by their cordate or sagittate shapes and are utilized in various traditional remedies to treat ailments such as skin inflammation and digestive issues. Because the leaves contain bioactive compounds, they are often prepared as poultices or infusions to harness their therapeutic potential.
