Heracleum dissectifolium

What's the taxonomical classification of Heracleum dissectifolium?

Heracleum dissectifolium belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the hierarchical structure of botanical classification, it falls under the order Apiales and is a member of the family Apiaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by its placement within the genus Heracleum, resulting in its unique binomial name, the species dissectifolium.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Heracleum dissectifolium has large, deeply divided compound leaves that are bipinnate or tripinnate in structure, featuring serrated leaflets with distinctively lobed edges. The foliage typically grows from a basal rosette and can reach significant sizes due to the complex branching of its petioles. These long stems are often hollow and covered in fine, coarse hairs that provide a textured surface. The plant produces large, flat-topped umbels composed of numerous small white flowers arranged in a broad, spreading cluster. This architectural structure supports heavy seed heads that develop once the flowering period concludes.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe skin irritation, phototoxicity, blistering, burns, and erythema upon contact with human tissue. The primary danger arises from furanocoumarins contained within the plant's sap which react intensely to ultraviolet light. Once the sap is on the skin, exposure to sunlight triggers a chemical reaction that leads to painful erythema. This inflammatory response often progresses into significant blistering and physical burns. These dermatological reactions can be quite severe and may last for several days or even weeks.
