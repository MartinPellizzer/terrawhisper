Physiology

The physiology of Kalanchoe petitiana is fundamentally defined by its specialized photosynthetic pathway, specifically utilizing Crassulacean Acid Metabolism (CAM). This metabolic strategy allows the plant to optimize water-use efficiency by decoupling the processes of carbon dioxide uptake and light-dependent reactions. During the nocturnal period, the plant opens its stomata to facilitate the diffusion of $CO_2$ into the mesophyll cells, where it is fixed by the enzyme phosphoenolpyruvate carboxylase (PEPC) into organic acids, primarily malate, which are then stored within large central vacuoles. During the subsequent diurnal cycle, the stomata remain tightly closed to prevent transpirational water loss in response to environmental stressors; simultaneously, the stored malate is decarboxylated to release $CO_2$ internally, allowing the Calvin cycle to proceed using the energy captured from sunlight. This CAM pathway is a critical physiological adaptation that enables the species to thrive in arid or semi-arid environments by maintaining high photosynthetic productivity while minimizing desiccation.
