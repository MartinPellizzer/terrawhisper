Centaurea napifolia

Centaurea napiifolia, commonly known as field knapweed, is a resilient herbaceous plant native to Europe. Valued in traditional herbalism for its diverse phytochemical properties, it is often studied for its potential anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects. This botanical species offers unique therapeutic possibilities within the vast Centaurea genus.

  • Scientific name: Centaurea napifolia
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, placing it within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Asterales and the family Asteraceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Centaurea, which includes the specific species Centaurea napifolia.

Common Names

This plant, Centauria napifolia, is known by various common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In English, it is frequently referred to as pink knapweed, while in German, it is commonly called rosa Flockenblume. In French, the species is known as centaurée napole, and in Italian, it is called centaurio napole.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution primarily concentrated within the Mediterranean region. In Southwestern Europe, its presence is documented across several key territories, including France and the Balearic Islands. It can also be found on the islands of Corsica and Sardinia. Furthermore, the species extends its range into Southeastern Europe, specifically within Italy. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's preference for the diverse landscapes of the southern European coastlines.
