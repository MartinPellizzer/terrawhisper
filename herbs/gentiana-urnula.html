Gentiana urnula

What's the taxonomical classification of Gentiana urnula?

Gentiana urnula belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Gentianaales. The plant is a representative of the family Gentianaceae and is further categorized into the genus Gentiana, with its specific designation being the species urnula.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Gentiana urnula has erect, unbranched stems that reach heights of 10 to 30 centimeters and bear opposite, lanceolate leaves with entire margins. The foliage is typically dark green and possesses a smooth, somewhat fleshy texture. Its most distinguishing feature is the solitary, urn-shaped flowers that emerge from the upper leaf axils. These blossoms are deep blue to violet in color and feature a characteristic funnel or bell-shaped corolla. The plant often produces small, rounded seed capsules following successful pollination.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires a cool, moist, and humus-rich environment with dappled shade to thrive in cultivation.

It should be planted in well-draining soil that remains consistently damp but never waterlogged to prevent root rot. Because it is a slow-growing species, it benefits from being placed in a container or a protected rock garden setting. What are the pharmacological activities of Gentiana urnula?

This plant has antioxidative pharmacological activities driven by its rich concentration of bioactive secondary metabolites. These natural compounds work by neutralizing free radicals and reducing oxidative stress within biological systems. Research indicates that the specific chemical profile of Gentiana urnula contributes to protective cellular mechanisms. Such properties suggest potential therapeutic applications in managing conditions linked to oxidative damage. Consequently, the plant serves as a significant subject for studying natural antioxidant sources.

According to a study published by "Natural product research", three known iridoid glucosides, gentiournoside A, gentiournoside E, and depressoside, were isolated from the flowers of Gentiana urnula Harry Sm. through activity-guided fractionations using a 1,1-diphenyl-2-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) assay. All three extracted compounds exhibited excellent DPPH radical scavenging activities with IC50 values of 10-20 μmol L(-1). Among these, depressoside showed a much higher scavenging activity against superoxide radicals with an IC50 of 45.5 μmol L(-1). This activity was significantly greater than the other two extracted compounds, which had an IC50 of more than 900 μmol L(-1).
