Clematis hirsuta

Clematis hirsuta, commonly known as the hairy clematis, is a climbing perennial native to various regions. Characterized by its fine, velvety hairs and delicate blooms, this plant holds significance in traditional herbalism. It is often studied for its unique bioactive compounds and potential therapeutic applications in holistic medicine.

  • Scientific name: Clematis hirsuta
  • Family: Ranunculaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Ranunculales, it is classified under the family Ranunculaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic designation is within the genus Clematis.

Common Names

This plant, Clematis hirsuta, is known by various regional names across different languages and cultures. In Kinyarwanda, it is referred to as Umukamba w'igikasa, while in Arabic, it is commonly called ظيانة (and similarly known as ظيانه in Egyptian Arabic). In Amharic, the plant is identified as ኣዞ ሓረግ, and in Hausa, it is known as Yamanza.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning across several distinct regions. In Northern Africa, it can be found growing in the territories of Algeria and Libya. Its distribution extends into the Macaronesian region, specifically within Cape Verde. Furthermore, the species is present in West Tropical Africa, where it inhabits Benin. Finally, its range includes the landlocked nation of Burkina Faso.
