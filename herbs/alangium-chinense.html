Reproduction

The reproduction of Alangium chinense is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, primarily driven by insect activity. The plant produces inflorescences that are relatively small, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 0.03 m to a maximum of 0.04 m. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fleshy fruits that vary in size, typically maintaining a mean length of 0.6 cm (ranging from 0.5 cm to 0.7 cm) and a mean width of 0.65 cm (ranging from 0.5 cm to 0.8 cm). The dispersal of the offspring follows a zoochorous syndrome, where animals play a key role in spreading the seeds. The seeds themselves are quite consistent in mass, with a mean, minimum, and maximum value of 0.05208 g. Morphologically, the seeds have a mean length of 7.8 mm, a maximum width of 5.1 mm, and a maximum height of 3.5 mm, with a consistent seed volume of 170 mm³.
