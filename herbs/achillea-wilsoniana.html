Achillea wilsoniana

What's the taxonomical classification of Achillea wilsoniana?

Achillea wilsoniana belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Asterales. This plant is a part of the family Asteraceae, which is a large and diverse group of flowering plants. Specifically, it is categorized under the genus Achillea, with its unique designation being the species wilsoniana.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Achillea wilsoniana has finely divided, fern-like foliage consisting of grey-green leaves that grow in dense basal clumps. The stems are upright and woody at the base, eventually supporting flat-topped clusters of small, daisy-like flower heads. These composite flowers typically display bright yellow ray florets surrounding central disc florets. The plant's texture is highly aromatic and feathery due to the deeply lobed structure of its leaves. This combination of delicate foliage and compact flowering structures makes it a distinct ornamental perennial.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires full sun and well-drained, sandy, or loamy soil to thrive in most garden settings.

It is a drought-tolerant perennial that performs best once its extensive root system is established. Regular deadheading during the growing season will encourage continuous blooms of its yellow flower clusters. You should avoid overwatering or planting in heavy clay to prevent root rot.

Once mature, this species is relatively low-maintenance and can tolerate various soil types as long as drainage remains adequate.
