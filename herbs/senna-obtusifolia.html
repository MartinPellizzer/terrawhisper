American Sicklepod

Senna obtusifolia · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (576 studies) · ★★★★★

American Sicklepod, scientifically known as Senna obtusifolia, is a versatile medicinal herb native to the Americas. Renowned for its potent laxative properties, this plant contains anthraquinone glycosides used to treat constipation. Beyond digestion, it holds significant value in traditional ethnobotany for treating skin conditions and various inflammatory ailments.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range that spans across multiple continents and climatic zones. In Middle Europe, its presence can be documented in Belgium, while it also grows within the Mediterranean landscapes of Spain in Southwestern Europe. Moving toward the African continent, the species is found in the regions of Libya within Northern Africa. Its distribution extends into the Atlantic through the Macaronesian islands of Cape Verde. Finally, the plant reaches the tropical reaches of West Africa, specifically within the borders of Benin.

Plant Parts

This plant, Senna obtusifolia, possesses various components that are utilized in traditional medicine, particularly through its reproductive and underground structures. The seeds of the species are of significant interest, with the individual seed often being harvested for its potent laxative properties. In botanical and pharmaceutical contexts, these are frequently referred to by their Latin designation, Semen Cassiae. Additionally, the plant features hairy roots that contribute to its overall biological profile, making the entire specimen a subject of study for its medicinal potential.
