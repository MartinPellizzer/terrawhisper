The physiology of Amelanchier alnifolia is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, a metabolic strategy common among many woody shrubs and trees that utilize the Calvin cycle to fix atmospheric carbon dioxide. Within this pathway, the initial carboxylation of CO2 is mediated by the enzyme ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase (RuBisCO) in the mesophyll cells, leading to the production of three-carbon compounds. This carbon fixation process is highly dependent on environmental factors such as light intensity, temperature, and water availability, which influence the efficiency of gas exchange through the stomata. Because it lacks the specialized carbon-concentrating mechanisms found in C4 or CAM plants, its physiological performance is closely tied to maintaining optimal hydration to prevent photorespiration, a process that occurs when RuBisCO reacts with oxygen instead of carbon dioxide, particularly under high-temperature or drought-stress conditions.
