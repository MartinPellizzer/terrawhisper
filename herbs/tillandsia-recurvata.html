The physiology of Tillandsia recurvata is characterized by specialized adaptations for survival in xeric and epiphytic environments, most notably through its use of the CAM (Crassulacean Acid Metabolism) photosynthetic pathway. By utilizing this CAM pathway, the plant can decouple carbon fixation from light-dependent reactions, allowing it to open its stomata primarily at night to absorb carbon dioxide while minimizing transpirational water loss during the heat of the day. This metabolic strategy is closely integrated with its morphological features, specifically the presence of dense, specialized multicellular trichomes on the leaf surface.
