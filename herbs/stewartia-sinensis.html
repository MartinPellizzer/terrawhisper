Botanical Identification

Morphology

The morphology of Stewartia sinensis is characterized by a woody growth habit, forming a deciduous shrub or small tree that typically reaches heights of 3 to 7 meters. Its trunk and branches exhibit a distinctively smooth, reddish-brown to grayish bark that often peels in thin, papery flakes, revealing a lighter inner surface. The branching pattern is spreading, supporting a canopy of simple, alternate leaves that are typically ovate to elliptical in shape, featuring finely serrated margins and acute tips. The leaf texture is somewhat leathery, often displaying a glossy upper surface with prominent pinnate venation. During the flowering season, the plant produces terminal clusters of beautiful, five-petaled flowers that range in color from pure white to delicate pink. These flowers feature numerous prominent stamens that lend a fringed appearance to the blossom. The fruit is a small, dehiscent capsule that eventually splits to release tiny, winged seeds, facilitating wind dispersal.
