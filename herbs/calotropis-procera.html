Sodomís apple milkweed

Calotropis procera · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (463 studies) · ★★★★★

Sodomís apple milkweed, scientifically known as Calotropis procera, is a versatile perennial shrub native to arid regions. Renowned in traditional medicine, its leaves, latex, and roots possess potent antibacterial and anti-inflammatory properties. This resilient plant offers significant therapeutic potential for treating various ailments, ranging from skin conditions to inflammation.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Calotropis procera is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, primarily facilitated by insects. The plant produces inflorescences in the form of an umbel, featuring flowers that exhibit a distinct violet colour. The flowering period typically begins in January and concludes in July, though it can be variable. Reproduction is dependent on cross-pollination, as self-fertilization is absent. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fruit classified as a follicle, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 8 cm to a maximum of 12 cm. The seeds produced within these follicles vary in mass, with a mean weight of 0.010136 g, a minimum of 0.002812 g, and a maximum of 0.023237 g. Once mature, the seeds are distributed via an anemochorous dispersal syndrome, utilizing wind to spread throughout the environment.
