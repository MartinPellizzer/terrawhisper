Calotropis procera

Sodomís apple milkweed

Calotropis procera, commonly known as giant milkweed or apple of Sodom, is a resilient perennial shrub found in arid regions. Renowned in traditional medicine, its diverse chemical constituents offer significant therapeutic potential, particularly for treating inflammation, skin ailments, and digestive issues, making it a vital botanical resource worldwide.

  • Scientific name: Calotropis procera
  • Family: Apocynaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Gentianaales, it is situated in the family Apocynaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Calotropis.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several countries located within the Northern Africa region. It can be found growing naturally in the landscapes of Algeria. Additionally, the species is well-established throughout the territory of Egypt. Its presence extends further west to include the nation of Libya. Finally, this medicinal plant is also commonly documented in both Morocco and Tunisia.

Plant Parts

This plant, Calotropis procera, is a versatile medicinal species where almost every component offers therapeutic potential. The most distinctive feature is the milky latex that exudes from the stems and foliage, which is widely used in traditional medicine for treating skin ailments and inflammation. The large, ovate leaves (or a single broad leaf) are frequently applied topically as poultices to reduce swelling or used in decoctions. The plant also produces striking, waxy flowers that are characteristic of the species. Furthermore, the root is highly valued in various herbal formulations, often used for its potent properties in treating various systemic conditions.
