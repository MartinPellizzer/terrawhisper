Cotton Thistle, scientifically known as Onopordum acanthium, is a striking perennial herb celebrated for its impressive height and silver-green foliage. Renowned in traditional medicine, it possesses potent anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, often utilized to support digestive health, liver function, and overall metabolic wellness through its diverse bioactive compounds.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Onopordum acanthium is characterized by a sexual reproductive cycle that follows a monocarpous pattern, with asexual reproduction being absent. The flowering period typically commences in July and extends through September. This process relies on biotic pollination syndromes, specifically utilizing insects such as bees to facilitate fertilization, although self-fertilization is also present. Following successful pollination, the plant produces indehiscent fruits categorized as achenes. These fruits maintain a consistent length of approximately 0.6 cm.
