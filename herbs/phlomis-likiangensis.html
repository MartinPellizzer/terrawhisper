Phlomis likiangensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Phlomis likiangensis?

Phlomis likiangensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the biological lineage of the order Lamiales. It is further categorized under the family Lamiaceae, which characterizes its botanical structure, and falls under the genus Phlomis. Finally, the specific identification of this plant is completed by its species designation, likiangensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Phlomis likiangensis has a perennial, subshrubby growth habit characterized by woody bases, densely tomentose grey-green leaves, and verticillaster inflorescences of hooded, bilabiate flowers. The foliage consists of large, ovate to oblong leaves that are covered in thick, woolly hairs to reduce moisture loss. Its flowering stems rise prominently above the foliage, bearing several whorls of tubular flowers that are typically pale yellow or cream in color. These flowers feature a distinct upper lip that protects the reproductive organs, a common trait among the Lamiaceae family. The overall structure of the plant is robust and bushy, adapted to the rocky, Mediterranean-style environments it inhabits.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is endemic to the Lycian region of southwestern Turkey, specifically inhabiting the Mediterranean coastal mountains. It typically grows within limestone-rich rocky outcrops and scrubby Mediterranean maquis vegetation. Its presence is restricted to specific altitudinal zones where seasonal moisture and rocky terrain meet. This narrow range makes the species a specialized component of the local Turkish flora. What are the pharmacological activities of Phlomis likiangensis?

This plant has significant pharmacological activities including vasodilatory, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties. The vasodilatory effects of Phlomis likiangensis contribute to its potential in managing cardiovascular conditions by relaxing smooth muscles in blood vessels. Research suggests that its bioactive compounds also play a role in reducing systemic inflammation and oxidative stress within the body. These diverse therapeutic actions make the species a subject of interest for developing new medicinal treatments. Such multifaceted biological responses highlight the medicinal value of its chemical constituents.

According to a study published by "Fitoterapia", Phlomis likiangensis is traditionally used in China to activate collaterals and protect the cardiovascular system. Twelve known iridoid glucosides, labeled 1-12, were isolated from Phlomis likiangensis to investigate their pharmacological properties. These 12 iridoid glucosides were found to significantly increase vasodilatation in rat aortic rings. This vasodilatory effect was abolished by removing the endothelium of the vessels or by eliminating the generation of nitric oxide. These findings provide the first evidence that the iridoid glucosides of Phlomis likiangensis may serve as the pharmacodynamic basis for its traditional efficacy.
