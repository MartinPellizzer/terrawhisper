Cynomorium coccineum subsp. songaricum

Cynomorium coccineum subsp. songaricum, commonly known as the red cynomorium, is a unique holoparasitic plant found in arid regions. Lacking chlorophyll, it derives nutrients from host roots. Highly valued in traditional medicine, particularly in East Asia, it is utilized for its purported tonic properties and ability to enhance vitality.

  • Scientific name: Cynomorium coccineum subsp. songaricum
  • Family: Cynomoriaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Saxifragales, it is classified under the family Cynomoriaceae and the genus Cynomorium. Specifically, it is identified as the subspecies songaricum.

Common Names

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions of Northern and Central Asia. In the northern reaches of its range, it can be found within the Altai region of Siberia. Moving southward, the species is widely distributed throughout the vast landscapes of Kazakhstan. Its presence continues through the mountainous terrains of Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan. Finally, the distribution extends to the arid environments of Turkmenistan, completing its presence in Central Asia.

Plant Parts

This plant is a unique holoparasitic species characterized by its lack of chlorophyll and specialized morphological structures. It lacks true leaves and roots, instead developing a thick, fleshy, and succulent stem that emerges from the ground to absorb nutrients from host plants. The most prominent feature of the plant is its large, dense inflorescence, which consists of numerous small, reddish flowers clustered together on the central axis. These succulent stems and specialized inflorescences are the primary parts used in traditional medicinal practices.
