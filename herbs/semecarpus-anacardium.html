Semecarpus anacardium

Anacardium semecarpus

Semecarpus anacardium, commonly known as the marking nut, is a medicinal plant native to South Asia. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, it is extensively used in Ayurveda to treat skin diseases, inflammation, and neurological disorders. Its unique properties make it a vital component in traditional healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Semecarpus anacardium
  • Family: Anacardiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Sapindales, it is classified under the family Anacardiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Semecarpus.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the cashew, possesses several components utilized in various medicinal and culinary applications. The most recognizable parts are the cashew nuts, which are harvested from the accessory fruit, though the true botanical nut is found within the fleshy cashew apple. Beyond the fruit, the stem bark is often employed in traditional medicine for its astringent properties. The seeds contained within the shell are highly potent, and various leaf extracts are frequently studied for their bioactive compounds and potential therapeutic benefits.
