Mangrove, scientifically known as Avicennia officinalis, is a resilient halophyte essential to coastal ecosystems. Renowned for its medicinal properties, this plant contains diverse bioactive compounds with potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial activities. Traditionally used in various cultures, it offers significant therapeutic potential for treating numerous ailments and skin conditions.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions of Asia. Within the Indian Subcontinent, it is widely documented in Bangladesh, India, and Sri Lanka. Its presence also extends into the Indo-China region, specifically spanning the Andaman Islands. Furthermore, the species can be found growing in the tropical landscapes of Cambodia. These scattered locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in varied environments across these territories.

Plant Parts

This plant, Avicennia officinalis, commonly known as the grey mangrove, utilizes its various anatomical structures for both survival in saline environments and medicinal purposes. The leaf is a primary component used in traditional medicine, often prepared as a decoction to treat ailments such as skin inflammation, fever, and digestive issues due to its rich bioactive compounds. Additionally, the bark of the tree is highly valued for its astringent properties and is frequently employed in herbal remedies to address wounds, diarrhea, and other inflammatory conditions.
