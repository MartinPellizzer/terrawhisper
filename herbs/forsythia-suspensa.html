Forsythia suspensa

Forsythia suspensa, commonly known as Goldenrain tree, is a versatile medicinal plant highly valued in traditional Chinese medicine. Renowned for its anti-inflammatory and antibacterial properties, its dried flowers and stems are frequently used to treat fevers, coughs, and skin infections, offering significant therapeutic benefits in holistic healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Forsythia suspensa
  • Family: Oleaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales, it is classified under the family Oleaceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Forsythia and the species Forsythia suspensa.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Forsythia suspensa, is referred to by various common names depending on the language and region. In German, it is called Hänge-Forsythie, while in Czech, it is known as zlatice převislá. Its Polish name is forsycja zwisła, and in Chinese, it is commonly called 连翘 (liánqiào). Additionally, in Cantonese, it is affectionately referred to as 一串金 (jat1 cyun3 gam1).

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical presence across several regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its distribution includes the islands of Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward Middle Europe, the species can be found growing within Germany and the Czechia-Slovakia region. Additionally, the plant extends its reach into Southwestern Europe, specifically within Spain. Plant Parts

This plant, Forsythia suspensa, utilizes various components for its medicinal properties, primarily focusing on the woody structures and reproductive organs. The stems, often referred to as Lianqiao in traditional medicine, are the most commonly harvested part used for their therapeutic effects. As the plant matures, it produces small, oblong fruits that contain the essential seeds necessary for reproduction. Once the fruit develops, it eventually dehisces to release these seeds, completing the plant's life cycle. While the leaves are a prominent part of the plant's foliage, they are less frequently used in clinical applications compared to the stems and the dried fruit.
