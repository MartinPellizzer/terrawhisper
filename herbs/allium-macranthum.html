Allium macranthum

What's the taxonomical classification of Allium macranthum?

Allium macranthum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the hierarchical structure of biological classification, it falls under the order Asparagales and is a member of the family Amaryllidaceae. Finally, the plant is defined by its specific genus, Allium, and its unique species designation, macranthum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Allium macranthum has upright, hollow stems that reach heights of up to 60 centimeters and terminate in large, spherical umbels containing numerous star-shaped, pale pink to white flowers. The leaves are linear, basal, and often slightly channeled, providing a structural contrast to the flowering stalk. Each individual flower possesses six tepals that spread widely to reveal prominent stamens. The bulb is typically globose and serves as the primary underground storage organ for the plant. These morphological features collectively distinguish this species within its genus.

This plant is native to the western regions of North America, specifically spanning from southern British Columbia through Washington and Oregon into northern California.
