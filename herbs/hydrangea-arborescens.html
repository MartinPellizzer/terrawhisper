Physiology

The physiology of Hydrangea arborescens is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, a mechanism typical of most woody deciduous shrubs inhabiting temperate forest understories. In this process, carbon dioxide is fixed directly into a three-carbon compound, 3-phosphoglycerate, via the enzyme RuBisCO during the Calvin cycle. Because it utilizes the C3 pathway, the plant relies on moderate light intensities and consistent moisture availability to optimize gas exchange and minimize photorespiration. The metabolic efficiency of this pathway supports the plant's seasonal growth cycles, allowing for efficient biomass accumulation during the spring and summer months when water is sufficiently available in the soil to maintain turgor pressure and facilitate transpiration-driven nutrient uptake.
