Dragon tree, scientifically known as Dracaena draco, is a legendary evergreen species native to the Macaronesian islands. Renowned for its thick, succulent trunk and striking architectural canopy, it produces a vivid red resin called "dragon's blood." This storied plant holds significant historical value in traditional medicine and ancient folklore.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Dracaena draco is characterized by a sexual process occurring in dioecious individuals, meaning male and female flowers are found on separate plants. The plant produces green flowers arranged in a panicle inflorescence, which are subject to biotic pollination, specifically via an insect syndrome. The flowering period is highly variable but typically spans from December through September. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fleshy, indehiscent berries that are brown in color. These fruits are relatively uniform in size, measuring approximately 1 cm in both length and width. Each berry is highly prolific, containing more than 1,000 seeds. The seeds themselves have a mean mass of approximately 0.344915 g and a consistent volume of 390 mm³. Dispersal of the seeds is achieved through a zoochorous syndrome, specifically endozoochory, where animals ingest the fleshy fruit and disperse the seeds internally.
