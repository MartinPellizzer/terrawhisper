Dragon tree, scientifically known as Dracaena draco, is a legendary evergreen species native to the Macaronesian islands. Renowned for its thick, succulent trunk and striking architectural canopy, it produces a vivid red resin called "dragon's blood." This storied plant holds significant historical value in traditional medicine and ancient folklore.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asparagales. Finally, it resides in the family Asparagaceae and is identified by the genus Dracaena.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a distinct geographical distribution spanning several regions across the Atlantic and Mediterranean. In Southwestern Europe, its presence can be found within the Baleares and Sardegna archipelagos. Moving toward the African continent, it is also established in Tunisia. The species extends its range into Macaronesia, specifically inhabiting the Azores. Finally, it is widely distributed throughout the Canary Is. within the same Macaronesian region.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Dragon Blood Tree, is highly valued for its unique medicinal components derived from various structures. The most significant substance is the deep red resin, often referred to as "dragon's blood," which is extracted from the inner layers of the tree. This precious exudate is obtained by making incisions in the bark or specifically targeting the stem bark of the plant. In addition to these woody elements, the aerial parts of the tree are also studied for their potential pharmacological properties, contributing to the overall medicinal profile of this remarkable species.
