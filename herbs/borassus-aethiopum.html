Borassus aethiopum

African fan palm

Borassus aethiopum, commonly known as the Palmyra palm, is a versatile tropical plant central to many traditional healing practices. Renowned for its medicinal properties, various parts of the tree are utilized to treat ailments ranging from digestive issues to skin conditions, offering significant ethnobotanical value across many African regions.

  • Scientific name: Borassus aethiopum
  • Family: Arecaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Arecales, falling into the family Arecaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Borassus.

Common Names

This plant, Borassus aethiopum, is known by various regional names across different languages and cultures. In German, it is referred to as the Äthiopische Palmyrapalme, while in Amharic, it is called ዘምባባ. In Hausa, the plant is commonly known as Giginya, and in Swahili, it is identified as Mvumo wa Afrika. Additionally, in Arabic, it is called بوراس أثيوبي.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical range concentrated within the West Tropical Africa region. Its distribution includes significant populations found in Benin and Burkina. It is also documented to grow within the borders of Gambia. Furthermore, the species can be located throughout the territory of Ghana. Finally, its presence extends into the coastal nation of Guinea-Bissau.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the African Palmyra palm, offers various medicinal and nutritional benefits derived from its different anatomical structures. The root is often utilized in traditional practices for its various therapeutic properties, while the large, fan-shaped leaf can be processed for diverse uses. The inflorescences serve as the reproductive structures that eventually give rise to the edible components. Within the resulting fruit, the fruit pulp provides a nutritious substance, and the internal kernels are highly valued both as a food source and for their potential medicinal applications.
