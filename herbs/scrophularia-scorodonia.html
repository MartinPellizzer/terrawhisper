Balm-leaved Figwort, scientifically known as Scrophularia scorodonia, is a distinctive perennial herb native to damp, shady woodlands. Characterized by its serrated, aromatic foliage and tubular purple flowers, this plant holds historical significance in traditional herbalism, often utilized for its purported soothing properties and medicinal applications in folk medicine.

The reproduction of Scrophularia scorodonia is characterized by a sexual process involving bisexual flowers, which ensures the potential for genetic diversity through biotic pollination. Following successful pollination, the plant develops a brown capsule fruit, which is a dehiscent structure. The individual seeds are extremely lightweight, with a mean mass of 0.000129 g (ranging from a minimum of 0.00009 g to a maximum of 0.00017 g), a trait that facilitates an anemochorous dispersal syndrome, allowing the seeds to be carried effectively by the wind.
