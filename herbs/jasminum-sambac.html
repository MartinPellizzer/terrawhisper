Jasminum sambac

What's the taxonomical classification of Jasminum sambac?

Jasminum sambac belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Lamiales. This aromatic plant is a member of the family Oleaceae, specifically falling under the genus Jasminum, with its unique biological identity defined by the species name sambac.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Jasminum sambac has woody stems that form evergreen shrubs or small trees reaching heights of up to three meters, featuring opposite, ovate leaves with entire margins and acute tips. The foliage typically presents a glossy, dark green upper surface and a lighter underside, often arranged in pairs along the branches. Its most distinctive morphological feature is the fragrant, white, salverform flowers that emerge from axillary cymes. These blossoms consist of multiple layers of petals, ranging from single-flowered to highly double varieties. The plant also develops small, fleshy berries that transition from green to red upon maturity.

According to a study published by "Natural product communications", the research focused on the extraction and characterization of volatile compounds in intact and plucked flowers of Jasminum sambac. The identified analytes included alcohols, esters, phenolic compounds, and terpenoids. Major aroma contributors such as cis-3-hexenyl acetate, linalool, and benzyl acetate were identified using PDMS/DVB, Carboxen/PDMDS, and DVB/Carboxen/PDMS fibers. The study utilized various fibers to monitor emissions, noting that PDMS/DVB recorded the highest emission for benzyl acetate. Additionally, the highest linalool content was observed increasing from morning to noon using mixed coating fibers.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains medicinal compounds including (E)-beta-ocimene, benzyl acetate, indole, linalool, and oleanolic acid. These chemical constituents contribute to the plant's diverse pharmacological properties and therapeutic potential. The presence of linalool and benzyl acetate is often associated with the soothing aromatic qualities of the jasmine flower. Additionally, oleanolic acid provides significant anti-inflammatory and antioxidant benefits to the organism. These specific molecules work in combination to define the plant's unique biochemical profile.

According to a study published by "Natural product communications", the volatile organic compounds identified in Jasminum sambac include alcohols, esters, phenolic compounds, and terpenoids. The main constituents identified in the flower aroma are cis-3-hexenyl acetate, (E)-beta-ocimene, linalool, benzyl acetate, and (E,E)-alpha-farnesene. Major aroma contributors in plucked and living flower extracts include cis-3-hexenyl acetate, linalool, and benzyl acetate when using PDMS/DVB, Carboxen/PDMS, and DVB/Carboxen/PDMS fibers. The PDMS fiber showed a high proportion of (E,E)-alpha-farnesene, while PDMS/DVB recorded the highest emission for benzyl acetate. In terms of fiber performance, the highest amounts extracted based on the sum of peak areas were achieved using Carboxen/PDMS and DVB/Carboxen/PDMS.

The study also evaluates effects on anxiety, depression, cognitive hypofunction, epilepsy, and convulsions. Essential oils have been shown to be effective on several pathological targets and have improved cognitive performance in animal models and human subjects.
