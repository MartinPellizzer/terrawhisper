Scientific literature: Very Extensive (772 studies) · ★★★★★

Mistletoe, scientifically known as Viscum album, is a fascinating hemiparasitic shrub found across Europe and Asia. Traditionally utilized in various folk medicines, it is noted for its unique ability to thrive on host trees. Modern research explores its bioactive compounds for potential therapeutic applications in cardiovascular and immune health.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further organized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Santalales, falling into the family Santalaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Viscum, specifically known by the species name Viscum album.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as mistletoe, is utilized in various traditional medicinal practices where the whole plant is often considered for its complex bioactive properties. In many herbal preparations, the planta tota is employed to ensure a complete profile of its constituent compounds. Specifically, the fresh mistletoe is frequently harvested to maintain high potency before it is processed into tinctures or extracts. While the entire organism is significant, the medicinal efficacy is often concentrated within the leaf structures, as the leaves contain the specific viscotoxins and lectins required for its pharmacological effects.
