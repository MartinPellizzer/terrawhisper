What's the taxonomical classification of Selaginella pulvinata?

Selaginella pulvinaata belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is classified in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Lycopodiidae. Its taxonomic progression continues through the order Selaginellales and the family Selaginellaceae, eventually placing it within the genus Selaginella. Ultimately, the specific identity of this plant is defined by its species designation, pulvinaata.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Selaginella pulvinaata has a creeping rhizomatous habit with small, scale-like leaves arranged in four distinct rows along each flattened, branched stem. The leaves are diminutive and triangular, featuring a central midrib that gives the foliage a textured, slightly ruffled appearance. These tiny leaves are often densely packed, creating a moss-like cushion that spreads across the substrate. The plant exhibits a unique ability to curl its fronds inward during periods of dehydration to protect its delicate tissues. Once moisture returns, the stems unfurl to resume their characteristic spreading growth pattern.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires high humidity, consistent moisture, and bright, indirect light to thrive in a stable environment.

Because it is a resurrection fern, it can survive periods of dehydration by curling inward, but it will recover most effectively with regular misting. The soil should be a well-draining peat-based mix that stays damp without becoming waterlogged. Maintaining a warm room temperature between sixty and seventy-five degrees Fahrenheit is essential for its growth.

Avoid placing the fern in direct sunlight, as this can scorch its delicate fronds.

What are the pharmacological activities of Selaginella pulvinata?

This plant has significant pharmacological activities including potent α-glucosidase inhibitory activity, antioxidant properties, and anti-inflammatory effects. The inhibition of the α-glucosidase enzyme suggests that extracts from Selaginella pulvinata may play a role in managing postprandial blood glucose levels in diabetic models. Beyond glucose regulation, the plant contains bioactive compounds like flavonoids and terpenoids that combat oxidative stress within cellular environments. These secondary metabolites contribute to its ability to reduce systemic inflammation and protect tissues from damage. Research into these diverse biological functions highlights the medicinal potential of this species in modern drug development.

According to a study published by "Chemistry & biodiversity", three new selaginelliin derivatives named selaginpulvilins V-X (1-3) were isolated from whole plants of Selaginella pulvinata. These three new derivatives, along with seven known analogs (4-10), were identified through spectroscopic methods. Biological evaluation of these compounds revealed specific pharmacological activities against α-glucosidase. Specifically, compounds 2, 6 and 8 displayed moderate inhibition with IC50 values of 3.71, 2.04 and 4.00 μM, respectively.
