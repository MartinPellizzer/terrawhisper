Glycyrrhiza pallidiflora

What's the taxonomical classification of Glycyrrhiza pallidiflora?

Glycyrrhiza pallidiflora belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Fabales. This plant is a representative of the family Fabaceae and is further categorized into the genus Glycyrrhiza, specifically identified by the species epithet pallidiflora.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Glycyrrhiza pallidiflora is used medicinally specifically through its root. The underground rhizomes and roots contain various bioactive compounds that contribute to their therapeutic properties. These parts are often harvested and dried to prepare extracts or powders for traditional treatments. Practitioners utilize the root to address diverse ailments due to its unique chemical profile. This specific component serves as the primary source of the plant's medicinal value.

According to a study published by "Phytochemistry", flavonoids were isolated from Glycyrrhiza pallidiflora hairy root cultures. Three flavonoids named licoaagroisdes D, E and F were identified alongside four known flavonoids. These four known flavonoids consist of medicarpin 3-O-glucoside, calycosin 7-O-glucoside, formononetin 7-O-(6"-malonylglucoside) and 2'-hydroxyformononetin 7-O-glucoside. Licoaagroisdes E and F represent the first examples of a 6a-hydroxypterocarpan glycoside and an alpha-O-glycosidic alpha-hydroxydihydrochalcone.

What are the pharmacological activities of Glycyrrhiza pallidiflora?

This plant has potent anticancer and cytotoxic activities that demonstrate its therapeutic potential in oncology research. Specific bioactive compounds isolated from its roots trigger programmed cell death in various malignant cell lines. These phytochemicals disrupt the cellular machinery of cancer cells, leading to significant growth inhibition. Experimental studies confirm that its cytotoxic properties are effective against several types of human tumor cells. Continued investigation into these pharmacological mechanisms may lead to the development of new cancer treatments.

According to a study published by "Natural product research", twenty-one phenolic compounds including dihydrocinnamic acid, isoflavonoids, flavonoids, coumestans, pterocarpans, chalcones, isoflavan and isoflaven were isolated from the roots of Glycyrrhiza pallidiflora Maxim. Compounds 2-4, 10, 11, 19 and 21 were evaluated for cytotoxic activity against model cancer cell lines CEM-13, MT-4, and U-937. The isoflavonoid calycosin (4) demonstrated the best potency against human T-cell leukaeamia cells MT-4 with a CTD50 of 2.9 μM. Pterocarpans medicarpin (10) and homopterocarpin (11) exhibit anticancer activity in the micromolar range with selectivity on human monocyte cells U-937. Additionally, the isoflavan (3R)-vestitol (16) was highly selective on lymphoblastoid leukaeamia cells CEM-13 and proved more active than the drug doxorubicin.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause hypertension, fluid retention, and low potassium levels due to its high concentration of glycyrrhizin. Excessive consumption often leads to significant increases in blood pressure and swelling in the extremities. These physiological changes can also trigger irregular heart rhythms or edema in sensitive individuals. Long-term use may result in more serious complications such as muscle weakness or hormonal imbalances. It is important to monitor intake to avoid these potentially dangerous systemic effects.
