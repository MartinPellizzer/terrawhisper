Senegalia macrostachya

Senegalia macrostachya, commonly known as the "wild mango" in certain regions, is a significant medicinal plant utilized in traditional African medicine. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, its various parts are frequently employed to treat inflammatory conditions, skin ailments, and digestive issues, offering valuable insights for modern pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Senegalia macrostachya
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales. Finally, it is a member of the Fabaceae family, specifically falling under the genus Senegalia (noted as Senegalia macrostachya).

Common Names

This plant, Senegalia macrostachya, is known by various regional names depending on the local language and geography. In English, it is commonly referred to as the long-pod acacia. Moving into regional languages, it is known as doun as in French, and in local West African dialects, it is called doun in Mandinka and doun in Wolof. These diverse names reflect the plant's integration into the linguistic and cultural landscapes of the regions where it grows.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the West Tropical Africa region. Within this geographical zone, it can be found growing in the nation of Benin. Its presence extends further to include the territory of Burkina. Additionally, the species is documented within the borders of Gambia and Guinea-Bissau. Finally, its natural habitat encompasses the country of Guinea.
