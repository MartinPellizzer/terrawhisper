Physiology

The physiology of Ipomoea intrapilosa is primarily characterized by its utilization of the C3 photosynthetic pathway, a mechanism where carbon fixation occurs through the direct carboxylation of ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate by the enzyme RuBisCO within the mesophyll cells. This metabolic strategy implies that the plant undergoes the Calvin cycle without the specialized spatial separation of CO2 fixation seen in C4 plants or the temporal separation found in CAM species, making its carbon assimilation processes highly dependent on ambient light intensity and leaf temperature. Consequently, its physiological efficiency is closely tied to the management of photorespiration, as the C3 pathway is susceptible to oxygenase activity when stomatal conductance decreases under water-limiting conditions. The plant's water-use efficiency and metabolic rate are thus intrinsically linked to the environmental availability of CO2 and the regulation of its stomatal aperture to balance gas exchange with transpiration.
