Banisteriopsis

Banisteriopsis, commonly known as Ayahuasca vine, is a powerful medicinal plant central to Amazonian shamanic traditions. Rich in harmala alkaloids, it acts as a potent monoamine oxidase inhibitor. Historically used in ritual healing, this botanical holds significant scientific interest for its profound psychoactive properties and potential therapeutic applications.

  • Scientific name: Banisteriopsis
  • Family: Malpighiaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, it is classified under the family Malpighiaceae. Its specific taxonomic designation is placed within the genus Banisteriopsis.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Banisteriopsis caapi, is characterized by its growth habit as a woody, perennial vine. As a climbing liana found in the tropical rainforests of South America, it uses its strong, twisting stems to scale the canopy of larger trees. In traditional medicinal practices, it is the bark and roots of this massive vine that are most highly valued, as they are harvested and decocted to create potent botanical brews.
