Indian shot

Canna indica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (235 studies) · ★★★★★

Indian shot, scientifically known as Canna indica, is a versatile perennial valued in traditional medicine. Renowned for its vibrant blooms, this plant offers therapeutic properties, particularly for treating inflammation, digestive issues, and kidney ailments. Its rhizomes are frequently utilized in various herbal remedies to promote holistic healing and wellness.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Canna indica involves both sexual and asexual processes, with the sexual aspect characterized by bisexual flowers that typically exhibit a yellow color. The flowering period generally begins in April and concludes in July, though it can be variable. This plant relies on biotic pollination, specifically utilizing an insect pollination syndrome. Self-fertilization is notably absent, ensuring genetic diversity through cross-pollination. Following successful fertilization, the plant produces dry, dehiscence-type fruit in the form of a capsule. These capsules vary in size, with a mean length of 1.75 cm (ranging from 1.2 to 3 cm) and a mean width of 1.5 cm. Each fruit contains a significant number of seeds, typically ranging from 11 to 100 per fruit. The seeds themselves are characterized by a mean mass of approximately 0.189655 g (with a range of 0.12024 g to 0.25 g), a length between 3 and 7 mm, and a width between 3 and 7 mm, resulting in a consistent seed volume of 210 mm³. Dispersal of these seeds is categorized as unspecialized, though it also exhibits anthropochorous tendencies, meaning human activity plays a role in its spread.
