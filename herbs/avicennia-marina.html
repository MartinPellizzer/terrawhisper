Avicennia marina

Gray mangrove

Avicennia marina, commonly known as the grey mangrove, is a resilient halophyte essential to coastal ecosystems. Thriving in saline intertidal zones, this species provides critical habitat and shoreline protection. Beyond its ecological importance, it holds significant medicinal potential, utilized in various traditional practices to treat inflammation and skin ailments.

  • Scientific name: Avicennia marina
  • Family: Acanthaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Lamiales and the family Acanthaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Avicennia.

Distribution

This plant, known scientifically as Avicennia marina, exhibits a specific distribution pattern across several key regions. In Northern Africa, it can be found growing within the borders of Egypt. Its presence extends into Northeast Tropical Africa, where it is native to Djibouti and Eritrea. Furthermore, the species is widely distributed throughout the Socotra archipelago. Finally, the plant is also established within the coastal areas of Somalia.

Plant Parts

This plant, Avicennia marina, exhibits specialized anatomical features essential for its survival in saline mangrove environments. The shoots emerge from the woody framework, giving rise to various twigs that support a dense canopy of salt-excreting leaves. The structural integrity of the plant is maintained by its robust stems, which anchor the organism within the shifting coastal substrate. Most notably, to combat the anaerobic conditions of waterlogged mud, the plant develops specialized aerial roots known as pneumatophores, which grow upward from the soil to facilitate gas exchange.
