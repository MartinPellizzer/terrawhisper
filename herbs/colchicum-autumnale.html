autumn crocus

Colchicum autumnale · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (246 studies) · ★★★★★

Autumn crocus, scientifically known as Colchicum autumnale, is a striking perennial wildflower renowned for its brilliant, goblet-shaped blooms. While celebrated for its seasonal beauty, this plant contains potent alkaloids like colchicine. Historically used in traditional medicine, it requires careful handling due to its significant toxicity if ingested improperly.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Liliales, falling into the family Colchicaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement in the genus Colchicum.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as autumn crocus, possesses specialized structures that are central to its medicinal and botanical profile. The most critical part for pharmacological use is the underground tubers, which serve as the primary storage organs and contain high concentrations of the alkaloid colchicine. Additionally, the Colchicum leaves emerge during the growing season, contributing to the plant's overall biological function before they eventually wither.
