Chuquiraga jussieui

Chuquiraga juscieui, commonly known as "Chuquiragua," is a resilient shrub native to the high-altitude Andean paramo. Renowned in traditional Ecuadorian medicine, this "flower of the Andes" is prized for its antioxidant properties. It is frequently used in herbal infusions to support kidney health and alleviate urinary tract infections.

  • Scientific name: Chuquiraga jussieui
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales. Finally, it is a member of the family Asteraceae and is specifically identified by the genus Chuquiraga.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Chuquiraga jussieui, is recognized by several distinct common names across different linguistic and cultural contexts. In the Quechua language, it is referred to as Chukirawa and Ch'ana, reflecting its deep roots in Andean tradition. In Spanish, it is widely known as Chuquiragua, but it is also poetically celebrated as Flor del caminante and Flor de los Andes, names that highlight its remarkable ability to thrive in the high-altitude landscapes of the mountain ranges.

This plant is widely distributed across the high-altitude regions of Western South America. It can be found growing within the mountainous landscapes of Colombia and Ecuador. Its presence extends further south into the diverse terrains of Peru. Additionally, the species occupies specific ecological niches located in Bolivia.
