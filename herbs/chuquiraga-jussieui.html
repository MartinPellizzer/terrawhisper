Flower of the Andes, scientifically known as Chuquiraga jussieui, is a resilient shrub endemic to the high-altitude paramo ecosystems. Renowned for its striking orange blooms, this sacred plant holds significant medicinal value in traditional Andean healing, frequently used to treat respiratory ailments, kidney issues, and to boost overall immunity.

Ecology

The ecology of Chuquiraga jussieui is defined by its specialized adaptation to the high-altitude environments of the Andean páramos. This species thrives within a specific vertical niche, maintaining an elevational range that begins at 3000 m AMSL and extends up to a maximum of 3750 m AMSL. Occupying these alpine tundra ecosystems, the plant is adapted to survive the intense solar radiation, significant diurnal temperature fluctuations, and high levels of moisture characteristic of these high-elevation tropical mountains. Its distribution within this specific altitudinal band allows it to play a vital role in the high-altitude landscape, contributing to the stability and biological complexity of the páramo habitat.

The morphology of Chuquiraga jussieui is characterized by its growth form as a woody shrub, exhibiting a robust and self-supporting structure.
