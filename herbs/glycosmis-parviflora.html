Glycosmis parviflora

Glycosmis parviflora, commonly known as the small holly, is a significant medicinal shrub found across tropical Asia. Renowned for its diverse pharmacological properties, various parts of this plant are traditionally utilized to treat ailments ranging from digestive issues to inflammation, offering valuable insights for modern drug discovery and herbalism.

  • Scientific name: Glycosmis parviflora
  • Family: Rutaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered under Sapindales and placed in the family Rutaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Glycosmis, specifically as the species Glycosmis parviflora.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range that spans several distinct regions. In South Tropical Africa, its presence is specifically noted in Angola. Moving toward East Asia, it can be found growing in Japan. Its distribution within China is quite extensive, covering the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan regions. Consequently, these locations highlight the plant's wide ecological footprint across multiple continents.
