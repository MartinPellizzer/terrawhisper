Galium bermudense

Galium bermudense, commonly known as Bermuda bedstraw, is a resilient herbaceous plant native to subtropical regions. Belonging to the Rubiaceae family, this species is characterized by its slender, whorled stems. While less documented than its relatives, it holds significant interest in ethnobotany for its potential bioactive medicinal properties.

  • Scientific name: Galium bermudense
  • Family: Rubiaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Gentianaales, it is classified under the family Rubiaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Galium.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Galium bermudense, is identified by various common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In English, it is frequently referred to as Bermuda bedstraw, while in Spanish, it is known as galio de Bermudas.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range that spans several distinct regions across the United States. In the Northeastern United States, its presence can be documented within the state of New Jersey. Moving toward the South-Central United States, the species is also found in Texas. The Southeastern United States serves as a significant hub for this plant, where it is located in Delaware. Furthermore, its distribution continues through the Southeast into Florida and Alabama.
