Gentiana pudica

What's the taxonomical classification of Gentiana pudica?

Gentiana pudica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Further taxonomic refinement places the plant in the order Gentianales and the family Gentianaecae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Gentiana, with its specific designation being the species pudica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Gentiana pudica has lanceolate to ovate leaves that are oppositely arranged along a stem and large, funnel-shaped blue flowers characterized by distinct longitudinal veins. The foliage typically exhibits a smooth texture with entire margins and a deep green coloration. Its corolla features five petals that merge into a tube, often displaying lighter stripes or spotting near the throat. The plant produces small, rounded capsules as fruit following successful pollination. These structural elements collectively define its identification within the Gentianaceae family.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude regions of the Himalayas, specifically spanning across parts of India, Nepal, Bhutan, and southern Tibet. It primarily thrives in subalpine and alpine zones where the terrain is rocky and well-drained. These populations are often found in meadows or scree slopes situated at elevations between 3,000 and 4,500 meters. The distribution is largely dictated by the specific microclimates and moisture levels found in these mountainous corridors. Because of its specialized habitat requirements, the species is restricted to these distinct high-elevation pockets within the Himalayan range.
