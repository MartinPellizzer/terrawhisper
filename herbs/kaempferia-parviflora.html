Kaempferia parviflora

Kaempferia parviflora, commonly known as Black Ginger, is a potent medicinal herb native to Thailand. Renowned for its bioactive compounds, particularly polymethoxyflavones, it is traditionally used to boost energy, enhance libido, and improve metabolic health. This versatile plant offers significant antioxidant and anti-inflammatory benefits for modern wellness.

  • Scientific name: Kaempferia parviflora
  • Family: Zingiberaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Zingiberales, it is classified under the family Zingiberaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Kaempferia, specifically the species parviflora.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Kaempferia parviflora, is recognized by various names across different languages and regions. In Thailand, it is widely called กระชายดำ, while in Japan, it is referred to as クラチャイダム. Its Chinese name is 小花山奈, and in Korea, it is known as 흑산내. Additionally, in the Czech Republic, it is commonly called kempferie drobnokvětá.

Distribution

This plant is native to several distinct regions across Southern and Southeast Asia. Within the Indian Subcontinent, its presence has been documented in Bangladesh. Moving into the Indo-China region, it can be found growing in Cambodia. Its distribution also extends to the country of Myanmar. Finally, this medicinal species is commonly located throughout Thailand.

Plant Parts

This plant, Kaempferia parviflora, is a ginger species primarily valued for its medicinal properties which are concentrated within its underground structures. The most significant part used for therapeutic purposes is the rhizomes, specifically the black rhizomes that are often dried and processed for extract. While the plant features green leaves that emerge from the ground to facilitate photosynthesis, it lacks traditional aerial stems, instead growing close to the earth. Each individual rhizome serves as a storage organ, containing the bioactive compounds that make this plant a subject of intense pharmacological interest.
