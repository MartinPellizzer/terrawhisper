Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1328 studies) · ★★★★★

Hop, scientifically known as Humulus lupulus, is a climbing herbaceous perennial essential to brewing. Beyond flavoring beer, it possesses significant medicinal properties. Rich in essential oils and bitter acids, it acts as a natural sedative, aids sleep, reduces anxiety, and offers antioxidant benefits, making it a versatile botanical remedy.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Humulus lupulus is characterized by a sexual process, as asexual reproduction is notably absent. The plant follows an abiotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on the wind for pollination. The flowering period is concentrated in July, with both the start and end of the flowering stage occurring during this month. Following pollination, the plant produces fruit in the form of an achene, which is indehiscent in nature. These fruits typically maintain a consistent length, with a mean, minimum, and maximum measurement all recorded at 1 cm. The resulting seeds are quite small, with a mean seed mass of approximately 0.0036835 g, ranging from a minimum of 0.00289 g to a maximum of 0.00346 g. Furthermore, the plant does not exhibit self-fertilization, and its dispersal syndrome is classified as anemochorous, meaning the seeds are dispersed via the wind.
