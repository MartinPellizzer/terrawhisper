Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1328 studies) · ★★★★★

Hop, scientifically known as Humulus lupulus, is a climbing herbaceous perennial essential to brewing. Beyond flavoring beer, it possesses significant medicinal properties. Rich in essential oils and bitter acids, it acts as a natural sedative, aids sleep, reduces anxiety, and offers antioxidant benefits, making it a versatile botanical remedy.

Distribution

This plant, known as Humulus lupulus, is widely distributed across several regions in Northern Europe. In the southern reaches of this area, it can be found growing in Denmark. Moving further north, the species is also established in the landscapes of Norway and Finland. The plant's range extends westward to include the islands of Great Britain and Ireland. Plant Parts

This plant, Humulus lupulus, is a climbing perennial known for its complex structural components used in various medicinal and traditional applications. The botanical structure features distinct leaves that are deeply lobed, often serving as the primary site for gathering leaf extracts for their chemical properties. During its flowering stage, the plant produces a specialized inflorescence which typically develops into dense, resinous hop cones. These cone-like structures are found on the female plants and are the most commercially and medicinally significant parts of the species, often growing in clusters that can be described as part of the plant's reproductive racemes.
