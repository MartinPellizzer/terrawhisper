Humulus lupulus

Hop

Humulus lupulus, commonly known as hops, is a perennial climbing vine celebrated for its aromatic flowers. Primarily utilized in brewing to provide bitterness and preservative qualities, it also possesses medicinal properties. Traditionally, hops have been employed to alleviate anxiety, improve sleep quality, and support digestive health through herbal remedies.

  • Scientific name: Humulus lupulus
  • Family: Cannabaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Humulus lupulus, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Rosales and the family Cannabaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Humulus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Humulus lupulus, is recognized by several common names across different Chinese linguistic contexts. In Simplified Chinese, it is referred to as 蛇麻 (zh-hans), which is also the standard name used in Mainland China (zh-cn) and Singapore (zh-sg). Similarly, in Traditional Chinese, it is called 蛇麻 (zh-hant), a designation that is consistently applied in Hong Kong (zh-hk).

Plant Parts

This plant, Humulus lupulus, is a climbing perennial known for its complex structural components used in various medicinal and traditional applications. The botanical structure features distinct leaves that are deeply lobed, often serving as the primary site for gathering leaf extracts for their chemical properties. During its flowering stage, the plant produces a specialized inflorescence which typically develops into dense, resinous hop cones. These cone-like structures are found on the female plants and are the most commercially and medicinally significant parts of the species, often growing in clusters that can be described as part of the plant's reproductive racemes.
