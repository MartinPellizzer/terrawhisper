Staghorn lavender, scientifically known as Lavandula coronopifolia, is a unique, low-growing Mediterranean shrub prized for its distinctive, antler-like foliage. Renowned for its calming aromatic properties, this resilient perennial offers a compact, silvery-green aesthetic, making it an exceptional choice for therapeutic gardens and natural aromatherapy enthusiasts seeking botanical elegance.

Distribution

This plant, Lavandula coronopifolia, is primarily distributed throughout the northern regions of the African continent. Its natural habitat encompasses several key territories, including the coastal and inland areas of Algeria and Morocco. Furthermore, the species can be found growing within the landscapes of Libya and Egypt. It also extends its range into the arid environments of Western Sahara. Collectively, these locations define the specific geographical footprint of this medicinal lavender species.

Plant Parts

This plant, Lavandula coronopifolia, is primarily utilized for its medicinal properties through the use of its aerial part. This portion of the plant includes the stems, leaves, and fragrant flowers that grow above the ground. The essential oils and therapeutic compounds concentrated within these above-ground structures are what make the aerial part the most significant component for harvesting and medicinal applications.
