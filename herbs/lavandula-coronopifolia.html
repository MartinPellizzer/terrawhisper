Lavandula coronopifolia

Lavandula coronopifolia, commonly known as Crowned Lavender, is a resilient Mediterranean shrub prized for its aromatic foliage. Renowned for its calming properties, this medicinal plant is used in traditional herbalism to soothe anxiety and promote sleep. Its fragrant blossoms and silver-green leaves offer both therapeutic benefits and aesthetic beauty.

  • Scientific name: Lavandula coronopifolia
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified within the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Lavandula.

Distribution

This plant, Lavandula coronopifolia, is primarily distributed throughout the northern regions of the African continent. Its natural habitat encompasses several key territories, including the coastal and inland areas of Algeria and Morocco. Furthermore, the species can be found growing within the landscapes of Libya and Egypt. It also extends its range into the arid environments of Western Sahara. Collectively, these locations define the specific geographical footprint of this medicinal lavender species.
