Rehmannia glutinosa

Rehmannia glutinosa, commonly known as the "sticky plant," is a significant medicinal herb native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its potent anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties, this plant is widely utilized in traditional medicine to treat skin ailments, wounds, and various inflammatory conditions, offering valuable bioactive compounds for modern pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Rehmannia glutinosa
  • Family: Orobanchaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales, it is classified under the family Orobanchaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Rehmannia, specifically as the species Rehmannia glutinosa.

Common Names

Plant Parts

This plant, Rehmannia glutinosa, is highly valued in traditional medicine for its various therapeutic components. The medicinal properties are primarily concentrated in the roots, which are often harvested for their potent healing qualities. Specifically, the root is utilized in numerous herbal formulations to treat inflammatory conditions. In addition to the primary structure, the plant develops specialized tuberous roots that serve as storage organs. Each individual tuberous root contains concentrated bioactive compounds, while the green leaves also contribute to the plant's overall chemical profile.
