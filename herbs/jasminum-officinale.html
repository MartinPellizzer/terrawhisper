Jasminum officinale

Common jasmine

Jasminum officinale, commonly known as common jasmine, is a highly fragrant climbing shrub celebrated for its exquisite white blossoms. Renowned in traditional medicine, it is utilized for its calming, sedative, and anti-inflammatory properties. This versatile plant remains a cornerstone in aromatherapy and herbal remedies worldwide for promoting relaxation.

  • Scientific name: Jasminum officinale
  • Family: Oleaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Jasminum officinale, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Lamiales. It is a member of the Oleaceae family and falls under the genus Jasminum.

Plant Parts

This plant, Jasminum officinale, is most highly valued for its fragrant flower, which serves as the primary part used in traditional and modern medicine. These delicate, white blossoms are harvested to create essential oils, infusions, and teas, often utilized for their calming properties and ability to alleviate stress or anxiety. Because the aromatic compounds are concentrated within the petals, the flower remains the most essential component for therapeutic applications.
