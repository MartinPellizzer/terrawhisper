Aconitum napellus subsp. napellus

Aconitum napellus subsp. napellus, commonly known as Monkshood or Wolfsbane, is a striking perennial herb famous for its helmet-shaped blue flowers. While revered in traditional herbalism for its potent physiological effects, it is highly toxic. Careful expertise is essential, as its alkaloids can be fatal if mishandled.

  • Scientific name: Aconitum napellus subsp. napellus
  • Family: Ranunculaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified within the order Ranunculales and the family Ranunculaceae. Specifically, it is a member of the genus Aconitum, categorized as the subspecies napellus.

Plant Parts

This plant, Aconitum napellus subsp. napellus, is characterized by its striking and distinctive flowers, which are typically a deep, vibrant blue or violet. These floral structures are arranged in loose racemes atop sturdy stems, featuring a unique shape often described as "hooded" or helmet-like due to the modified upper petals. Beyond their aesthetic appeal, the flowers serve as a critical reproductive component of the plant, though it is important to note that all parts of this species contain potent alkaloids.
