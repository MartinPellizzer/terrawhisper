Ficus hispida

Opposite-leaved fig

Ficus hispida, commonly known as the hairy fig, is a significant medicinal plant found across tropical Asia. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, various parts of this species are utilized in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to digestive issues, offering valuable insights for pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Ficus hispida
  • Family: Moraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Rosalales, specifically residing in the family Moraceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Ficus as the species Ficus hispidas.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ficus hispida, is recognized by various traditional names across different linguistic and regional contexts. In Malayalam, it is referred to as എരുമനാക്ക് (erumanakku), while in Bengali, it is commonly called কাকডুমুর (kakdumur). In Nepali, the plant is known as डुम्री (dumri), and in Odia, it is identified as କାଠ ଡିମ୍ବିରି (katha dimbiri). Furthermore, in Thailand, it is known by the name มะเดื่อปล้อง (ma-duea plong).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across various regions of Asia. Within China, it can be found in the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan areas. Its presence extends into the Indian Subcontinent, specifically within the state of Assam. Additionally, the species is documented to grow in Bangladesh. Together, these locations highlight its significant range across both Chinese and South Asian landscapes.
