Corymbia maculata

Corymbia maculata, commonly known as Spotted Gum, is a distinguished eucalypt native to Australia's eastern coast. Renowned for its striking mottled bark and robust timber, this species plays a vital role in forest ecosystems, providing essential habitat and nectar for diverse wildlife while offering significant ecological and commercial value.

  • Scientific name: Corymbia maculata
  • Family: Myrtaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following its placement in the subclass Magnoliidae and order Myrtales, it is a member of the family Myrtaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Corymbia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide distribution across several key regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence is specifically noted in Rwanda. The species is also widely found throughout East Tropical Africa, including Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda. Additionally, its range extends into South Tropical Africa within the country of Zambia. Together, these locations highlight the diverse tropical habitats where the plant can be found.
