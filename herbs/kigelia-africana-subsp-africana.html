Kigelia africana subsp. africana

Kigelia africana subsp. africana, commonly known as the sausage tree, is a remarkable medicinal plant native to sub-Saharan Africa. Renowned for its large, pendulous fruits, it holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditional practitioners utilize its various parts to treat skin conditions, inflammation, and various ailments, showcasing its profound healing potential.

  • Scientific name: Kigelia africana subsp. africana
  • Family: Bignoniaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Lamiales, it is classified under the family Bignoniaceae. Specifically, this medicinal species is identified by the genus Kigelia and the subspecies africana.

Common Names

This plant, Kigelia africana subsp. africana, is known by various common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In English, it is most frequently referred to as the sausage tree due to its distinctive, elongated fruit. Within South Africa, it is known as the sausage tree in English, the sosatieboom in Afrikaans, and the mophu in Sotho. Other regional names include the kwitshaka in Tsonga and the mupfura in Shona.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several regions within West Tropical Africa. Specifically, its natural habitat includes the country of Benin. It is also documented to grow within the borders of Burkina Faso. Furthermore, the species can be found in the territories of Gambia and Ghana. Finally, its geographical range extends into the nation of Guinea.
