Lilium pensylvanicum

Lilium pensylvanicum, commonly known as Pennsylvania lily, is a graceful perennial native to Eastern North America. Distinguished by its nodding, bell-shaped orange blossoms, this woodland gem thrives in shaded, moist environments. Historically valued for its aesthetic charm, it remains a significant species for botanical study and naturalistic garden design.

  • Scientific name: Lilium pensylvanicum
  • Family: Liliaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Liliales, it is further categorized into the family Liliaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Lilium as Lilium pensylvanicum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution spanning across Eastern Europe and much of Northern Asia. In Eastern Europe, its presence is noted within the regions of South European Russia. The species also extends deep into Siberia, where it can be found in several distinct territories. Specifically, it inhabits the areas of Buryatiya, Chitа, and Irkutsk. Furthermore, its range continues through the Krasnoyarsk region of Siberia.
