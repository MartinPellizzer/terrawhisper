Life history

The life history of Convolvulus hystrix is characterized by a specialized survival strategy dictated by its classification as a hemicryptophyte, meaning its perennating buds are located at or just below the soil surface, protected by leaf litter or the soil layer itself. This growth habit allows the plant to persist through unfavorable seasons by regenerating from these protected buds during more favorable environmental conditions. While its structural complexity can occasionally align with the characteristics of a nanophanerophyte due to its low-growing, woody-based architecture, its primary life cycle relies on this hemicryptophytic mechanism to ensure long-term survival. By maintaining its dormant buds near the ground level, the species effectively navigates seasonal fluctuations, transitioning from a dormant state to active vegetative growth and subsequent reproductive phases when moisture and temperature levels are optimal.
