Sedum drymarioides belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Sedum drymarioides has succulent, cylindrical to slightly flattened leaves that are typically glaucous in color and arranged in dense, overlapping clusters along its stems. These fleshy leaves are often somewhat blunt or rounded at the tips and can vary in size depending on the plant's hydration levels. The stems of this species are relatively short and creeping, forming mats that allow the plant to spread across rocky substrates. During its flowering period, small, star-shaped blossoms emerge from the leaf axils, usually presenting in shades of white or pale pink. The overall structure of the plant is compact, making it well-adapted to arid environments where water conservation is essential.
