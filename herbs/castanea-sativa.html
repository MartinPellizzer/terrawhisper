Sweet Chestnut

Castanea sativa · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (539 studies) · ★★★★★

Sweet Chestnut, scientifically known as Castanea sativa, is a versatile deciduous tree celebrated for its nutritional and medicinal properties. Renowned for its antioxidant-rich kernels and tannin-heavy bark, it has been traditionally used to support digestive health, promote skin healing, and reduce inflammation, offering a rich legacy in herbal medicine.

The reproduction of Castanea sativa is primarily achieved through sexual means, utilizing bisexual flowers arranged in a paniculate inflorescence. The flowering period typically begins in June and concludes in July. The pollination process is categorized as abiotic but specifically relies on an insect pollination syndrome to facilitate fertilization, as self-fertilization is notably absent. Following successful pollination, the plant develops dry, brown, indehiscent fruits classified as achenes. These fruits typically reach a maximum length of 3 cm, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 2 cm. Within these fruits, the seed mass varies significantly, with a mean mass of approximately 7.59 g, a minimum of 2.59 g, and a maximum of 30.30 g; the fruit is highly prolific, capable of containing more than 1000 seeds.
