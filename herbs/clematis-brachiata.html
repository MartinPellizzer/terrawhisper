Clematis brachiata

Clematis brachiata, commonly known as the Brachiate Clematis, is a climbing medicinal plant native to specific tropical regions. Valued in traditional healing practices, it possesses unique phytochemical properties. This vine is often studied for its potential therapeutic applications, offering a fascinating glimpse into the diverse world of botanical medicine.

  • Scientific name: Clematis brachiata
  • Family: Ranunculaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Clematis brachiata, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Ranunculales. Finally, it is a member of the family Ranunculaceae and the genus Clematis.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse distribution across several key regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is noted specifically in Senegal. Moving toward the center of the continent, it can be found within West-Central Tropical Africa in both Cameroon and Congo. The species also extends into Northeast Tropical Africa, where it inhabits the territory of Chad. Finally, its geographical range reaches as far as Ethiopia in the northeast.

The leaves are typically arranged along these climbing stems, providing the necessary surface area for photosynthesis. While the plant is characterized by its delicate aerial parts, the stem bark can be observed as the outer protective layer of the mature woody climbing sections. Beneath the soil, the root system anchors the plant firmly, serving as the vital foundation for nutrient and water absorption.
