Nile Sage, scientifically known as Salvia nilotica, is a perennial medicinal shrub native to the arid regions of Africa. Renowned for its aromatic foliage, this plant is traditionally utilized in various cultures for its potent antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties, serving as a vital resource in natural healing practices.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Lamiales, it is a member of the family Lamiaceae. Finally, it is identified under the genus Salvia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution concentrated within the tropical regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in Burundi, Rwanda, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Moving toward the eastern part of the continent, its range extends into Northeast Tropical Africa. Within this northeastern region, the species can be found in both Eritrea and Ethiopia. Consequently, the habitat of Salvia nilotica spans across diverse ecological zones from central to northeast Africa.
