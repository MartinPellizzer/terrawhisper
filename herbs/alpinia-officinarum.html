Chinese-ginger

Alpinia officinarum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (442 studies) · ★★★★★

Chinese-ginger, scientifically known as Alpinia officiinarum, is a versatile medicinal plant deeply rooted in Traditional Chinese Medicine. Renowned for its aromatic rhizomes, it possesses potent anti-inflammatory, digestive, and antimicrobial properties. Often used to alleviate gastrointestinal distress and stimulate circulation, this aromatic herb remains a vital component in natural therapeutics.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed throughout specific regions of Southeast Asia and East Asia. In China, its presence is noted in Hainan and across China Southeast. Moving into the Indo-China peninsula, it can be found growing in Cambodia. The species also inhabits various parts of Myanmar within the same region. Finally, its geographical range extends into Vietnam, completing its documented distribution.

Plant Parts

This plant, Alpinia officiinarum, is a perennial herb characterized by a distinct morphological structure used in traditional medicine. The most significant part of the plant is the underground rhizome, which is often harvested and dried for its therapeutic properties; in some contexts, these fleshy rhizomes are referred to collectively as the medicinal source of the species. Above the soil, the plant develops an upright stem that supports a lush growth of foliage, including each elongated green leaf that contributes to the plant's photosynthetic process. While the primary medicinal value lies in the storage organs, the entire system, from the fibrous root structures to the aerial parts, forms a complete biological unit essential for its growth and survival.
