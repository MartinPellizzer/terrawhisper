Florist's daisy

Chrysanthemum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (70 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Florist's daisy, scientifically known as Chrysanthemum, is a versatile medicinal plant celebrated for its therapeutic properties. Rich in essential oils and bioactive compounds, it is traditionally used to soothe inflammation, relieve respiratory ailments, and reduce stress. Beyond its ornamental beauty, this resilient herb offers profound benefits for holistic wellness.

Plant Parts

This plant is highly valued in traditional medicine, with various components offering unique therapeutic benefits. The most common part used is the dried flowers, which are often brewed into a soothing tea to reduce inflammation. A single flower can contain a complex array of bioactive compounds, especially when harvested as a young flower before it has fully opened, as this stage often preserves a higher concentration of essential oils. The delicate petals are also frequently utilized in topical applications or infusions due to their aesthetic and medicinal qualities, while the seed can be processed for its oil content, which is rich in nutrients.
