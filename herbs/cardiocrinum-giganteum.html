Giant Himalayan Lily

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (5 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Giant Himalayan Lily, scientifically known as Cardiocrinum giganteum, is a magnificent perennial herb native to the Himalayas. Renowned for its striking white blooms, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditionally, its bulbs are utilized in various medicinal practices to treat ailments ranging from skin disorders to digestive issues.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Liliales. Ultimately, it is a member of the family Liliaceae and is situated within the genus Cardiocrinum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several distinct regions of Asia. In China, it can be found throughout the South-Central and North-Central territories. Its presence also extends into the Southeast regions of China and the high-altitude areas of Tibet. Beyond China, the species is native to the Indian Subcontinent. Specifically, it is documented to grow within the Assam region.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Cardiocrinum giganteum, derives much of its medicinal significance from its underground structures. The primary part utilized for therapeutic purposes is the large, fleshy bulbs, which are stored deep within the soil to sustain the plant through dormant periods. These bulbs contain the concentrated bioactive compounds required for various traditional remedies, making them the most critical component for harvesting the plant's medicinal properties.
