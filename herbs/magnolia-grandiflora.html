Magnolia grandiflora

Southern magnolia

Magnolia grandiflora, commonly known as the Southern Magnolia, is a majestic evergreen celebrated for its large, fragrant white blooms. Beyond its ornamental beauty, this resilient plant holds historical significance in traditional medicine, where its bark and leaves have been utilized for their antioxidant and anti-inflammatory therapeutic properties.

  • Scientific name: Magnolia grandiflora
  • Family: Magnoliaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and order Magnoliales, it is a member of the family Magnoliaceae. Finally, it is specifically categorized under the genus Magnolia.

Common Names

This plant, Magnolia grandiflora, is known by several different names across various Chinese linguistic contexts. In Simplified Chinese as used in mainland China (zh-cn), Singapore (zh-sg), and in the standard writing style of zh-hans, it is referred to as 荷花玉兰. This same name is utilized in the Traditional Chinese scripts specific to Hong Kong (zh-hk) and Taiwan (zh-hant), which is written as 荷花玉蘭.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a remarkably wide geographical distribution across several distinct continents. In Europe, it can be found specifically within the regions of Italy. Its presence extends to the Indian Subcontinent, where it inhabits both Assam and the East Himalaya. Moving across the ocean, the species is established in the South-Central U.S.A., particularly in Texas. Finally, its range reaches the Southeastern U.S.A., including the state of Alabama.

Plant Parts

This plant, Magnolia grandiflora, is widely recognized for its large, glossy, and evergreen leaves, which serve as a significant component of its structure. These leathery leaves are thick and elliptical in shape, featuring a deep green upper surface and a brownish, fuzzy underside that helps protect the plant from moisture loss. In the context of medicinal and botanical studies, the leaves are often examined for their aromatic properties and chemical compounds, contributing to the plant's overall biological profile.
