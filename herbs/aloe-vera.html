Aloe vera

Aloe vera, commonly known as medicinal aloe, is a versatile succulent renowned for its healing properties. Characterized by thick, fleshy leaves filled with a soothing gel, it has been used for centuries to treat burns, skin irritations, and wounds. This remarkable plant remains a staple in modern skincare and wellness.

  • Scientific name: Aloe vera
  • Family: Not available
  • Native range: Europe

Distribution

This plant exhibits a varied geographical presence across several regions of Europe. In Middle Europe, it can be found specifically in Austria. Its distribution extends into Southwestern Europe, where it grows in the Baleares, Portugal, and Spain. Additionally, the species is present in Southeastern Europe, specifically in Greece. Together, these locations highlight its ability to inhabit diverse European climates.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Aloe vera, is a succulent characterized by its unique anatomy. The most vital part used for medicinal purposes is the fleshy leaves, which grow in a rosette pattern. Each individual leaf is thick and contains a clear, soothing gel that is highly valued for its healing properties. Unlike many other plants, it has very minimal stems, as the leaves appear to grow directly from the base. To anchor the plant and absorb nutrients from the soil, it utilizes a system of shallow roots.
