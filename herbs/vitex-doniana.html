Vitex doniana

Vitex doniana, commonly known as Black Plum, is a versatile medicinal shrub native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its therapeutic properties, various parts of the plant are used in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from fever and inflammation to digestive issues, offering significant pharmacological potential for modern healthcare.

  • Scientific name: Vitex doniana
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Vitex doniana, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Vitex.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Vitex doniana, is referred to by various local names across different linguistic groups. In the Fulfulde language (ff), it is called Ngalbiihi, while in the Dagbani language (dag), it is known as Ŋariŋga. Among speakers of the Sango language (sg), the plant is called Bûlûngû, and in the Kicayo language (kcg), it is recognized by the names A̱lu and a̱ka̱lu.

Distribution

This plant, Vitex doniana, exhibits a specific geographical distribution across several regions of West Tropical Africa. It can be found growing naturally within the borders of Benin. Additionally, its presence is documented throughout the nation of Burkina. The species also inhabits various parts of Ghana and the Gambia. Finally, its range extends into the coastal territories of Guinea-Bissau.

Plant Parts

This plant, Vitex doniana, possesses various anatomical components that are utilized for different therapeutic purposes. The leaves and individual leaf extracts are frequently employed in traditional medicine to treat various ailments, while the stem bark is often decocted to manage inflammatory conditions. Furthermore, the root serves as a vital medicinal source in many ethnobotanical practices. Beyond its internal medicinal uses, the plant also provides wooden chewing sticks derived from its branches, which are commonly used for oral hygiene and dental care.
