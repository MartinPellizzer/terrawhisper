Black Plum, scientifically known as Vitex doniana, is a versatile multipurpose tree native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its medicinal properties, various parts of the plant are used to treat ailments like malaria, inflammation, and digestive issues. Beyond medicine, its nutrient-rich fruits serve as a vital food source.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Vitex doniana is characterized by a seasonal cycle that begins with a flowering period typically centered around March, though the timing can be variable across different months. The pollination process is biotic, specifically utilizing bees as the primary pollination syndrome to facilitate fertilization. Following pollination, the plant enters its fruiting stage, which is concentrated around June. The resulting fruit is a fleshy drupe that is indehiscent, meaning it does not split open at maturity to release its contents. This fleshy nature supports an endozoochorous dispersal syndrome, a specialized form of zoochory where seeds are dispersed via the digestive tracts of animals. The seeds produced during this cycle exhibit specific physical properties, with a mean seed mass of approximately 0.90801 g (ranging from a minimum of 0.38488 g to a maximum of 1.69456 g) and a consistent seed volume of 2300 mm³.
