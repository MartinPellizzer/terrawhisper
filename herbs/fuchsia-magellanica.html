The reproduction of Fuchsia magellanica is characterized by a sexual process facilitated by bisexual flowers, which rely on biotic pollination, specifically through a bird-driven pollination syndrome. The flowering period is quite extensive and variable, typically beginning in December and concluding in May. To ensure genetic diversity, self-fertilization is absent in this species. These berries vary in size, with a minimum length of 1 cm, a mean length of 2 cm, and a maximum length reaching up to 1.5 cm. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing more than 1,000 seeds, with an average seed mass of 0.0001425 g. The dispersal of these seeds follows an unspecialized syndrome, though it is also noted to be anthropochorous, meaning it can be influenced by human activity.
