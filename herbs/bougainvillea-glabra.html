Paper-flower, scientifically known as Bougainvillea glabra, is a vibrant, woody climber celebrated for its stunning ornamental bracts. Beyond its beauty, this resilient plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Used in traditional medicine to treat inflammation, respiratory issues, and digestive ailments, it offers a fascinating intersection of aesthetic appeal and healing potential.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several continents and distinct climate zones. In Europe, it can be found across the Mediterranean region, specifically in Spain within Southwestern Europe and Italy in Southeastern Europe. Its range extends southward into Northern Africa, where it is established in Algeria. The species also inhabits the Macaronesian islands, most notably the Canary Islands. Finally, its presence reaches into West-Central Tropical Africa, particularly within the nation of Cameroon.

Plant Parts

This plant, Bougainvillea glabra, possesses various functional components that are often studied for their medicinal potential. The aerial portions of the plant are the most visible, consisting of woody stems that support a lush canopy of foliage. The green, ovate leaves (or a single leaf when examined closely) serve as the primary site for photosynthesis, contributing to the plant's overall vitality. While the vibrant, colorful structures often mistaken for petals are actually modified bracts, the true flower parts are small, tubular, and inconspicuous, tucked within these brightly colored bracts.
