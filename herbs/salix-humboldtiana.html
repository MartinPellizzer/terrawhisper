Salix humboldtiana

Humboldt's willow

Salix humboldtiana, commonly known as the Humboldt willow, is a versatile riparian species native to Mexico and the Southwestern United States. Renowned for its medicinal properties, it contains salicin, a precursor to salicylic acid. Traditionally, its bark has been utilized to alleviate pain, reduce inflammation, and treat fevers.

  • Scientific name: Salix humboldtiana
  • Family: Salicaceae
  • Native range: Pacific

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Malpighiales and the family Salicaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Salix.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Salix humboldtiana, is referred to by various common names across different languages and cultures. In Guarani, it is called Yvyrapuku (gn), while in Lithuanian, it is known as Čilinis gluosnis (lt). It carries the name Wayaw (qu) in Quechua, and in Bulgarian, it is termed хумболтова върба (bg). Additionally, in Hebrew, the plant is called ערבה צריפית (he).
