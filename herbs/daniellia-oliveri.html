Daniellia oliveri

Daniellia oliveri, commonly known as the "Wild Olive" of Madagascar, is a precious medicinal plant endemic to the island's unique ecosystems. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, it is traditionally utilized in local healing practices to treat various ailments, offering significant potential for modern pharmacological research and botanical conservation.

  • Scientific name: Daniellia oliveri
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Daniellia olivei, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae within the order Fabales. Finally, it is a member of the Fabaceae family under the genus Daniellia.

Common Names

This plant, Daniellia oliveri, is known by various regional and linguistic names across different cultures. In Hausa, it is commonly referred to as Maje, while in the Fula language, it is called Kayarlaahi. It is also recognized as Nyoo in Dagbani and Sana in Bambara. Furthermore, in Chinese, the plant is identified by the name 丹妮莉亚奥利韦里.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the West Tropical Africa region. Specifically, its presence has been documented within the borders of Benin. It can also be found growing throughout the nation of Burkina Faso. Furthermore, the species extends its range into Gambia and Ghana. Finally, its geographical reach includes the coastal territory of Guinea-Bissau.
