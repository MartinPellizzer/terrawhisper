Ylang-ylang tree

Cananga odorata · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (270 studies) · ★★★★★

Ylang-ylang tree, scientifically known as Cananga odorata, is a tropical evergreen prized for its intoxicatingly fragrant yellow blossoms. Widely used in perfumery, its essential oils offer calming aromatherapy benefits. Beyond its scent, this versatile plant holds significant medicinal value in traditional practices for treating skin ailments and anxiety.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Magnoliales, it is further categorized into the family Annonaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Cananga.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is noted in Guinea and Senegal. Its range extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically around the Gulf of Guinea Islands. Moving toward the eastern side of the continent, it can be found in East Tropical Africa. Within this eastern region, the species is documented in both Kenya and Tanzania.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Ylang-ylang, utilizes its fruits as a notable component of its biological structure. The fruits are small, capsule-like structures that develop from the flowers and eventually split open to release seeds. While the fragrant petals are the primary source for essential oil extraction, the fruits play a vital role in the plant's reproductive cycle and are studied for their specific chemical properties within the species.
