Ylang-ylang tree

Cananga odorata · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (270 studies) · ★★★★★

Ylang-ylang tree, scientifically known as Cananga odorata, is a tropical evergreen prized for its intoxicatingly fragrant yellow blossoms. Widely used in perfumery, its essential oils offer calming aromatherapy benefits. Beyond its scent, this versatile plant holds significant medicinal value in traditional practices for treating skin ailments and anxiety.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Cananga odorata is characterized by the production of yellow flowers that typically range in width from 0.4 cm to 0.5 cm. Following pollination, the plant develops fleshy, indehiscent fruit categorized as a berry. These fruits vary in size, with a mean length of 1.71 cm (ranging from a minimum of 1.5 cm to a maximum of 2 cm) and a mean width of 1.23 cm (ranging from 1 cm to a maximum of 1 cm). Each fruit contains a relatively low number of seeds, typically between 1 and 10. The seeds themselves are small, with a mean mass of 0.0616 g (ranging from 0.0524 g to 0.0708 g) and a consistent volume of approximately 520 mm³. The dispersal of these seeds follows a zoochorous syndrome, meaning they are spread by animals, though they are also noted to be anthropochorous, suggesting human involvement in their dispersal.
