Cananga odorata

Ylang-ylang tree

Cananga odorata, commonly known as Ylang-ylang, is a tropical flowering tree celebrated for its intensely fragrant, yellow blossoms. Beyond its role in luxury perfumery, this botanical treasure holds significant medicinal value, offering calming, antiseptic, and anti-inflammatory properties used in traditional wellness practices across various warm, humid climates worldwide.

  • Scientific name: Cananga odorata
  • Family: Annonaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Magnoliales, it is further categorized into the family Annonaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Cananga.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is noted in Guinea and Senegal. Its range extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically around the Gulf of Guinea Islands. Moving toward the eastern side of the continent, it can be found in East Tropical Africa. Within this eastern region, the species is documented in both Kenya and Tanzania.

The fruits are small, capsule-like structures that develop from the flowers and eventually split open to release seeds. While the fragrant petals are the primary source for essential oil extraction, the fruits play a vital role in the plant's reproductive cycle and are studied for their specific chemical properties within the species.
