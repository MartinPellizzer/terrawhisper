Allium macrostemon

Long-stamen chive

Allium macrosystem, commonly known as the large-stemmed onion, is a remarkable medicinal plant valued for its potent bioactive compounds. Renowned for its antimicrobial and antioxidant properties, this species serves as a natural remedy in traditional practices. Understanding its therapeutic potential offers valuable insights into modern pharmacological applications and holistic wellness.

  • Scientific name: Allium macrostemon
  • Family: Amaryllidaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered under Asparagales and falls into the family Amaryllidaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Allium.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Allium macrosystem, is referred to by various common names across different Chinese linguistic regions. In Simplified Chinese, it is called 薤白 (zh-hans), and this same terminology is used in Mainland China (zh-cn), Singapore (zh-sg), and Hong Kong (zh-hk). Similarly, in Traditional Chinese, the plant is identified as 薤白 (zh-hant).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions in East Asia. Within the Russian Far East, its presence is specifically noted in the Primorye region. In China, the species is widely scattered, spanning from the North-Central areas to the South-Central territories. It can also be found throughout the Manchuria region of the country. Additionally, its range extends northward into the Inner Mongolia province.
