Eugenia dysenterica

Stenocalyx dysentericus

Eugenia dysenterica, commonly known as the dysentery berry, is a medicinal shrub native to the tropical forests of South America. Renowned for its potent bioactive properties, this plant is traditionally utilized to treat gastrointestinal ailments, particularly dysentery, while offering significant antioxidant and antimicrobial benefits in holistic medicine.

  • Scientific name: Eugenia dysenterica
  • Family: Myrtaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It further falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Myrtales, placing it in the family Myrtaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Eugenia, specifically as the species Eugenia dysenterica.

Common Names

This plant, scientifically known as Eugenia dysenterica, is recognized by several different names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In Portuguese, it is commonly referred to as Cagaita or Cagaiteira, and it is also identified by the botanical synonym Stenocalyx dysentericus. In the Middle Eastern context, it is transliterated as يوجينيا ديسينتيريكا, while in the Munduruku language, it is known as Iñaçará.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found across various regions of Western South America, with documented occurrences in Bolivia. Its distribution extends significantly into Brazil, where it inhabits a wide range of ecological zones. Within the country, it can be located throughout the North and Northeast regions. Additionally, the species is present in the West-Central areas of Brazil. Finally, the plant is also distributed across the Southeast portion of the Brazilian territory.
