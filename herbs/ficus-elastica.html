Native to Southeast Asia, this robust plant offers diverse therapeutic benefits, ranging from traditional wound healing to modern pharmacological applications, making it a significant subject in ethnobotanical and herbal studies.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered under Rosales and situated in the family Moraceae. Ultimately, it is identified by the genus Ficus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across several continents. In Europe, it can be found in Spain within the southwestern region and in Sicilia throughout the southeast. Its reach extends into West Tropical Africa, where it is specifically located in Gambia. Furthermore, it inhabits the West-Central Tropical Africa region, particularly in Cameroon. Finally, the species is also distributed across the Gulf of Guinea Islands in West-Central Tropical Africa.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the rubber plant, possesses various components that are utilized in traditional medicinal practices. The root bark is often employed for its specific therapeutic properties in folk remedies, while the milky, white latex that exudes from the stems and leaves is frequently used for its topical or internal applications depending on the culture. Additionally, the dried leaves are prepared and used in decoctions or infusions to address various ailments, showcasing the versatility of the different anatomical parts of Ficus elastica.
