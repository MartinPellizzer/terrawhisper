Curcuma mangga

Curcumma mangga, commonly known as Mango Turmeric, is a vibrant medicinal plant prized for its unique aromatic properties. Distinguished by its mango-like scent, this perennial herb offers potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory benefits. It is widely utilized in traditional wellness practices to support digestive health and promote overall holistic healing.

  • Scientific name: Curcuma mangga
  • Family: Zingiberaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Zingiberales and the family Zingiberaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Curcuma.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Curcuma mangga, is recognized by various regional names depending on the linguistic context. In French, it is referred to as Temu mangga, while in Indonesian, it shares the same name, Temu mangga. Additionally, in Bengali, the plant is commonly called Temu poh.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions in Asia. Within the Indo-China region, it can be found inhabiting the Andaman Islands and the Nicobar Islands. Additionally, its presence is well-documented throughout Thailand. Moving into the Malesia region, the species is known to grow on the island of Jawa. Finally, its range extends across the Malay Peninsula, covering the area of Malaya.

Plant Parts

This plant, Curcuma mangga, is primarily valued for its underground structures known as rhizomes. The medicinal properties of the species are concentrated within the rhizome, which grows horizontally beneath the soil surface. These fleshy rhizomes are harvested and processed to extract their bioactive compounds, making the rhizome the most significant part of the plant for therapeutic use.
