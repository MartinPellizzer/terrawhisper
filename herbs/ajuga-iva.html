Southern Bugle, scientifically known as Ajuga iva, is a perennial herb native to North America. Characterized by its whorled leaves and spikes of small, tubular flowers, this plant belongs to the mint family. Traditionally used in folk medicine, it is valued for its various bioactive properties and medicinal potential.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution concentrated within the Mediterranean region of Southwestern Europe. Additionally, its presence is documented across the island of Corsica and within the mainland territory of France. The species also extends its range to the coastal landscapes of Portugal. Finally, its habitat includes the island of Sardegna, completing its localized European distribution.

Plant Parts

This plant, Ajuga iva, is highly valued in traditional medicine, particularly for its therapeutic properties derived from its foliage. The medicinal efficacy is primarily concentrated in the leaves, which are harvested to prepare various remedies. When prepared as a leaves extract, the plant offers potent bioactive compounds that are utilized to treat ailments such as digestive issues and respiratory conditions. Ultimately, the quality and potency of the treatment depend heavily on the freshness and processing of the Leaves.
