Bupleurum yunnanense

What's the taxonomical classification of Bupleurum yunnanense?

Bupleurum yunnanense belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Apiales. This plant is a member of the family Apiaceae, which is further categorized into the genus Bupleurum, ultimately reaching its specific designation as the species yunnanense.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Bupleurum yunnanense has a perennial growth habit characterized by long, slender, green stems that branch out to support small, alternate, lanceolate leaves. At the ends of the stems, the plant produces small, yellowish-green flowers arranged in compact umbels. These inflorescences are supported by distinct peduncles that elevate the tiny blossoms above the leafy base. The overall structure of the plant is delicate yet upright, fitting its highland meadow environment.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is endemic to the Yunnan province of southwestern China, specifically occurring within the high-altitude regions of the Hengduan Mountains. It grows primarily in montane environments where the terrain is characterized by rocky slopes and alpine meadows. These habitats are typically situated at elevations ranging from 2,500 to 3,500 meters above sea level. The species thrives in the cool, moist climates provided by these mountainous ecosystems. Its distribution is restricted by these specific topographical and climatic requirements.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant is used medicinally through its dried roots, which are processed to create the traditional herb known as Chai Hu.

Practitioners typically prepare the roots as decoctions or powders to treat various ailments related to liver function and emotional regulation. The medicinal properties are concentrated within the underground structure rather than the stems or leaves.
