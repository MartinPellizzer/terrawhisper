Cladocolea micrantha

Cladocolea micrantha, commonly known as the "medicinal climbing shrub," is a significant botanical species valued in traditional African medicine. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, this plant is frequently utilized to treat various ailments, ranging from skin infections to gastrointestinal issues, making it a vital resource in ethnopharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Cladocolea micrantha
  • Family: Loranthaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Santalales and the family Loranthaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Cladocolea, with the specific species being Cladocolea micrantha.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several regions within Northern South America. It can be found growing in the territories of French Guiana, Guyana, and Suriname. Additionally, its presence extends into the country of Venezuela. Beyond these northern areas, the species is also located within the Northeast region of Brazil. These specific locations define the primary geographical range for Cladocolea micrantha.
