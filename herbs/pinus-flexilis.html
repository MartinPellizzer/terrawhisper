Limber pine, scientifically known as Pinus flexilis, is a resilient conifer native to high-altitude Rocky Mountains. Highly valued for its ecological importance, this hardy species offers nutritional seeds and medicinal potential. Its ability to thrive in harsh, windswept environments makes it a vital component of alpine ecosystems.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Pinidae and order Pinales, it is further categorized into the family Pinaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement within the genus Pinus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across the mountainous regions of Western North America. In Western Canada, its presence is noted throughout the provinces of Alberta and British Columbia. Moving into the Northwestern United States, it can be found across the state of Idaho. The species also populates the rugged landscapes of Montana. Finally, its range extends further south into the high elevations of Colorado.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Limber Pine (Pinus flexilis), is valued for its specific chemical properties found within its foliage. The leaf essential oils derived from its needles contain various aromatic compounds that are often studied for their therapeutic potential. These volatile oils, extracted from the plant's photosynthetic structures, serve as a primary source of the medicinal constituents used in various traditional and contemporary applications.
