Bolivian coca

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (8 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Bolivian coca, scientifically known as Erythroxylum coca var. coca, is a sacred plant deeply rooted in Andean culture. Renowned for its stimulant properties, the leaves are traditionally chewed to alleviate altitude sickness and fatigue. Beyond its cultural significance, it remains a subject of intense pharmacological and botanical study worldwide.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning multiple continents and tropical regions. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found specifically within Guinea-Bissau. Its presence extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, where it inhabits Cameroon and the Gulf of Guinea Islands. Moving toward East Tropical Africa, the species is also documented in Tanzania. Finally, its range reaches into Eastern Asia, specifically within the Kazan-retto region.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Erythroxylum coca var. coca, is most highly valued for its leaves, which contain the primary medicinal and psychoactive alkaloids. These elliptical, green leaves are harvested and processed to extract substances such as cocaine, which has historically been used in both traditional Andean medicine and various pharmacological applications. While the entire shrub is a biological entity, it is specifically the chemical composition within these leaves that defines the plant's significance in global ethnobotany.
