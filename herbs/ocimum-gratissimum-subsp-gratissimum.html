African Basil

Ocimum gratissimum subsp. gratissimum · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Extensive (181 studies) · ★★★★☆

African Basil, scientifically known as Ocimum gratissimum subsp. gratissimum, is a versatile aromatic shrub native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its distinct spicy fragrance, this medicinal powerhouse is used in traditional healing to treat respiratory ailments, digestive issues, and skin infections, offering potent antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Lamiales, it is a member of the family Lamiaceae. Specifically, it is identified by the genus Ocimum as the subspecies Ocimum gratissimum subsp. gratissimum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several key regions across the Atlantic and African continents. Within the Macaronesian islands, its presence is specifically noted in Cape Verde. Moving to the mainland, it is widely distributed across West Tropical Africa. In this region, it can be found inhabiting the landscapes of Benin, Burkina, and Gambia. Furthermore, its range extends through the coastal territories of Ghana.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Holy Basil or Clove Basil, possesses distinct morphological features across its various structures. The leaves are typically ovate or lanceolate, characterized by a strong, aromatic scent and a slightly hairy texture that contains essential oils. The stem-leaf arrangement often shows a square-shaped stem characteristic of the Lamiaceae family, with leaves emerging in opposite pairs along the length of the stalk. Finally, the plant produces terminal inflorescences, which consist of small, delicate flowers clustered in spikes that emerge from the upper portions of the stems.
