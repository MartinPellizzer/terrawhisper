Ligustrum sinense

What's the taxonomical classification of Ligustrum sinense?

Ligustrum sinense belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is further organized into the order Lamiales. It is a member of the family Oleaceae, specifically falling under the genus Ligustrum, with its unique classification completed by the species name sinese.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Ligustrum sinense has opposite, simple, elliptical to lanceolate leaves that typically measure 5 to 15 centimeters in length and possess entire margins. The foliage is dark green on the upper surface and often slightly paler underneath, with a smooth, somewhat leathery texture. Its stems are woody and can develop a grayish bark as the shrub matures into a large hedge or small tree. During the blooming season, the plant produces terminal panicles of small, white, tubular flowers that emit a strong fragrance. These flowers eventually give way to small, fleshy, black drupes that serve as a primary dispersal mechanism.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to China and parts of Southeast Asia, where it thrives in subtropical and tropical climates. It has since been introduced to many other regions around the world, including North America, Europe, and Australia. In many of these non-native areas, the species can become invasive due to its ability to spread rapidly. It typically colonizes disturbed sites, forest edges, and riparian zones where sunlight and moisture are available. How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, Ligustrum sinense, is cultivated by planting semi-hardwood cuttings or seeds in well-draining, fertile soil under full sun or partial shade conditions.

It thrives in temperate to subtropical climates where it can maintain consistent moisture without waterlogging the roots. Regular pruning is required to manage its growth habit and promote the development of dense, lush foliage. Once established, the shrub is relatively drought-tolerant but benefits from consistent watering during dry summer months.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Ligustrum sinense is primarily utilized for its medicinal properties through the use of its dried ripe fruits and its bark.

The fruits, often referred to as che biao zao, are frequently processed to create decoctions or extracts used to treat fever, inflammation, and various infections. What are the pharmacological activities of Ligustrum sinense?

This plant has anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antimicrobial, antidiabetic, and hepatoprotective pharmacological activities. Its antioxidant properties help neutralize free radicals to prevent oxidative stress within the body's cells. The antimicrobial effects allow it to combat various pathogenic microorganisms effectively. Additionally, it demonstrates antidiabetic potential by helping to regulate blood glucose levels. Finally, its hepatoprotective qualities assist in protecting the liver from damage and supporting overall hepatic function.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Ligustrum sinense is traditionally used to treat pain, hepatitis, and breast inflammation.
