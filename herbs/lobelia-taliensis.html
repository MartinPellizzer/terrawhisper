Lobelia taliensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Lobelia taliensis?

Lobelia taliensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this group, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving further down the taxonomic hierarchy, it falls under the order Asterales and the family Campanulaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Lobelia, and its specific species name, taliensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Lobelia taliensis has erect, glandular-pubescent stems that typically reach heights of 20 to 40 centimeters and bear oppositely arranged, lanceolate leaves with serrated margins. The foliage is characterized by a soft, hairy texture caused by fine trichomes covering the leaf surfaces. Its inflorescence consists of a dense, terminal spike containing numerous zygomorphic flowers. These flowers feature a distinct two-lipped corolla that is predominantly blue or violet in color. The reproductive structures include a long, protruding style and stamens that are fused into a tube around the filament.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires consistent moisture, high humidity, and bright indirect light to thrive in a well-draining, peat-based potting mix.

Because it is native to high-altitude cloud forests, it cannot tolerate drying out completely or sitting in stagnant water. Maintaining a cool to moderate temperature range is essential to prevent the foliage from wilting or burning. Regular misting or the use of a pebble tray helps simulate its natural damp environment.
