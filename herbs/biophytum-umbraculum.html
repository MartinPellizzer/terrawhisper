Biophytum umbraculum

Biophytum umbraculum, commonly known as the "Creeping Oxalis," is a fascinating medicinal plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its unique creeping habit and heart-shaped leaves, it is traditionally used in various cultures to treat inflammatory conditions, digestive ailments, and skin irritations, offering significant therapeutic potential in herbal medicine.

  • Scientific name: Biophytum umbraculum
  • Family: Oxalidaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Biophytum umbraculum, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, falling within the order Oxalidales. Finally, it is a member of the family Oxalidaceae and is placed in the genus Biophytum.

Common Names

This plant, Biophytum umbraculum, is known by various names across different languages and regions. In Sinhala, it is referred to as Nyambere, while in French, it is called Makku rumbu. It is known as Naa naa kporosoŋ in Dagbani and as Jutugunin in Bambara. Additionally, in Chinese, the plant is identified as 罗伞草.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern concentrated within the West Tropical Africa region. It can be found growing naturally in the country of Benin. The species also inhabits various areas throughout Burkina Faso. Additionally, its range extends into the territory of Gambia. Finally, its geographical presence is documented within Guinea-Bissau and Ghana.
