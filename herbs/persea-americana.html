Reproduction

The reproduction of Persea americana is a complex biological process driven primarily by biotic pollination mechanisms. The flowering period is highly variable, with flowering start times recorded as early as December and occurring throughout the year across months such as January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, and November. The plant produces small flowers, with lengths ranging from 0.5 cm to 0.6 cm, arranged in inflorescences that measure between 0.08 m and 0.14 m in length. Pollination is achieved through biotic syndromes, specifically involving insects and bees. Notably, self-fertilization is absent in this species, necessitating these external vectors for successful fertilization. Following pollination, the plant develops fleshy fruits that vary in size, with a mean length of 11 cm (ranging from 8 cm to 20 cm) and a mean width of 6 cm (up to a maximum of 10 cm). Each fruit typically contains between 1 and 10 seeds. The seeds themselves are substantial, characterized by a mean length, width, and seed width of 25 mm, with lengths reaching up to 100 mm. The seed mass varies from a minimum of 9.945 g to a maximum of 20.67 g, with a mean mass of approximately 15.3075 g, and a consistent seed volume of 65,000 mm³. Dispersal of the seeds occurs via zoochorous and anthropochorous syndromes, meaning they are spread by animals and humans. Asexual reproduction is absent in this species, making sexual reproduction via seeds the primary means of propagation.
