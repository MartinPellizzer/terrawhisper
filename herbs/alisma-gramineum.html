What's the taxonomical classification of Alisma gramineum?

Alistma gramineum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae. The taxonomic lineage continues through the order Alismatales and the family Alismataceae. Finally, the plant is placed within the genus Alisma, with its specific designation being the species gramineum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Alisma gramineum has slender, creeping rhizomes and narrow, linear leaves that typically measure between 2 and 5 millimeters in width. The foliage is dark green and grows in dense, tufted mats that can spread extensively through aquatic environments. Its flowering stems rise above the leaf canopy, bearing small, inconspicuous flowers arranged in terminal spikes. The plant features a fine-textured, grass-like appearance that allows it to weave through surrounding vegetation. These morphological adaptations enable the species to thrive in both submerged and emergent zones of wetlands.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, commonly known as tufted hairgrass, thrives in moist to wet soils and requires partial shade to full sun for optimal growth.

It is best cultivated in garden borders, riparian zones, or as a groundcover in areas with consistent moisture. For successful establishment, provide well-draining but damp organic soil and avoid letting the root zone dry out completely. Once mature, the grass forms dense, low-growing clumps that offer excellent texture and stability for erosion control.

Regular maintenance involves cutting back the foliage in early spring to encourage fresh, vibrant new growth.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant uses its rhizome as the primary part for medicinal applications. The underground stems contain bioactive compounds that are processed for various traditional remedies. Practitioners often harvest these structures to address specific health concerns through decoctions or poultices. Because the rhizome stores essential nutrients, it serves as a concentrated source of therapeutic properties. This part of the plant is vital for those seeking its specific healing benefits.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", the rhizome of Alisma gramineum was investigated to identify its triterpenoid chemical constituents. Through the use of silicon gel chromatography and HPLC techniques, 5 protosane-type tetracyclic triterpenoids were isolated and identified. These specific compounds include 16,23-oxidoaalisol B(1), 11-deoxyalisol C(2), alisol F(3), 16beta-methoxyalisol B acetate (4), and 16beta-hydroxyalisol B acetate (5). All 5 of these identified compounds were found from Alisma gramineum for the first time.
