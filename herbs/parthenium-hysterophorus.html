Parthenium hysterophorus

Wild wormwood

Parthenium hysterophorus, commonly known as Parthenium weed or Congress grass, is a highly invasive annual herb native to the Americas. While it poses significant threats to biodiversity and human health through allergic reactions, it holds medicinal potential in traditional practices for its antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and wound-healing properties.

  • Scientific name: Parthenium hysterophorus
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and order Asterales, it is further categorized into the family Asteraceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Parthenium.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution that spans multiple continents and climate zones. In Middle Europe, its presence can be documented in countries such as Germany and Poland. Moving toward the Atlantic, it is also found within the Macaronesian region, specifically in Cape Verde. Its range extends into Northeast Tropical Africa, where it inhabits parts of Eritrea. Additionally, the species is widely distributed across the Ethiopian highlands.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Parthenium weed, is a highly invasive species characterized by several distinct anatomical structures. The aerial parts of the plant are prominently visible, consisting of an upright growth habit that can reach significant heights. Its stem is typically branched and can vary from green to a reddish hue, providing structural support for the foliage. The leaves are deeply lobed and serrated, playing a crucial role in the plant's rapid growth and photosynthetic capacity. The reproductive cycle is driven by the flower, which appears as small, white, discoid heads arranged in clusters. Finally, the roots form a robust system that allows the plant to anchor itself firmly and spread effectively through the soil, contributing to its status as a persistent medicinal and environmental weed.
