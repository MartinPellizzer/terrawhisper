Wild wormwood, scientifically known as Parthenium hysterophorus, is a highly invasive, herbaceous annual plant native to the Americas. While utilized in traditional medicine for its anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties, it is globally recognized as a problematic weed that causes severe allergic reactions and disrupts diverse ecological systems worldwide.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Parthenium weed, is a highly invasive species characterized by several distinct anatomical structures. The aerial parts of the plant are prominently visible, consisting of an upright growth habit that can reach significant heights. Its stem is typically branched and can vary from green to a reddish hue, providing structural support for the foliage. The leaves are deeply lobed and serrated, playing a crucial role in the plant's rapid growth and photosynthetic capacity. The reproductive cycle is driven by the flower, which appears as small, white, discoid heads arranged in clusters. Finally, the roots form a robust system that allows the plant to anchor itself firmly and spread effectively through the soil, contributing to its status as a persistent medicinal and environmental weed.
