Pongamiopsis pervilleana

Pongamiopsis pervilleana, commonly known as mahuta, is a rare and distinctive tree endemic to the island of Réunion. Valued in traditional medicine, this resilient species offers unique biochemical properties. Research into its bioactive compounds continues to explore its potential therapeutic applications, highlighting its significance in both ecology and ethnobotany.

  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales, specifically within the family Fabaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Pongamiopsis.

Common Names

This plant, Pongamiopsis pervilleana, is known by various names depending on the region and language. In English, it is commonly referred to as the Mauritius tamarind, while in French, it is called tamarind de Maurice. Locally in its native Mauritius, it is known as tamarind de Maurice or simply tamarind, and in Mauritian Creole, it is called tamarin de Maurice.
