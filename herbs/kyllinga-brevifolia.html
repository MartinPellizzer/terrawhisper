Kyllinga brevifolia

What's the taxonomical classification of Kyllinga brevifolia?

Kyllinga brevifolia belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the taxonomic hierarchy further, it falls under the order Poales and is a member of the family Cyperaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by its specific genus, Kyllinga, and its species designation, brevifolia.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Kyllinga brevifolia has a perennial herbaceous growth habit characterized by small, trifoliate leaves and terminal white flower heads composed of numerous tiny florets. The leaves are typically short and narrow, emerging from a creeping rhizomatous base that allows the plant to spread through soil. Its inflorescence consists of compact, globular clusters situated at the tip of the stems. The flowering structures are supported by slender, upright stalks that emerge from the dense foliage. Small, inconspicuous bracts often surround the delicate flower heads during the blooming period.

What health conditions is this plant used for?

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", the crude hydro-alcoholic rhizome extract of Kyllinga brevifolia Rottb. is a popular medicine used in Paraguay. The extract is used in traditional medicine to alleviate stress or as a sedative agent. Oral administration of 1, 10, and 100 mg/kg produced a significant increase in the hypnotic effect induced by pentobarbital in a dose-dependent manner. At doses of 1, 10, and 100 mg/kg, the extract produced a decrease in spontaneous locomotor activity, piloerection, passivity, palpebral ptosis, catatonia, and stereotyped behaviour. Additionally, a significant decrease in respiration rate was observed at doses of 1, 10, and 100 mg/kg.

Such preparations are frequently studied for their potential pharmacological properties in traditional medicine. Detailed analysis of these extracts helps determine their efficacy in treating different ailments.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", the crude hydro-alcoholic rhizome extract of Kyllinga brevifolia Rottb. is a popular medicine used in Paraguay. The intraperitoneal LD50 of the extract was found to be 575 mg/kg, while oral administration of doses up to 3.0 g/kg did not provoke toxic symptoms. Oral administration of 100 mg/kg induced a significant increase in gastrointestinal transit. In open field studies, oral doses of 1, 10 and 100 mg/kg produced a decrease in spontaneous locomotor activity, piloerection, passivity, palpebral ptosis, catatonia, and stereotyped behaviour. A significant decrease in respiration rate was observed at doses of 1, 10 and 100 mg/kg. Additionally, doses of 1, 10 and 100 mg/kg produced a significant increase in the hypnotic effect induced by pentobarbital in a dose-dependent manner.
