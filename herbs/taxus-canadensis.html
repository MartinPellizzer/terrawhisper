Taxus canadensis

American yew

Taxus canadensis, commonly known as Canada Yew, is a slow-growing coniferous evergreen native to North American forests. Renowned for its dense, needle-like foliage and red, fleshy arils, this resilient shrub holds significant botanical interest. While historically studied for medicinal properties, it requires careful handling due to its inherent toxicity.

  • Scientific name: Taxus canadensis
  • Family: Taxaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Pinidae and the order Pinales, falling into the family Taxaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identification is placed within the genus Taxus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Taxus canadensis, is recognized by various common names depending on the region and language. In Germany, the species is known as Kanadische Eibe, and in Azerbaijan, it is identified as Kanada qaraçöhrəsi. Additionally, in Poland, the plant is commonly called cis kanadyjski.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across the diverse landscapes of Canada. In the western region, its presence can be identified within the province of Manitoba. Moving toward the east, it is found throughout the rugged terrain of Labrador. The species also inhabits the coastal and forested areas of New Brunswick. Finally, its range extends to the Atlantic provinces of Newfoundland and Nova Scotia.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Canada yew, features distinctive dark green, flat needles that are arranged spirally along the stems but often appear two-ranked. While the needles are the most recognizable foliage component, the plant can be propagated through rooted cuttings, a method used to create new specimens from stem segments that have developed an established root system.
