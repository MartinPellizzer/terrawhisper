Sea Daffodil, scientifically known as Pancratium maritimum, is a striking perennial bulb found along Mediterranean coastlines. Beyond its aesthetic beauty, it holds significant ethnobotanical value, offering diverse medicinal properties used in traditional healing practices worldwide.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Pancratium maritimum is characterized by a sexual process involving bisexual flowers arranged in an umbel inflorescence. These white flowers, which measure between 3.5 cm and 5 cm in length, typically bloom during the month of August. The plant relies on biotic pollination, specifically utilizing an insect pollination syndrome, and self-fertilization is notably absent. Following successful pollination, the plant develops a dehiscent capsule fruit that measures approximately 3 cm to 4 cm in length and 2 cm to 3 cm in width. Each capsule is highly productive, containing a large number of seeds ranging from 101 to 1000 per fruit. The seeds themselves vary in size, with lengths between 13 mm and 16 mm and widths between 9 mm and 12 mm, possessing a mean mass of approximately 0.0439858335 g (ranging from 0.0374 g to 0.0483 g). Dispersal of the seeds occurs through both hydrochorous (water) and anthropochorous (human) syndromes, facilitating the spread of the species across its habitat.
