Physiology

The physiology of Cornus alternifolia is fundamentally characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, a mechanism typical of most temperate woody angiosperms where carbon fixation occurs directly through the Calvin cycle within the mesophyll cells. This metabolic strategy involves the primary carboxylation of ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP) by the enzyme RuBisCO, which results in the production of two molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate. Because this plant utilizes the C3 pathway, it is physiologically optimized for environments with moderate temperatures and sufficient moisture, though it remains susceptible to photorespiration when stomatal conductance decreases under high heat or water stress. The plant's physiological processes are closely tied to its seasonal phenology, with metabolic activity and transpiration rates fluctuating significantly in response to photoperiod and temperature changes throughout the growing season.
