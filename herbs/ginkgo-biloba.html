Ginkgo biloba · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (2603 studies) · ★★★★★

Maidenhair tree, scientifically known as Ginkgo biloba, is a living fossil with a history spanning millions of years. Renowned for its unique fan-shaped leaves, this resilient species is widely used in traditional medicine to support cognitive function, enhance memory, and improve circulation, offering profound benefits for brain health.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Ginkgoidae. It is uniquely positioned within the order Ginkgoales and the family Ginkgoaceae. Ultimately, it is classified under the single-species genus Ginkgo.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution that spans across several distinct regions. In Asia, its natural range includes China South-Central, China North-Central, and China Southeast. Moving toward the European continent, the species can be found in Southeastern Europe, specifically within Romania. Additionally, the plant is present in Middle Europe, notably in Austria. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to establish itself across various environmental zones.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the maidenhair tree, offers various components used in traditional and modern medicine. Most therapeutic applications focus on the leaves, as the dried leaf is often processed into a concentrated leaf extract to support cognitive function and blood circulation. While the seeds are also edible when prepared correctly, the seed itself contains different chemical compounds than the foliage, making the leaf-based preparations the most frequent choice for medicinal supplements.
