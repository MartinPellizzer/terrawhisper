Ginkgo biloba

What's the taxonomical classification of Ginkgo biloba?

Ginkgo biloba belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Ginkgoidae. The plant's taxonomic progression continues through the order Ginkgoales and the family Ginkgoaceae, which leads to its specific designation within the genus Ginkgo. Ultimately, its scientific identity is completed by its species name, biloba.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Ginkgo biloba has unique, fan-shaped leaves with a central notch that can be deeply cleft or nearly entire, arranged alternately on woody branches. These leaves feature a distinctive reticulate venation pattern where veins radiate from the base without forming a network. The tree produces large, woody, cone-like structures on male trees and fleshy, ovule-containing structures on female trees. Its bark is thick, gray, and deeply fissured, providing protection for the long-lived trunk. The overall growth habit is typically pyramidal when young but becomes more spreading and irregular as the specimen matures.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to a very limited range centered primarily around the Zhejiang province in eastern China. While its natural occurrence is confined to these specific regions, it has been widely introduced to various temperate climates across the globe. Today, Ginkgo biloba is commonly cultivated in urban parks and botanical gardens throughout North America, Europe, and parts of Asia. It thrives in diverse environments where it is often used as a hardy street tree due to its resistance to pollution. This global distribution is entirely the result of human cultivation rather than natural expansion.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant grows best in well-drained, loamy soil within USDA hardiness zones 4 through 8, requiring full sun exposure for optimal development.

Growers typically start the process by planting seeds or using vegetative cuttings to ensure consistent genetic traits. Because Ginkgo biloba is exceptionally hardy, it can tolerate various soil types and resists most common pests and diseases. Regular watering is necessary during the establishment phase, but mature trees become quite drought-tolerant once their root systems are deep.

Careful selection of planting sites is important to provide sufficient space for the tree's massive canopy and expansive root network.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant provides medicinal value primarily through its leaves and seeds, while the fruit, bark, and roots are rarely utilized for therapeutic purposes. The leaves are most commonly processed into extracts to improve cognitive function and blood circulation. Within the seeds, specific compounds are used in traditional practices, although they must be prepared carefully to avoid toxicity. In contrast, the bark and roots are typically ignored in modern herbal medicine. Most clinical research focuses exclusively on the concentrated nutrients found within the leaf extracts.

According to a study published by "Journal of separation science", Ginkgo biloba leaf extracts are used in traditional Chinese medicine. A new capillary electrophoretic method was developed to separate the components of these Ginkgo biloba leaf extracts. Under best separation conditions, the separation was reached within 10 min using a 36 mM borate BGE at pH 9.2 and 1 mM gamma-CD. The process utilized a hydrodynamic mode for sample injection of 2 s and UV detection at 270 nm. This validated method was applied for the analysis of various extracts and Ginkgo biloba products.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, known as Ginkgo biloba, has been utilized for thousands of years within Traditional Chinese Medicine to treat various respiratory and circulatory ailments. Practitioners historically used the seeds more frequently than the leaves to address conditions like asthma, coughs, and bladder dysfunction. The leaves were often prepared in decoctions or powders to support lung health and improve cognitive functions. Beyond medicinal applications, the tree held significant cultural and spiritual value in ancient East Asian societies. These historical practices laid the foundational knowledge that modern researchers now use to study the plant's bioactive compounds.

What are the pharmacological activities of Ginkgo biloba?

This plant has diverse pharmacological activities including cognitive enhancement, antioxidant activity, blood circulation-promoting effects, anti-platelet properties, and neuroprotection. The flavonoids and terpenoids within the leaves provide potent antioxidant activity that neutralizes free radicals to prevent cellular damage. Its ability to improve blood circulation-promoting functions helps increase oxygen delivery to vital organs. Furthermore, its anti-platelet action reduces blood viscosity and helps prevent excessive clotting. These combined mechanisms contribute to significant neuroprotection by shielding neurons from degeneration. Ultimately, these processes support cognitive enhancement by maintaining healthy brain function.

According to a study published by "Current opinion in clinical nutrition and metabolic care", the daily intake of ginkgo biloba extract can enhance cognitive performance. This plant extract has also been proved to delay cognitive decline in dementia. The effects of nutritional supplements are modest but do not seem to be very different from those of medicinal or investigational cognition-enhancing or anti-dementia drugs.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains flavonoids, terpene trilactones, bilobalide, ginkgolides, and ginkgolic acids as its primary medicinal compounds. The flavonoids serve as potent antioxidants that help protect cells from oxidative stress. Within the terpene trilactone category, specific substances known as ginkgolides and bilobalide provide significant neuroprotective and circulatory benefits. While these compounds offer therapeutic potential, the plant also contains ginkgolic acids which can trigger allergic reactions in some individuals. Together, these diverse chemical constituents define the pharmacological profile of Ginkgo biloba.

According to a study published by "Naunyn-Schmiedeberg's archives of pharmacology", bilobalide is a constituent of Ginkgo biloba that inhibits NMDA-induced phospholipase A2 activation and phospholipid breakdown. In rat hippocampal slices, glutamate at 1 mM caused a large release of choline through phospholipid breakdown. Bilobalide inhibited this NMDA-induced efflux of choline with an IC50 value of 2.3 microM and prevented the formation of lyso-PC and GPCh. When administered systemically at 20 mg/kg i.p., bilobalide completely inhibited the choline release caused by NMDA in vivo. Furthermore, bilobalide almost totally suppressed the convulsions observed in NMDA-treated rats.

Many people utilize its extracts to enhance mental clarity and support memory retention in aging populations. It is believed to work by increasing circulation to the brain and protecting neurons from oxidative stress. Some studies suggest it may help mitigate the progressive symptoms of neurodegenerative disorders. These therapeutic properties make it a popular supplement for maintaining overall neurological health.

According to a study published by "CNS & neurological disorders drug targets", Ginkgo is known for its anti-oxidative and anti-inflammatoty responses. Its diverse phytochemicals play a protective role against amyloid-beta-induced neurotoxicity and improve cognitive and learning impairments. These chemicals scavenge free radicals, lower the amyloid burden, improve memory dysfunction, and inhibit acetylcholinesterase activity. Ginkgo and other medicinal plants can help reduce AD pathogenesis by controlling different pathways and could be used as a therapeutic agent against the symptoms.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is used to create various medicinal herbal preparations such as EGb 761, GBE50, Ginkgoselect Phytosome, Ginkgolid C, and Lü Kang Yin Xing Ye Pian. Standardized extracts like EGb 761 and GBE50 are frequently utilized in clinical studies to support cognitive function and blood circulation. Advanced delivery systems such as Ginkgoselect Phytosome aim to enhance the bioavailability of the plant's active compounds within the body. Specific isolated components like Ginkgolid C are studied for their targeted neurological effects. Additionally, traditional formulations like Lü Kang Yin Xing Ye Pian demonstrate the long-standing history of this plant in herbal medicine.

According to a study published by "Current molecular pharmacology", extracts of Ginkgo biloba L. seeds and leaves contain components like flavonol glycosides, terpene lactones, and alkylphenols used in traditional Chinese medicine and dietary supplements. Standard forms of the extract include EGb 761 and LI 1370. Six different 6-alkylsalicylic acids, known as ginkgolic acids with alkyl substituents C13:0, C15:0, C15:1, C17:0, C17:1, and C17:2, have been identified. Specifically, ginkgolic acids C13:0, C15:0, and C17:1 isolated from the leaves exhibited cytotoxic activity against human cancers in various in vitro and in vivo models. These natural agents show therapeutic potential for inflammatory and oncologic diseases.
