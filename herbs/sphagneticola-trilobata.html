Sphagneticola trilobata

Indian daisy

Sphagneticola trilobatata, commonly known as Bay Biscayne creeping-oxeye, is a resilient perennial herb frequently used in traditional medicine. Renowned for its bioactive compounds, it exhibits significant antimicrobial, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory properties. While widely studied for its therapeutic potential, its invasive nature requires careful management in diverse ecosystems.

  • Scientific name: Sphagneticola trilobata
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered under Asterales and placed in the family Asteraceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Sphagneticola.

It also extends its range to the island of Madeira in the same region. Moving toward West Tropical Africa, the species is documented in the country of Benin. Finally, its presence is noted in the West African nations of Guinea and Nigeria.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Bay Biscayne creeping weed, is a versatile medicinal herb where various components contribute to its therapeutic profile. The whole plant is often utilized in traditional remedies, as it contains a complex array of bioactive compounds distributed throughout its structure. Specifically, the leaves are highly valued for their chemical properties, frequently being the primary focus in pharmacological studies. Supporting the foliage are the sturdy stems, which facilitate the plant's creeping growth habit across the ground. In many preparations, the aerial parts are harvested to create extracts or infusions, while the root system serves as the foundational anchor, absorbing the nutrients necessary for the plant's vitality.
