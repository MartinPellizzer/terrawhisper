Indian Daisy, scientifically known as Sphagneticola trilobata, is a resilient creeping perennial valued for its medicinal potential. Often found in tropical regions, this plant is traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat skin ailments, inflammation, and digestive issues. Its bioactive compounds offer promising therapeutic properties for modern pharmacological research.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several distinct regions. Within the Macaronesian archipelago, it can be found inhabiting the Canary Islands. It also extends its range to the island of Madeira in the same region. Moving toward West Tropical Africa, the species is documented in the country of Benin. Finally, its presence is noted in the West African nations of Guinea and Nigeria.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Bay Biscayne creeping weed, is a versatile medicinal herb where various components contribute to its therapeutic profile. The whole plant is often utilized in traditional remedies, as it contains a complex array of bioactive compounds distributed throughout its structure. Specifically, the leaves are highly valued for their chemical properties, frequently being the primary focus in pharmacological studies. Supporting the foliage are the sturdy stems, which facilitate the plant's creeping growth habit across the ground. In many preparations, the aerial parts are harvested to create extracts or infusions, while the root system serves as the foundational anchor, absorbing the nutrients necessary for the plant's vitality.
