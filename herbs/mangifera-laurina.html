Mangifera laurina

Mangifera laurina, commonly known as the laurel mango, is a significant medicinal plant prized for its diverse therapeutic properties. Native to tropical regions, its leaves, bark, and fruit contain bioactive compounds used in traditional medicine to treat inflammation, infections, and digestive ailments, offering vast potential for modern pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Mangifera laurina
  • Family: Anacardiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Sapindales and the family Anacardiaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Mangifera.

Common Names

This plant, Mangifera laurina, is known by various regional names across different languages and cultures. In Thailand, it is referred to as มะม่วงกะเลิง (th), while in Bangladesh, it is called উরি আম (bn). In Indonesia, the species is commonly known as mangga pari (id), and in Java, it goes by the name Pelem kecik (jv). Additionally, in Chinese, it is identified as 长梗杧果 (zh).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning several distinct regions across Asia. In China, its presence is specifically noted within the South-Central areas. Moving toward the Indian Subcontinent, it can be found growing in Bangladesh. The Indo-China region also hosts the species, with documented populations in Cambodia and Myanmar. Finally, its distribution extends further into Vietnam, completing its regional coverage.
