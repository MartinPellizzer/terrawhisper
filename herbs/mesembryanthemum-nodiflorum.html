Reproduction

The reproduction of Mesembryanthemum nodiflorum is characterized by a bisexual sexual reproductive system where self-fertilization is absent, necessitating biotic pollination. The flowering period typically begins in February and concludes in April, during which the plant produces white flowers that are serviced by an insect pollination syndrome. Following successful pollination, the plant develops a capsule-type fruit, which measures between 0.8 cm and 1 cm in width. This fruit is dehiscence-driven, releasing a prolific number of seeds, with each fruit containing more than 1,000 seeds. The individual seeds are quite small, exhibiting a mean mass of approximately 0.002720625 g (ranging from a minimum of 0.000115 g to a maximum of 0.02023 g) and a consistent seed volume of 5 mm³. Dispersal of these seeds occurs through multiple mechanisms, primarily utilizing a hydrochorous (water-based) syndrome as well as endozoochorous methods, ensuring the plant's ability to colonize new environments.
