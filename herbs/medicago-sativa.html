Reproduction

The reproduction of Medicago sativa involves both sexual and asexual processes, with its sexual reproduction characterized by bisexual flowers that exhibit a violet color. The flowering period typically begins in May and concludes in July, featuring flowers that range in length from 0.7 cm to 0.8 cm. The plant relies on biotic pollination, specifically utilizing an insect pollination syndrome, and is noted to lack self-fertilization. Following pollination, the plant develops black, dry fruit in the form of a pod. These pods are relatively small, with a mean length of 0.5 cm and a width varying between 0.4 cm and 0.8 cm. The fruit is dehiscent, facilitating the release of a high volume of seeds, often exceeding 1000 seeds per fruit. The individual seeds are quite small, with a mean length of 3.5 mm (ranging from 3 to 4 mm), a mean width of 4 mm, and a mean volume of 4.2 mm³. The seed mass is highly variable, with a mean of approximately 0.00228 g, ranging from a minimum of 0.00057 g to a maximum of 0.00373 g. Dispersal is primarily an anemochorous process, meaning the seeds are dispersed by the wind.
