Laurus nobilis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1004 studies) · ★★★★★

Bay laurel, scientifically known as Laurus nobilis, is a versatile evergreen shrub celebrated for centuries in both culinary and medicinal traditions. Renowned for its aromatic leaves, it possesses potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties, making it a valuable natural remedy for digestive health and skin wellness.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae, the order Laurales, and the family Lauraceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Laurus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range stretching across different regions of Europe. In Northern Europe, it can be found growing in Great Britain and Ireland. Its distribution extends significantly into Southwestern Europe as well. Within this southern region, the species is present in France and on the island of Corse. Additionally, it inhabits the Baleares archipelago, completing its Mediterranean presence.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Laurus nobilis, utilizes various components for its medicinal and culinary properties. The most commonly used part is the aromatic leaves, which are harvested for their essential oils, though even a single leaf can provide a potent infusion. During its blooming season, the plant produces small, pale yellow flowers, and each individual flower contributes to the overall beauty of the shrub. Once pollination is complete, the plant develops dark, berry-like fruits that serve as a key part of its reproductive cycle.
