Reproduction

The reproduction of Senecio vulgaris is characterized by sexual processes through bisexual flowers arranged in a panicle inflorescence. These flowers are yellow in color, with dimensions ranging from 0.8 to 1 cm in length and 0.4 to 0.45 cm in width. The plant utilizes a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on insects such as bees to facilitate fertilization, although self-fertilization is also present. The flowering period is highly variable, potentially spanning from December through April. Following successful pollination, the plant produces dry, indehiscent fruits classified as achenes. These fruits typically measure between 0.15 and 0.25 cm in length, with an average length of 0.2 cm and an average width of 0.2 cm. The seeds contained within are extremely prolific, with more than 1,000 seeds per fruit. Each seed has a mean length of 1.8 mm, a mean volume of 0.23 mm³, and a mean mass of approximately 0.00046025 g (ranging from 0.00016 g to 0.00033 g). Dispersal is achieved through an anemochorous syndrome, meaning the seeds are dispersed by the wind. Asexual reproduction is noted to be absent in this species.
