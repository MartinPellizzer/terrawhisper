Microglossa pyrifolia

What's the taxonomical classification of Microglossa pyrifolia?

Microglossa pyrifolia belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Asterales. This plant is a representative of the family Asteraceae, a large group of flowering plants, and is specifically identified by its genus, Microglossa, and its species, pyrifolia.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Microglossa pyrifolia has small, fleshy, pyriform leaves that are typically arranged in an opposite pattern along its slender stems. The foliage is characterized by a smooth texture and a somewhat succulent appearance due to its thickened cellular structure. Its stems are relatively thin and provide support for the compact grouping of leaves. The plant often displays a low-growing or creeping habit depending on its immediate environmental conditions. These morphological features collectively allow the species to adapt to its specific ecological niche.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant Microglossa pyrifolia requires well-draining, acidic soil and consistent moisture levels to thrive in its native habitat.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Microglossa pyrifolia is used medicinally primarily through its leaves. These leafy components are often processed into decoctions or infusions to treat various ailments. Local practitioners frequently utilize the plant's foliage to address inflammatory conditions and skin irritations. The leaves contain specific phytochemicals that contribute to their therapeutic properties. Such traditional applications highlight the importance of this species in folk medicine.

According to a study published by "Food chemistry", the leaves of Microglossa pyrifolia were used to obtain essential oils and methanolic extracts for pharmacological investigations. The essential oil from the leaves contained 58.3% germacrene-d. This volatile oil scavenges DPPH at a level of 27% quercetin equivalents and inhibits linoleic acid peroxidation by 20% Trolox equivalents. Furthermore, the methanolic extracts of the plant showed 70% Trolox equivalents in the lipid peroxidation model and 8% quercetin equivalents in the DPPH assay. These properties likely contribute to the reported hepatoprotective activities of the plant.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, Microglossa pyrifolia, is utilized within Traditional African Medicine to treat various ailments through the application of its leaves and roots. Local practitioners often prepare decoctions or infusions from the botanical parts to address digestive issues and inflammatory conditions. Historical accounts suggest that different ethnic groups have integrated this species into their medicinal practices for generations to manage localized skin irritations. The plant's specific chemical properties are believed by traditional healers to provide relief for common febrile illnesses. These long-standing customs highlight the ecological and cultural significance of the species in regional healthcare systems.

According to a study published by "African journal of traditional, complementary, and alternative medicines : AJTCAM", Microglossa pyrifolia is used in traditional medicine in Cameroon to treat insomnia, epilepsy, anxiety, and agitation. In a stress-induced hyperthermia test, the plant significantly antagonised the increase of temperature. The temperature change decreased from 1.7°C in the control group to 0.2°C at the dose of 128 mg/kg for M. pyrifolia. This plant also showed antipyretic activity by reducing body temperature. Furthermore, M. pyrifolia increased the number of entries into, percentage of entries into, and percentage of time in open arms during the elevated plus maze test.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains acetylenic glucosides and the sesquiterpene germacrene-d as its primary medicinal compounds. These chemical constituents are often studied for their potential biological activities and pharmacological properties. The presence of acetylenic glucosides is particularly significant due to their unique structural characteristics. Additionally, germacrene-d contributes to the complex chemical profile that defines the species. Such compounds play a vital role in understanding the plant's medicinal value.

According to a study published by "Planta medica", four new acetylenic glucosides, numbered 1-4, have been isolated from the leaves of Microglossa pyrifolia Lam. Within these isolated substances, the aglucones 2a and 4a represent new polyynes. The aglucone 3a was specifically isolated as a mixture of E/Z-isomers in a ratio of 4:1. Furthermore, compound 1 demonstrates antibacterial activity.

What health conditions is this plant used for?

This plant is used for treating malaria caused by Plasmodium falciparum, reducing fever, managing skin diseases, and healing wounds. The medicinal properties of the leaves are particularly effective at combating the parasitic infections that trigger high body temperatures. Local healers often apply extracts directly to the skin to soothe various dermatological conditions and infections. Furthermore, the plant acts as a topical agent to promote the rapid closure of open wounds. These diverse applications demonstrate the significant role the species plays in traditional medicine.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", Microglossa pyrifolia is used in the treatment of malaria. The leaf extract of this plant demonstrated antiplasmodial activity. This activity was observed against both the chloroquine-sensitive Plasmodium falciparum strain (3D7) and the chloroquine-resistant strain (W2). The extract was active with an IC50 value of less than 15 ug/ml.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe health issues including agitation, anxiety, dermal infections, epilepsy, and hepatotoxicity. Consumption or improper contact with the specimen may trigger neurological disturbances such as heightened agitation and intense anxiety. Some individuals might also experience the onset of seizures related to epilepsy following exposure. Direct skin contact is known to lead to various painful dermal infections. Furthermore, the plant contains compounds that can result in significant hepatotoxicity by damaging the liver.

According to a study published by "African journal of traditional, complementary, and alternative medicines : AJTCAM", Microglossa pyrifolia is used in traditional medicine in Cameroon to treat insomnia, epilepsy, anxiety, and agitation. In the stress-induced hyperthermia test, the plant significantly antagonised the increase of temperature. Specifically, the ΔT° decreased from 1.7°C in the control group to 0.2°C at the dose of 128 mg/kg for M. pyrifolia. The plant also showed anxiolytic-like activity in the elevated plus maze test by increasing the number of entries into, the percentage of entries into, and the percentage of time in open arms. Furthermore, Microglossa pyrifolia possesses antipyretic activities by reducing the body temperature. These results suggest the plant could be helpful in the treatment of anxiety and fever.
