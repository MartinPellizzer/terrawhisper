White water-lily, scientifically known as Nymphaea alba, is a perennial aquatic plant renowned for its striking white blooms and historical medicinal significance. Traditionally used to treat inflammation, anxiety, and digestive issues, its sedative properties stem from unique alkaloids. This elegant species remains a vital subject in ethnobotanical studies.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Nymphaea alba is characterized by a primarily sexual process through bisexual flowers, as asexual reproduction is absent. The flowering period begins in June and extends through September. These large flowers, which range in length from 10 cm to a maximum of 20 cm, rely on biotic pollination, specifically utilizing an insect pollination syndrome. Notably, self-fertilization is absent in this species. Following successful pollination, the plant produces fleshy, indehiscent fruit categorized as a berry. These fruits typically measure between 2.5 cm and 3 cm in length, with a mean length of 2.75 cm. Each fruit is prolific, containing between 101 and 1000 seeds. The seeds themselves are uniform in their dimensions, with a mean length of 3 mm and a maximum volume of 8.2 mm³, while maintaining a mean mass of 0.002451 g. Dispersal is achieved through a hydrochorous syndrome, where the seeds are distributed by water.
