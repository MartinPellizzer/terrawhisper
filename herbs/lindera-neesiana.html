Lindera neesiana

What's the taxonomical classification of Lindera neesiana?

Lindera neesiana belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Its taxonomic hierarchy continues through the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, placing it within the order Laurales. Furthermore, this plant is a member of the family Lauraceae, falling under the genus Lindera, with its specific designation being the species neesiana.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Lindera neesiana has small, elliptical to lanceolate leaves that are typically arranged oppositely or sub-oppositely along its woody stems. The leaf margins are often serrated or finely toothed, providing a textured appearance to the foliage. Its stems exhibit a characteristic bark texture that becomes more rugged as the plant matures into a shrub or small tree. The flowers are minute and inconspicuous, often appearing in clusters during the spring season. Small, fleshy drupes serve as the fruit, which typically transition to a distinct color upon ripening.

According to a study published by "Journal of biomolecular structure & dynamics", the essential oils of Lindera neesiana contain phytocompounds that act as potential inhibitors of the SARS-CoV-2 main protease. To determine binding affinity, 107 phytochemical substances were docked inside the binding pocket of the protease. Among these 107 compounds, copaene demonstrated the highest binding affinity with an energy of -7.90 kcal/mol. The study utilized computational techniques including molecular docking, MD simulation, and N-layered Integrated molecular Orbital and Molecular mechanics (ONIOM) calculations. These in silico results indicate that the specific phytocompounds in the essential oils of L. neesiana have significant inhibitor ability.
