Reproduction

The reproduction of Persicaria capitaata is characterized by bisexual flowers that are primarily pink in color and arranged in spike-type inflorescences. These inflorescences are relatively small, ranging in length from 0.006 m to 0.01 m. The individual flowers are minute, with lengths measuring between 0.25 cm and 0.3 cm. Pollination is a biotic process facilitated by insects. The plant exhibits no self-fertilization, indicating a reliance on cross-pollination. Following fertilization, the plant produces dry, black achenes, which are indehiscent fruits. These fruits are quite small, with a mean length of 0.175 cm (ranging from 0.15 cm to 0.2 cm). The seeds produced are highly prolific, with a count exceeding 1000 seeds per fruit. Each seed is extremely light, with a mean mass of 0.000805 g (ranging from 0.00061 g to 0.00061 g) and a consistent volume of 0.54 mm³. The flowering period is quite broad and variable, typically beginning in December and concluding in July, though it can span various months throughout the year. Dispersal is categorized as both unspecialized and anthropochorous, suggesting that while it lacks a highly specialized natural dispersal mechanism, it is often spread via human activity.
