Artemisia vachanica

Artemisia vachanica, commonly known as Vacchan sage, is a resilient perennial herb native to arid regions. Distinguished by its aromatic, silvery foliage, this medicinal plant has long been studied for its bioactive compounds. It offers significant therapeutic potential, particularly in traditional remedies targeting inflammatory and antimicrobial applications.

  • Scientific name: Artemisia vachanica
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Artemisia vachanica, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, falling under the order Asterales. Ultimately, it is a member of the family Asteraceae and the genus Artemisia.

This plant, Artemisia vachanica, is known by several different common names depending on the language and region. In English, it is most frequently referred to as vachanica wormwood, while in Spanish, it is commonly called ajenjo de vachani.

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning several distinct regions of Asia. In Central Asia, its presence is documented within the borders of Tajikistan. Moving into Western Asia, it can also be found growing in Afghanistan. The species extends its reach into the Indian Subcontinent, specifically inhabiting the territory of Pakistan.
