Ipomoea batatas

Sweet potato

Ipomoea batatas, commonly known as the sweet potato, is more than just a dietary staple. Beyond its nutritional value, this versatile plant possesses significant medicinal properties. Rich in antioxidants and bioactive compounds, it is traditionally used to support digestive health, boost immunity, and combat inflammation in various cultures.

  • Scientific name: Ipomoea batatas
  • Family: Convolvulaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Solanales and the family Convolvulaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Ipomoea.

Distribution

This plant, Ipomoea batatas, exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions. Within Southwestern Europe, it can be found growing in both Portugal and Spain. Its presence extends into Southeastern Europe, specifically within the country of Greece. Moving toward the African continent, the species is established in Northern Africa. This widespread range includes significant populations located in Algeria and Egypt.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Ipomoea batatas, is a multifaceted medicinal and nutritional resource where various components offer distinct benefits. The underground structures are particularly significant, as the plant develops thickened tubers that serve as its primary energy storage organs. Within these tubers, as well as in the roots, there is a high concentration of complex polysaccharides, which provide essential carbohydrates and contribute to the plant's therapeutic properties. Furthermore, the leaves are highly nutritious and are often consumed as sweet potato leaf in many cultures, providing a rich source of vitamins and antioxidants that complement the dietary value of the edible roots.
