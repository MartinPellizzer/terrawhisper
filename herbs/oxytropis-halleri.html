Oxytropis halleri

Oxytropis halleri, commonly known as Halleri's milkvetch, is a resilient perennial herb native to specific mountainous regions. Belonging to the Fabaceae family, this medicinal plant is valued for its unique phytochemical properties. Researchers study its bioactive compounds to explore potential therapeutic applications in traditional and modern herbal medicine.

  • Scientific name: Oxytropis halleri
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales, it is positioned in the family Fabaceae. Specifically, it is identified by its genus classification as Oxytropis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Oxytropis halleri, is referred to by several different common names depending on the language and region. In Chinese, it is identified as 变异黄耆 (including the variants 变异黄耆 for zh, zh-hans, and zh-cn), while in Polish, it is called Ostrołódka Hallera. Additionally, in Welsh, the species is known as Meingel porffor.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across several regions of Europe. In Northern Europe, its presence is noted within Great Britain. Moving into Middle Europe, it can be found across the landscapes of Austria and Poland. The species also inhabits the mountainous or temperate areas of Switzerland. Furthermore, its range extends through the territories of Czechia and Slovakia.
