Crotalaria tetragona

What's the taxonomical classification of Crotalaria tetragona?

Crotalaria tetragona belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. It falls under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, situated in the order Fabales. As a member of the family Fabaceae, it is further categorized under the genus Crotalaria, specifically identifying as the species tetragonia.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Crotalaria tetragonia has an erect, perennial herbaceous habit characterized by slender, woody stems that typically reach heights of 1 to 2 meters. The leaves are arranged alternately along the stem and feature small, ovate to lanceolate blades with entire margins. Small, bright yellow flowers emerge from the axils, often appearing in clusters to attract pollinators. The plant produces distinctive, inflated legume pods that contain seeds and often make a rattling sound when dried. These pods are typically oblong in shape and serve as a primary diagnostic feature for the species.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe health issues due to its high concentration of toxic pyrrolizidine alkaloids. These chemical compounds are known to induce significant liver damage when ingested by humans or livestock. Consumption often results in immediate gastrointestinal distress, specifically manifesting as nausea and vomiting. In many cases, these symptoms serve as early warning signs of more profound organ toxicity. Regular or high-dose exposure may lead to permanent hepatic failure or chronic health complications.
