Ligularia wilsoniana

What's the taxonomical classification of Ligularia wilsoniana?

Ligularia wilsoniana belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is further organized into the order Asterales. It is a member of the family Asteraceae, which is a diverse group of flowering plants, and is specifically categorized under the genus Ligularia. Finally, its unique biological identity is defined by its specific designation as the species wilsoniana.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Ligularia wilsoniana has large, broadly ovate to heart-shaped leaves that can reach up to 30 centimeters in width and feature coarsely serrated margins. These leaves are characterized by a distinctively textured surface with prominent, sunken veins that create a rugose appearance. The foliage typically displays a deep green color, though some varieties exhibit reddish hues along the leaf edges. From the center of the basal rosette, tall, upright stems emerge to support bright yellow, daisy-like flower heads. Each flower head is composed of numerous ray florets surrounding a central disc of tubular florets.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude regions of central and western China, specifically within the mountainous provinces of Sichuan and Yunnan. It thrives in the temperate, moist environments typically found along forest edges and near mountain streams. These specific habitats provide the consistent humidity and cool temperatures necessary for its survival in the wild. Because its range is restricted to these specific elevations, it is considered an endemic species to these Asian highlands. Natural populations are largely concentrated in these rugged, alpine ecosystems.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires consistently moist, organic-rich soil and a location that provides partial shade to protect its large leaves from scorching sunlight.

It thrives best in temperate climates where it can benefit from cool summers and well-draining but damp ground. Dividing the clumps every few years helps prevent overcrowding and encourages fresh, healthy foliage.
