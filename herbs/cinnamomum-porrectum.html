What's the taxonomical classification of Cinnamomum porrectum?

Cinnamomum porrectum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it falls under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic progression through the order Laurales, it is situated within the family Lauraceae. Finally, the plant is defined by its genus, Cinnamomum, and its specific epithet, porrectum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Cinnamomum porrectum has small, simple, opposite leaves that are typically elliptical or lanceolate in shape with entire margins and an entire or slightly wavy edge. The plant exhibits a woody structure with slender branches that can grow in an upright or spreading manner depending on the environment. Small, inconspicuous flowers are produced in axillary clusters, which eventually develop into small drupes. These fruits are typically ovoid and change color as they reach maturity.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Cinnamonum porrectum utilizes its bark and wood for various medicinal applications. The inner bark is harvested to extract essential oils that possess potent antimicrobial properties. Additionally, the wood is processed to create decoctions used in traditional remedies to treat digestive ailments. These specific components are valued for their ability to stimulate circulation and reduce inflammation within the body. Careful preparation of these plant parts ensures the effective delivery of their therapeutic compounds.

According to a study published by "Journal of the Medical Association of Thailand = Chotmaihet thangphaet", the wood of Cinnamomum porrectum is used traditionally as a spice, vegetable, carminative, tonic, febrifuge, and for postpartum care due to its anti-inflammatory properties. Researchers investigated the anti-inflammatory activity of crude extracts obtained from the wood using steam distillation, maceration in 50% or 95% ethanol, or a decoction method. The 50% ethanolic extract showed high anti-inflammatory activity with an IC50 value of 19.28±2.43 μg/ml. Additionally, the 95% ethanolic extract demonstrated high activity with an IC50 value of 13.78±3.76 μg/ml. In contrast, the water extract of the plant showed low anti-inflammatory activity with IC50 values >100 μg/ml.
