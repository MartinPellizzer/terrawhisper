What's the taxonomical classification of Pyrrosia lingua?

Pyrrosia lingua belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this group, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Polypodiinae. Following the taxonomic hierarchy further, it is placed in the order Polypodiales and belongs to the family Polypodiaceae. Finally, its specific identity is defined by the genus Pyrrosia and the species lingua.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Pyrrosia lingua has lanceolate, bright green leaves that are sessile and possess distinctively serrated margins along their entire length. These leaves typically grow in a basal rosette pattern, spreading outward from a central point near the soil surface. The leaf texture is relatively thin and smooth, lacking significant pubescence on the upper surface. Small, inconspicuous flowers emerge from the center of the foliage on slender, upright stalks. This structural arrangement allows the plant to efficiently capture sunlight while maintaining a low profile against the ground.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Pyrrosia lingua is used medicinally through its folium, which contains the primary active therapeutic compounds. These leaves are often harvested to prepare infusions or decoctions aimed at treating various inflammatory conditions. The chemical profile of the foliage provides the necessary properties required for traditional healing practices. Practitioners typically focus on the leaf structure to ensure maximum potency during extraction. This specific part of the plant is valued most highly for its concentrated medicinal benefits.

According to a study published by "Zhong yao cai = Zhongyaocai = Journal of Chinese medicinal materials", the medicinal part of the plant Pyrrosia lingua used is the Folium Pyrrosiae. The study analyzed 44 samples to establish an HPLC fingerprint for identification purposes. This identification process utilized a Diamonsil C18 column measuring 250 mm x 4.6 mm with a 5 microm particle size. A mobile phase consisting of a mixture of acetonitrile and 0.8% acetic acid in a gradient elution was employed for the analysis. The resulting common pattern for Pyrrosia lingua was set up separately through similarity, cluster, and principal component analysis.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds chlorogenic acid, isomangiferin, and mangiferin. These bioactive substances are primarily recognized for their potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Chlorogenic acid plays a significant role in regulating metabolic processes and protecting cells from oxidative stress. The presence of mangiferin and its isomer, isomangiferin, provides additional therapeutic benefits such as neuroprotective and anti-diabetic effects. Together, these phytochemicals contribute to the complex pharmacological profile of Pyrrosia lingua.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", three active constituents were determined in seven species of Folium Pyrrosiae. The research investigated species included in the 1985 edition of Chinese Pharmacopoeia along with six species used in some districts of China. The study also addressed two adulterant species during the botanical survey and identification.

According to a study published by "Zhong yao cai = Zhongyaocai = Journal of Chinese medicinal materials", an HPLC method was developed to establish the fingerprint of Folium Pyrrosiae using a Diamonsil C18 column measuring 250 mm x 4.6 mm with 5 microm particles. The study analyzed 44 samples to set up the HPLC fingerprint common pattern of Pyrrosia lingua. Through the use of similarity, cluster, and principal component analysis, the fingerprints of Pyrrosia lingua were evaluated. Results showed that the fingerprints of Pyrrosia lingua have a high degree of similarity to Pyrrosia sheareri. This method provides a reliable reference standard for identifying Folium Pyrrosiae from different habitats and species.
