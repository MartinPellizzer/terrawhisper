What's the taxonomical classification of Rubus pectinellus?

Rubus pectinellus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the evolutionary lineage of the order Rosales. Further taxonomic refinement places it within the family Rosaceae, a group known for its diverse flowering plants. Within this family, it is categorized under the genus Rubus, and it is specifically identified by its species name, pectinellus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Rubus pectinellus has woody, arching canes that are densely covered in sharp, recurved prickles and feature large, pinnately compound leaves with deeply lobed, serrated leaflets. The foliage is characterized by a distinctive reddish or purplish tint on the undersides of the leaves and along the stems. These leaves typically consist of five to seven leaflets that possess a coarse, sandpaper-like texture. During the flowering stage, the plant produces clusters of small, white, five-petaled flowers. Once pollinated, the blossoms develop into aggregate fruits known as blackberries, which transition from green to a deep, glossy black when ripe.
