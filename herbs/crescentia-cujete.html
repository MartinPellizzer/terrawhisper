Crescentia cujete

Tzimah tree

Crescentia cujete, commonly known as the Calabash tree, is a versatile tropical plant celebrated in traditional medicine. Renowned for its hard, woody fruit, various parts of the tree are utilized to treat ailments ranging from respiratory issues to digestive discomfort, offering significant therapeutic potential within ethnobotanical practices worldwide.

  • Scientific name: Crescentia cujete
  • Family: Bignoniaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Crescentia cujete, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Lamiales and the family Bignoniaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Crescentia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution that spans several distinct global regions. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in both Benin and Gambia. Moving across the continent, it can also be found in the Northeast Tropical African nation of Chad. The species extends its range to the Western Indian Ocean, specifically within the Comoros. Finally, its distribution reaches as far as the Indian Subcontinent in Bangladesh.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the calabash tree, is highly valued for its medicinal properties, particularly those derived from its unique anatomy. The most significant part utilized in traditional remedies is the large, woody fruit, which serves as a natural vessel for its therapeutic contents. Inside this hard exterior lies a thick, mucilaginous pulp that is frequently harvested and processed into syrups, decoctions, or ointments to treat various ailments such as coughs, respiratory issues, and digestive disturbances.
