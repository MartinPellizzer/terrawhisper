Custard-apple, scientifically known as Annona squamosa, is a tropical fruit-bearing tree celebrated for both its delicious flavor and significant therapeutic potential. Widely utilized in traditional medicine, its various parts possess antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties, offering diverse health benefits ranging from digestive support to managing metabolic disorders.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and order Magnoliales, it is a member of the family Annonaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Annona.

Distribution

This plant, known as Annona squamosa, exhibits a specific distribution across several distinct regions. In the Macaronesian archipelago, it can be found growing in Cape Verde. Its presence is also well-documented throughout various parts of West Tropical Africa. Specifically, it inhabits the territories of Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, and Guinea. Furthermore, the species is widely distributed within the nation of Senegal.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as sugar apple, utilizes various components for medicinal purposes. The leaves are frequently prepared as decoctions to treat ailments such as inflammation and fever, while the individual leaf extracts are often studied for their bioactive properties. The stem bark is also significant in traditional practices, often used in similar ways to the bark itself to address different health concerns. Furthermore, the seed contains potent compounds that are utilized in specific medicinal applications, demonstrating how almost every part of the plant holds therapeutic potential.
