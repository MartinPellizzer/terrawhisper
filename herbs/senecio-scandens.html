What's the taxonomical classification of Senecio scandens?

Seneciio scandens belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this group, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving through its taxonomic hierarchy, it falls under the order Asterales and is a member of the family Asteraceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Senecio, and its unique species designation, scandens.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Senecio scandens has a climbing or trailing growth habit characterized by slender, herbaceous stems that often develop a procumbent form. The inflorescence consists of numerous small heads that emerge from the leaf axils along the length of the vine. What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains several specific pyrrolizidine alkaloids, including the compounds senkirkine, senecionine, seneciphylliine, and jacozine N-oxide. These medicinal compounds are secondary metabolites that serve as the primary chemical constituents of Senecio scandens. The presence of these alkaloids characterizes the chemical profile of the species and influences its biological activity. Researchers often study these particular molecules to understand their potential pharmacological properties. Understanding the concentrations of these substances is essential for identifying the plant's medicinal potential.

According to a study published by "Zhong yao cai = Zhongyaocai = Journal of Chinese medicinal materials", the alkaloids of Senecio scandens growing in Guangdong were analyzed using rapid resolution liquid chromatography-electrospray ionization mass spectrometry (RRLC-ESI-MS/MS). Four alkaloids were identified in the plant, which are senkirkine, dehydrosenkirkine, monocrotaline, and adonifoline. Senkirkine was isolated and purified by silica gel column chromatography. This study notes that senkirkine was firstly isolated from Senecio scandens growing in Guangdong. Senkirkine serves as the main component of Senecio scandens growing in Guangdong.

According to a study published by "Rapid communications in mass spectrometry : RCM", Senecio scandens Buch.-Ham is a plant source for the traditional Chinese medicinal herb Qianliguang used for the treatment of sinusitis. An aqueous extract of S. scandens collected in the Shanxi Province of China was found to contain a total of 9 toxic and 2 non-toxic pyrrolizidine alkaloids. The estimated total content of toxic pyrrolizidine alkaloids in S. scandens was 10.82 microg/g herb, which is significantly higher than the limit of 1 microg/g herb or more recommended by Belgium and Germany. Among the definitively identified alkaloids, 6 were characterized, specifically neoplatyphylline, senecionine, senecionine N-oxide, seneciphylline, seneciphylline N-oxide, and senkirkine. Other alkaloids tentatively assigned include jacobine, jacozine N-oxide (or erucifoline N-oxide), 7-tigloylplatynecine, usaramine, and an isomer of yamataimine.
