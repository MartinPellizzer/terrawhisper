Chelidonium majus

Greater celandine

Chelidonium majus, commonly known as Greater Celandine, is a perennial herb renowned for its bright yellow, latex-rich sap. Historically utilized in traditional medicine, it is primarily recognized for its cholagogue properties, supporting liver and gallbladder health. While potent, its bioactive alkaloids require careful handling and precise medicinal application.

  • Scientific name: Chelidonium majus
  • Family: Papaveraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further organized under the order Ranunculales and the family Papaveraceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Chelidonium.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Greater Celandine, is highly valued in herbal medicine for various components found throughout its structure. The aerial parts, including the leaves and stems, are frequently harvested for their therapeutic properties. A distinguishing characteristic of this species is the presence of a bright, yellow-colored latex that oozes from the stems and leaves when they are damaged. This potent plant sap contains the active alkaloids necessary for its medicinal effects. While the roots are also utilized in traditional preparations, many modern applications rely on a whole plant extract to ensure a comprehensive profile of its bioactive constituents.
