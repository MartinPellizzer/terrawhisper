Trillium erectum, scientifically known as Trillium erectum, is a striking perennial wildflower native to eastern North America. Often called Red Trillium, this woodland plant is celebrated for its deep crimson petals. Beyond its beauty, it holds significant historical importance in traditional herbal medicine for various therapeutic applications.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide distribution across several regions in North America. In Eastern Canada, it can be found growing in the provinces of New Brunswick and Nova Scotia. Its presence also extends through the provinces of Ontario and Québec. Moving southward, the species is located within the North-Central U.S.A. specifically in Illinois. These various locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in diverse habitats across these territories.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Trillium erectum, is a perennial herb where the most significant medicinal component is the underground root. The root is a thick, fleshy rhizome that serves as the plant's primary storage organ for nutrients and energy. In traditional herbal medicine, this rhizome is the part most frequently harvested and utilized, often being dried and processed for its various bioactive compounds.
