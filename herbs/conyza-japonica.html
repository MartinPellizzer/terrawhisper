What's the taxonomical classification of Conyza japonica?

Conyza japonica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Asterales. This plant is a member of the family Asteraceae, falling into the genus Conyza, and is specifically identified by its species name, japonica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Conyza japonica has an erect, herbaceous annual habit characterized by square stems that reach heights of up to 1 meter and are covered in fine, spreading hairs. The leaves are oppositely arranged, ovate to lanceolate in shape, and feature serrated margins with distinctively long-acuminate tips. Small, white or pale lavender flowers are clustered in dense, terminal spikes that emerge from the upper leaf axils. Each flower possesses five petals and is supported by a tubular calyx that becomes more prominent during fruiting. The plant eventually produces small, dry achenes that are essential for its dispersal in disturbed habitats.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Conyza japonica is utilized medicinally through its aerial parts, which include the leaves, stems, and flowers. These components are often prepared as decoctions or infusions to treat various ailments such as inflammation and fever. The leaves are frequently applied topically to soothe skin irritations or wounds. Additionally, the entire above-ground portion is sometimes dried and powdered for traditional herbal remedies. This multifaceted use highlights the diverse therapeutic potential found within its vegetative structures.

According to a study published by "Fitoterapia", four new sesquiterpenoids named conyterpenol A-D (1-4) and nineteen known analogues (5-23) were isolated from the aerial parts of Conyza japonica. Compounds 3, 4, and 12 displayed moderate inhibition against LPS-induced nitric oxide production in RAW264.7 macrophages with IC50 values in the range of 26.4-33.6 μM. Conyterpenol D (4) is identified as a new type of sesquiterpenoid featuring a seven-membered lactone ring. All 23 compounds showed no obvious cytotoxicity against the human hepatoma cell line (HepG2) and human breast adenocarcinoma cell line (MCF-7) at 100 μM.
