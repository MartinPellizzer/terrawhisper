Jamaican cherry, scientifically known as Muntingaia calabura, is a versatile tropical shrub celebrated for its nutritional and therapeutic properties. Often found in coastal regions, its small, sweet fruits and nutrient-rich leaves are utilized in traditional medicine to treat inflammation, promote wound healing, and support overall immune system health.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning several distinct regions across the globe. In East Tropical Africa, it can be found growing within the countries of Kenya and Tanzania. Its presence extends into the Arabian Peninsula, specifically documented in Oman. Moving toward the Indian Subcontinent, the species is well-established in both Bangladesh and India.
