Muntingia calabura · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (246 studies) · ★★★★★

Jamaican cherry, scientifically known as Muntingaia calabura, is a versatile tropical shrub celebrated for its nutritional and therapeutic properties. Often found in coastal regions, its small, sweet fruits and nutrient-rich leaves are utilized in traditional medicine to treat inflammation, promote wound healing, and support overall immune system health.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Muntingia calabura is driven by biotic pollination processes, specifically utilizing insect vectors such as bees to facilitate fertilization. The plant is capable of self-fertilization, ensuring reproductive success through its pink-colored flowers, which typically measure between 0.4 cm and 0.6 cm in width. Following successful pollination, the plant produces fleshy, indehiscent berries that serve as the fruit type. These fruits are characterized by a mean length of 1.17 cm and a mean width of 1.27 cm. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing more than 1,000 seeds. The seeds themselves are quite small, with a mean mass of approximately 9.33335e-05 g (ranging from a minimum of 2e-05 g to a maximum of 5e-04 g) and a maximum length of 0.5 mm. The seed volume varies significantly, with a minimum of 0.024 mm³, a maximum of 14 mm³, and a mean volume of 3.56525 mm³. Dispersal is achieved through a zoochorous syndrome, where animals play a critical role in distributing the high volume of seeds produced by the fleshy berries.
