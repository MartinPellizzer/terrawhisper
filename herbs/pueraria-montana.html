Scientific literature: Very Extensive (673 studies) · ★★★★★

Kudzu, scientifically known as Pueraria montana, is a perennial climbing vine native to East Asia. Renowned in traditional medicine for its bioactive isoflavones, it is frequently utilized to support liver health, alleviate menopausal symptoms, and manage alcohol consumption. While ecologically aggressive, its medicinal properties remain a subject of significant study.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Pueraria montana is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, primarily driven by insects, with bees serving as a key functional group in the process. The plant exhibits an absence of self-fertilization, necessitating cross-pollination to ensure genetic diversity. Following successful fertilization, the plant produces fruit in the form of a pod, which is a dehiscent type, meaning it splits open at maturity to release its contents. The seeds produced are relatively small, with a mean seed mass of 0.0122625 g, ranging from a minimum of 0.0114 g to a maximum of 0.0132 g. Regarding the spread of its progeny, the species utilizes an anthropochorous dispersal syndrome, indicating that human activity plays a role in the movement and distribution of its seeds.
