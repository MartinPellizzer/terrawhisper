Flemingia macrophylla

What's the taxonomical classification of Flemingia macrophylla?

Flemingia macrophylla belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is further organized into the order Fabales. It is a member of the family Fabaceae, which places it within the well-known legume group. Ultimately, its specific biological identity is defined by its placement in the genus Flemingia, with the specific epithet macrophylla denoting its unique species classification.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Flemingia macrophylla has woody, perennial stems that support large, pinnately compound leaves typically consisting of five to seven leaflets. The leaflets are broadly ovate to elliptical in shape and possess smooth margins with prominent venation. Small, bright yellow flowers emerge in dense racemes from the leaf axils or terminal positions. The plant produces legumes that develop into elongated, brownish pods containing several seeds. Its root system is robust and often features nodules for nitrogen fixation.

Its distribution is largely limited to these specific Asian latitudes due to its preference for warm, humid environments. How is this plant cultivated?

This plant Flemingia macrophylla thrives best in subtropical climates with well-drained, moist, and acidic soils rich in organic matter.

It is typically propagated through stem cuttings or seeds during the early spring growing season. Regular application of organic mulch helps regulate soil temperature and suppress weed competition around the root systems.

According to a study published by "Physiologia plantarum", Flemingia macrophylla is used as ethnic medicine in southwestern China with genistein and genistin as the primary medicinal components. Magnesium treatment significantly promotes rooting, whereas magnesium deficiency restricts root growth. The study utilized six different magnesium concentrations, specifically T0 (0 g L-1), T0.5 (0.045 g L-1), T1 (0.09 g L-1), T2 (0.18 g L-1), T3 (0.27 g L-1), and T4 (0.36 g L-1). Magnesium concentrations of 0.27 and 0.36 g L-1 were found to significantly enhance genistein synthesis and inhibit glycosylation of genistin. Research identified eight gene-protein pairs related to the synthesis of these two specific compounds.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains medicinal compounds including bound CT, condensed tannins, extractable CT, genistein, and genistin. These specific phytochemicals contribute to the diverse pharmacological profile observed in the species. The presence of condensed tannins and various condensed tannins fractions plays a significant role in its bioactive properties. Additionally, the isoflavones genistein and genistin provide essential therapeutic potential. Together, these diverse chemical constituents define the medicinal value of Flemingia macrophyllia.

According to a study published by "Journal of animal science", Flemingia macrophylla contains extractable condensed tannins at a concentration of 90 g/kg DM. When these extractable tannins were reduced from 90 to 50 g/kg of DM using PEG, forage intake increased by an average of 10% (P < .01). This plant showed greater (P < .01) N flow to the duodenum, N absorbed from the intestine, and fecal N compared to Desmodium ovalifoliuum. The addition of PEG resulted in less (P < .05) ruminal escape protein and less (P < .01) fecal N for this legume.
