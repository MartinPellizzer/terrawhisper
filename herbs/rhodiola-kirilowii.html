Rhodiola kirilowii

What's the taxonomical classification of Rhodiola kirilowii?

Rhodiola kirilowii belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is situated in the subclass Magnoliidae and falls under the order Saxifragales. This plant is a member of the family Crassulaceae, which defines its broader botanical grouping. More specifically, it is identified within the genus Rhodiola, with its unique biological identity finalized as the species kirilowii.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Rhodiola kirilowii has a thick, fleshy rhizome that serves as its primary storage organ and grows horizontally beneath the soil surface. During its flowering stage, the plant produces small, inconspicuous flowers clustered on terminal inflorescences. What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to high-altitude regions of the Tibetan Plateau in China, specifically occurring in the provinces of Qinghai and Sichuan. It thrives in alpine meadows and rocky slopes located at elevations typically ranging from 3,500 to 4,500 meters above sea level. These specific habitats provide the cool, moist, and sun-exposed conditions necessary for its survival. The species is primarily restricted to these mountainous ecosystems due to its specialized adaptation to harsh alpine climates. Consequently, its distribution is highly localized within these specific Himalayan and Hengduan mountain corridors.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Rhodiola kirilowii is used medicinally through the harvesting of its rhizomes and roots. These underground structures are collected and processed to extract bioactive compounds that support various health functions. The rhizomes serve as the primary source for medicinal preparations due to their concentrated chemical profile. Practitioners often utilize the roots in traditional remedies to address physical and mental fatigue. By utilizing these specific parts, the plant provides essential elements for therapeutic applications.

According to a study published by "Central-European journal of immunology", the roots and rhizomes of Rhodiola kirilowii are used to create aqueous (RKW) and hydro-alcoholic (RKW-A) extracts. HPLC analysis demonstrated that the RKW-A extract possessed a generally higher concentration of all identified polyphenols, with the highest differences observed for (+)-catechin, p-coumaric acid and narijenin. While everyday addition of these extracts did not change the length of pregnancy in mice, both types significantly increased the number of mated females without offspring. Only in the RKW-A group were a few neonatal deaths noticed within the first 5 days after delivery. Consequently, the study results do not encourage the use of Rhodiola kirilowii hydro-alcoholic extracts during pregnancy or lactation.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains salidroside, phenylpropanoids and their glycosides, phenylethanoids, polyphenols, and p-coumaric acid. These diverse chemical constituents contribute to the unique pharmacological profile of Rhodiola kirilowii. The presence of phenylpropanoids and their glycosides is particularly significant for its bioactive properties. Furthermore, the polyphenols and phenylethanoids work together to provide antioxidant benefits. The inclusion of p-coumaric acid also plays a role in the plant's medicinal efficacy.

According to a study published by "Central-European journal of immunology", Rhodiola kirilowii contains 15 identified polyphenols as detected by HPLC. Of these 15 polyphenols, 8 were present in the sera of mice that received 20 mg/kg of aqueous (RKW) or hydro-alcoholic (RKW-A) extracts. The hydro-alcoholic extract (RKW-A) resulted in higher concentrations of salidroside, kaempferol, chlorogenic acid, bFGF, and VEGF in the sera compared to the RKW group. While RKW sera contain salidroside and antiangiogenic catechins, they did not harm the proliferation of endothelial cells. The study suggests that RKW-A extract and its sera may contain other unidentified substances influencing endothelial cell proliferation.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe health complications including impaired proliferation of endothelial cells, morphometric abnormalities in the kidney structure, neonatal deaths, and oral toxicity. Research indicates that ingestion of the plant leads to significant oral toxicity in subjects. Furthermore, exposure can result in the impairment of endothelial cell proliferation, which may disrupt vascular health. The plant is also linked to morphometric abnormalities within the kidney structure, potentially leading to renal dysfunction. Most critically, studies have documented instances of neonatal deaths following exposure.

According to a study published by "Central-European journal of immunology", Rhodiola kirilowii aqueous and hydro-alcoholic extracts were administered at 20 mg/kg to pregnant and lactating mice. While the aqueous extract (RKW) did not harm the proliferation of endothelial cells, the hydro-alcoholic extract (RKW-A) impaired the proliferation of endothelial cells in comparison to other groups. HPLC identified 15 polyphenols in the extracts, with 8 present in the sera. Chemical analysis showed that concentrations of salidroide, kaempferol, chlorogenic acid, bFGF, and VEGF were higher in RKW-A sera than in RKW sera. These findings suggest that the RKW-A extract is potentially able to modulate pre- and post-natal angiogenesis, which might influence the development of organs in progeny.
