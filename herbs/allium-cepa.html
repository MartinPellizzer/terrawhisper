Plant Parts

This plant, Allium cepa, is primarily characterized by its specialized underground storage organs known as bulbs. The most significant medicinal and culinary part is the large bulb, which develops from the thickened, fleshy bases of the leaves. Within the soil, the plant establishes a network of fibrous roots that originate from the basal plate to absorb nutrients and water. At the very extremity of these structures, the delicate root tips are responsible for active growth and environmental interaction. While Allium cepa refers to the common onion, it belongs to a genus that includes other variations, such as the smaller, more pungent shallot bulb, which shares similar structural characteristics.
