Physiology

The physiology of Colubrina texensis is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, a metabolic strategy typical of many woody dicotyledonous shrubs in the Rhamnaceae family. Under this C3 mechanism, carbon fixation is initiated by the enzyme RuBisCO, which directly attaches atmospheric carbon dioxide to ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP) within the mesophyll cells. This process results in the immediate production of a three-carbon compound, 3-phosphoglycerate, which serves as the foundation for the Calvin cycle. Because it lacks the specialized Kranz anatomy or temporal separation found in C4 or CAM plants, its physiological efficiency is highly sensitive to photorespiration, particularly when stomatal conductance decreases under high temperature or water-deficit conditions.
