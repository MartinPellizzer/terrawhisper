Cibotium barometz

Cibotium barometz, commonly known as the Devil's Claw or Konjac, is a unique medicinal plant prized in traditional Asian medicine. Renowned for its tuberous rhizome, it is frequently used to treat inflammatory conditions, bolster circulation, and manage pain, offering a fascinating intersection of botanical curiosity and therapeutic potential.

  • Scientific name: Cibotium barometz
  • Family: Cyatheaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Polypodiinae, it is ordered under Cyatheales and placed in the family Cyatheaceae. Specifically, it is identified by the genus Cibotium.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Cibotium barometz, is recognized by various local names across different regions. In Nan Chinese, it is referred to as Káu-chit, while in Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, and the Hong Kong dialect, it is commonly called 金狗毛蕨. Additionally, in Indonesian, the plant is known as Penawar jambi.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical presence across several key regions of East Asia. Within China, it can be found spanning from the South-Central provinces to the island of Hainan. Its distribution also extends into the China Southeast region and reaches the high-altitude areas of Tibet. Beyond mainland China, the species is located within the Nansei-shoto islands of Eastern Asia. Together, these locations define the specific ecological range where the species thrives.
