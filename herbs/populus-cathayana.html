Populus cathayana

What's the taxonomical classification of Populus cathayana?

Populus cathayana belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Its taxonomic progression continues through the order Malpighiales and into the family Salicaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by its specific genus, Populus, and its unique species designation, cathayana.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Populus cathayana has an upright, deciduous growth habit characterized by an elongated trunk, lanceolate leaves with serrated margins, and slender, pendulous catkins. The foliage is typically bright green during the growing season and transitions to yellow in the autumn. Its bark is relatively smooth when young but develops shallow longitudinal fissures as the tree matures. The reproductive structures consist of small, inconspicuous flowers that hang in drooping clusters. These morphological features are consistent with other members of the Salicaceae family.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Populus cathayana is utilized medicinally through its leaves, roots, and shoots to treat various ailments. The leaves are often prepared as a decoction to reduce inflammation and alleviate respiratory symptoms. Extracts from the roots are frequently applied to manage digestive issues or skin irritations. Additionally, the young shoots provide specific bioactive compounds that support overall metabolic health. Each of these components offers distinct therapeutic properties depending on the preparation method used.

According to a study published by "Physiologia plantarum", the research examined female and male Populus cathayana subjected to four N fertilization levels ranging from 0-200 mg kg-1. The study focused on biomass accumulation, root-to-shoot (R/S) ratios, and leaf economic traits including leaf thickness, leaf mass per area (LMA), and leaf vein density (LVD). Nitrogen fertilization increased biomass and net photosynthetic rate while reducing the R/S ratio in both sexes. Nitrogen resorption efficiency (NRE) decreased under nitrogen enrichment, while phosphorus resorption efficiency (PRE) increased, resulting in NRE:PRE ratios < 1 across fertilization levels. NRE was positively associated with R/S ratio, leaf thickness, LMA, and LVD, but negatively related to total biomass.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe gastrointestinal distress, acute kidney injury, and potential neurological impairment if ingested in improper doses. Consuming the plant often leads to intense nausea, vomiting, and abdominal cramping as the body reacts to its toxic compounds. Some individuals may also experience significant renal stress, which can result in long-term kidney damage or failure. Furthermore, the chemical properties of the plant are known to interfere with central nervous system functions, leading to dizziness or confusion. These systemic complications make the plant extremely dangerous for human consumption.
