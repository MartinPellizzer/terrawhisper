Cichorium intybus subsp. intybus

Cichorium intybus subsp. intybus, commonly known as chicory, is a versatile medicinal plant valued for its bitter properties. Rich in inulin and sesquiterpene lactones, it is traditionally used to support digestive health, stimulate bile production, and support liver function. This perennial herb remains a cornerstone in herbal digestive therapies.

  • Scientific name: Cichorium intybus subsp. intybus
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, scientifically known as Cichorium intybus subsp. intybus, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Asterales. Finally, it is a member of the family Asteraceae and falls under the genus Cichorium.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific presence across several regions within Northern Europe. In the western parts of the continent, it can be found growing in both Great Britain and Ireland. Moving further north, its distribution extends into the Scandinavian Peninsula, including Norway. The species is also documented within the borders of Denmark. Finally, its geographical range reaches as far as Finland in the northeastern reaches of Northern Europe.
