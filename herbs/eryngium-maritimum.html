Sea-holly, scientifically known as Eryngium maritimum, is a striking perennial herb native to coastal sand dunes. Distinguished by its spiny, blue-tinted leaves and metallic flowers, it thrives in saline environments. Historically valued in traditional medicine, this resilient plant plays a vital role in stabilizing sandy habitats and supporting biodiversity.

Ecology

The ecology of Eryngium maritimum is defined by its specialized adaptation to highly dynamic and harsh coastal environments. It thrives primarily in habitats characterized by shifting sand, sea shores, and stony places, where it occupies the pioneer zones of maritime ecosystems. As a psammophyte, the plant is uniquely evolved to endure the physical stresses of its surroundings, including salt spray, high wind speeds, and the unstable nature of sandy substrates. Its presence in these nutrient-poor, saline, and frequently disturbed sites allows it to avoid competition from less resilient species. By anchoring itself in sandy and stony coastal terrains, the plant plays a functional role in stabilizing these maritime fringes, navigating the extreme fluctuations in moisture and salinity inherent to the interface between land and sea.

The reproduction of Eryngium maritimum is characterized by a sexual process, as asexual reproduction is notably absent. This species follows a monocarpic life cycle, meaning it typically flowers once before completing its life cycle.
