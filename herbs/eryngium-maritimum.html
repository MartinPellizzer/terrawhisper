Eryngium maritimum

Sea-holly

Eryngium maritimum, commonly known as sea holly, is a resilient perennial herb native to coastal sand dunes. Distinguished by its striking blue-tinged, spiny leaves and architectural flower heads, this plant thrives in saline environments. Historically valued in traditional medicine, it offers unique bioactive properties for various therapeutic applications.

  • Scientific name: Eryngium maritimum
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Apiales, it is classified under the family Apiaceae and is identified by the genus Eryngium.

Common Names

This plant is known by a variety of different common names depending on the region and language. In Poland, it is referred to as Mikołajek nadmorski, while in French Brittany, it is called Dünemfisel. In Catalonia, the plant is known as card marí, and in Hungary, it goes by the name tengerparti iringó. Furthermore, in Corsica, it is commonly identified as Cardu marinu.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several regions within Northern Europe. Specifically, it can be found growing along the coastal areas of Denmark and Great Britain. Its presence also extends to the shores of Ireland, where it thrives in sandy habitats. Furthermore, the species is documented in the northern territories of Norway. Finally, it can be located across the coastline of Sweden.
