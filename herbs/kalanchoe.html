Kalanchoe

Kalanchoë

Kalanchoe, commonly known as Mother of Thousands, is a resilient succulent celebrated for its striking rosette shapes and unique propagation method. Beyond its ornamental beauty, various species possess medicinal properties, traditionally used in folk medicine to support wound healing, reduce inflammation, and manage certain ailments through their bioactive compounds.

  • Scientific name: Kalanchoe
  • Family: Crassulaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and order Saxifragales, it is further categorized into the family Crassulaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Kalanchoe.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution primarily concentrated within the Mediterranean region. In Southwestern Europe, it can be found inhabiting the various islands of the Baleares. Its presence extends to the rugged landscapes of Corse and the coastal areas of Sardegna. Furthermore, the species is widely distributed across the mainland territories of Spain and Portugal. Together, these locations define the key environmental niches where this medicinal plant thrives.
