This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Under the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls into the order Fabales and the family Fabaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Biancaea.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning both the Macaronesian islands and parts of West Tropical Africa. Within the Macaronesian region, its distribution includes the Canary Islands and Madeira. Moving toward the African continent, it can be found across several West Tropical African nations. Specifically, it is documented to grow in Liberia and Sierra Leone. Furthermore, its presence extends eastward to include Nigeria within its natural habitat.

Plant Parts

This plant, Biancaea decapetalous (formerly known as Mitragyna decapetala), possesses medicinal properties that are derived from specific underground structures. The medicinal value is primarily concentrated in the roots and the root bark, which are harvested for their bioactive compounds. In traditional practices, these parts are often processed to prepare decoctions or infusions used to treat various ailments, making the root system and its protective bark essential components of the plant's therapeutic profile.
