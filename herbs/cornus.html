Cornus

Dogwood

Cornus, commonly known as dogwood, is a diverse genus of flowering shrubs and trees valued for both ornamental beauty and medicinal potential. Historically used in traditional practices, various species offer antioxidant properties and anti-inflammatory benefits, making them a subject of growing interest in modern herbal pharmacology and wellness.

  • Scientific name: Cornus
  • Family: Cornaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Cornales and the family Cornaceae, it is classified under the genus Cornus.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Cornus, offers medicinal value primarily through its fruit, which is referred to in traditional pharmacopoeia as Corni fructus. These drupes, often harvested when ripe, contain various bioactive compounds that are utilized in various herbal formulations. While the plant itself is characterized by its distinctive bark and foliage, it is the processed fruit that serves as the primary therapeutic component in many medicinal applications.
