Nicotiana glauca

Nicotiana glauca, commonly known as tree tobacco, is a perennial shrub native to South America. Characterized by its silvery-green foliage and tubular yellow flowers, it is widely recognized for its potent alkaloid content. While studied for pharmacological properties, it is considered invasive and toxic if ingested by humans or animals.

  • Scientific name: Nicotiana glauca
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Solanales, it is a member of the Solanaceae family. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is established within the genus Nicotiana.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Nicotiana glauca, is referred to by various names depending on the region and language. In Russian, it is called Табачное дерево, while in Quechua, it is known as Hatun sayri. It is called Blåwe toubak in Waadhu, whereas in Catalan, it is referred to as tabac de jardí. Finally, in German, the species is commonly known as Blaugrüner Tabak.

Plant Parts

This plant, Nicotiana glauca, exhibits distinct morphological features across its various structures. The leaves are typically large, lanceolate, and possess a characteristic glaucous, bluish-green hue that gives the species its name. From the central stems, which are sturdy and upright, numerous branches extend outward to create a bushy growth habit. These branches eventually support clusters of trumpet-shaped flowers, which are often pale yellow or creamy in color and serve as a vital component for attracting pollinators.
