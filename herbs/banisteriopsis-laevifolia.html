Banisteriopsis laevifolia

Banisteriopsis laevifolia, commonly known as a key component of traditional ayahuasca brews, is a woody vine native to the Amazon rainforest. Renowned for its potent psychoactive properties, it contains harmala alkaloids that act as reversible MAO inhibitors, facilitating profound spiritual and therapeutic experiences within indigenous healing rituals.

  • Scientific name: Banisteriopsis laevifolia
  • Family: Malpighiaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malpighiales, it is specifically nested within the family Malpighiaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Banisteriopsis, with the specific epithet laevifolia.

Common Names

This plant, Banisteriopsis laevifolia, is known by various regional names depending on the linguistic and cultural context. In English, it is commonly referred to as the smooth-leaved woody vine, while in Spanish, it is known as la vid de la ayahuasca or la liana de la ayahuasca. In Portuguese, the plant is frequently called ayahuasca or cipó ayahuasca.
