Arabic coffe

Coffea arabica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (787 studies) · ★★★★★

Arabic coffee, scientifically known as Coffea arabica, is a premier species celebrated for its exceptional quality and complex flavor profiles. Originating from the Ethiopian highlands, this medicinal plant is prized not only for its stimulating caffeine content but also for its rich antioxidant properties and diverse therapeutic potential worldwide.

The genetics of Coffea arabica are characterized by a complex polyploid nature that distinguishes it from many other species within the Rubiaceae family. The species is fundamentally an allotetraploid, possessing a diploid base number that results in a total chromosome number of 2n = 4x = 44, although variations in chromosomal counts such as 22, 66, or 88 can be observed in different cytogenetic contexts or related lineages. Specifically, the ploidy level of 2 (referring to its tetraploid status relative to its diploid progenitors) reflects its hybrid origin, arising from the natural hybridization of Coffea canephora and Coffea eugenioides. This genetic configuration contributes to its unique genomic architecture, which plays a critical role in its phenotypic expression, including its specific chemical profiles and adaptation to diverse environmental conditions.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Coffea arabica begins with the development of cyme inflorescences featuring white flowers that typically measure between 0.7 cm and 0.8 cm in length. The flowering period generally commences in July and concludes in September, though this timing can be variable. These flowers rely on biotic pollination, specifically utilizing insects such as bees to facilitate fertilization, which is also noted to be present through self-fertilization. Following pollination, the plant produces fleshy drupes that are initially green in color. These fruits vary in size, with a length ranging from 1 cm to 1.8 cm and a width between 1.2 cm and 1.5 cm. Each fruit typically contains between 1 and 10 seeds. The resulting seeds exhibit a mean mass of approximately 0.32095875 g (ranging from a minimum of 0.1323 g to a maximum of 0.4717 g) and a consistent volume of 440 mm³. Dispersal of the species is characterized as anthropochorous, occurring through human intervention.
