Coffea arabica

Arabic coffe

Coffea arabica, commonly known as Arabica coffee, is a premier medicinal and stimulant plant native to the Ethiopian highlands. Rich in antioxidants and bioactive compounds like caffeine, it is widely utilized for enhancing cognitive focus, boosting metabolic rates, and providing significant neuroprotective benefits through its complex chemical profile.

  • Scientific name: Coffea arabica
  • Family: Rubiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Coffea arabica, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Gentianales and the family Rubiaceae. Finally, it is classified under the genus Coffea.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several key regions in the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in Guinea-Bissau and Guinea. Its presence also extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, specifically within the Congo. Furthermore, the species inhabits the nation of Gabon. Finally, its geographical range includes the islands located within the Gulf of Guinea.

Plant Parts

This plant, Coffea arabica, possesses several distinct botanical components that contribute to its lifecycle and medicinal potential. The process begins with the delicate, fragrant flowers that emerge to facilitate pollination. Once fertilized, these blossoms develop into small, fleshy fruits, commonly known as coffee cherries. Within these cherries, one typically finds the green seeds, which represent the unroasted state of the plant's reproductive core. These green seeds are later processed and roasted to become the familiar coffee seeds used globally for their stimulating properties. Additionally, the lush, dark leaves of the plant are often studied for their antioxidant content and various phytochemical benefits.
