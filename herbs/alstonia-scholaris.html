Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range across several key regions in Asia. In China, its distribution extends through the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan areas. Moving toward the Indian Subcontinent, the species can be found growing in the state of Assam. Additionally, it is also naturally occurring within the borders of Bangladesh. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's presence across both East and South Asian landscapes.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Scholar Tree, possesses various medicinal components that are utilized in traditional practices. The broad, green leaves are frequently harvested for their bioactive properties, with each individual leaf often prepared in decoctions or infusions to treat specific ailments. Beyond the foliage, the woody portions of the tree are equally significant; the bark is a key source of medicinal compounds, specifically when extracting nutrients from the stem bark of younger branches. In many traditional applications, even the thicker trunk bark is utilized, demonstrating that almost every structural part of the tree contributes to its therapeutic profile.
