Caryocar villosum, scientifically known as Caryocar villosum, is a majestic tropical tree native to the Amazon rainforest. Often called the "Piquiá," it is highly valued for its nutritious, fatty fruits and medicinal properties. Traditionally, its extracts are used to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and various digestive issues.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several regions of the South American continent. In Northern South America, it can be found growing within French Guiana and Venezuela. Moving toward the western part of the continent, its range extends into Ecuador and Bolivia. Additionally, the species is present within the West-Central region of Brazil. Together, these locations define the diverse geographical footprint of Caryocar villosum.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as pé-de-boi, possesses distinct morphological features that contribute to its medicinal and nutritional value. The fruit is a large, drupaceous structure containing a rich, oily pulp that is highly valued for its fatty acid content and traditional therapeutic uses. Supporting the plant's substantial growth is a robust, woody stem that provides the necessary structural integrity to carry the weight of its heavy canopy and large fruits.
