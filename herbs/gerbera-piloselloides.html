Gerbera piloselloides

Gerbera piloselloides, commonly known as the hairy gerbera, is a resilient medicinal herb native to various tropical regions. Valued in traditional medicine, this plant possesses significant bioactive properties, including anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects. Its unique chemical composition makes it a subject of interest for developing natural therapeutic treatments.

  • Scientific name: Gerbera piloselloides
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales, it falls into the family Asteraceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Gerbera and the specific species Gerbera piloselloides.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in both Guinea and Togo. The species also extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, where it can be found in the Central African Republic. Furthermore, it inhabits parts of Cameroon within the same central region. Finally, the geographical range of this medicinal plant includes the Congo.

Plant Parts

This plant, Gerbera piloselloides, is utilized in various traditional medicinal practices where different components offer unique therapeutic benefits. Often, the whole plant is harvested and processed to capture its full spectrum of bioactive compounds, though in some specific preparations, the whole plants are dried and decocted. The chemical profile of the species is enriched by the presence of volatile oil, which contributes to its aromatic and medicinal properties. Furthermore, the medicinal efficacy can also be derived from the subterranean structures, including the roots, which are frequently employed in topical or systemic treatments, as well as the individual root itself when used in concentrated extracts.
