Ligularia songarica

What's the taxonomical classification of Ligularia songarica?

Ligularia songarica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Following its taxonomic hierarchy, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. As a member of the order Asterales, it falls under the family Asteraceae, which is a large group of flowering plants. Finally, its specific biological identity is defined by the genus Ligularia and the species songarica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Ligularia songarica has a perennial growth habit characterized by large, cordate to ovate leaves with coarsely serrated margins and a basal rosette arrangement. The foliage is typically bright green and possesses a somewhat rugose or wrinkled texture on the upper surface. During the flowering season, the plant produces tall, erect flowering stalks that emerge from the center of the leaf cluster. These stems support composite flower heads featuring bright yellow ray florets surrounding a central disc. The root system is composed of thick, creeping rhizomes that allow the plant to spread effectively through moist soil.

    What side effects this plant can have?

    This plant can cause severe gastrointestinal distress, liver toxicity, and potential neurological issues due to the presence of pyrrolizidine alkaloids. These specific chemical compounds are known to induce damage to the liver cells upon ingestion. Consuming even small amounts may lead to symptoms such as nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain. Prolonged exposure or higher doses can result in more serious conditions like hepatic veno-occlusive disease. It is essential to avoid consuming any part of this species to prevent these toxic reactions.
