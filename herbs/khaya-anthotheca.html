East African mahogany, scientifically known as Khaya anthotheca, is a majestic timber tree indigenous to African tropical forests. Beyond its valuable wood, this species holds significant ethnobotanical importance. Various parts of the tree are utilized in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to digestive issues.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Sapindales and the family Meliaceae. Ultimately, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Khaya.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical range concentrated within the tropical regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is recorded in the nation of Guinea. Moving into West-Central Tropical Africa, the species can be found spanning across Burundi and the Central African Republic. It is also distributed throughout the Congo and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Consequently, its habitat is primarily defined by these specific West and West-Central African territories.

Plant Parts

This plant, Khaya anthotheca, is a valuable medicinal species where various components are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The bark of the tree is frequently harvested for traditional remedies, specifically through the use of the stem bark to prepare decoctions or infusions. Depending on the specific medicinal application, practitioners may collect the stem barks from different sections of the tree to ensure potency. Furthermore, the reproductive elements, including the seeds, are also recognized for their biological importance, with individual seed extracts sometimes playing a role in local pharmacological practices.
