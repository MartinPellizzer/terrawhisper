Hollyhock, scientifically known as Alcea rosea, is a towering biennial or perennial cherished for its vibrant, bell-shaped blooms. Beyond its ornamental beauty, this plant holds historical significance in traditional herbalism. Renowned for its soothing properties, it has long been utilized to support respiratory health and skin vitality.

This plant, known scientifically as Alcea rosea, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae within the order Malvales. Finally, it is a member of the family Malvaceae and is categorized under the genus Alcea.

Plant Parts

This plant, Alcea rosea, also known as hollyhock, possesses various parts that are utilized for their medicinal properties. The large, colorful flowers are often used to make soothing teas or syrups, while the seeds can be harvested for their nutritional value. Each individual seed contains concentrated compounds that are studied for potential health benefits. Below the surface, the deep roots are frequently employed in traditional remedies to treat respiratory issues and inflammation. Additionally, the broad, textured leaf is sometimes used in topical applications or infusions to support overall wellness.
