Citrus japonica

Fortunella japonica

Citrus japonica, commonly known as the kumquat, is a versatile citrus species prized for its edible peel and nutritional density. Beyond its culinary appeal, this plant holds significant medicinal value in traditional practices, offering potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and digestive benefits through its vitamin-rich fruit and aromatic leaves.

  • Scientific name: Citrus japonica
  • Family: Rutaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindaales, falling into the family Rutaceae. Finally, its taxonomic placement is completed within the genus Citrus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Citrus japonica, is referred to by various names depending on the region and language. In Arabic, it is called برتقال ياباني, while in Japanese, it is written as チートルス・ジャポニカ. In Azerbaijani, it is known as Yapon sitrusu, and in Lithuanian, the name used is Japoninis kinkanas. Additionally, in Lower Sorbian, it is called Japanski kumkwat.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range that stretches across several distinct regions. In East Asia, its presence is firmly established in both Japan and Korea. The species also thrives within China, specifically inhabiting the Hainan island and the Southeast regions. Moving toward a more distant territory, it can be found in West Tropical Africa within the nation of Gambia. Consequently, the distribution of Citrus japonica spans from the far east to the western tropical zones.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the kumquat, utilizes various components for its therapeutic properties, with a primary focus on its aromatic constituents. While the fruit itself is often consumed whole, including the peel, the most potent medicinal value is frequently derived from its essential oils. These essential oils, concentrated within the rind, contain bioactive compounds that contribute to the plant's distinctive fragrance and are widely utilized in aromatherapy and traditional medicine for their stimulating and calming effects.
