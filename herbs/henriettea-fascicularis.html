Henriettea fascicularis

Henriettea fascicularis, commonly known as the Banded Henriettea, is a rare shrub endemic to the coastal forests of Brazil. Valued for its unique botanical characteristics, this plant holds significant potential in ethnobotanical research. Exploring its chemical properties offers promising insights into discovering new bioactive compounds for traditional medicinal applications.

  • Scientific name: Henriettea fascicularis
  • Family: Melastomataceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae in the order Myrtales. Finally, it is a member of the family Melastomataceae and falls under the genus Henriettea.

This plant, Henriettea fascicularis, is known by various common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In English, it is frequently referred to as the banded henriquea, while in Spanish, it is known as henriquea bandeada.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning across parts of North and Central America. In Mexico, its presence is noted in both the Southwest and Southeast regions of the country. Moving southward, the species can be found throughout various parts of Central America. Specifically, its range includes the nations of Belize and Guatemala.
