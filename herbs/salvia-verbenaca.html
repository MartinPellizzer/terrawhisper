The reproduction of Salvia verbenaica is characterized by a sexual process through bisexual flowers, with asexual reproduction being absent. The flowering period is quite extensive, beginning in April and continuing through December. These flowers are blue in color and vary in length from a minimum of 0.3 cm to a maximum of 1.6 cm. The pollination syndrome is biotic, specifically relying on insects such as bees to facilitate fertilization; notably, self-fertilization is absent in this species. Following successful pollination, the plant produces dry, black fruits classified as achenes. These fruits are relatively small, ranging from 0.16 cm to 0.23 cm in width and 0.17 cm to 0.24 cm in length, and they are indehiscent. Each fruit is prolific, containing between 101 and 1000 seeds.
