Morphology

The morphology of Bolbosstemum paniculatum is characterized by a herbaceous growth habit, being strictly non-woody in nature. The plant typically features a robust, tufted structure arising from creeping, scaly rhizomes. The stems are cylindrical, smooth, and erect, supporting a complex inflorescence known as a panicle, which is composed of numerous small, spreading branches. The leaves are long, linear, and grass-like, emerging from the base or along the lower portions of the stem with parallel venation. The flowers are relatively small and arranged in dense clusters along the panicle, exhibiting a delicate structure typical of the Cyperaceae family. The overall architecture reflects a perennial, non-woody life cycle adapted to wetland or marshy environments.
