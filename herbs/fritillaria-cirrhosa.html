Fritillaria cirrhosa

Fritillaria cirrhosa, commonly known as the Bhutanese fritillary, is a precious medicinal herb native to the high altitudes of the Himalayas. Renowned in traditional medicine, its bulbs are highly valued for treating respiratory ailments, coughs, and fevers, making it a significant botanical treasure in various ancient healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Fritillaria cirrhosa
  • Family: Liliaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Under the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized into the order Liliales and the family Liliaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Fritillaria.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across several specific regions within the high-altitude landscapes of Asia. In China, its presence is noted in the South-Central and North-Central territories. It also grows extensively within the Qinghai province and the Tibet autonomous region. Furthermore, its natural habitat extends into the East Himalaya region of the Indian Subcontinent. Together, these locations define the unique geographical range of Fritillaria cirrhosa.
