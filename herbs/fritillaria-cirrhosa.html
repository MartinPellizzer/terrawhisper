Himalayan Fritillary

Fritillaria cirrhosa · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Moderate (100 studies) · ★★★☆☆

Himalayan Friillary, scientifically known as Fritillaria cirrhosa, is a precious medicinal herb native to the high-altitude Himalayan regions. Renowned in traditional medicine, particularly in Tibet and China, its bulbs are highly valued for treating respiratory ailments, coughs, and phlegm, serving as a vital component in many healing formulas.

This plant is primarily distributed across several specific regions within the high-altitude landscapes of Asia. In China, its presence is noted in the South-Central and North-Central territories. It also grows extensively within the Qinghai province and the Tibet autonomous region. Furthermore, its natural habitat extends into the East Himalaya region of the Indian Subcontinent. Plant Parts

This plant, Fritillaria cirrhosa, is a perennial herb characterized by its specialized underground storage organ, the bulb (or bulbus), which contains the essential medicinal compounds used in traditional treatments. From this base, a slender stem emerges to support the plant's reproductive structures. During its early stages of development, the plant produces delicate seedling leaves that eventually give way to more mature foliage. The adult leaf structure is typically linear or lanceolate, contributing to the plant's overall photosynthetic capacity as it grows in its high-altitude habitat.
