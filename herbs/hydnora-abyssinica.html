Physiology

The physiology of Hydnora abyssica is defined by its highly specialized holoparasitic lifestyle, which renders it entirely dependent on its host plants for survival. Lacking chlorophyll, the plant is incapable of photosynthesis, meaning it does not engage in autotrophic carbon fixation. Instead, its physiological processes are centered around the absorption of organic nutrients, water, and minerals directly from the root systems of its hosts via a specialized haustorium. This parasitic connection allows the plant to bypass the need for traditional vegetative structures like leaves or stems. Regarding its metabolic interactions with soil nutrients, Hydnora abyssica is not a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms, instead relying on the host to provide all necessary nitrogenous compounds. Its physiological cycle is heavily geared toward rapid, episodic growth, where metabolic energy is primarily diverted toward the development of large, subterranean-anchored reproductive structures designed to attract pollinators through chemical signaling.
