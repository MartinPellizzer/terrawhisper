Crataegus scabrifolia

What's the taxonomical classification of Crataegus scabrifolia?

Craeataegus scabrifolia belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the biological hierarchy further, it falls under the order Rosales and the family Rosaceae. Finally, the plant is defined by its genus, Crataegus, and its specific species epithet, scabrifolia.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Crataegus scabrifolia has small, ovate to elliptic leaves with serrated margins and a distinctly scabrous or rough texture on the upper surface. The foliage is characterized by a somewhat leathery consistency and a deep green coloration. Its stems are often armed with sharp spines that emerge from the woody branches. The plant produces small, fleshy fruits that typically develop in clusters. These morphological traits collectively distinguish the species from other members of the hawthorn genus.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the mountainous regions of southern and central China, specifically within the provinces of Sichuan, Yunnan, Guizhou, and Hubei. It thrives primarily in temperate forest ecosystems situated at moderate to high elevations. These habitats often consist of rocky slopes or forest margins where well-drained soil is available. The species is typically found in cool, moist environments characteristic of subtropical montane zones. Its range is relatively localized compared to more widespread hawthorn species.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds hyperoside, rutin, ursolic acid, vitexin, and daucosterol. These specific phytochemicals contribute to the diverse therapeutic potential observed in the species. Flavonoids like hyperoside, rutin, and vitexin play significant roles in the plant's antioxidant properties. Additionally, the presence of ursolic acid and daucosterol supports its complex chemical profile. Together, these constituents form the basis for its various biological activities.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", six compounds were obtained and identified from the leaves of Crataegus scabrifolia. These constituents include rutin, hyperoside, vitexin, ursolic acid, daucosterol, and nonacosanol. The main constituents found in the leaves of the plant are flavonoids. Column chromatography was used to isolate and purify these specific compounds.
