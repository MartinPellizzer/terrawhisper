Reproduction

The reproduction of Rumex conglomeratus is primarily sexual, characterized by bisexual flowers that allow for self-fertilization. The flowering period typically spans from June or July through August. Pollination is an abiotic process, specifically utilizing a wind-driven syndrome to facilitate fertilization. The resulting fruit is a dry, indehiscent achene that is black in color. These fruits are relatively small, with a mean length of 0.2 cm (ranging from 0.16 cm to 0.2 cm). The seeds contained within are highly prolific, with counts exceeding 1,000 seeds per fruit. Each individual seed maintains a consistent length of approximately 1.8 mm and a volume of 1.4 mm³. The seed mass is quite fine, with a mean ranging between approximately 0.0013 g and 0.0034 g, and a maximum recorded mass of 0.00387 g. Dispersal of the seeds is achieved through multiple syndromes, including anemochory (wind dispersal), hydrochory (water dispersal), and epizoochory (dispersal via attachment to animals). Asexual reproduction is absent in this species.
