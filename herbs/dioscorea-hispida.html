Dioscorea hispida

What's the taxonomical classification of Dioscorea hispida?

Dioscorea hispida belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Dioscoreales. The plant is further categorized into the family Dioscoreaceae, which contains the genus Dioscorea, and is specifically identified by the species name hispida.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Dioscorea hispida has a creeping rhizome and climbing vines characterized by cordate or sagittate leaves with scabrid surfaces and small, greenish-white flowers arranged in axillary clusters. The stems are herbaceous and twining, often reaching significant lengths as they ascend through surrounding vegetation. Leaves typically feature prominent venation and a rough, hairy texture on both the upper and lower surfaces. The reproductive structures consist of inconspicuous, dioecious flowers that lack showy petals. This climbing habit and specific leaf shape are primary identifiers for the species within its habitat.

What are the pharmacological activities of Dioscorea hispida?

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

These chemical compounds are highly valued in the pharmaceutical industry for producing drugs such as cortisone, progesterone, and various oral contraceptives. What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause severe health side effects including nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea, dizziness, convulsions, respiratory depression, hallucinations, and paralysis. These toxic reactions occur because the plant contains potent alkaloids that disrupt the central nervous system. Ingestion often leads to immediate gastrointestinal distress such as stomach pain and vomiting. As the toxins progress, they can trigger neurological symptoms ranging from dizziness and hallucinations to life-threatening convulsions. The most critical complications involve the failure of vital functions, specifically manifesting as respiratory depression and complete paralysis.
