Dioscorea hispida

Yam (vegetable)

Dioscorea hispida, commonly known as Wild Yam, is a climbing perennial vine native to East Asia. Valued in traditional medicine, its tubers contain bioactive diosgenin. This compound serves as a crucial precursor for synthesizing steroid hormones, making the plant significant in both herbal remedies and modern pharmaceutical developments.

  • Scientific name: Dioscorea hispida
  • Family: Dioscoreaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Dioscoreales and the family Dioscoreaceae. Ultimately, its specific taxonomic identification is placed within the genus Dioscorea.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Dioscorea hispida, is referred to by various regional names depending on the language and location. In Vietnam, it is commonly called Củ mài trắng, while in Indonesia, it is known as Gadung. The name กลอย is used in Thailand, and it shares the name Gadung in Malaysia. Additionally, in Chinese, the plant is identified as 白薯莨.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide distribution across several key regions in Asia. Within China, its presence is noted in Hainan, Tibet, and throughout Southeast China. The species also extends into Eastern Asia, specifically being found in Taiwan. Furthermore, it inhabits parts of the Indian Subcontinent, particularly in the Assam region. Collectively, these locations highlight its significant range across diverse Asian landscapes.
