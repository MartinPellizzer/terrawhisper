Nicotiana tabacum

Tobacco

Nicotiana tabacum, commonly known as tobacco, is a prominent member of the Solanaceae family. While widely recognized for its commercial cultivation, this plant holds significant historical and pharmacological importance. It contains nicotine, a potent alkaloid that serves as both a natural insecticide and a complex subject in medicinal studies.

  • Scientific name: Nicotiana tabacum
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Nicotiana tabacum, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, falling under the order Solanales and the family Solanaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Nicotiana.

Common Names

This plant, scientifically known as Nicotiana tabacum, is recognized by various regional names across different languages. In German, it is referred to as Virginischer Tabak, while in Catalan, it is known as tabaquera. It is called tabák virginský in Czech and Tobaka in Czechia (specifically in certain dialects/contexts), and in Estonian, the plant is called Vääristubakas.

Distribution

Moving toward Middle Europe, the species can be found growing within the borders of Austria and Belgium. Furthermore, its range extends into the central regions of Czechia-Slovakia.
