This plant, Nicotiana tabacum, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, falling under the order Solanales and the family Solanaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Nicotiana.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across several regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its distribution includes the territories of Denmark and Great Britain. Moving toward Middle Europe, the species can be found growing within the borders of Austria and Belgium. Furthermore, its range extends into the central regions of Czechia-Slovakia. Plant Parts

This plant, Nicotiana tabacum, possesses several distinct morphological components that contribute to its chemical profile. The most economically and medicinally significant parts are the leaves, specifically the mature tobacco leaves which serve as the primary site for alkaloid accumulation. On a microscopic level, the surface of each leaf is covered in specialized glandular trichome secretions that play a crucial role in the plant's defense and chemical synthesis. Furthermore, the plant's physiological processes are supported by its underground root tissue, which facilitates the uptake of nutrients necessary for the development of its bioactive compounds.
