Artemisia richardsoniana

Artemisia richardsoniana, commonly known as Richardson's sagebrush, is a hardy perennial herb native to North American prairies. Distinguished by its silvery foliage and aromatic leaves, this plant thrives in dry, open habitats. Historically utilized in traditional medicine, it offers intriguing botanical properties for studying ecological resilience and natural pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Artemisia richardsoniana
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asterales, it is classified under the family Asteraceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Artemisia, with its species designation being richardsoniana.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across much of the northern hemisphere's cold climates. In Russia, its presence is widespread throughout Siberia, specifically within the regions of Krasnoyarsk, West Siberia, and Yakutiya. The species also extends into the Russian Far East, where it can be found in Magadan. Moving across the Pacific, the plant's range continues into the Subarctic regions of North America. Ultimately, this distribution highlights its ability to thrive in the rugged environments of Alaska and various Siberian territories.
