Fragaria orientalis

What's the taxonomical classification of Fragaria orientalis?

Fragaria orientalis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the plant hierarchy, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving down through the taxonomic ranks, it falls under the order Rosales and the family Rosaceae. Finally, its specific classification is defined by the genus Fragaria and the species orientalis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Fragaria orientalis has a low-growing, perennial growth habit characterized by a cluster of trifoliate leaves with serrated margins and shallowly lobed leaflets. The foliage typically features long petioles that emerge from a central basal rosette. Small, white, five-petaled flowers bloom on slender pedicels, each containing numerous stamens surrounding a central receptacle. As the plant matures, this receptacle swells to form a red, aggregate fruit known as a strawberry. The plant often spreads via stolons, which are horizontal stems that develop new plantlets at their nodes.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-drained, acidic soil with a pH between 5.5 and 6.5 and consistent moisture to thrive.

Growers should plant it in a location that receives full sun to ensure maximum berry production and sweetness. Mulching with organic matter helps maintain even soil temperature and suppresses competing weeds around the runners. Regular irrigation is necessary to prevent fruit cracking and to support the vigorous growth of new stolons.
