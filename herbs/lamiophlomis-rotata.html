What's the taxonomical classification of Lamiophlomis rotata?

Lamiophlomis rotaata belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Its taxonomic journey continues through the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae, eventually narrowing down to its specific designation within the genus Lamiophlomis and the species rotaata.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Lamiophlomis rotata has square stems, opposite leaves, and large, tubular, violet-blue flowers arranged in dense, terminal whorls. The foliage consists of rugose, ovate leaves with crenate margins that are typically dark green and textured. Its inflorescence features prominent bilabiate corollas that emerge from bracts, creating a striking visual display. The plant's woody base and spreading habit allow it to form substantial clumps over time. These morphological characteristics are typical of the Lamiaceae family, emphasizing its structural complexity.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Lamiophlomis rotata is utilized medicinally through its aerial parts, specifically the leaf, as well as its underground rhizome and root. The leaves are frequently harvested to prepare various traditional remedies and decoctions. While the stem constitutes a significant portion of the aerial structure, it is often processed alongside the foliage for therapeutic use. The rhizome and root contain concentrated bioactive compounds that are essential for their medicinal efficacy. These different components are carefully selected based on the specific healing properties required for treatment.

According to a study published by "RSC advances", Lamiophlomis rotata is a Tibetan medicinal herb that contains iridoid glycosides (IGs) which serve as pharmacologically active ingredients. A total of 26 IGs were detected in the total ion current (TIC) profile, and 22 of these were identified by comparing retention times and mass spectra to authentic standards. Among these, 5 IGs with the same molecular formula (C17H26O11) were identified for the first time based on different hydroxyl group-substituted positions. Multivariate statistical analysis of the UPLC-TOF-MS data of 24 specimens was used to determine the differences between the aerial and underground parts. While the aerial parts have a high content of total IGs and high antioxidant capacity, these levels are not higher than those of the roots. Results suggest that the dosage should be increased when the aerial parts are used as crude medicinal materials instead of the underground parts.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", Lamiophlomis rotata contains seven constituents including shanzhiside methyl ester, chlorogenic acid, 8-O-acetyl shanzhiside methylester, forsythiaside B, rutin, acteoside and galuteolin. The mean recoveries for these seven compounds were 96.47%-102.2% with an RSD of 0.70%-2.2%. These aerial parts contain more kinds of composition and higher content than the underground parts of L. rotata. This data supports the reasonable use of the aerial part as the medicinal part.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is Lamiophlomis rotaata, which is utilized in traditional medicine to produce various medicinal herbal preparations including Zhixue Zhentong capsules, aqueous extract, and powder. The aqueous extract is commonly prepared by boiling the plant material in water to isolate its active bioactive compounds. These extracts can then be processed into a fine powder for easier administration and storage. Many practitioners utilize the Zhixue Zhentong capsules as a standardized dosage form to treat specific ailments. These different preparation methods allow for a wide range of therapeutic applications in herbal medicine.

According to a study published by "Frontiers in pharmacology", Zhixue Zhentong capsules are a Tibetan medicine preparation solely composed of Lamiophlomis rotata. A total of 36 metabolites of these capsules were identified, which included 13 iridoid glycosides, 9 flavonoids, 9 phenylethanol glycosides, and 4 phenylpropanoids. Among these, 11 metabolites of the capsules entered the blood of normal rats. The study performed quantitative analysis on 6 main metabolites, specifically shanzhiside methyl ester, chlorogenic acid, 8-O-acetyl shanzhiside methyl ester, forsythin B, luteoloside, and verbascoide. The biological effects of Lamiophlomis rotata include hemosis, analgesia, and the removal of blood stasis and swelling.
