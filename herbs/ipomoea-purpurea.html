Tall morning-glory, scientifically known as Ipomoea purpurea, is a vigorous climbing vine celebrated for its vibrant, trumpet-shaped blooms. Beyond its ornamental beauty, this plant holds historical significance in traditional medicine, often utilized for its diverse pharmacological properties, including its potential sedative, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial effects in various cultures.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Solanales, it is classified under the family Convolvulaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic designation is within the genus Ipomoea.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse presence across several distinct regions of the European continent. In Northern Europe, its range can be observed within Great Britain. Moving into Middle Europe, the species is found in both Austria and Germany. The distribution extends further into Southwestern Europe, where it inhabits the Baleares. Additionally, this medicinal plant is also documented growing throughout France.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as morning glory, utilizes its broad, heart-shaped leaves to facilitate photosynthesis and support its rapid climbing growth habit. These leaves serve as a vital component of the organism's structure, capturing sunlight to fuel the production of its vibrant, trumpet-shaped flowers. In various traditional medicinal contexts, the chemical properties contained within the foliage are often studied, although care must be taken due to the presence of alkaloids.
