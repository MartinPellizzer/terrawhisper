Hyptis dilatata

Hyptis dilatata, commonly known as the False Guava or Hyptis, is a versatile medicinal shrub native to tropical regions. Renowned for its vibrant blooms, this plant is traditionally utilized in herbal medicine for its anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant properties, offering significant therapeutic potential in treating various skin and digestive ailments.

  • Scientific name: Hyptis dilatata
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement in the genus Hyptis.

Common Names

This plant, Hyptis dilatata, is known by various names depending on the region and language. In English, it is commonly referred to as the expanded-leaf hyptis, while in Spanish, it is frequently called hiptis dilatada or hiptis de hojas dilatadas. In Portuguese, the species is identified as hiptis-dilatada.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several regions of the Americas. In Central America, its presence is documented within the country of Panamá. Moving into Northern South America, it can be found in both Guyana and Venezuela. The species also extends into Western South America, specifically throughout Colombia. Finally, its range reaches Brazil, where it is located in the West-Central region.
