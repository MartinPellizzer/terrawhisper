Shoreline purslane, scientifically known as Sesuvium portulacastrum, is a resilient succulent native to coastal regions. Renowned for its adaptability to saline environments, this plant offers significant medicinal potential. It is traditionally utilized for its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, serving as a vital resource in various ethnobotanical healing practices.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several distinct regions within the North African and Macaronesian territories. In North Africa, it can be found growing in Morocco, Tunisia, and Western Sahara. Its range extends further into the Macaronesian islands, specifically inhabiting the Canary Islands. Additionally, the species is documented to grow in the Cape Verde archipelago. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's presence across diverse coastal and island environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, Sesuvium portulacastrum, is characterized by its fleshy, succulent leaf structures that are well-adapted to saline environments. These leaves are typically cylindrical or oblong in shape, possessing a smooth texture and a vibrant green color that allows the plant to store significant amounts of water. In medicinal applications, the succulent nature of the leaf is often utilized, as it contains various bioactive compounds essential for its therapeutic properties.
