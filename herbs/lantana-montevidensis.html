Lantana montevidensis

Trailing lantana

Lantana montevidensis, commonly known as trailing lantana, is a versatile evergreen perennial prized for its vibrant purple blooms. While primarily ornamental, this hardy plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Historically, various cultures have utilized its leaves and stems in traditional remedies to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to digestive issues.

  • Scientific name: Lantana montevidensis
  • Family: Verbenaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, specifically classified within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further categorized under the order Lamiales and the family Verbenaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Lantana.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several diverse regions of the world. In Southwestern Europe, it can be found growing within the borders of Spain. Moving toward Southeastern Europe, its presence is also noted in Italy. The species extends to the Macaronesian islands, specifically inhabiting the Azores. Finally, its range reaches the Indian Subcontinent in Bangladesh and extends to New Zealand North in New Zealand.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Lantana montevidebsis, utilizes its leaf as a primary component in various traditional medicinal applications. The leaves are characterized by their serrated edges and a distinct, somewhat pungent aroma that is released when crushed. In many folk medicine practices, these leaves are processed into poultices or decoctions, where their chemical constituents are believed to provide therapeutic benefits for skin conditions or topical ailments.
