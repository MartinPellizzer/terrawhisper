Yellow lupin

Lupinus luteus · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Moderate (117 studies) · ★★★☆☆

Yellow lupin, scientifically known as Lupinus luteus, is a versatile perennial legume valued for its medicinal and nutritional properties. Rich in alkaloids and essential amino acids, it has been traditionally used to support digestive health and manage inflammation. This vibrant plant remains a significant subject in ethnobotanical studies worldwide.

The reproduction of Lupinus luteus is characterized by a primarily sexual process through bisexual flowers, with asexual reproduction being absent. The plant features yellow flowers that exhibit a biotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on bees and other insects for fertilization; notably, self-fertilization is absent in this species. The flowering period is highly variable, spanning from December through August. The reproductive structures include inflorescences that range in length from 0.05 m to 0.25 m. Following pollination, the plant develops fruit in the form of a pod, with dimensions typically ranging from 4 cm to 6 cm in length and 1 cm to 1.2 cm in width. These pods are prolific, containing between 101 and 1000 seeds per fruit. The individual seeds possess a mean width of 7 mm and a mean mass of approximately 0.1277 g, with weights varying between a minimum of 0.08221 g and a maximum of 0.155 g. Dispersal of the seeds is categorized as unspecialized, though it also exhibits anthropochorous tendencies, meaning it can be spread via human activity.
