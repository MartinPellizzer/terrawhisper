Strychnos guiarensis, scientifically known as Strychnos guiarensis, is a potent tropical woody climber native to the Amazon rainforest. Renowned for its high concentration of alkaloids, particularly strychnine, this plant has historically played a critical role in pharmacology and traditional medicine due to its intense, complex biological activity.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical range that extends through parts of both Central and South America. In Central America, its presence is documented within the borders of Costa Rica. Moving into Northern South America, the species is widely distributed across several distinct territories. It can be found throughout the tropical landscapes of French Guiana, Guyana, and Suriname. Finally, its distribution reaches into the northern reaches of Venezuela, completing its regional footprint.

Plant Parts

This plant, Strychnos guianenis, is a woody climber characterized by a robust and climbing stem that allows it to ascend through forest canopies to reach sunlight. While the most pharmacologically significant components of the species are typically the seeds, which contain potent alkaloids, the structural integrity of the plant is provided by this climbing stem, which serves as the primary support system for its foliage and reproductive organs.
