What's the taxonomical classification of Desmodium elegans?

Desmodium elegans belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving through its higher taxonomic ranks, it falls under the order Fabales and is a member of the family Fabaceae. Finally, the plant is placed within the genus Desmodium, with its specific identification being the species elegans.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Desmodium elegans is used medicinally through the application of its leaves, flowers, and stem. The leaves are often prepared as a decoction to treat various ailments or as a topical poultice for skin irritation. The flowers are frequently utilized in traditional remedies to soothe inflammation within the body. Additionally, the stem provides essential components that contribute to the overall therapeutic efficacy of the plant. These various parts work together to offer a wide range of healing properties in folk medicine.

According to a study published by "Asian Pacific journal of tropical biomedicine", the stem of Desmodium elegans was evaluated for its medicinal importance through methanolic extract and various solvent fractions. At a tested concentration of 500 µg/mL, the methanolic extract showed 75% phytotoxicity, while the n-hexane, DCM, ethyl acetate, and n-butanol fractions showed 85%, 85%, 65%, and 5% phytotoxicity respectively. The DCM and ethyl acetate fractions were effective against F. solani with 10% and 20% inhibition. The methanolic, DCM, ethyl acetate, and n-butanol fractions also exhibited insecticidal effects against Callosobruchus analis and Rhyzopertha dominic. All tested samples were found to be devoid of cytotoxic and antibacterial effects.
