Gerbera nivea

What's the taxonomical classification of Gerbera nivea?

Gerbera nivea belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its descent through the order Asterales, the plant is situated within the family Asteraceae. Specifically, it is identified by the genus Gerbera, with its unique classification completed at the species level as nivea.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Gerbera nivea has a rosette of basal leaves that are covered in dense, white, felt-like hairs and bright white ray florets surrounding a yellow central disc. The foliage is characterized by a deeply lobed or pinnatifid structure with a soft, velvety texture caused by these trichomes. These hairy surfaces serve to protect the plant from moisture loss and intense sunlight. The flower heads are composite structures where the outer rays provide a stark contrast to the inner disc florets. Each stem is relatively short and sturdy to support the weight of the large, snowy blooms.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is endemic to the high-altitude regions of South Africa, specifically within the Drakensberg mountain range. It thrives in the rocky crevices and alpine grasslands found at significant elevations. Because it is native to these specific montane environments, its range is quite restricted compared to other members of the genus. The species is typically found in cool, moist habitats that characterize these mountain ecosystems. According to a study published by "Yakugaku zasshi : Journal of the Pharmaceutical Society of Japan", Tu-er-feng is a famous Chinese folk medicine used for common cold with cough, rheumatism, and other conditions. The sources for this medicine are said to be either whole plants of some Gerbera species or Ainsliaea species of family Compositae. This paper studies Tu-er-feng derived from Gerbera species by comparing the internal morphologies of the leaves and roots of G. piloselloides, G. delaavayi, G. nivea, G. anandria, and G. jamesonii. As a result of the study, G. piloselloides is determined as the botanical origin of Tu-er-feng obtained from the recent 16 markets.
