Prunus africana

Pygeum africanum

Prunus africana, commonly known as African cherry, is a medicinal tree native to the montane forests of Africa. Renowned for its bark, it contains phytosterols essential in treating benign prostatic hyperplasia. This precious resource is vital to traditional medicine, though sustainable harvesting remains crucial for its conservation.

  • Scientific name: Prunus africana
  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further organized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Rosales, falling into the family Rosaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Prunus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is specifically noted in Ghana. Moving into West-Central Tropical Africa, the species can be found in Burundi and Rwanda. It also inhabits the Gulf of Guinea Islands within the West-Central Tropical Africa zone. Additionally, the plant is documented as growing in Cameroon.

Plant Parts

This plant, Prunus africana, is highly valued in traditional medicine for its various therapeutic components, with different parts offering unique medicinal properties. The bark is perhaps the most significant part used, as it is frequently harvested for its ability to treat symptoms of benign prostatic hyperplasia. Additionally, the leaves are often utilized in various decoctions to address different ailments, while the root is also employed in traditional healing practices to treat various conditions. Together, these plant parts make Prunus africana a vital resource in both folk and pharmacological applications.
