Typha domingensis

Typha domingensis, commonly known as a species of cattail, is a versatile aquatic plant valued in traditional medicine. Often found in wetlands, its rhizomes and pollen possess significant therapeutic potential. This introduction explores its botanical characteristics, ecological importance, and the diverse medicinal applications used to treat various ailments.

  • Scientific name: Typha domingensis
  • Family: Typhaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Poales, it is classified under the family Typhaceae and the genus Typha.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across several distinct regions of Europe. In Middle Europe, its presence is noted within the borders of Switzerland. Moving toward the Mediterranean, it can be found throughout various parts of Southwestern Europe. Specifically, its range includes the islands of the Baleares and Corse. Finally, the species is also distributed across the mainland territories of France and Portugal.
