African mile-a-minute, scientifically known as Mikania cordata, is a vigorous climbing vine native to tropical regions. While celebrated in traditional medicine for its wound-healing and anti-inflammatory properties, it is also classified as a highly invasive species. Understanding its medicinal potential requires balancing its benefits with ecological management.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found within the diverse ecosystems of West Tropical Africa. Its specific geographical range includes the nation of Gambia, where it grows in local habitats. It is also documented to occur throughout the coastal and inland regions of Ghana. Furthermore, the species can be identified within the territories of Guinea-Bissau and Guinea. Finally, its distribution extends to the tropical landscapes of the Ivory Coast.

Plant Parts

This plant, Mikaenia cordata, is a climbing vine known for its various therapeutic applications involving different components of its anatomy. While the whole plant is often utilized in traditional herbal preparations to harness its full spectrum of bioactive compounds, specific focus is frequently placed on its lush, heart-shaped leaves. These leaves serve as a primary source of medicinal potency, particularly when processed into concentrated leaf extracts that are used for their antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Additionally, although less commonly cited than the foliage, the root system also contributes to the plant's overall medicinal profile, completing the diverse array of parts used in botanical healing.
