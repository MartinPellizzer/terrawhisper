Brown-cedar, scientifically known as Ehretiia acuminata, is a versatile evergreen shrub prized in traditional medicine. Native to tropical regions, it is widely recognized for its therapeutic properties, particularly in treating skin ailments, inflammation, and digestive issues. This robust plant serves as a vital natural resource in ethnobotanical practices.

Distribution

Reproduction

The reproduction of Eretia acuminata is characterized by a flowering period that begins in October and concludes in January, though the timing can remain variable throughout the year. The plant produces a fleshy fruit classified as a drupe, which is notably indehiscent, meaning it does not split open at maturity to release its contents. Within this fruit, the seeds maintain a highly consistent size, with a minimum, maximum, and mean seed volume all measured at exactly 13 mm³.
