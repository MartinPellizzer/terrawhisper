Reproduction

The reproduction of Acacia saligna is characterized by a complex flowering and fruiting cycle that relies heavily on biotic interactions. The plant produces yellow flowers arranged in a raceme inflorescence, which serves as a primary attractant for pollinators. The pollination syndrome is categorized as biotic, specifically utilizing insects to facilitate the transfer of pollen. Notably, self-fertilization is absent in this species, necessitating these insect visitors for successful reproduction. The flowering period is extensive and variable, beginning in January and continuing through to December. Following successful pollination, the plant develops dry fruit in the form of a pod. These pods vary in size, with lengths ranging from 5 cm to a maximum of 14 cm and widths between 0.5 cm and 0.6 cm. The fruit is dehiscent, meaning it splits open to release its seeds. The seeds themselves are relatively small, with a mean mass of approximately 0.015695926 g (ranging from a minimum of 0.00838 g to a maximum of 0.02076 g), a length between 5 mm and 6 mm, and a consistent volume of 16 mm³. Dispersal is achieved through a zoochorous syndrome, where animals play a key role in spreading the seeds to new locations.
