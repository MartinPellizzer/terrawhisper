Jicama, scientifically known as Pachyrhizus erosus, is a versatile tuberous root celebrated in traditional medicine and culinary arts. Rich in inulin and essential vitamins, it offers significant dietary fiber. Beyond nutrition, it is traditionally utilized for its hydrating properties and potential to support digestive health and metabolic balance.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions of the African continent and surrounding islands. In West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence is noted in both Cameroon and Gabon. Moving toward the eastern part of the continent, it can also be found in East Tropical Africa, specifically within Tanzania. The species extends into the Western Indian Ocean, where it inhabits the island of Mauritius. Finally, its range includes the large island of Madagascar located within the same ocean region.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as jicama, is primarily cultivated for its edible tuberous roots, but its various botanical components hold different characteristics. While the fleshy roots are the most utilized part for nutrition and traditional medicine, the plant also produces distinct seeds within its legume-like pods. Unlike the root, the seeds of Pachyrhizus erosus contain various bioactive compounds, though they are generally not consumed in the same manner as the tuber due to their different chemical composition.
