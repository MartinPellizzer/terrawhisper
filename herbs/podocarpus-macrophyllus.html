Podocarpus macrophyllus

Buddhist pine

Podocarpus macrophyllus, commonly known as the Buddhist Pine, is an evergreen conifer valued for its ornamental beauty and medicinal potential. Traditionally used in various cultures, it possesses antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Research explores its bioactive compounds for therapeutic applications, highlighting its significance in both landscaping and natural pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Podocarpus macrophyllus
  • Family: Podocarpaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Pinidae, it is categorized under the order Pinales. Finally, it is classified under the family Podocarpaceae within the genus Podocarpus.

Common Names

This plant, Podocarpus macrophyllus, is widely recognized across various Chinese-speaking regions by its common names. In Simplified Chinese, it is known as 罗汉松 (zh-hans and zh-cn), as well as 罗汉松 in Singapore (zh-sg). In Traditional Chinese contexts, it is referred to as 羅漢松 (zh-hanth and zh-hk).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across several key regions of Eastern and Southern Asia. In China, it can be found extensively throughout both the South-Central and Southeast territories. Its range extends into Eastern Asia, where it is native to the landscapes of Japan and Korea. Interestingly, its presence also stretches toward Northern Europe, specifically within Great Britain. This diverse geographical footprint highlights the plant's adaptability to different climates across the continent.
