Podocarpus macrophyllus

What's the taxonomical classification of Podocarpus macrophyllus?

Podocarpus macrophyllus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida, it is further organized into the subclass Pinidae and the order Pinales. Within this taxonomic hierarchy, the plant is a representative of the family Podocarpaceae and is situated in the genus Podocarpus. Ultimately, its specific biological identity is defined by its species name, macrophyllus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Podocarpus macrophyllus has an evergreen habit characterized by linear, leathery leaves that are typically 5 to 15 centimeters long and dark green in color. The foliage grows in a dense, spirally arranged pattern along woody, branching stems. Its reproductive structures consist of small, inconspicuous male cones and fleshy, berry-like female receptacles that turn reddish or purple when mature. The tree develops a sturdy, upright trunk with fissured gray-brown bark as it reaches maturity. The overall structure is often used for ornamental hedging due to its compact and resilient growth form.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the subtropical and tropical regions of East Asia, specifically spanning across southern China, Taiwan, and Japan. What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant uses its leaves and twigs for various medicinal applications in traditional practices. The leaves are frequently prepared as infusions or decoctions to treat respiratory ailments and inflammation. These foliage extracts are also valued for their potential to regulate blood pressure and reduce fever. Similarly, the twigs are harvested to create medicinal teas aimed at soothing stomach issues or improving circulation. Together, these specific plant parts provide a diverse range of therapeutic properties.

According to a study published by "Tropical biomedicine", methanol leaf extracts of Podocarpus macrophyllus are used to examine effects on Cryptosporidium parvum in experimentally infected mice. Treatment with these extracts showed a significant reduction of the number of C. parvum oocysts shed in the stool compared to the infected control group and the Nitazoxanide-infected treated group at P < 0.001. Histopathological inspection of sections from the ileum and colon displayed signs of improvement after treatment with P. macrophyllus extracts. The study also involved spectrophotometric estimation of the total phenolic and flavonoid content of these extracts.

This research demonstrated the secondary metabolites and the variety of their derivatives present in Podocarpus macrophyllus.
