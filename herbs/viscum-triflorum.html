Physiology

The physiology of Viscum triflorum is characterized by its specialized status as a hemiparasitic plant, a lifestyle that dictates its metabolic and nutrient acquisition strategies. While it possesses sufficient chlorophyll to perform autotrophic functions, its physiological processes are heavily integrated with its host plant through a specialized interface known as the haustorium. This organ facilitates the physiological transfer of water and mineral nutrients from the host's xylem to the parasite's own vascular system. Despite this parasitic dependency for inorganic resources, the plant maintains its own carbon fixation mechanisms. Specifically, its photosynthetic pathway follows the C3 cycle, utilizing the enzyme RuBisCO to fix atmospheric carbon dioxide into organic compounds during the light-independent reactions.
