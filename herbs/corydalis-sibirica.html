Corydalis sibirica

What's the taxonomical classification of Corydalis sibirica?

Corydalis sibirica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is placed in the order Ranunculales. This plant is a member of the family Papaveraceae, which is further categorized into the genus Corydalis, specifically identifying it as the species sibirica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Corydalis sibirica has a basal rosette of pinnately lobed, bright green leaves and upright flowering stems that typically reach heights of ten to thirty centimeters. The plant produces zygomorphic flowers characterized by long, nectar-filled spurs that emerge from a tubular corolla. These blooms often display a range of colors from pale yellow to light blue or violet depending on the specific variety. Following pollination, the plant develops elongated, slender capsules that contain numerous small seeds. The root system consists of a tuberous rhizome that serves as a storage organ for the perennial herb.

What are the pharmacological activities of Corydalis sibirica?

This plant has analgesic, sedative, anti-inflammatory, and antitussive pharmacological activities driven primarily by its diverse isoquinoline alkaloids. Research indicates that these alkaloids also possess significant antimicrobial properties and can suppress inflammatory responses in various biological models. Furthermore, the plant exhibits potential spasmolytic effects, which may assist in relaxing smooth muscle tissues.
