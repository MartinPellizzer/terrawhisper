Alpinia hainanensis

Alpinia hainanenensis, commonly known as a medicinal member of the ginger family, is a significant botanical species native to specific subtropical regions. Valued in traditional medicine, it possesses unique bioactive compounds. Research focuses on its potential therapeutic properties, particularly for its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activities in herbal treatments.

  • Scientific name: Alpinia hainanensis
  • Family: Zingiberaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Zingiberales, it is classified under the family Zingiberaceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Alpinia, with its species designation being Alpinia hainanensis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Alpinia hainanenensis, is referred to by several different names depending on the language and region. In Chinese, it is commonly called 海南山姜 (zh), 海南山姜 (zh-hans), or 海南山姜 (zh-cn), while in Vietnamese, it is known as Riềng Hải Nam (vi). In Japanese, the name used is アルピニア・ハイナネンシス (ja).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution concentrated within certain tropical and subtropical regions. In China, its presence is notably documented on the island of Hainan. Furthermore, the species can be found throughout the broader Southeast China region. Its range extends beyond Chinese borders into the Indo-China peninsula. Specifically, this medicinal plant is also known to grow within the natural habitats of Vietnam.
