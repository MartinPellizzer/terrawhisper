Gomphrena celosioides

What's the taxonomical classification of Gomphrena celosioides?

Gomphrena celosioides belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further categorized under the order Caryophyllales. This plant is a member of the family Amaranthaceae, falling under the genus Gomphrena, and is specifically identified by the species name celosioides.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Gomphrena celosioides has an erect, herbaceous growth habit characterized by branched stems and opposite, ovate to lanceolate leaves with entire margins. The foliage is typically green and can exhibit a slightly pubescent texture depending on the specific variety. Its most striking feature is the dense, globose inflorescence that appears in terminal clusters of pink, purple, or white bracts. These colorful bracts surround small, inconspicuous flowers and create a long-lasting, papery texture. The plant also develops a fibrous root system that supports its upright structure throughout the growing season.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Gomphrena celosioides is used medicinally through its aerial parts, specifically the leaves. These leaves are frequently utilized in traditional medicine to treat various ailments such as inflammation and skin disorders. The aerial portions are often prepared as decoctions or poultices to facilitate healing. Researchers study these specific components to identify bioactive compounds that contribute to the plant's therapeutic effects. Through the application of these harvested parts, practitioners address diverse health concerns.

According to a study published by "Regulatory toxicology and pharmacology : RTP", the ethanolic extract from the aerial parts of Gomphrena celosioides is used as a natural analgesic. In the acute toxicity test, 2000 mg/kg of the extract was administered orally to male and female Wistar rats. For the subacute toxicity test, Swiss mice received 75, 150, or 300 mg/kg of the extract. Animals treated with a dose of 2000 mg/kg showed no clinical signs of toxicity, indicating that the LD50 is higher than this dose. According to the Globally Harmonized System of classification, the dosages can be in Category 5, which is the least toxic or non-toxic one.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds aurantiamide and aurantiamide acetate. These specific alkaloids are primarily located within the plant's tissues and contribute to its pharmacological profile. Researchers study these substances to understand their potential therapeutic applications in various medical treatments. The presence of such chemical constituents distinguishes Gomphrena celosioides from other species in its genus. Understanding these molecular structures is essential for advancing botanical drug discovery.

According to a study published by "Iranian journal of pharmaceutical research : IJPR", the plant Gomphrena celosioides C. Mart. was subjected to soxhlet extraction with n-hexane. From the flakes recovered from the n-hexane extract on cooling, two specific compounds were isolated. These medicinal compounds were separated using column chromatography with 5% chloroform in n-hexane. The structures of the isolated aurantiamide and aurantiamide acetate were elucidated through spectroscopic analysis using IR, NMR ((1)H and (13)C), and EI-MS. This research represents the first report of the isolation of these compounds in Gomphrena celosioides.

The study observed that Gomphrena celosioides did not enhance the phagocytic function of macrophages.
