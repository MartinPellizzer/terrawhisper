Prunus campanulata

Prunus campanulata, commonly known as the Taiwan cherry, is a striking ornamental shrub prized for its bell-shaped, vibrant red blossoms. Beyond its aesthetic appeal, this resilient species holds traditional significance in various cultures, often studied for its potential bioactive compounds and its ability to thrive in diverse subtropical environments.

  • Scientific name: Prunus campanulata
  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae, the order Rosales, and the family Rosaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Prunus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution that spans several key regions across East Asia and beyond. Within China, its presence is noted in both the Hainan province and the broader China Southeast region. Its range extends further into Eastern Asia, specifically encompassing the Nansei-shoto islands and Taiwan. Interestingly, the species has also established itself in the Southern Hemisphere within New Zealand North. Collectively, these locations illustrate a wide-reaching habitat for Prunus campanulata.
