Indian bdellium-tree, scientifically known as Commiphora wightii, is a precious medicinal plant renowned for its aromatic resin. Found primarily in dry deciduous forests, it offers diverse therapeutic benefits, including anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and wound-healing properties. This species remains vital in traditional medicine for treating various skin and digestive ailments.

Plant Parts

This plant, Commiphora wightii, is valued primarily for its exudates, which are harvested from the stem and bark of the tree. The most significant medicinal components are the resins and the oleo-gum resin produced by the plant as a defense mechanism. When the bark is wounded, it exudes a sticky substance known as gum resin, which contains a complex mixture of aromatic compounds used in traditional healing practices. While much of the focus is on these secretions, the root system also plays a vital role in the overall biological structure and survival of the species in its natural habitat.
