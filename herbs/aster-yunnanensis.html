Aster yunnanensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Aster yunnanensis?

Aster yunnanensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the hierarchy of plant life, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its progression through the taxonomic ranks, it falls under the order Asterales and is a member of the family Asteraceae. Finally, the plant is categorized within the genus Aster, specifically identified by its species name, yunnanensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Aster yunnanensis has perennial, herbaceous stems that reach heights of 20 to 60 centimeters and bear lanceolate to ovate leaves with serrated margins. The leaf arrangement is typically alternate, featuring distinct veins and a texture that can range from smooth to slightly pubescent. Its inflorescences consist of small, radiate flower heads that display violet or pale purple ray florets surrounding a central disc of yellow tubular florets. These composite flowers are arranged in terminal clusters or solitary heads at the ends of the stems. The plant's root system is composed of a woody rhizome that allows it to persist through seasonal changes in its mountainous habitat.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the high-altitude regions of southwestern China, specifically within the provinces of Yunnan and Sichuan. It thrives in montane environments characterized by rocky slopes and alpine meadows. The species is typically found at elevations ranging from 2,000 to 4,000 meters above sea level. Its distribution is closely tied to the specific temperate climates found in these mountainous corridors. This narrow ecological niche limits its presence to these specific geographical coordinates in East Asia.
