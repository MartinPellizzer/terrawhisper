Morning Mallow

Sida acuta · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (45 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Morning Mallow, scientifically known as Sida acuta, is a versatile medicinal herb found in tropical regions. Widely used in traditional medicine, it possesses potent anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and diuretic properties. This resilient plant offers significant therapeutic potential for treating respiratory ailments, skin issues, and various inflammatory conditions worldwide.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malvales, specifically within the family Malvaceae. Ultimately, Sida acuta is identified by its genus, Sida.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning multiple regions across the African continent and its surrounding islands. In Northern Africa, its presence is specifically documented within the borders of Egypt. The species also inhabits the Macaronesian region, occurring naturally in both the Canary Islands and Cape Verde. Moving toward West Tropical Africa, the plant can be found growing in the country of Benin. Finally, its range extends further inland to include the territory of Burkina Faso.

Plant Parts

This plant, Sida acuta, is a versatile medicinal herb where various components are utilized for their therapeutic properties. Practitioners often employ the whole plants in traditional remedies to harness the full spectrum of its bioactive compounds. In many preparation methods, the whole plant is dried and powdered for decoctions, though specific treatments may focus on particular sections such as the stem segments to address different ailments. Additionally, the leaf is one of the most commonly used parts, frequently prepared as a topical poultice or an infusion due to its concentrated medicinal potency.
