Laportea ovalifolia

Laportea ovalifolia, commonly known as a species of stinging nettle, is a significant medicinal plant found in tropical regions. Characterized by its distinctive stinging hairs, it is traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat various ailments, including skin inflammations, digestive issues, and pain relief, offering valuable ethnobotanical insights.

  • Scientific name: Laportea ovalifolia
  • Family: Urticaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Rosales, eventually reaching the family Urticaceae. Within this family, it is specifically categorized under the genus Laportea.

Common Names

This plant, Laportea ovalifolia, is known by various names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In Spanish, the plant is known as ortiga de hojas ovaladas, and in Portuguese, it is called ortiga de folhas ovais.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found within the diverse ecosystems of West Tropical Africa. Its geographical range specifically includes the nation of Benin, where it grows in local habitats. It is also documented to inhabit parts of Guinea-Bissau and Guinea. Furthermore, the species can be located throughout the territory of Ghana. Finally, its distribution extends into the forested regions of the Ivory Coast.
