Reproduction

The reproduction of Fragaria vesca is characterized by a primarily sexual process through bisexual flowers that typically bloom between May and June. These flowers are white in color and rely on biotic pollination, specifically utilizing an insect syndrome with a strong association with bees to facilitate fertilization, although self-fertilization is present. The resulting fruit is a fleshy, indehiscent, red-colored structure categorized as an achene (technically an aggregate of achenes), with an average length of 1.22 cm (ranging from 1 cm to 1.7 cm) and an average width of 1.075 cm. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing between 101 and 1000 seeds. The seeds themselves are minute, characterized by a mean length of 1.3 mm, a consistent volume of 0.52 mm³, and a mean mass of approximately 0.00424 g (ranging from 0.00027 g to 0.00053 g). Dispersal is achieved through a zoochorous syndrome, specifically via endozoochory, where animals consume the fleshy fruit and disperse the seeds. Notably, asexual reproduction is absent in this species.
