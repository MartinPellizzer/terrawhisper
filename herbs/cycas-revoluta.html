Sago palm, scientifically known as Cycas revoluta, is a striking cycad native to subtropical forests. While often mistaken for a palm, it is actually an ancient gymnosperm. In traditional medicine, its starch and extracts are utilized for their unique properties, though careful handling is required due to inherent toxicity.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several distinct regions. In the Atlantic, its presence is noted within the Macaronesian islands of the Azores. Moving toward the Asian continent, it is found throughout Southeast China. Its range extends significantly across Eastern Asia, specifically inhabiting the islands of Japan and the Nansei-shotō archipelago. Finally, the species is also naturally distributed within the coastal areas of Taiwan.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Sago Palm, is characterized by its striking and architectural foliage. The most prominent medicinal and structural feature of Cycas revoluta is its large, pinnately compound leaves that emerge from the center of the plant in a rosette formation. These long, stiff, and dark green leaves consist of numerous narrow leaflets that are often slightly curved or revolute at the edges, giving the plant its specific name. While the entire plant contains toxins, the leaves are the most visually defining part of its morphology.
