Wild poinsettia, scientifically known as Euphorbia heterophyllia, is a resilient succulent native to arid regions. Renowned for its striking, fleshy leaves and unique growth patterns, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditional medicine utilizes its bioactive compounds to treat various ailments, highlighting its importance in both ecology and pharmacology.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further organized into the order Malpighiales and the family Euphorbiaceae. Finally, its taxonomic placement concludes within the genus Euphorbia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning multiple continents and climatic zones. In Middle Europe, its presence is specifically noted in Germany. Moving toward the Mediterranean, it can be found throughout Southeastern Europe, particularly in Greece. The species also inhabits the Macaronesian islands, including the Canary Islands and Cape Verde. Finally, its range extends to West Tropical Africa, where it is recorded in Benin.
