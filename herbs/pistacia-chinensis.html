Chinese pistachio, scientifically known as Pistacia chinensis, is a versatile medicinal plant native to East Asia. Renowned for its therapeutic properties, it is traditionally used to treat skin conditions, inflammation, and digestive issues. Its bark and resin contain bioactive compounds that offer significant antioxidant and healing benefits in herbal medicine.

The physiology of Pistacia chinensis is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, which dictates its carbon fixation processes and metabolic efficiency. Reproduction

The reproduction of Pistacia chinensis is characterized by an abiotic pollination syndrome, specifically relying on wind for the transfer of pollen. The flowering period typically begins in March and concludes in May, though the timing can be variable. This species does not exhibit self-fertilization, as the process of self-fertilization is absent. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fruit classified as a drupe. These fruits are indehiscent, meaning they do not split open at maturity to release their contents. The seeds produced within the fruit exhibit varying masses, with a minimum seed mass of 0.04132 g, a maximum of 0.0917 g, and an average (mean) seed mass of approximately 0.07029 g.
