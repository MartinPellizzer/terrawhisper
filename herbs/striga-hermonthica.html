Physiology

The physiology of Striga hermonthilica is characterized by its specialized parasitic lifestyle, which necessitates a complex biochemical signaling mechanism to initiate infection. The plant relies heavily on host-derived chemical cues, specifically strigolactones, to trigger the germination of its seeds in the rhizosphere. Once germination occurs, the physiological development shifts toward the formation of a haustorium, a specialized organ designed to penetrate host root tissues to establish a vascular connection. This connection allows the plant to divert water, minerals, and photoassimilates from the host to itself. Regarding its nutritional capabilities, Striga hermonthilica is not a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms, instead depending entirely on the host plant for its nitrogen requirements. This parasitic dependency dictates its entire metabolic strategy, prioritizing the rapid acquisition of host resources to support its reproductive cycle.
