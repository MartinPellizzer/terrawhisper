Garuga pinnata

What's the taxonomical classification of Garuga pinnata?

Garuga pinnata belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the taxonomic order Sapindales. This plant is specifically situated within the family Burseraceae, belonging to the genus Garuga, with the species name pinnata.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Garuga pinnata has pinnately compound leaves consisting of several pairs of oblong to lanceolate leaflets arranged along a central rachis. The leaflets are typically leathery in texture and feature entire margins with acute tips. This plant is characterized by its woody structure, often forming small to medium-sized trees with distinct branching patterns. The flowers are small and usually appear in axillary clusters, while the fruit develops into a woody capsule. The overall morphology supports its adaptation to tropical and subtropical forest environments.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Garuga pinnata is utilized medicinally through the use of its stem bark, leaves, and roots. The stem bark is specifically processed to treat various ailments including skin diseases and gastrointestinal issues. Additionally, the leaves are often decocted to serve as a remedy for inflammatory conditions. The roots are also integrated into traditional formulations to address specific systemic health concerns. These various components provide a diverse range of therapeutic applications in herbal medicine.

According to a study published by "Phytochemistry", three macrocyclic diarylheptanoids were isolated from the stem bark of Garuga pinnata. These compounds include 6'-hydroxygaruganin V (1), 9'-desmethylgarugamblin I (2), and 1,9'-didesmethylgaruganin III (3). The isolation process utilized petroleum ether and dichloromethane extracts of the stem bark. Structural identification of these 3 compounds was achieved through extensive spectroscopic studies. These studies specifically involved high field NMR and MS measurements.
