Distribution

This plant, known as Corynanthe johimbé, exhibits a specific distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found growing within the borders of Nigeria. Its range extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, where it is present in the province of Cabinda. Furthermore, the species is documented throughout Cameroon and the Republic of the Congo. Finally, its geographical footprint includes the territory of Equatorial Guinea.

Physiology

The physiology of Corynanthe johimbe is characterized by its metabolic processes focused on the synthesis of complex indole alkaloids, primarily yohimbine, which are produced through specialized secondary metabolic pathways within its tissues. As a woody perennial, its physiological functions are geared toward long-term growth and the maintenance of structural integrity in tropical forest environments. Regarding its nutrient acquisition and symbiotic relationships, the plant does not function as a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the specialized root nodules or symbiotic associations with diazotrophic bacteria required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms. Instead, its physiological nitrogen uptake is dependent on the absorption of nitrates and ammonium from the soil through its root system. Its carbon assimilation is driven by photosynthetic processes that support both primary growth and the energy-intensive biosynthesis of its alkaloid profile.
