Lemon bush, scientifically known as Lippia javanica, is a resilient, aromatic shrub native to Southern Africa. Renowned for its potent medicinal properties, it is widely utilized in traditional healing to treat respiratory ailments, digestive issues, and skin infections. Its refreshing citrus scent and bioactive compounds make it a versatile botanical.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across several key regions of the African continent. Within West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence is noted in both the Central African Republic and the DR Congo. Moving toward the northeast, the species is also found in Ethiopia. Its range extends further into East Tropical Africa, where it inhabits Kenya. Finally, the plant is documented as being native to Tanzania.

Plant Parts

This plant, Lippia javanica, is a medicinal shrub where various components are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The most significant parts are the leaves, which are frequently harvested to prepare medicinal decoctions. Often, concentrated leaf extracts are used in traditional remedies to harness their bioactive compounds. While the woody stems provide the structural framework for the plant, it is the individual leaf morphology that helps in its identification.
