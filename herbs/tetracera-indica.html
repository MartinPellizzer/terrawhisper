Tetracera indica, scientifically known as Tetracera indica, is a versatile perennial herb native to tropical Asia. Renowned in traditional medicine, particularly Ayurveda, it possesses significant therapeutic properties. This plant is widely utilized for its anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant capabilities, offering natural remedies for various skin and digestive ailments.

This plant is widely distributed across various regions of the Indian Subcontinent and Indo-China. Within the Indian Subcontinent, it can be specifically found in Assam and Bangladesh. The physiology of Tetraceras indica is characterized by its specialized metabolic processes and its role within its ecological niche. A key physiological distinction of this species is its relationship with soil nutrients; specifically, it is not a nitrogen fixer, meaning it lacks the symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria (such as Rhizobium) required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia. Consequently, its growth and physiological vigor are heavily dependent on the availability of nitrates and ammonium present in the soil. The plant exhibits robust photosynthetic efficiency and adaptive mechanisms to thrive in varied environmental conditions, often demonstrating high tolerance to stress. Its metabolic pathways are optimized for survival in disturbed or open habitats, where it manages nutrient uptake through an extensive root system designed to scavenge available minerals rather than relying on biological nitrogen fixation.
