Physiology

The physiology of Euphorbia drupifera is characterized by specialized metabolic adaptations designed to facilitate survival in arid or rocky environments. As a member of the Euphorbiaceae family, its physiological processes are heavily influenced by the presence of a complex milky latex, which serves as a defensive mechanism and a reservoir for secondary metabolites. This latex contains various diterpene esters that play a critical role in the plant's chemical defense and wound healing responses. Regarding its nutrient acquisition and soil interactions, the plant does not function as a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria (such as Rhizobium) necessary to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms, meaning its nitrogen uptake is entirely dependent on the availability of nitrates and ammonium in the substrate. Its photosynthetic efficiency is optimized through specialized leaf morphology and stomatal control to minimize transpiration, allowing it to maintain turgor pressure and metabolic function despite fluctuating water availability.
