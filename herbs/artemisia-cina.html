Artemisia cina

Santonica

Artemisia cina, commonly known as silver wormwood, is a resilient perennial herb distinguished by its silvery, pubescent foliage. Native to arid regions, this aromatic plant belongs to the Asteraceae family. It holds significant ethnobotanical value, traditionally utilized in various cultures for its diverse medicinal and therapeutic properties.

  • Scientific name: Artemisia cina
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asterales. Finally, it is a member of the family Asteraceae, falling under the genus Artemisia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Artemisia cinerea, is recognized by various local names across different linguistic regions. In Poland, it is referred to as bylica cytwarowa, while in Azerbaijan, it is called Çin yovşanı. German speakers identify it as Wurmsamen, and in Kazakhstan, it is known as Дәрмене. Additionally, in Russia, it is commonly called Полънь цитварная.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions of Asia. In Central Asia, it can be found growing within the borders of Kazakhstan, Tajikistan, and Uzbekistan. Its range extends further into Western Asia, where it is documented in Iran. Additionally, the species is present in the North-Central part of China. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to inhabit various terrains across the continent.
