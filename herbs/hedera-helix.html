European Ivy

Hedera helix · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (375 studies) · ★★★★★

European Ivy, scientifically known as Hedera helix, is a versatile evergreen climber renowned for its medicinal potential. Traditionally used to treat respiratory ailments, its extracts possess mucolytic and antitussive properties that soothe coughs. This resilient plant offers significant therapeutic value in modern herbal pharmacology for managing bronchial congestion.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Hedera helix is driven by a sexual process, as both asexual reproduction and self-fertilization are noted to be absent. The plant produces green flowers arranged in an umbel inflorescence, with individual flowers measuring between 0.28 cm and 0.35 cm in length. The flowering period typically begins in September and extends through December. Pollination is a biotic process primarily facilitated by insects, specifically bees. Following successful pollination, the plant develops fleshy, indehiscent fruits categorized as berries. These berries are black in color and typically measure approximately 0.7 cm in both mean length and width, though they can reach a maximum dimension of 0.8 cm in length and width, with a minimum width of 0.75 cm. The seeds contained within the fruit have a consistent length of 5.7 mm and a seed volume of 16 mm³. The seed mass varies, with a mean of approximately 0.0403 g, a minimum of 0.01549 g, and a maximum of 0.03165 g. Dispersal is achieved through a zoochorous syndrome, specifically endozoochory, meaning the seeds are dispersed via the internal passage of animals.
