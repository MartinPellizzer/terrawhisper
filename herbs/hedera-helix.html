European Ivy

Hedera helix · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (375 studies) · ★★★★★

European Ivy, scientifically known as Hedera helix, is a versatile evergreen climber renowned for its medicinal potential. Traditionally used to treat respiratory ailments, its extracts possess mucolytic and antitussive properties that soothe coughs. This resilient plant offers significant therapeutic value in modern herbal pharmacology for managing bronchial congestion.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several regions in Northern Europe. It is commonly found throughout the various landscapes of Great Britain and Ireland. The species also thrives within the territories of Denmark and Sweden. Furthermore, its presence extends northward into the coastal and forest areas of Norway. Consequently, these five nations represent a significant portion of its northern geographical range.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as English ivy, utilizes its various components for medicinal purposes, particularly its foliage. The lush, evergreen leaves are the primary source of therapeutic compounds, with each individual leaf containing bioactive substances used in pharmaceutical preparations. In many cough remedies, a concentrated dry extract is employed to provide consistent dosing of active ingredients. These medicinal properties are often harnessed through different methods, such as using various leaf extracts to formulate syrups or tinctures. Ultimately, a standardized leaf extract serves as the essential active component in many modern herbal treatments.
