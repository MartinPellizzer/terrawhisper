Sida cordata

Sida cordata, commonly known as heart-leaved broom, is a versatile medicinal herb native to tropical regions. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, this plant is frequently utilized in traditional medicine to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and digestive issues. Its unique botanical characteristics make it a significant subject in ethnobotany.

  • Scientific name: Sida cordata
  • Family: Malvaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Under the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized into the order Malvales and the family Malvaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the specific genus Sida.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad and diverse geographical distribution spanning multiple continents and island groups. In Northeast Tropical Africa, it is specifically documented to grow on the island of Socotra. Its presence extends across the Western Indian Ocean, where it can be found on both Mauritius and Réunion. Within the Arabian Peninsula, the species is located in the nation of Oman. Finally, its range reaches as far east as China South-Central.
