Rubia tibetica

Rubia tibetica, commonly known as Tibetan Madder, is a perennial herb native to the high-altitude regions of the Himalayas. Valued in traditional medicine, its roots contain bioactive compounds used to treat inflammation, skin conditions, and blood circulation issues. This resilient plant remains a vital component of regional herbal pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Rubia tibetica
  • Family: Rubiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Gentianaales, it is further categorized into the family Rubiaceae. Specifically, it is identified by the genus Rubia and the species name Rubia tibetica.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several key regions in Central and Western Asia. In Central Asia, its presence is specifically noted in Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan. Moving toward Western Asia, the species can be found within the borders of Afghanistan. The distribution also extends significantly into China, where it inhabits the high-altitude regions of Tibet. Additionally, it is documented to grow within the Xinjiang province of China.
