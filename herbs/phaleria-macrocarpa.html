Phaleria macrocarpa

Phaleria macrocarpra, commonly known as Mahkota Dewa, is a medicinal plant native to Indonesia. Highly valued in traditional medicine, its fruits and leaves contain potent bioactive compounds with antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-tumor properties. This versatile botanical is widely studied for its potential to support overall health and wellness.

  • Scientific name: Phaleria macrocarpa
  • Family: Thymelaeaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Malvales and the family Thymelaeaceae. Ultimately, it is identified by the genus Phaleria.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution spanning across parts of Southeast Asia and Oceania. Within the Indo-China region, its presence can be identified in the Nicobar Islands. Moving further east into the Papuasia region, the species is also found in New Guinea. These distinct locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in diverse tropical island environments. Consequently, its natural range is characterized by these specific maritime and mainland territories.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Mahkota Dewa, utilizes various anatomical structures for its medicinal properties. The most significant component is the fruit, which houses the bioactive compounds sought after in traditional medicine. Within the fruit, the pericarp serves as the outer protective layer, while the fleshy mesocarp provides the internal structure surrounding the core. At the center of this structure lies the seed, which contains concentrated chemical constituents that must be handled with caution due to their potency. Additionally, the leaf of the plant is also frequently harvested and processed for its own unique set of therapeutic benefits.
