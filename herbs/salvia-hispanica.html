Salvia hispanica

Chía

Salvia hispanica, commonly known as chia, is a nutrient-dense plant revered for its medicinal and nutritional properties. Rich in omega-3 fatty acids, fiber, and antioxidants, these tiny seeds support heart health, digestion, and sustained energy. This versatile superfood has been a cornerstone of traditional wellness for centuries.

  • Scientific name: Salvia hispanica
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, placing it within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae, belonging to the genus Salvia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Salvia hispanica, is recognized by various common names across different languages and cultures. In Russian, it is referred to as чиа, while in French, it is called chia, and in Dutch, it is known as Chia. Its historical roots are reflected in the Nahuatl name, Chiyan, and in German, it is commonly identified as Mexikanische Chia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a fragmented geographical distribution across several distinct regions of Europe. In Northern Europe, its presence can be identified within Great Britain. Moving into Middle Europe, it is found across Germany, Austria, and the Czechia-Slovakia region. The species also extends into Southwestern Europe, specifically inhabiting the Baleares. Together, these locations define the varying territories where the species thrives.
