Albizia antunesiana

Purple-leaved false-thorn

Albizia antunesiana, commonly known as the Antunes' Albizia, is a versatile medicinal tree native to Southern Africa. Renowned for its therapeutic properties, various parts of the plant are utilized in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from inflammation and skin conditions to digestive issues, showcasing its significant ethnobotanical importance.

  • Scientific name: Albizia antunesiana
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales, it is further nested within the family Fabaceae. Specifically, it is identified under the genus Albizia.

Distribution

This plant is native to several distinct regions across the African continent. Within West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence is recorded in Burundi, Rwanda, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Moving toward the eastern part of the continent, it can also be found in Tanzania. Furthermore, its distribution extends into South Tropical Africa, specifically within the borders of Angola. Together, these locations highlight a broad range spanning from West-Central to Southern Africa.

Plant Parts

This plant, Albizia antunesiana, possesses various anatomical features that are utilized in traditional medicine. The leaves are bipinnately compound, and a single leaf can often be found displaying a delicate, feathery texture. Below the surface, the extensive roots serve as a vital storage system for the tree, and the root itself is frequently harvested to prepare decoctions for treating various ailments.
