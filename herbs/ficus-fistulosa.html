Ficus fistulosa

Ficus fistulosa, commonly known as the hollow-stemmed fig, is a significant tropical tree native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its distinctive morphology, this species plays a vital role in forest ecosystems. Beyond its ecological importance, it holds traditional medicinal value, utilized in various cultures to treat diverse ailments.

  • Scientific name: Ficus fistulosa
  • Family: Moraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Further taxonomic details place it in the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Rosaleles, specifically within the family Moraceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Ficus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ficus fistulosa, is referred to by various common names depending on the region and language. Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning several key regions across Asia. In China, it is commonly found throughout the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan provinces. Its presence extends further east to the island of Taiwan within the Eastern Asia region. Additionally, the species can be located within the Indian Subcontinent, specifically in the area of Assam. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in various subtropical environments.
