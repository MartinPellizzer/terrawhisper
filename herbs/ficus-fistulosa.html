Reproduction

The reproduction of Ficus fistulosa is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, primarily driven by insects. The plant does not practice self-fertilization, as the trait is noted to be absent, necessitating external agents for successful sexual reproduction. Following pollination, the development of fleshy fruits facilitates a zoochorous dispersal syndrome, where animals aid in the spreading of seeds. The seeds themselves are quite small and uniform in their physical properties; they possess a mean seed mass of 3e-04 g (with both minimum and maximum values recorded at this same weight), a mean seed length of 1.5 mm, and a maximum seed height of 0.3 mm. Furthermore, the seed dimensions include a maximum width of 0.8 mm and a consistent seed volume of 0.11 mm³, which is observed as both the minimum and mean volume.
