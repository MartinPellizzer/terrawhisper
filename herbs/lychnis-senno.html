Lychnis senno

What's the taxonomical classification of Lychnis senno?

Lychnis senno belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic progression, it falls under the order Caryophyllales and is a member of the family Caryophyllaceae. Ultimately, the plant is identified by its specific genus, Lychnis, and its species name, senno.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Lychnis senno has linear, lanceolate leaves that are oppositely arranged along a slender, upright stem and terminal clusters of bright pink flowers. The foliage typically exhibits a smooth texture and a deep green color that contrasts with the delicate petals. Each flower consists of five distinct petals which are often notched at the tips to create a heart-like appearance. The plant also features small, glandular hairs that may cover the stems and calyx, providing a slight tactile difference. What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant is used medicinally through its aerial parts, which include the leaves, stems, and flowers. These above-ground components are typically harvested to prepare various traditional remedies. The chemical constituents found within these structures are believed to provide specific therapeutic benefits. Practitioners often utilize these parts in the form of infusions or tinctures to treat different ailments. Such applications rely on the concentrated compounds present in the entire upper structure of the herb.

According to a study published by "Natural product communications", the aerial parts of Lychnis senno Siebold et Zucc. are used to isolate specific medicinal compounds. Four new flavone C-glycosides were isolated from these parts for the first time, including 5-O-acetyl-2"-alpha-rhamnopyranosylisovitexin (1), 2"-alpha-rhamnopyranosylisovitexin (2), 2"-a-rhamnopyranosylisoorientin (3), and isoorientin 3'-mehyl ether (4). The antioxidative activity of these compounds was analyzed using the 2,2'-azino-bis (3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) diammonium salt (ABTS) radical-scavenging method. Among these, compound 3 demonstrated moderate antioxidative activity.
