Achillea fragrantissima

Achillea fragrantissima, commonly known as fragrant yarrow, is a resilient perennial native to arid regions. Characterized by its aromatic, golden-yellow flower clusters, this plant offers significant medicinal potential. Traditionally used for its antiseptic and anti-inflammatory properties, it serves as a vital botanical resource in various holistic healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Achillea fragrantissima
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Asterales and the family Asteraceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its membership in the genus Achillea.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical range concentrated within the Middle East and North Africa. In Northern Africa, its presence is documented in Egypt. The species also extends into Western Asia, where it can be found throughout Iraq. Its distribution continues through the Levant, specifically covering the regions of Lebanon-Syria and Palestine. Finally, it inhabits the Sinai Peninsula, completing its regional footprint.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as fragrant yarrow, is characterized by its vibrant flowering shoot structures that rise above the foliage. The medicinal properties are concentrated within the aerial parts, which include both the slender stems and the finely divided, aromatic leaves. When harvesting the aerial part of the specimen for therapeutic use, it is often processed to extract the potent essential oil contained within the plant tissues.
