Reproduction

Reproduction in Lantana canescens is driven by a variety of mechanisms that ensure its widespread colonization, primarily characterized by a complex dispersal syndrome. While the plant exhibits multiple modes of movement, its most prominent method is zoochorous, where seeds are distributed by animals that consume its fleshy drupes and subsequently deposit the seeds in new locations via their waste. This biotic dispersal is complemented by other strategies, as the species can also exhibit anemochorous (wind-driven), autochorous (self-dispersal), and hydrochorous (water-driven) tendencies.
