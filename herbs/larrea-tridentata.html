Larrea tridentata

Creosote bush

Larrea tridentata, commonly known as creosote bush, is a resilient evergreen shrub native to the arid deserts of North America. Renowned for its distinct resinous aroma, this hardy plant has been utilized for centuries in traditional medicine to treat skin ailments, inflammation, and various respiratory conditions through its potent compounds.

  • Scientific name: Larrea tridentata
  • Family: Zygophyllaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered as Zygophyllales and placed in the family Zygophyllaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Larrea.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as creosote bush, possesses distinct morphological features that contribute to its medicinal properties. The stems are woody and often become gnarled and branched as the plant ages, providing a sturdy structural framework. Its leaves are small, resinous, and evergreen, coated in a waxy substance that helps the plant retain moisture in arid environments. Collectively, the aerial parts of Larrea tridentata are highly valued in traditional medicine, as the resins found on the surface of the foliage and stems contain bioactive compounds used for their antiseptic and anti-inflammatory effects.
