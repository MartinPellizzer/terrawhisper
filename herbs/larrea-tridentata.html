Creosote bush, scientifically known as Larrea tridentata, is a resilient evergreen shrub native to the arid landscapes of the American Southwest. Renowned for its distinctive, pungent aroma and medicinal properties, it has been used for centuries by indigenous cultures to treat skin ailments, inflammation, and various respiratory conditions.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as creosote bush, possesses distinct morphological features that contribute to its medicinal properties. The stems are woody and often become gnarled and branched as the plant ages, providing a sturdy structural framework. Its leaves are small, resinous, and evergreen, coated in a waxy substance that helps the plant retain moisture in arid environments. Collectively, the aerial parts of Larrea tridentata are highly valued in traditional medicine, as the resins found on the surface of the foliage and stems contain bioactive compounds used for their antiseptic and anti-inflammatory effects.
