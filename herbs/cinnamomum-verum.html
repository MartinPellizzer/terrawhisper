Cinnamomum verum

True cinnamon

Cinnamomum verum, commonly known as true cinnamon, is a highly valued aromatic plant native to Sri Lanka. Renowned for its distinctively sweet flavor and therapeutic properties, this medicinal spice is widely utilized in traditional medicine to support digestion, reduce inflammation, and improve blood sugar regulation through its potent antioxidants.

  • Scientific name: Cinnamomum verum
  • Family: Lauraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Laurales and the family Lauraceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Cinnamomum.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Cinnamomum verum, is recognized by various common names across different languages and regions. In English, specifically in Great Britain, it is referred to as Sri Lanka cinnamon, while in German, it is called Ceylon-Zimtbaum. The plant's identity is further reflected in Catalan as canyeller and in Hungarian as ceyloni fahéjffa. Additionally, in Sinhala, it is known as Cimet.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning several diverse tropical regions. In West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence can be found around the Gulf of Guinea Islands. Moving eastward, it is documented within the tropical landscapes of Tanzania. Its range extends into South Tropical Africa, specifically within the territory of Angola. Finally, the species inhabits various parts of the Western Indian Ocean, including the Comoros and Mauritius.

Plant Parts

This plant, Cinnamomum verum, is highly valued in traditional medicine for its various therapeutic components derived from different botanical structures. The most commonly utilized part is the bark, which is harvested and dried to be used in numerous medicinal preparations. Within this bark, and throughout the plant, are concentrated essential oils that provide its distinct aromatic and bioactive properties. These essential oils are often extracted as a potent oil used for topical applications or flavoring. While specific parts are often isolated for targeted use, the medicinal profile of the species can also be represented by the whole herb, encompassing the complex chemical synergy found across its entire structure.
