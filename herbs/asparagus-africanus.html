Asparagus africanus

Bush asparagus

Asparagus africanus, commonly known as the African asparagus, is a resilient climbing shrub native to various African regions. Renowned in traditional medicine, this plant is frequently utilized for its diverse therapeutic properties, including treating inflammation and digestive issues, making it a significant botanical resource in ethnobotanical studies worldwide.

  • Scientific name: Asparagus africanus
  • Family: Asparagaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Asparagus africanus, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Asparagales and the family Asparagaceae. Finally, its taxonomic position is defined by its placement within the genus Asparagus.

Common Names

The Jeŋkpibrigoo name is used in Dagbani (dag), and in Arabic (ar) it is known as خرایص. Distribution

This plant is primarily found within the diverse landscapes of West Tropical Africa. Its documented presence includes the coastal and inland regions of Ghana and Benin. Furthermore, it can be located within the territories of Burkina Faso and Guinea-Bissau. The species also extends its range into the nation of Gambia. Together, these locations highlight a specific distribution pattern across several West African countries.
