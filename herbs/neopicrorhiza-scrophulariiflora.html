What's the taxonomical classification of Neopicrorhiza scrophulariiflora?

Neopicrorhiza scrophulariiflora belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Lamiales. This specific plant is a representative of the family Plantaginaceae and is further categorized under the genus Neopicrorhiza, ultimately reaching its unique scientific designation at the species level as scrophulariiflora.

According to a study published by "Frontiers in plant science", Neopicrorhiza scrophulariiflora is a wild medicinal plant whose main active ingredients are picroside I (P-I) and picroside II (P-II). These specific active ingredients possess anti-inflammatory, anticancer, and antibacterial properties. Researchers identified a total of 196 flavonoids and 63 terpenoids within the plant tissues. From 158,254 annotated genes, 74 were related to iridoid synthesis, and 43 candidate genes were identified for the biosynthesis of picroside-I and picroside-II. Among these, 26 genes were significantly correlated with the synthesis of picrosides and their intermediates.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds picroroside A, picroroside B, picroroside C, scrophoside A, and picroside I. These specific iridoid glycosides are primarily responsible for the biological activities observed in the species. Researchers study these molecules to understand their potential roles in anti-inflammatory and hepatoprotective processes. The concentration of these active constituents can vary depending on the plant's growing environment. Such chemical profiles make the plant a subject of significant interest in traditional and modern pharmacology.

According to a study published by "Phytochemistry", three iridoid glycosides, picrosides A (1), B (2) and C (3), were isolated from the rhizomes of Neopicrorhiza scrophulariiflora. One cucurbitacin glycoside, scrophoside A (4), was also isolated from the rhizomes of the plant. Additionally, the study identified two known iridoid glycosides, picrosides I (5) and II (6). Three known cucurbitacin glycosides (7-9) were also found within the plant.
