Gentiana algida belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Within this family, the plant is categorized under the genus Gentiana, and its specific designation is the species algida.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Gentiana algida has a low-growing, perennial habit characterized by basal leaves that are elliptical to oblong, fleshy, and possess entire margins. The stems are typically short and may be branched, supporting upright, funnel-shaped flowers that range in color from pale blue to white. These blossoms feature a prominent, deep throat and five to seven fused petals that form a distinct corolla. The foliage remains mostly concentrated near the ground to withstand alpine environments, while the fruit develops into small, ovoid capsules containing numerous tiny seeds. This compact structure allows the plant to thrive in the harsh, rocky soils of high-altitude tundra ecosystems.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Gentiana algida is utilized medicinally through the use of its whole herb. Practitioners typically employ the entire structure to extract bitter compounds necessary for digestive support. These components are often prepared as tinctures or decoctions to stimulate appetite and improve gastrointestinal function. The bitter properties found throughout the plant help stimulate bile production in the liver. Such applications are common in traditional herbal practices involving this specific species.

According to a study published by "Phytochemistry", the whole herb of Gentiana algida was used to produce an aqueous acetone extract. Fractionation of this extract yielded 1 new [2'-(o,m-dihydroxybenzyl)sweroside] and 5 known secoiridoids. Other components identified from the whole herb included anofinic acid, fomannoxin acid, sitosterol, daucosterol, stigmasterol, oleanolic acid, orientin, and gentianose. The anofinic acid and fomannoxin acid were found to be active against the plant pathogenic fungus Cladosporium cucumerinum. Additionally, the methyl esters of these acids were found to be active against the human pathogenic yeast Candida albicans.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the bitter glycoside gentianose along with the triterpenoid oleanolic acid and the phytosterols sitosterol, stigmasterol, and daucosterol. These specific chemical constituents contribute to the various therapeutic properties observed in the species. The presence of gentianose is particularly significant for its ability to stimulate digestive functions. Furthermore, the sterol profile consisting of sitosterol, stigmasterol, and daucosterol supports the plant's complex biochemical makeup. Together, these medicinal compounds form the basis of its pharmacological potential.

According to a study published by "Phytochemistry", fractionation of an aqueous acetone extract of the whole herb of Gentiana algida yielded 1 new [2'-(o,m-dihydroxybenzyl)sweroside] and 5 known secoiridoiids. The extraction also identified anofinic acid, fomannoxin acid, sitosterol, daucosterol, stigmasterol, oleanolic acid, orientin, and gentianose. Both anofinic acid and fomannoxin acid showed activity against the plant pathogenic fungus Cladosporium cucumerinum. While the carboxylic moieties in these acids were linked to fungal activity, their methyl esters were found to be active against the human pathogenic yeast Candida albicans.
