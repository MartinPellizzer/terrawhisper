Apteranthes europaea

Apteranthes europaea, commonly known as European Apteranthes, is a distinctive medicinal herb found in diverse habitats. Renowned for its unique botanical properties, this plant has historically been utilized in traditional healing practices. Understanding its chemical composition and therapeutic potential offers valuable insights into its role within natural pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Apteranthes europaea
  • Family: Apocynaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified within the order Gentianaales and the family Apocynaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Apteranthes.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Apteranthes europaea, is recognized by several different common names depending on the regional context. In both Spanish and Slovenian, it is referred to by the name Caralluma europaea. Furthermore, within Spanish-speaking regions, it is frequently called Chumberillo de lobo, Chumberilla, or Chumberillo del lobo.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse distribution across the Mediterranean basin. In Southwestern Europe, its presence is noted within the borders of Spain. Moving toward Southeastern Europe, it can be found growing in Sicilia. The species also extends into Northern Africa, where it inhabits Algeria. Furthermore, its range continues through the North African territories of Libya and Egypt.
