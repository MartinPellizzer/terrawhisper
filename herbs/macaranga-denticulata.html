Macaranga denticulata

Macaranga denticulata, commonly known as the Macaranga plant, is a significant medicinal shrub found in tropical regions. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, it is frequently utilized in traditional medicine to treat skin ailments, inflammation, and digestive issues. Exploring its phytochemical potential offers promising insights into modern pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Macaranga denticulata
  • Family: Euphorbiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Malpighiales, it is classified under the family Euphorbiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Macaranga, specifically as the species Macaranga denticulata.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Macaranga denticulata, is referred to by various common names across different languages and regions. In Chinese, it is known as 中平树 (zh), 中平树 (zh-cn), and 中平樹 (zh-tw), as well as 牢麻 (zh). In Indonesia, it is commonly called Walih upik (id).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of habitats across several distinct regions of Asia. Within China, its presence is noted in the South-Central areas as well as the Southeast provinces. It is also found in the tropical environment of Hainan and the high-altitude regions of Tibet. Moving beyond China, the species extends its range into the Indian Subcontinent. Specifically, it can be located within the lush landscapes of Assam.
