Fynbos aloe

Aloe succotrina · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1383 studies) · ★★★★★

Fynbos aloe, scientifically known as Aloe succotrina, is a distinctive succulent native to South Africa. Renowned for its medicinal properties, this species is primarily cultivated for its high-quality bitter aloes. Extracted from its fleshy leaves, the resin serves as a potent natural laxative and is widely used in pharmacology.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across several distinct regions. In Southwestern Europe, it can be found growing within the borders of France. It also maintains a presence in Spain, which is part of the same European region. Moving to a different continent, the species is located in Southern Africa. Specifically, it thrives within the Cape Provinces of that area.

Plant Parts

This plant, Aloe succotrina, is highly valued for its therapeutic properties, which are primarily derived from its succulent structure. The most significant parts used in medicinal applications are the thick, fleshy leaves, which serve as the storage vessels for its healing compounds. By carefully processing a single leaf, one can extract the translucent, nutrient-rich leaf gel found within the inner parenchyma. This substance is often compared to the well-known Aloe vera gel due to its soothing and hydrating qualities. Ultimately, the concentrated gel serves as the primary source of bioactive molecules used to treat skin irritations and digestive issues.
