Aloe succotrina

Aloe vera

Aloe succotrina, commonly known as bitter aloe, is a succulent plant native to South Africa. Renowned for its potent medicinal properties, its dried leaves are harvested to produce a bitter resin. Traditionally used as a powerful laxative, it also possesses significant antimicrobial and healing qualities in various herbal remedies.

  • Scientific name: Aloe succotrina
  • Family: Asphodelaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asparagales, it falls under the family Asphodelaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement within the genus Aloe.

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across several distinct regions. In Southwestern Europe, it can be found growing within the borders of France. It also maintains a presence in Spain, which is part of the same European region. Moving to a different continent, the species is located in Southern Africa. Plant Parts

This plant, Aloe succotrina, is highly valued for its therapeutic properties, which are primarily derived from its succulent structure. The most significant parts used in medicinal applications are the thick, fleshy leaves, which serve as the storage vessels for its healing compounds. By carefully processing a single leaf, one can extract the translucent, nutrient-rich leaf gel found within the inner parenchyma. This substance is often compared to the well-known Aloe vera gel due to its soothing and hydrating qualities. Ultimately, the concentrated gel serves as the primary source of bioactive molecules used to treat skin irritations and digestive issues.
