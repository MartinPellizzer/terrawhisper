Fynbos aloe

Aloe succotrina · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1383 studies) · ★★★★★

Fynbos aloe, scientifically known as Aloe succotrina, is a distinctive succulent native to South Africa. Renowned for its medicinal properties, this species is primarily cultivated for its high-quality bitter aloes. Extracted from its fleshy leaves, the resin serves as a potent natural laxative and is widely used in pharmacology.

Physiology

The physiology of Aloe succotrina is characterized by specialized adaptations for water conservation and efficient resource management in arid environments. The plant utilizes a CAM (Crassulacean Acid Metabolism) photosynthetic pathway, which allows it to fix carbon dioxide at night to minimize transpirational water loss during the heat of the day. This metabolic efficiency is supported by a high degree of succulence, enabling the plant to store significant quantities of water within its specialized tissues. Morphologically, the plant features a rosette leaf arrangement, with leaves that typically range from a minimum length of 15 cm to a mean length of 25 cm, though they can reach a maximum length of 60 cm. These leaves possess a width varying between a minimum of 4 cm and a maximum of 7 cm. Furthermore, the plant does not function as a nitrogen fixer, relying instead on the availability of nutrients within its immediate substrate.
