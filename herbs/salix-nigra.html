Black willow, scientifically known as Salix nigra, is a resilient riparian shrub native to North America. Renowned for its high salicin content, this plant has a long history in traditional medicine for treating pain and inflammation. It plays a vital ecological role in stabilizing riverbanks and supporting diverse ecosystems.

Distribution

This plant, known as Salix nigra, exhibits a broad geographical distribution across much of the North American landscape. In Eastern Canada, it can be found growing in the provinces of New Brunswick, Ontario, and Québec. Moving southward into the North-Central United States, its range extends significantly into the Midwest. Specifically, it is documented to inhabit the regions of both Illinois and Iowa. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in various wetland environments across these specific territories.
