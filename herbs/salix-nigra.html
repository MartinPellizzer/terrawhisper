Salix nigra

Salix nigra, commonly known as black willow, is a resilient deciduous tree native to North American wetlands. Renowned for its medicinal history, its bark contains salicin, a natural precursor to aspirin. This versatile species plays a vital role in stabilizing riverbanks and supporting diverse riparian ecosystems through its growth.

  • Scientific name: Salix nigra
  • Family: Salicaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Malpighiales and the family Salicaceae. Finally, it is classified under the genus Salix.

Distribution

This plant, known as Salix nigra, exhibits a broad geographical distribution across much of the North American landscape. In Eastern Canada, it can be found growing in the provinces of New Brunswick, Ontario, and Québec. Moving southward into the North-Central United States, its range extends significantly into the Midwest. Specifically, it is documented to inhabit the regions of both Illinois and Iowa. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in various wetland environments across these specific territories.
