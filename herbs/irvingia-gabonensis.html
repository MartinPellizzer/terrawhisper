Irvingia gabonensis

Irvingia gabonenis, commonly known as African mango, is a versatile medicinal plant native to West and Central Africa. Renowned for its nutrient-dense seeds, it is traditionally used to manage diabetes, obesity, and digestive issues. Beyond nutrition, its bioactive compounds offer significant antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties in holistic medicine.

  • Scientific name: Irvingia gabonensis
  • Family: Irvingiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Malpighiales and the family Irvingiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Irvingia.

Distribution

This plant, known as Irvingia gabonenis, exhibits a specific distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in countries such as Benin and Nigeria. Moving into the West-Central Tropical Africa region, the species can be found in the Central African Republic and Cameroon. Furthermore, its range extends to the coastal area of Cabinda within the same West-Central zone. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's importance within the tropical forest ecosystems of Africa.
