African mango

Irvingia gabonensis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (24 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

African mango, scientifically known as Irvingia gabonenis, is a versatile medicinal plant native to West and Central Africa. Renowned for its nutrient-dense seeds, it is traditionally used to manage diabetes, obesity, and inflammation. Beyond nutrition, its bioactive compounds offer significant therapeutic potential in modern pharmacological research and wellness.

Distribution

This plant, known as Irvingia gabonenis, exhibits a specific distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is documented in countries such as Benin and Nigeria. Moving into the West-Central Tropical Africa region, the species can be found in the Central African Republic and Cameroon. Furthermore, its range extends to the coastal area of Cabinda within the same West-Central zone. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's importance within the tropical forest ecosystems of Africa.

The physiology of Irvingia gabonenis is characterized by a standard C3 photosynthetic pathway, which involves the direct fixation of atmospheric carbon dioxide through the Calvin cycle using the enzyme RuBisCO. This metabolic strategy is typical for many tropical forest species, allowing the plant to optimize carbon assimilation under the shaded or partially sunlit conditions of its natural habitat. Furthermore, regarding its nutrient acquisition processes, the plant is not a nitrogen fixer, meaning it lacks the specialized symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into a usable biological form; instead, it relies on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium present in the soil through its root system.
