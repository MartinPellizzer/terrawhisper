Hamamelis mollis

What's the taxonomical classification of Hamamelis mollis?

Hamamelis mollis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is further categorized into the subclass Magnoliidae. Its taxonomic progression continues through the order Saxifragales and into the family Hamamelidaceae. Finally, the plant is identified under the genus Hamamelis, with its specific designation being the species mollis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Hamamelis mollis has deciduous, elliptical to obovate leaves that measure up to 15 centimeters in length and feature finely serrated margins and a soft, pubescent texture. The plant typically grows as a large shrub or small tree, reaching heights between 3 and 5 meters. Its most distinctive feature is the fragrant, strap-shaped yellow flowers that emerge from woody stems during late winter or early spring. These blossoms often appear before the foliage develops, providing a striking visual contrast against the bare branches. The bark is grayish and develops a somewhat furrowed texture as the specimen matures.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the mountainous regions of central and western China, specifically occurring in provinces such as Sichuan, Gansu, and Shaanxi. It thrives primarily in high-altitude environments where cool, temperate climates prevail. These plants are typically found growing in the understory of deciduous or mixed forests. They prefer well-drained soils within these rugged, hilly terrains. Due to its specific habitat requirements, it is not naturally occurring in the wild outside of these East Asian territories.

How is this plant cultivated?
