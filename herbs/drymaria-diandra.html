Drymaria diandra

Drymaria dianendra, commonly known as the creeping herb, is a small, resilient plant belonging to the Rubiaceae family. Often found in tropical regions, it is valued in traditional medicine for its diverse pharmacological properties, including anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial effects, making it a subject of significant ethnobotanical interest.

  • Scientific name: Drymaria diandra
  • Family: Caryophyllaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, placing it within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified under the order Caryophyllales and the family Caryophyllaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Drymaria.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution that spans across multiple continents and regions. In West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence is specifically noted within the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Moving toward the southern portion of the continent, it can also be found in Angola. Within Asia, the species is widely distributed across several parts of China, including the South-Central region. Furthermore, its habitat extends to the Southeast Chinese mainland as well as the tropical island of Hainan.
