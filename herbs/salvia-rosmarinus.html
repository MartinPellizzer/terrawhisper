Salvia rosmarinus

What's the taxonomical classification of Salvia rosmarinus?

Salvia rosmarinus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Lamiales and is a representative of the family Lamiaceae. Within this botanical hierarchy, the plant is categorized under the genus Salvia, with its specific designation being the species rosmarinus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Salvia rosmarinus has woody, perennial stems that support narrow, linear leaves measuring approximately 2 to 5 centimeters in length with entire margins and a revolute edge. The foliage is characterized by a grayish-green color due to a dense covering of fine, soft hairs on both the upper and lower surfaces. Small, tubular flowers emerge from the axils of the leaves, typically presenting in shades of pale blue, lavender, or white. These blossoms are organized into verticillaster inflorescences that bloom along the elongated, upright flowering stalks. The plant's overall structure forms a compact, rounded shrub that can reach heights of up to 50 centimeters.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the Mediterranean Basin, specifically spanning across regions of Southern Europe, North Africa, and Western Asia. It thrives in coastal areas where the climate remains relatively mild and stable throughout the year. These plants are commonly found growing in rocky terrain and dry, sandy soils characteristic of Mediterranean scrublands. Due to its hardiness, it has been widely introduced to other temperate regions around the world for ornamental use. Cultivation is most successful in areas that mimic its original sunny and well-drained habitat.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, commonly known as rosemary, requires full sun exposure, well-draining sandy or loamy soil, and a Mediterranean-style climate with minimal irrigation once established.

It thrives in USDA hardiness zones 7 through 10 where temperatures rarely drop below freezing for extended periods. To prevent root rot, avoid waterlogged conditions by ensuring the planting site has excellent drainage. Propagation is most successful through softwood cuttings taken during the active growing season.

Regular pruning helps maintain a bushy shape and encourages the production of aromatic new growth.

The study promotes the large-scale cultivation of cultivar R1 which yields 1.7% in the summer season, alongside R14 at 0.95% and R3 at 0.93% in the winter season.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, Salvia rosmarinus, has been utilized across various historical medical traditions to treat ailments ranging from digestive issues to respiratory distress. In Egyptian folk medicine, practitioners employed the herb to support overall wellness and address specific physical discomforts. Ancient Greek and Roman societies also valued the plant for its aromatic properties and its perceived ability to enhance memory and mental clarity. Throughout the Middle Ages in Europe, it was frequently integrated into herbal remedies intended to boost circulation and ward off infections. These diverse historical applications demonstrate the long-standing importance of the species in global ethnobotany.

According to a study published by "The Journal of the Egyptian Public Health Association", phytotherapy has a long-standing history in Egypt. Egyptian housewives use extracts of twenty five herbs and spices in folk medicine to treat gastrointestinal disorders. Among the screened plants, rosemary extracts exhibited notable antimicrobial activities against most of the seven bacterial strains tested. These extracts, used in Egyptian folk medicine for treating many gastrointestinal disorders, could be successfully applied as natural antimicrobials for the elimination of food borne bacteria and pathogens growth.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains the medicinal compounds rosmarinic acid, carnosic acid, carnosol, camphor, and borneol. Rosmarinic acid serves as a potent antioxidant that provides significant anti-inflammatory benefits to the body. The diterpenes carnosic acid and carnosol contribute to the plant's ability to combat oxidative stress and protect cellular health. Additionally, the volatile monoterpenes camphor and borneol offer distinct aromatic properties and therapeutic effects. Together, these diverse chemical constituents make the species highly valued in traditional and modern medicine.

According to a study published by "Forschende Komplementarmedizin (2006)", ethanolic extracts from Salvia rosmarinus were phytochemically characterised. Rosmanic acid, a typical compound in Lamiaceae species, was identified in these extracts. Additionally, some other phenolic compounds such as apiigenin- and luteolin-derivatives were identified in different amounts. All extracts exhibited high and concentration-dependent levels of antiviral activity against free acyclovir-sensitive and acyclovir-resistant HSV-1 strains with 50% inhibitory concentrations of 0.05-0.82 microg/ml.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is Salvia rosmarinus, which is processed into various medicinal forms including essential oil, Salvía rosmarinus hydrolate, aqueous extract, decoction, and ethanol extracts. The essential oil provides a highly concentrated aromatic profile used for topical or olfactory stimulation. Hydrolates serve as gentle, water-based byproducts of distillation that are suitable for skin care. For direct therapeutic use, an aqueous extract or a decoction can be prepared by simmering the plant material in water to release its water-soluble compounds. Additionally, ethanol extracts are created by soaking the herb in alcohol to capture a broader range of bioactive constituents.

According to a study published by "Plants (Basel, Switzerland)", the medicinal plant Salvia rosmarinus Spenn. 'Severn seas' was investigated regarding its growth and essential oil yield under a dynamic agrivoltaics system in Southern Italy in 2023. In the consistently shaded area identified as UP, the plant experienced an ETc reduction of 81.1% relative to the T plot. While the species showed a significant reduction in fresh biomass under the high shading of UP compared to T, no biomass reductions were observed in the BP plot. Essential oil yields for the plant were higher in both the UP and BP plots, ranging from 0.60-2.63%. These yields were greater than the 0.51-1.90% recorded in the T plot.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause seizures if consumed in high doses due to its significant camphor content. Other common side effects include nausea, dizziness, and vomiting. Some individuals may also experience gastric irritation following ingestion. Furthermore, certain people might suffer from an allergic reaction to the plant.

  • Nausea

What herbs are paired with Salvia rosmarinus?

This plant pairs most effectively with woody and pungent herbs such as thyme, sage, oregano, and rosemary to create robust flavor profiles.

The camphor-like notes of rosemary complement the earthy undertones found in sage and the citrusy brightness of thyme. When used in Mediterranean cooking, it integrates seamlessly with oregano to enhance savory meat dishes and roasted vegetables. These combinations work best when the herbs are used in similar quantities to balance their strong aromatic intensities.

Careful measurement ensures that the dominant scent of rosemary does not overwhelm the more delicate notes of its companions.
