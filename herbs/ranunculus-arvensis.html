Ranunculus arvensis

Corn buttercup

Ranunculus arvensis, commonly known as Field Buttercup, is a creeping perennial herb characterized by its small, bright yellow flowers and deeply lobed leaves. Frequently found in pastures and disturbed soils, this plant belongs to the Ranunculaceae family and contains protoanemonin, which can cause skin irritation upon contact.

  • Scientific name: Ranunculus arvensis
  • Family: Ranunculaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Ranunculales. Ultimately, it is a member of the family Ranunculaceae and is identified by the genus Ranunculus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ranunculus arvensis, is recognized by various common names depending on the region and language. In French, it is called la Renoncule des champs, while in Dutch, it is referred to as the Akkerboterbloem. Moving to Scandinavia, the Swedish name for this species is Åkerranunkel. In Catalonia, it is known as ranuncle arvense, and in German-speaking areas, it is commonly called der Acker-Hahnenfuß.
