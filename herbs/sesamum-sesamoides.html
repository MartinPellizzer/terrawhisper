Sesamum sesamoides

Sesamum sesamoides, commonly known as wild sesame, is a versatile medicinal plant valued in traditional healing practices. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, it is frequently utilized to treat various ailments, including skin conditions and digestive issues. This resilient species offers significant pharmacological potential for modern natural medicine research.

  • Scientific name: Sesamum sesamoides
  • Family: Pedaliaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further organized into the order Lamiales and the family Pedaliaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Sesamum.

Common Names

This plant, Sesamum sesamoides, is known by various regional names depending on the linguistic and cultural context. In English, it is frequently referred to as the African sesame, while in Swahili, it is commonly called simsim. Within different local dialects and regions, it also carries the names of kulu, mshiriki, and sesamum.

Distribution

This plant, Sesamum sesamoides, is primarily distributed across the West Tropical Africa region. Its presence is specifically documented within the borders of Benin. Furthermore, it can be found growing in the landscapes of Burkina and Gambia. The species also extends its range into the country of Ghana. Finally, its geographical reach includes the territory of Guinea-Bissau.
