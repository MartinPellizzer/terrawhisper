European black elderberry

Sambucus nigra · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1140 studies) · ★★★★★

European black elderberry, scientifically known as Sambucus nigra, is a versatile flowering shrub celebrated for its potent medicinal properties. Rich in anthocyanins and antioxidants, its dark berries are widely used to support immune health and combat seasonal ailments. This ancient botanical remains a cornerstone of traditional herbal medicine globally.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Sambucus nigra is primarily sexual, utilizing bisexual flowers that facilitate genetic diversity. The flowering period typically begins in April and extends through July. This reproductive process relies on biotic pollination, specifically following an insect pollination syndrome, and is characterized by the absence of self-fertilization. The plant produces fleshy, indehiscent fruit classified as a berry, with an average fruit length of 0.615 cm and an average width of 0.55 cm. These fruits serve as the vehicle for zoochorous dispersal, specifically via an endozoochorous mechanism, where seeds are distributed through the digestive tracts of animals. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing more than 1,000 seeds. The individual seeds are small, with a consistent mean length of 3 mm and a mean mass of approximately 0.013038 g (ranging from a minimum of 0.0019 g to a maximum of 0.145 g). The seed volume varies, with a minimum of 3.8 mm³, a maximum of 7.1 mm³, and a mean volume of 5.45 mm³. Asexual reproduction is noted to be absent in this species.
