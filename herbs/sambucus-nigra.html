European black elderberry

Sambucus nigra · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1140 studies) · ★★★★★

European black elderberry, scientifically known as Sambucus nigra, is a versatile flowering shrub celebrated for its potent medicinal properties. Rich in anthocyanins and antioxidants, its dark berries are widely used to support immune health and combat seasonal ailments. This ancient botanical remains a cornerstone of traditional herbal medicine globally.

This plant, known scientifically as Sambucus nigra, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae within the order Dipsacales. Finally, it is a member of the family Viburnaceae and falls under the genus Sambucus.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as elderberry, utilizes various components for its medicinal and culinary applications. The delicate, creamy-white flowers are often harvested to create soothing syrups and teas, while the dark, nutrient-dense berries are widely celebrated for their immune-boosting properties. The green leaves contain certain compounds that have been used traditionally, though they must be used with caution due to their toxicity if prepared incorrectly. Even the bark of the elder tree has historical uses in traditional medicine, and the pollen produced by the blossoms plays a vital role in the plant's reproductive cycle and local ecosystem.
