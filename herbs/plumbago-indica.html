Plumbago indica

Indian leadwort

Plumbago indica, commonly known as Ceylon Leadwort, is a perennial shrub renowned for its striking clusters of white or pink flowers. Widely distributed across tropical Asia, this medicinal plant is highly valued in traditional medicine for its diverse therapeutic properties, particularly in treating skin ailments and various inflammatory conditions.

  • Scientific name: Plumbago indica
  • Family: Plumbaginaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified within the order Caryophyllales and the family Plumbaginaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Plumbago.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions in Asia. In China, it can be found specifically in the South-Central areas as well as on Hainan island. Moving toward the Indian Subcontinent, its presence is well-documented in the state of Assam. Additionally, the species is widely distributed throughout Bangladesh within the same subcontinent. Finally, its range extends further into the mountainous terrain of the East Himalaya.

From this main structure, the plant develops specialized tuberous roots, which function as storage organs to hold nutrients.
