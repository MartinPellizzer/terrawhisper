Indian leadwort

Plumbago indica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (49 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Indian leadwort, scientifically known as Plumbago indica, is a perennial herb celebrated in traditional Ayurvedic medicine. Characterized by its delicate pink flowers, this plant contains bioactive compounds like plumbagin. It is widely utilized for its potent anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and wound-healing properties, offering significant therapeutic potential in modern pharmacology.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions in Asia. In China, it can be found specifically in the South-Central areas as well as on Hainan island. Moving toward the Indian Subcontinent, its presence is well-documented in the state of Assam. Additionally, the species is widely distributed throughout Bangladesh within the same subcontinent. Finally, its range extends further into the mountainous terrain of the East Himalaya.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Plumbago indica, possesses a complex underground system featuring a primary root that serves as a central anchor. From this main structure, the plant develops specialized tuberous roots, which function as storage organs to hold nutrients. These are accompanied by a network of hairy roots that increase the surface area for efficient water and mineral absorption from the soil. Above the ground, the stem emerges to support the foliage and flowers, providing the necessary structural framework for the plant's growth. Although the roots are vital for stability, the entire subterranean system is essential for the medicinal properties often associated with this species.
