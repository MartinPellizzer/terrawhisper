Cussonia holstii

Cussonia holstii, commonly known as the Holst's Cussionia, is a distinctive woody shrub native to Southern Africa. Renowned for its unique architectural foliage, this plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditionally, various parts are utilized in folk medicine to treat ailments ranging from skin irritations to digestive discomforts.

  • Scientific name: Cussonia holstii
  • Family: Araliaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Apiales, it is classified under the family Araliaceae and is specifically identified by the genus Cussonia.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence is documented within the countries of Rwanda and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Moving toward the eastern side of the continent, the species is also found throughout Northeast Tropical Africa. Specifically, it inhabits the diverse landscapes of Eritrea and Ethiopia. Furthermore, its range extends into the Horn of Africa, specifically within Somalia.
