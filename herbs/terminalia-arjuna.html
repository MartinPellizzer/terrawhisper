Arjuna

Terminalia arjuna · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (727 studies) · ★★★★★

Arjuna, scientifically known as Terminalia arjuna, is a revered medicinal tree in Ayurveda, primarily celebrated for its profound cardioprotective properties. Found along riverbanks, its bark contains potent antioxidants and flavonoids. It is traditionally utilized to strengthen heart muscles, regulate blood pressure, and support overall cardiovascular health and vitality.

Physiology

The physiology of Terminalia arjuna is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, which dictates its carbon fixation mechanisms and metabolic efficiency within its natural habitat. As a C3 plant, it utilizes the Calvin cycle to fix atmospheric carbon dioxide directly through the enzyme RuBisCO, a process that is highly dependent on environmental temperature and moisture availability. Furthermore, this species does not function as a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the specialized symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria, such as Rhizobium, required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms, and instead relies on the uptake of nitrates and ammonium from the soil through its root system.

Reproduction in Terminalia arjuna is facilitated through the production of seeds contained within drupaceous fruits, which serve as the primary unit of dispersal and regeneration. The reproductive success of the species is closely tied to the physical characteristics of its seeds, which exhibit a notable range in size and weight. This weight can vary significantly depending on environmental conditions and maturity, ranging from a minimum of 0.43917 g to a maximum of 2 g per seed.
