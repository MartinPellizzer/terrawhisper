Aloe arborescens subsp. arborescens

Aloe arborescens subsp. arborescens, commonly known as the tree aloe, is a striking succulent native to South Africa. Characterized by its woody, tree-like stem and fleshy, serrated leaves, this species is prized in traditional medicine for its soothing properties, often utilized to treat skin irritations and promote healing.

  • Scientific name: Aloe arborescens subsp. arborescens
  • Family: Asphodelaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asparagales, it is classified in the family Asphodelaceae and the genus Aloe. As a subspecies, Aloe arborescens subsp. arborescens represents a specific taxonomic rank within this botanical lineage.

Common Names

This plant, Aloe arborescens subsp. arborescens, is known by various regional names depending on the language spoken. In English, it is commonly referred to as Krantz aloe, while in Afrikaans, it is called Kransalie. Additionally, it is known as Aloë arborescens in Latin.

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across several regions of Southern and Western Europe. It can be found inhabiting parts of Southwestern Europe, specifically within Spain and Portugal. Its presence extends to the Balearic Islands, which are also situated in Southwestern Europe.
