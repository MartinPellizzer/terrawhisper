Calotropis gigantea

Giant milkweed

Calotropis gigantea, commonly known as Giant Milkweed or Crown Flower, is a resilient perennial shrub found in tropical regions. Renowned in Ayurvedic medicine, this plant contains bioactive compounds used to treat inflammatory conditions, skin diseases, and digestive issues. Its milky latex and nectar hold significant therapeutic and ecological value.

  • Scientific name: Calotropis gigantea
  • Family: Apocynaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Gentianales, it is classified under the family Apocynaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Calotropis.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several diverse regions. In East Tropical Africa, it can be found growing in Kenya and Tanzania. Its range extends into South Tropical Africa, where it is present in Angola and Mozambique. Additionally, the species is located in the China South-Central region. These scattered locations demonstrate the plant's ability to thrive in various tropical and subtropical environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Calotropis gigantea, is a versatile medicinal species where almost every component serves a therapeutic purpose. The milky white latex is perhaps its most distinctive feature, often utilized in traditional medicine for treating skin conditions and inflammatory issues. The large, broad leaves are frequently employed in topical applications, such as heating them to relieve joint pain, while the dried leaves can also be used in various herbal preparations. Below the surface, the roots contain potent alkaloids used for their various pharmacological properties, and even a single root extract can be processed to derive significant medicinal benefits.
