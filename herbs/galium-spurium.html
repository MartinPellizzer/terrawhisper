Galium spurium

What's the taxonomical classification of Galium spurium?

Galium spurium belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is classified in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, it falls under the order Gentianaales and is a member of the family Rubiaceae. Finally, its specific identity is defined by the genus Galium and the species epithet spurium.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Galium spurium has whorled leaves arranged in circles of six to eight, square-shaped stems that are covered in fine bristly hairs, and small, four-petaled white flowers. The plant typically grows in a creeping or decumbent manner, allowing it to spread across the ground through its rooting stems. Its leaves are lanceolate or linear in shape and possess a distinct texture due to the presence of small hairs along the margins and surfaces. The inflorescence consists of small, inconspicuous flowers that emerge from the leaf axils in clusters. These structural features collectively allow the species to thrive in disturbed habitats and shaded areas.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Galium spurium is utilized medicinally through its aerial parts, which encompass the stems and leaves. These above-ground structures contain bioactive compounds that are often harvested for herbal preparations. Traditional practitioners frequently use these components to address various skin conditions or inflammatory issues. The preparation methods typically involve creating infusions or tinctures from the dried plant material. Such applications rely on the specific chemical properties found within the foliage and stems.

According to a study published by "Journal of ethnopharmacology", the aerial parts of Galium spurium are used for their anticonvulsant and neuroprotective effects. In pentylenetetrazoe-induced seizure models, the ethanol extract at doses of 250 and 1000mg/kg prolonged the onset of seizures. At doses of 250 and 500mg/kg, the plant provided 12.5% and 17% protection against picrotoxin-induced seizures in mice. Against maximal electroshock-induced seizures, the extract showed protective effects of 50% at 250mg/kg and 37.5% at 1000mg/kg. The extract also exhibited 16.2% inhibition against butrylcholinesterase at a 200μg/mL concentration. No motor co-ordination issues were observed with intraperitoneal injection at doses of 500 and 1000mg/kg.

What are the pharmacological activities of Galium spurium?

This plant has antibacterial, anticonvulsant, antitumor, and neuroprotective pharmacological activities. Its secondary metabolites contribute to suppressing the growth of various pathogenic bacteria. The plant also demonstrates potential in managing seizure activity through its anticonvulsant properties. Research suggests it may inhibit certain cancer cell lines, highlighting its antitumor potential. Furthermore, it provides neuroprotective effects that may help shield nerve cells from damage.

According to a study published by "Asian Pacific journal of tropical medicine", the pharmacological activities of Galium spurium L. were investigated among 51 different extracts prepared with 3 types of solvents. These extracts were evaluated for antibacterial activity against 10 different bacteria using the disc diffusion method. The study also included an evaluation of antitumor activity through an Agrobacterium tumefaciens-induced potato disc tumor assay. Although the research involved 16 different plant species grown in Turkey, specific data regarding the antibacterial or tumor inhibition percentages for Galium spurium L. were not provided in the results.
