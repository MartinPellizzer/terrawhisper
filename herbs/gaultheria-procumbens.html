Eastern teaberry, scientifically known as Gaultheria procumbens, is a low-growing evergreen shrub native to North America. Renowned for its aromatic, sweet-tasting berries, this plant is a significant source of methyl salicylate. Traditionally used in herbal medicine, it offers potent anti-inflammatory and analgesic properties for soothing various aches and pains.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as wintergreen, utilizes various components for its medicinal properties. The low-growing, creeping stems act as rhizomes that allow the plant to spread across the forest floor, providing a sturdy base for its growth. The dark green, leathery leaves are evergreen and serve as a primary source of essential oils, most notably methyl salicylate. Ultimately, it is the collection of these aerial parts, including the foliage and occasionally the flowers, that is harvested to extract the aromatic compounds used in various therapeutic applications.
