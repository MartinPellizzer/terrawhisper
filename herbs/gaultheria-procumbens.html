Gaultheria procumbens

Eastern teaberry

Gaultheria procumbens, commonly known as wintergreen, is a perennial evergreen shrub native to North America. Renowned for its aromatic leaves and sweet berries, it contains methyl salicylate, a natural precursor to aspirin. This potent plant is widely utilized in traditional medicine and modern topical analgesics to relieve pain.

  • Scientific name: Gaultheria procumbens
  • Family: Ericaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Ericales and the family Ericaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Gaultheria.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as wintergreen, utilizes various components for its medicinal properties. The low-growing, creeping stems act as rhizomes that allow the plant to spread across the forest floor, providing a sturdy base for its growth. The dark green, leathery leaves are evergreen and serve as a primary source of essential oils, most notably methyl salicylate. Ultimately, it is the collection of these aerial parts, including the foliage and occasionally the flowers, that is harvested to extract the aromatic compounds used in various therapeutic applications.
