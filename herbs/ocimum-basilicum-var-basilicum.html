Ocimum basilicum var. basilicum

Ocimum basilicum var. basilicum, commonly known as sweet basil, is a fragrant annual herb celebrated in culinary and medicinal traditions worldwide. Rich in essential oils like linalool, it offers potent antioxidant, antibacterial, and anti-inflammatory properties. This versatile plant serves as a natural remedy for digestive health and stress relief.

  • Scientific name: Ocimum basilicum var. basilicum
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales and family Lamiaceae, it is classified under the genus Ocimum. Specifically, this variety is known as Ocimum basilicum var. basilicum.

Common Names

This plant, scientifically known as Ocimum basilicum var. basilicum, is recognized by various common names across different linguistic contexts. Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of presence across several European regions. In Northern Europe, its distribution can be observed within Great Britain. Moving into Middle Europe, it is commonly found in Germany. The species also populates Southeastern Europe, specifically within the territories of Bulgaria and Romania. Finally, its range extends into Eastern Europe, reaching as far as South European Russia.

Plant Parts

This plant, scientifically known as Ocimum basilicum var. basilicum, is highly valued for its therapeutic properties, which are most commonly derived from the leaf extract. The leaves contain a rich concentration of essential oils, such as linalool and estragole, which provide the plant with its characteristic aromatic profile and medicinal potency. When prepared as a concentrated extract, these leaves serve as a powerful source of antioxidants and antimicrobial compounds used in various traditional and modern wellness applications.
