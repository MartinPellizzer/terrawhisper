Uvaria littoralis

Uvaria littoralis, commonly known as the coastal uvaria, is a versatile climbing shrub native to tropical Southeast Asia. Renowned in traditional medicine, this plant is valued for its diverse bioactive compounds. It is frequently utilized to treat various ailments, including inflammation, skin disorders, and digestive issues, showcasing its significant therapeutic potential.

  • Scientific name: Uvaria littoralis
  • Family: Annonaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Uvaria littoralis, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae within the order Magnoliales. Finally, it is a member of the family Annonaceae and falls under the genus Uvaria.

Common Names

This plant, Uvaria littoralis, is known by various names depending on the region and local language. In English, it is commonly referred to as the coastal uvaria, while in Tamil, it is known as kattu kattu. In Sinhala, the plant is called katu katu, and in Malay, it is identified as uvaria littoralis.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several key regions within the Asian continent. Within the Indian Subcontinent, it is documented to grow in India, Bangladesh, and Sri Lanka. Additionally, its presence is noted specifically in the state of Assam. The species also extends into the Indo-China region, specifically including the Andaman Islands. Together, these locations highlight the diverse habitats where the plant can be found.
