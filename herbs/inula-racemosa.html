Inula racemosa

Inula racemosa, commonly known as elecampane, is a perennial herb cherished in traditional medicine for its potent therapeutic properties. Renowned for its restorative effects on the respiratory and digestive systems, this plant features aromatic rhizomes rich in bioactive compounds, making it a cornerstone of herbal healing and wellness practices.

  • Scientific name: Inula racemosa
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Asterales and is a member of the family Asteraceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus name, Inula.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across several continents. In Northern Europe, it can be found growing in Great Britain. Its presence extends into Middle Europe, where it is documented in both Belgium and Germany. Moving toward Western Asia, the species is also located in Afghanistan. Finally, its range reaches into China, specifically within the South-Central region.

Plant Parts

This plant, Inula racemosa, possesses several distinct morphological features that are utilized in traditional medicine. The most therapeutically significant portions are the underground structures, which include both the roots and the thick, fleshy rhizomes that store essential compounds. While the individual root system provides structural stability, the sprawling rhizomes are often the primary part harvested for their medicinal potency. Above the ground, the plant features a sturdy stem that supports the foliage, and a collection of green leaves that facilitate photosynthesis to sustain the plant's growth.
