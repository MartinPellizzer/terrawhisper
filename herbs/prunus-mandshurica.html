Prunus mandshurica

Prunus mandshurica, commonly known as the Manchurian cherry, is a resilient deciduous shrub native to East Asia. Renowned for its delicate blossoms and medicinal properties, it has long been utilized in traditional practices. Its bark and fruit offer therapeutic potential, making it a significant subject in ethnobotanical studies.

  • Scientific name: Prunus mandshurica
  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is situated in the order Rosales and the family Rosaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identification is completed by its placement in the genus Prunus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across several key regions of Northeast Asia. In the Russian Far East, its presence is notably documented within the Primorye territory. Moving south, it is widely distributed throughout the Manchuria region of China. The species also extends its range into the Korean Peninsula, representing a significant part of its Eastern Asian habitat. Consequently, its geographical footprint is concentrated within these distinct northern and eastern territories.
