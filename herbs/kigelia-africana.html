Sausage Tree

Kigelia africana · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Moderate (120 studies) · ★★★☆☆

Sausage Tree, scientifically known as Kigelia africana, is a remarkable African species celebrated for its massive, pendulous fruits. Renowned in traditional medicine, this botanical wonder offers diverse therapeutic properties, ranging from anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial effects to skin-healing benefits, making it a vital subject for modern pharmacological research and ethnobotany.

This plant, Kigelia africana, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, falling under the order Lamiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Bignoniaceae and is categorized under the genus Kigelia.

Distribution

This plant, known as Kigelia africana, exhibits a specific distribution across several regions of West Tropical Africa. It can be found growing within the borders of Benin and Burkina Faso. Additionally, the species is present in the nation of Gambia. Its geographical range further extends into the territory of Ghana. Finally, the plant is also documented to inhabit the country of Guinea.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the sausage tree, utilizes various components for its medicinal properties. The stem bark is frequently harvested and prepared to treat conditions such as skin infections and inflammation. The leaves, often studied individually as a single leaf in botanical analyses, are used in decoctions to address ailments ranging from fever to digestive issues. Most notably, the large, pendulous fruits are a defining feature of the species; each individual fruit contains bioactive compounds that are traditionally employed in various healing rituals and topical applications.
