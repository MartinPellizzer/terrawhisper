Indian fleabane

Pluchea indica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Sparse (69 studies) · ★★☆☆☆

Indian fleabane, scientifically known as Pluchea indica, is a versatile perennial shrub native to coastal regions of Asia. Renowned for its aromatic foliage and small purple flowers, this medicinal plant is widely used in traditional medicine to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and digestive issues due to its potent bioactive properties.

This plant, known scientifically as Pluchea indica, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is further classified under the order Asterales. It is a member of the Asteraceae family, falling under the genus Pluchea.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution spanning across several distinct regions of Asia. In the Middle East, its presence can be noted within the Arabian Peninsula, specifically in Yemen. Its range extends through Eastern Asia, where it is found in the Kazan-retto and Nansei-shoto areas. Within China, the species is distributed across the southeastern regions as well as the island of Hainan. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to inhabit various coastal and subtropical environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, Pluchea indica, is highly valued in traditional medicine, and various components are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The most commonly used part is the leaves, which are often processed to create a potent leaf extract used in topical or oral applications. Each individual leaf contains essential phytochemicals that contribute to its medicinal efficacy.
