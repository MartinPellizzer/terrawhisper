Pluchea indica

Pluchea indica, commonly known as Indian Camphorweed, is a versatile medicinal shrub native to tropical regions. Renowned for its aromatic properties, this plant is extensively used in traditional medicine to treat skin ailments, digestive issues, and inflammation. Its potent bioactive compounds offer significant therapeutic potential in modern pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Pluchea indica
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Pluchea indica, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is further classified under the order Asterales. It is a member of the Asteraceae family, falling under the genus Pluchea.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Pluchea indica, is recognized by various common names depending on the region and language. In Indonesia, it is widely referred to as Beluntas in Indonesian (id), while in Javanese (jv) it is called Luntas and in Sundanese (su) it is known as Baluntas. In Vietnam (vi), the plant is commonly called Cúc tần, and in Thailand (th), it is known as ขลู่.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution spanning across several distinct regions of Asia. In the Middle East, its presence can be noted within the Arabian Peninsula, specifically in Yemen. Its range extends through Eastern Asia, where it is found in the Kazan-retto and Nansei-shoto areas. Within China, the species is distributed across the southeastern regions as well as the island of Hainan. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to inhabit various coastal and subtropical environments.
