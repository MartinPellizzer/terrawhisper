Ceylon citronella

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (295 studies) · ★★★★★

Ceylon citronella, scientifically known as Cymbopogon nardus, is a perennial grass celebrated for its potent essential oils. Renowned for its refreshing citrus aroma, it serves as a powerful natural insect repellent and antiseptic. Traditionally used in aromatherapy and skincare, this versatile plant offers significant therapeutic benefits in holistic wellness.

The morphology of Cymbopogon nardus is characterized by its graminoid growth form, functioning as a non-woody herb. As a strictly terrestrial species, it is neither aquatic nor an epiphyte. Physiology

The physiology of Cymbopogon nardus is characterized by a highly efficient metabolic framework designed for survival in warm, sun-exposed environments. It utilizes a C4 photosynthetic pathway, which allows the plant to concentrate carbon dioxide around the enzyme Rubisco, thereby minimizing photorespiration and maximizing water-use efficiency compared to C3 plants. This specialized carbon fixation mechanism enables the plant to maintain high rates of biomass production even under high light intensities and elevated temperatures. Furthermore, Cymbopogon nardus is not a nitrogen fixer, meaning it does not possess a symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria to convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms; instead, it relies on the uptake of inorganic nitrogen, such as nitrates and ammonium, directly from the soil through its root system.
