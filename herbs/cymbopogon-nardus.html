Ceylon citronella

Cymbopogon nardus · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (295 studies) · ★★★★★

Ceylon citronella, scientifically known as Cymbopogon nardus, is a perennial grass celebrated for its potent essential oils. Renowned for its refreshing citrus aroma, it serves as a powerful natural insect repellent and antiseptic. Traditionally used in aromatherapy and skincare, this versatile plant offers significant therapeutic benefits in holistic wellness.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across several key regions of the African continent. In Northern Africa, it can be found growing within the borders of Egypt. Moving toward West Tropical Africa, the species is documented in Nigeria. Its presence extends into West-Central Tropical Africa, where it inhabits the nations of Burundi and Rwanda. Finally, the plant is also widely distributed within the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Plant Parts

This plant, Cymbopogon nardus, commonly known as citronella grass, utilizes various components for its therapeutic properties. While the roots serve as a foundational structural element, much of the medicinal interest lies in the aromatic compounds found within the plant. The inflorescences contribute to the plant's reproductive cycle, but the most potent bioactive agents are concentrated in the essential oils harvested from the foliage. Scientific studies often utilize root methanolic extracts to investigate specific phytochemical profiles, yet it is the high-quality essential oil extracted from the leaves that remains the most widely used component in aromatherapy and insect repellent formulations.
