Siberian elder

Sambucus sibirica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (14 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Siberian elder, scientifically known as Sambucus sibirica, is a resilient deciduous shrub native to the harsh climates of Northern Asia. Renowned for its dark berries and fragrant blossoms, this medicinal plant is highly valued in traditional herbalism for its potent antioxidant properties and ability to support immune health.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Dipsacales, it is further categorized under the family Viburnaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Sambucus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions of Northern Asia and Europe. In Eastern Europe, its presence is notably documented throughout East European Russia. Moving into the vast territory of Siberia, it can be found in the Altay region. The species also inhabits the areas of Buryatiya and Chita within the Siberian landscape. Finally, its range extends further into the Irkutsk region of Siberia.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Sambucus sibirica, possesses several distinct parts that are utilized for various medicinal and practical purposes. Its compound leaves are characterized by their pinnate structure and are often studied for their bioactive compounds. The plant produces clusters of dark, fleshy fruits that are rich in antioxidants and are frequently used in traditional remedies to support the immune system. Additionally, the woody branches provide the structural framework for the shrub, and in some traditional practices, different elements of the plant's stems and bark are examined for their chemical properties.
