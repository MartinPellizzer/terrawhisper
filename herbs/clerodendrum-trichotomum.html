Clerodendrum trichotomum

Harlequin gataxon comlorybower

Clerodendrum trichotomum, commonly known as Harunaga or Siamese Glory Bower, is a deciduous shrub prized in traditional medicine. Renowned for its aromatic foliage and striking red berries, it possesses diverse therapeutic properties, including anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial effects, making it a vital botanical resource in various Asian healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Clerodendrum trichotomum
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae, it is classified under the genus Clerodendrum.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Clerodendrum trichotomum, is recognized by a variety of unique common names across different languages and cultures. In Denmark, it is referred to as Almindelig Skæbnetræ, while in France, it is called Arbre du clergé. In East Asia, its names reflect deep cultural roots, being known as クサギ (kusagi) in Japan, 누리장나무 (nurijangnamu) in Korea, and 海州常山 (hǎizhōu chángshān) in China.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range that spans across several distinct regions. In Europe, it can be found in Great Britain within Northern Europe and in Italy throughout Southeastern Europe. Its presence is also significant across various parts of China, including the South-Central regions and the island of Hainan. Furthermore, the species extends its reach into the Manchuria region of China. These diverse locations highlight the wide environmental adaptability of the species.
