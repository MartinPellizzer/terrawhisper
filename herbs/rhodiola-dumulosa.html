This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and order Saxifrages, it is a member of the family Crassulaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Rhodiola.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several distinct regions of China. It can be found growing within the landscapes of China South-Central and China North-Central. Additionally, its habitat extends into the northern territories of Inner Mongolia and Manchuria. The species is also documented to inhabit the high-altitude areas of Qinghai. Collectively, these locations highlight the diverse environmental range that Rhodiola dumulosa occupies throughout the country.

Plant Parts

This plant, Rhodiola dumosa, is characterized by its specialized structural components that contribute to its survival in harsh alpine environments. The most pharmacologically significant part is the rhizome, a thick, fleshy underground stem that stores essential nutrients and bioactive compounds used in traditional medicine. Above the ground, the stems are organized into distinct sections, where the length and development of the internodes—the segments of stem located between the nodes—play a role in the plant's overall growth habit and its ability to occupy space within its rocky habitat.
