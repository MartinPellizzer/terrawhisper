What's the taxonomical classification of Anisodus luridus?

Anisodus luridus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the hierarchy of classification, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Its taxonomic lineage continues through the order Solanales and the family Solanaceae, eventually reaching its specific designation within the genus Anisodus and the species luridus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Anisodus luridus has an erect, herbaceous growth habit characterized by slender, smooth stems and oppositely arranged, ovate to lanceolate leaves with entire margins. The foliage typically displays a dull green color and a somewhat pubescent texture on the surface. Its reproductive structures consist of small, inconspicuous flowers that emerge from the leaf axils. These flowers eventually develop into small, globose capsules containing numerous tiny seeds. The overall structure of the plant is relatively delicate and sprawling in its natural habitat.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Anisodus luridus provides medicinal value through the utilization of both its leaves and its roots. The leaves are frequently harvested to prepare decoctions or infusions used to treat various ailments. Meanwhile, the roots are processed to extract specific compounds believed to possess therapeutic properties. These different plant components are integrated into traditional healing practices depending on the desired effect. Practitioners carefully select between the foliage and the root system to address particular health concerns.

According to a study published by "Natural product research", three new acyltyramines were isolated from the n-hexane extract of the leaves of Anisodus luridus. These specific compounds include N-[2-(4-hydroxyphenyl)ethyl]hentriacontanamide (1), N-[2-(4-hydroxyphenyl)ethyl]nonacosanamide (2), and N-[2-(4-hydroxyphenyl)ethyl]heneicosanamide (3). Successive extraction of defatted leaves of A. luridus with methanol produced a residue that was partitioned using chloroform and n-butanol. Chromatographic resolution of the n-BuOH extract yielded 6 known compounds, which are apigenin (4), luteolin (5), quercetin (6), quercetin 3-O-alpha-l-rhamnoside (7), kaempferol 3-O-alpha-rhamnoside (8), and quercetin 3-O-alpha-l-rhamnopyranosyl-(1 to 6)-beta-d-glucopyranoside (9). This research represents the first report regarding the isolation of these compounds from this plant.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains several medicinal compounds including the tropane alkaloids anisodamine, hyoscyamine, and scopolamine, as well as the flavonoid apiigenin. These specific alkaloids are known for their significant pharmacological effects on the nervous system and smooth muscles. Anisodamine is often studied for its ability to improve microcirculation and treat various types of shock. Hyoscyamine and scopolamine act as anticholinergic agents that can relieve spasms and reduce secretions. Additionally, the presence of apiigenin provides the plant with unique antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties.

According to a study published by "ACS omega", Anisodus luridus is a medicinal plant abundant in tropane alkaloids such as hyoscyamine and scopolamine within its roots. The actual tropane alkaloid yield for this plant was 6.211 mg/g, which closely aligned with the predicted value of 6.194 mg/g. Alkaloid content in tested batch samples ranged from 3.2044 to 22.0037 mg/g, while regulatory standards require the content not be lower than 0.05%. Quantitative analysis showed linearity for hyoscyamine in the range of 20.00-2000.00 μg/mL and scopolamine from 2.00 to 256.00 μg/mL. Additionally, anisodaamine 1 and 2 showed linear ranges of 1.08-69.12 and 0.92-58.88 μg/mL, respectively. Average relative recoveries for these compounds were 98.91, 99.73, 100.05, and 97.01%.
