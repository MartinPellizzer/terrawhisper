Entada africana

Entada africana, commonly known as the African Dream Bean, is a robust woody climber native to tropical Africa. Renowned for its large, impressive pods, this medicinal plant is deeply embedded in traditional healing practices, where its roots and bark are utilized to treat various ailments, including inflammation and digestive issues.

  • Scientific name: Entada africana
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fabales, it is classified under the family Fabaceae and is specifically identified by the genus Entada. As a member of the Entada africana species, it occupies a distinct position within this hierarchical taxonomic structure.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Entada africana, is referred to by various names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In Fulfulde (ff), it is called Faɗo waanduuhi, while in Dagbani (dag), it is known as Ʒugulikɔŋga. In Chinese, the plant is identified as 非洲榼藤 across several notations, including Traditional Chinese (zh), Simplified Chinese (zh-hans), and the specific regional designation for Mainland China (zh-cn).

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several regions within West Tropical Africa. It can be found growing in the territory of Benin. The species also extends its range into Burkina. Additionally, its presence is documented in Gambia and Ghana. Finally, the geographical reach of this medicinal plant includes Guinea-Bissau.
