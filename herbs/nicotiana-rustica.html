Nicotiana rustica

What's the taxonomical classification of Nicotiana rustica?

Nicotiana rustica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. It is situated under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, following the taxonomic lineage of the order Solanales. Within this order, the plant is a member of the family Solanaceae, specifically falling under the genus Nicotiana, with the specific epithet rusticus identifying its species.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant Nicotiana rustica has been utilized within Amazonian medicine for centuries to treat various physical and spiritual ailments. Indigenous groups across the Amazon basin traditionally use its potent leaves to prepare ceremonial infusions and smoke for ritual purification. Beyond ritualistic use, it has historically served as a powerful topical remedy for skin infections and insect bites. A liquid remedy taken orally is the most commonly used preparation, which can cause acute or sub-acute effects involving a pronounced psychoactive component and physiological responses such as emesis and nausea.

What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is Nicotiana rustica, a species used to produce medicinal herbal preparations such as ethanolic extracts and liquid tinctures. These preparations are often created by soaking the dried plant material in high-proof alcohol to pull out bioactive alkaloids. The resulting ethanolic extracts serve as concentrated bases for various therapeutic applications in traditional medicine. Once the extraction process is complete, the substances can be used as a liquid for topical application or diluted for specific medicinal uses. Practitioners carefully control the concentration of these liquids to ensure safety and efficacy during treatment.

According to a study published by "Insects", ethanolic extracts of Nicotiaana rustica L. contain variable amounts of phytochemicals including alkaloids, flavonoids, saponins, diterpenes, phytosterol, and phenols, as well as total phenolic and flavonoid compounds. The plant extracts were applied at six different concentrations of 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0% in four replications. Following a 96-h exposure period, the toxicity of Nicotiaana rustica L. was measured with an LC50 of 0.98% and an LC90 of 22.06%. At a 3% concentration, Nicotiaana rustica L. demonstrated a 74% repellency, which is classified as Class-IV. These findings highlight the potential of this species in the formulation of new biocidal agents.
