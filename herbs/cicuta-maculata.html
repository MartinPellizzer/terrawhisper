, scientifically known as Cicuta maculata, is a highly toxic perennial herb native to North America. Commonly called spotted water hemlock, it thrives in moist habitats. This plant is notoriously dangerous due to its potent cicutoxin, which can cause severe neurological distress or death if even small amounts are ingested.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several key regions of northern and western North America. In the Subarctic regions of America, its presence is documented in Nunavut, the Northwest Territories, and the Yukon. Moving south, the species is also commonly found throughout the Western Canadian provinces. Specifically, it inhabits the landscapes of both Alberta and British Columbia. Together, these locations define the broad geographical range of Cicuta maculata.
