, scientifically known as Cicuta maculata, is a highly toxic perennial herb native to North America. Commonly called spotted water hemlock, it thrives in moist habitats. Physiology

The physiology of Cicuta maculata is characterized by its specialized metabolic and biochemical processes, most notably its reliance on the C3 photosynthetic pathway for carbon fixation. As a C3 plant, it utilizes the enzyme Rubisco to catalyze the initial carboxylation of CO2 within the mesophyll cells, forming a three-carbon compound during the Calvin cycle. This pathway is typical for plants inhabiting temperate, moist environments where water availability is sufficient to avoid the carbon-concentrating mechanisms found in C4 or CAM species. Beyond its carbon assimilation, the plant's physiology is defined by the intense biosynthesis of highly potent, lipid-soluble neurotoxins, specifically cicutoxin, which acts as a potent GABA receptor antagonist. These secondary metabolites are integrated into the plant's physiological framework as chemical defenses, altering the electrochemical signaling in nervous systems upon ingestion. The plant's overall physiological vigor is closely tied to its preference for hydric soils, which supports the high transpiration rates necessary to maintain the turgor and metabolic flux required for its rapid growth and complex biochemical synthesis.
