South China ginseng

Panax notoginseng · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (1629 studies) · ★★★★★

South China ginseng, scientifically known as Panax notoginseng, is a highly prized medicinal root celebrated in traditional Chinese medicine. Renowned for its adaptogenic properties, it is used to enhance vitality, improve circulation, and reduce inflammation. This potent botanical serves as a cornerstone for promoting overall wellness and longevity.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is placed in the order Apiales and the family Araliaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Panax, specifically as the species Panax notoginseng.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across specific regions of East and Southeast Asia. In China, its presence is noted throughout the South-Central territories. It is also found extensively within the Southeast regions of China. Beyond Chinese borders, the species extends into the Indo-China peninsula. Specifically, it can be located within the geographical boundaries of Vietnam.

Plant Parts

This plant, Panax notoginseng, is a perennial herb where the most pharmacologically significant part is the tuberous roots, which are harvested for their medicinal properties. The individual root structure grows underground, serving as the primary storage organ for active ginsenosides. Above the ground, the plant features several large, palmately compound leaves that emerge from the base to facilitate photosynthesis. During its reproductive stage, the plant produces small, clustered flowers that grow atop a central stalk. Although the stems are relatively short and often stay close to the soil surface, they provide the essential structural support required to lift the foliage and floral clusters toward the light.
