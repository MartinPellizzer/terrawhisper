Mallotus repandus

What's the taxonomical classification of Mallotus repandus?

Mallotus repandus belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is further organized into the order Malpighiales. The plant is a member of the family Euphorbiaceae, falling under the genus Mallotus, and is specifically identified by its species name, repandus.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Mallotus repandus has prostrate or climbing stems with simple or branched leaves that are typically ovate to elliptic in shape and possess entire or slightly wavy margins. The leaves are characterized by an acute or acuminate apex and a cuneate base, often displaying a somewhat leathery texture. Small, inconspicuous flowers are produced in axillary clusters, leading to the development of small, globose capsules. These capsules eventually release tiny seeds that are characteristic of the Euphorbiaceae family. The overall growth habit allows the plant to spread across the forest floor or climb over surrounding vegetation.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Mallotus repandus utilizes its seeds and stems for various medicinal purposes. The seeds are often processed to treat skin conditions and inflammatory ailments due to their unique chemical compounds. Additionally, the stem is harvested to create decoctions used in traditional remedies for digestive issues. These specific plant parts provide essential bioactive substances that contribute to the species' therapeutic value. Researchers continue to study these components to better understand their pharmacological potential.

According to a study published by "TheScientificWorldJournal", the seeds of Mallotus repandus (Willd.) Müll.Arg. were found to exhibit prominent antibacterial effects. When using 1 mg/disc of methanolic extract, the ethyl acetate fraction of the seeds showed the highest efficacy. This fraction produced a 29.00 mm inhibition zone against the Gram-negative bacteria S. typhi. Additionally, the ethyl acetate fraction of Mallotus repandus seeds displayed the lowest MIC and MBC values of 0.25 and 0.53 mg/mL, respectively, against S. typhi. Consequently, the ethyl acetate extract of these seeds could serve as a useful source to inhibit Gram-negative bacteria.

According to a study published by "PloS one", Mallotus repandus is the main component of the pain relief formula Ya Pa Som Kho-Khlan (YPSKK) which is officially recorded in the Natural List of Essenitial Medicines (NLEM) of Thailand. The M. repandus extract exhibits a distinct HPTLC profile that can be used for differentiation. DNA barcoding for identification involves the rbcl, matk, ITS and psba-trnh intergenic spacer regions. For Bar-HRM analysis, the rbcl region was used to obtain 102 bp amplicons encompassing 9 polymorphic nucleotides. The melting temperature (Tm) for M. repandus was measured at 80.05±0.07°C. Analysis of crude drugs showed that the HPTLC profiles of CD2-6 displayed distinct bands of M. repandus.
