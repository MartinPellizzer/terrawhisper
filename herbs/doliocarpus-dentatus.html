The ecology of Doliocarpus dentatus is characterized by a broad environmental adaptability, allowing it to thrive across a diverse array of Brazilian biomes and forest types. It is found in varied habitats including the Caatinga (strictu sensu), Campinarana, Cerrado (lato sensu), and several distinct forest ecosystems such as Floresta Ciliar or Galeria, Floresta de Igapó, Floresta de Terra Firmre, Floresta de Várzea, and Floresta Ombrófila (also known as Floresta Pluvial). Reproduction

The reproduction of Doliocarpus dentatus is characterized by the production of fleshy fruits classified as drupes. These fruits are indehiscent, meaning they do not split open at maturity to release their contents, and instead rely on a fleshy consistency to facilitate dispersal. The plant exhibits a zoochorous dispersal syndrome, suggesting that its seeds are spread via animals. The seeds themselves are relatively uniform in size, with a recorded seed volume that remains constant at a mean, minimum, and maximum of 57 mm³. Additionally, the average mass of a single seed is approximately 0.26 g.
