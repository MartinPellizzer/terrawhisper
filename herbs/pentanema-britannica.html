Reproduction

The reproduction of Pentaema britannica is characterized by a sexual process through bisexual flowers, with asexual reproduction being entirely absent. The flowering period typically commences in July and concludes in September. The pollination process is categorized as biotic, specifically relying on an insect pollination syndrome to facilitate fertilization, which is notably present within the species. Following successful fertilization, the plant produces fruit in the form of an achene. These fruits are indehiscent, meaning they do not split open at maturity to release seeds. The resulting seeds are extremely lightweight, with a mean mass of 0.0001195 g and a recorded mass range between 0.0001195 g and 0.000152 g. For the spread of the next generation, the plant utilizes an anemochorous dispersal syndrome, relying on wind to transport its minute seeds.
