Chamaecyparis lawsoniana

Lawson's cypress

Chamaecyparis lawsoniana, commonly known as Leyland cypress, is a versatile evergreen prized for its dense foliage. Beyond landscaping, it holds significant medicinal potential. Rich in essential oils and bioactive compounds, it is traditionally used for its antimicrobial and antiseptic properties, offering therapeutic benefits in various folk medicine practices worldwide.

  • Scientific name: Chamaecyparis lawsoniana
  • Family: Cupressaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Pinidae and the order Pinales, falling into the family Cupressaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement within the genus Chamaecyparis.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Chamaecyparis lawsoniana, is recognized by several different names across various Chinese language regions. In Simplified Chinese, it is referred to as 美国扁柏 (zh-hans) and is also commonly called 美国扁柏 in mainland China (zh-cn) and Singapore (zh-sg). In Traditional Chinese, the name is written as 美國扁柏 (zh-hanth), which is the standard nomenclature used in Hong Kong (zh-hk).
