Oenanthe javanica

Java waterdropwort

Oenanthe javaica, commonly known as Java Water Dropwort, is a perennial herb native to tropical regions. Traditionally utilized in various folk medicines, it possesses unique bioactive properties. While valued for its potential therapeutic applications, careful attention must be paid to its toxicity, as improper usage can lead to serious health risks.

  • Scientific name: Oenanthe javanica
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Apiales and the family Apiaceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Oenanthe.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Oenanthe javanica, is recognized by various common names across different regions and languages. In Japan, it is called セリ (seri), while in South Korea, it is referred to as 미나리 (minari). In Vietnam, the plant is known as Cần nước, and in Suriname, it goes by the name Téspong. Additionally, in the Netherlands, it is commonly called Javaanse peterselie.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across various regions of the Eurasian continent. In Northern Europe, its presence can be recorded in Great Britain. Moving toward Southeastern Europe, the species is found inhabiting parts of Italy. The distribution extends into Siberia, specifically within the Chitá region. Finally, it reaches the Russian Far East, where it is located in both the Kuril Islands and Primorye.
