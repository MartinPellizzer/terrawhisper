Croton echioideus

Croton echioideus, commonly known as the "Croton" or "Wild Croton," is a versatile medicinal shrub native to tropical regions. Renowned in traditional medicine, its leaves and roots are frequently utilized to treat digestive issues, skin inflammations, and respiratory ailments, offering significant therapeutic potential through its bioactive phytochemical compounds.

  • Scientific name: Croton echioideus
  • Family: Euphorbiaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Malpighiales and the family Euphorbiaceae. Ultimately, its specific taxonomic placement is within the genus Croton.

Common Names

This plant, Croton echioideus, is known by various regional names across different linguistic groups. In English, it is commonly referred to as the Croton or the Croton plant. In Portuguese, it is recognized as croton, and in Spanish, it is called croton. Within local contexts, such as in Swahili, it is known as mwalio, while in Zulu, it is referred to as umwalio.

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern within the South American continent. It is notably documented across the Northeast region of Brazil. Furthermore, the species can also be found growing within the Southeast part of the country. These two areas represent the primary geographical ranges for the species in Brazil.
