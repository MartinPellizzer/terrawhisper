Miscanthus sinensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Miscanthus sinensis?

Miscanthus sinensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. It is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the lineage of the order Poales, this plant is a member of the family Poaceae. Within this family, it is situated in the genus Miscanthus, and its specific biological identity is defined by the species name sinensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Miscanthus sinensis has upright, clumping perennial culms that typically reach heights of 1 to 3 meters and are characterized by linear, lanceolate leaves with prominent parallel venation. The leaf blades are often several inches long and taper to a sharp point at the apex. Its inflorescence consists of narrow, contracted panicles that emerge from the upper nodes of the stems. The individual spikelets are small and arranged in dense clusters along the branches of the panicle. These morphological features allow the plant to form dense, grassy stands in its native habitats.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant uses its leaves and rhizomes for various medicinal purposes in traditional practices. The leaves are often prepared as decoctions to help reduce fever and treat inflammation. Meanwhile, the rhizomes serve as a key component in formulas designed to clear heat and detoxify the body. These specific parts are harvested and processed carefully to ensure their therapeutic properties are preserved. Practitioners rely on these distinct botanical elements to address different physiological ailments.

According to a study published by "Phytochemistry", the leaf tissue of Miscanthus sinensis contains high levels of soluble phenols, including hydroxycinnamates and flavonoids, which may have applications in the nutraceutical, cosmetic, and pharmaceutical industries. Researchers identified and quantified ten hydroxycinnamates, specifically O-cinnamoylquinic acids, within the leaf tissue of two hundred progeny and maternal and paternal plants. The study also identified several flavones, consisting of one mono-O-glycosyl flavone, eight mono-C-glycosyl flavones, two di-C-glycosyl flavones, five O-glycosyl-C-glycosyl flavones, and nine 2″-O-glycosyl-C-glycosyl flavones. These phenols were studied during the seedling stage and again at seven months of growth to determine abundance at highest biomass. Overall, concentrations of these phenols generally decreased as the leaves matured.
