Aframomum citratum

Aframomum citratum, commonly known as lemon cardamom, is a fragrant medicinal plant native to tropical Africa. Valued for its distinct citrus aroma, it is widely used in traditional medicine to treat digestive issues, respiratory ailments, and inflammation. This versatile herb serves as both a culinary spice and a therapeutic remedy.

  • Scientific name: Aframomum citratum
  • Family: Zingiberaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Zingiberales, it is classified under the family Zingiberaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Aframomum, specifically as the species citratum.

Common Names

This plant, Aframomum citratum, is known by various common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In English, it is frequently referred to as lemon cardamom, while in French, it is known as cardamom citron. Within different African linguistic traditions, it is called m'pala in Lingala, kofit in Swahili, and siko in Mende.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern concentrated within the tropical regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is notably documented in Nigeria. Moving into the West-Central Tropical Africa zone, the species can be found in Cameroon. Its range further extends through West-Central Tropical Africa to include Equatorial Guinea. Finally, the geographical footprint of this medicinal plant encompasses Gabon as well.
