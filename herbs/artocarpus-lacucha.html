Artocarpus lacucha

Monkey fruit

Artocarpus lacucha, commonly known as Monkey Jack, is a versatile medicinal plant native to South and Southeast Asia. Renowned for its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, the fruit and bark are utilized in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from digestive issues to skin infections, offering significant therapeutic potential.

  • Scientific name: Artocarpus lacucha
  • Family: Moraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Rosales and family Moraceae, it is classified under the genus Artocarpus.

Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as Monkey Jack, possesses various components that are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The roots are frequently employed in traditional medicine to treat ailments such as inflammation and fever, often being prepared as decoctions. The latex, a milky sap that exudes from the bark or wounded stems, is valued for its antiseptic qualities and can be applied to skin conditions. Additionally, the wood of the tree is noted for its durability and is sometimes used in traditional practices where its specific chemical constituents are believed to offer subtle medicinal benefits.
