Mango-ginger, scientifically known as Curcuma amada, is a unique rhizomatous plant prized in Ayurvedic medicine. Distinguished by its distinct mango-like aroma, this tropical herb offers potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. It is widely utilized to support digestive health, boost immunity, and provide therapeutic benefits in traditional healing practices.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Zingiberales, it is a member of the Zingiberaceae family. It is specifically classified under the genus Curcuma.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the vast landscapes of the Indian Subcontinent. Within this region, it is commonly found growing in both India and Bangladesh. Beyond the subcontinent, its natural range extends into the Indo-China peninsula. Specifically, it is indigenous to the tropical environments of Myanmar. Finally, the species also thrives within the diverse ecosystems of Thailand.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Curcuma longa (often referred to in botanical contexts related to Curcuma amada), is characterized by a unique underground structure and above-ground growth. The most pharmacologically significant part is the rhizomes, which are thick, fleshy stems that store essential oils and curcuminoids. Within this root system, one can find smaller, secondary structures known as microrhizomes that aid in the plant's expansion and nutrient storage. Above the soil, the plant produces vibrant green shoots that emerge to facilitate photosynthesis, providing the energy necessary for the development of the medicinal components held within the underground parts.
