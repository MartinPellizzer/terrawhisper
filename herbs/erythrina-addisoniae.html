Erythrina addisoniae

Erythrina addisoniae, commonly known as the Addison's coral tree, is a distinctive medicinal plant native to Southern Africa. Renowned for its vibrant red flowers, various parts of this species are traditionally utilized in ethnomedicine to treat ailments ranging from skin infections to inflammatory conditions, showcasing its significant pharmacological potential.

  • Scientific name: Erythrina addisoniae
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further categorized under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales, falling into the family Fabaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Erythrina, specifically as the species Erythrina addisoniae.

Common Names

This plant, Erythrina addisoniae, is known by various regional names depending on the linguistic context. In English, it is commonly referred to as the coral tree, while in isiZulu, it is known as umkhanyakude. In isiXhosa, the plant is called umkhanyakude, and in Sesotho, it is identified as mofatle.

Distribution

This plant is found throughout specific regions of West Tropical Africa. Its natural range includes the coastal and inland areas of Ghana and Ivory Coast. Furthermore, it is documented to grow within the territories of Liberia and Sierra Leone. The distribution also extends significantly into Nigeria. Consequently, these five nations represent the primary geographical locations for this medicinal species.
