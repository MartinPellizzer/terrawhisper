Hybrid Black Poplar, scientifically known as Populus × canadensis, is a vigorous deciduous tree celebrated for its rapid growth and versatile applications. Beyond its ecological role, its bark contains salicin, a precursor to aspirin, offering traditional medicinal properties for managing pain and inflammation through its potent, natural chemical compounds.

The physiology of Populus × canadensis is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, a mechanism typical of many temperate deciduous trees that involves the direct fixation of carbon dioxide through the Calvin cycle. This pathway facilitates efficient carbon assimilation under moderate light and temperature conditions, supporting the rapid growth rates often observed in this hybrid taxon.
