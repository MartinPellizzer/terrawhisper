Common Yellowwood, scientifically known as Afrocarpus falcatus, is a resilient evergreen tree native to Southern Africa. Renowned for its striking architectural form, it holds significant ethnobotanical value. Various indigenous communities have traditionally utilized its bark and leaves in medicinal preparations to treat ailments such as skin irritations and inflammation.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several key regions of the African continent. In South Tropical Africa, its presence is documented within the borders of Malawi and Mozambique. Moving into the Southern African region, it can be found throughout the Cape Provinces. Additionally, the species is known to inhabit the coastal areas of KwaZulu-Natal. Finally, its range extends to include the territory of Eswatini.
