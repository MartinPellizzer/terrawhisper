What's the taxonomical classification of Cleome rutidosperma?

Cleome rutidosperma belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. It falls under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, following the biological lineage of the order Brassicales. Within this order, the plant is a member of the family Cleomacaceae and is specifically categorized under the genus Cleome, with its unique designation as the species rutidosperma.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Cleome rutidosperma has an erect, herbaceous growth habit characterized by slender, branched stems and palmately compound leaves consisting of five to seven distinct, lanceolate leaflets. The leaves are arranged alternately along the stem and feature small, glandular hairs on their surfaces. Small, fragrant, four-petaled flowers emerge from the leaf axils, displaying white or pale pink coloration. These blossoms are followed by elongated, slender seed capsules that contain numerous tiny, dark seeds. The plant typically reaches a height of up to sixty centimeters, maintaining a delicate and airy structure throughout its life cycle.

HPLC-DAD analysis of the 70% ethanolic extracts identified 10 predominant phenolic constituents. Regarding cytotoxicity, the 70% ethanolic extract of Cleome rutidosperma showed a cell death of ~13%. This value was higher than the control cell death of ~10-12%.
