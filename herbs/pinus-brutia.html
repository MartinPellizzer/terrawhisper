Turkish Pine

Pinus brutia · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Sparse (3 studies) · ★☆☆☆☆

Turkish Pine, scientifically known as Pinus brutia, is a resilient coniferous species native to the Mediterranean region. Renowned for its adaptability to arid climates, it holds significant ethnobotanical value. Its resin, needles, and bark are utilized in traditional medicine for their antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant therapeutic properties.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several key regions across Southeastern and Eastern Europe. In Southeastern Europe, its presence is firmly established in Bulgaria and throughout Greece. It can also be found specifically on the island of Kriti within the same Southeastern European territory. Furthermore, the species is widely distributed across Türkiye-in-Europe. Finally, its range extends into Eastern Europe, where it grows in the region of Krym.

Plant Parts

This plant, Pinus brutia, commonly known as the Turkish pine, possesses various components that are utilized for their therapeutic properties, most notably its bark. The bark serves as a vital part of the tree's structure and is often harvested for its rich concentration of bioactive compounds, such as tannins and essential oils, which contribute to its medicinal applications. While other parts like the resin and needles may also be used, the bark remains a significant element in traditional and pharmacological studies due to its potential antioxidant and anti-inflammatory benefits.
