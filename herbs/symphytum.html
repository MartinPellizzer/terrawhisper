Comfrey, scientifically known as Symphytum, is a perennial herb renowned for its potent healing properties. Traditionally used in herbal medicine, its leaves and roots contain allantoin, which promotes cell regeneration and skin repair. Often applied topically for bruises and sprains, this versatile plant remains a cornerstone of traditional phytotherapy.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as comfrey, is a perennial herb characterized by its large, hairy, and lance-shaped leaves that grow in a basal rosette. As a robust herb, it features a thick, fleshy rhizome that stores essential nutrients and is often the most potent part of the plant used in traditional medicine. The stem is sturdy and covered in fine hairs, supporting a cluster of bell-shaped flowers that typically bloom in shades of purple or cream, while the entire herb is valued for its ability to thrive in moist, nutrient-rich soils.
