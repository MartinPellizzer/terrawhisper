Cornus mas

Cornelian-cherry

Cornus mas, commonly known as cornelian cherry, is a deciduous shrub cherished for its vibrant spring blooms and nutritious red drupes. Traditionally used in herbal medicine, its fruits and bark possess antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and astringent properties, making it a versatile botanical for supporting digestive health and seasonal immunity.

  • Scientific name: Cornus mas
  • Family: Cornaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Cornus mas, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, falling under the order Cornales. Finally, it is a member of the family Cornaceae and the genus Cornus.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Cornelian cherry, offers various parts that are of medicinal and culinary interest. In the spring, the shrub produces delicate yellowish flowers that are among the first to bloom. As the season progresses, it develops small, oval fruits that turn a deep crimson color when ripe. The edible pulp within these drupes is highly valued for its antioxidant properties and tart flavor. Additionally, while the leaves are primarily used for shade or aesthetic purposes, the entire fruit is often processed into syrups or preserves to support digestive health.
