Reproduction

The reproduction of Quercus helferiana is characterized by a sexual reproductive strategy, specifically functioning as a monoeciouis species, while asexual reproduction is noted as absent. The flowering period typically commences in April and concludes in June. During this stage, the flowers exhibit a size range with lengths varying from a minimum of 3 cm to a maximum of 8 cm. The pollination process follows an abiotic syndrome, specifically utilizing wind as the primary mechanism for pollen transfer. Following successful fertilization, the plant produces dry, black-colored fruits. These fruits vary in dimensions, with a minimum length of 1.5 cm and a mean of 1.7 cm, reaching a maximum of 3.5 cm, and a width ranging from 0.8 cm to a maximum of 1.8 cm. The seeds produced are relatively substantial, possessing a mean mass of approximately 2.725 g, with individual seed masses ranging from a minimum of 1.953 g to a maximum of 2.5 g. Finally, the dispersal of the reproductive units is achieved through a zoochorous syndrome, relying on animals to aid in the distribution of the seeds.
