Quercus helferiana

Holm oak

Quercus helferiana, commonly known as Helfer's oak, is a distinctive deciduous tree native to the mountainous regions of Turkey. Characterized by its rugged bark and unique lobed leaves, this resilient species plays a vital role in its ecosystem, offering ecological stability and traditional medicinal potential within its natural habitat.

  • Scientific name: Quercus helferiana
  • Family: Fagaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Fagales and the family Fagaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Quercus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Quercus helferiana, is referred to by various regional names across different languages. In Basque, it is called Arte, while in Polish, it is known as dąb ostrolistny. Among the Quechua-speaking populations, it is identified as Insina, and in Hungarian, it is referred to as magyaltölgy. Finally, in Latvian, the species is known as Akmens ozols.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad and diverse geographical distribution across several key Asian regions. Within China, its presence is noted in the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan areas. Moving toward the Indian Subcontinent, the species can be found in the Assam region. Additionally, it inhabits the rugged terrains of the East Himalaya.
