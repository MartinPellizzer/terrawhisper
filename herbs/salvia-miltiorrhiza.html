Salvia miltiorrhiza

Red sage

Salvia miltiorrhiza, commonly known as the mountain sage, is a perennial herb native to specific high-altitude regions. Renowned for its aromatic foliage and striking blooms, this medicinal plant contains bioactive compounds traditionally used to support respiratory health, reduce inflammation, and provide calming effects within various holistic healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Salvia miltiorrhiza
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Lamiales, falling into the family Lamiaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement within the genus Salvia.

Common Names

This plant, Salvia miltiorrhiza, is recognized by various common names across different languages and cultures. In German, it is known as Rotwurzel-Salbei, while in East Asian traditions, it is widely referred to as 丹参 in both Chinese (zh) and Japanese (ja). Similarly, it is called 단삼 in Korean (ko) and Đan sâm in Vietnamese (vi).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions in Asia. Within China, its presence is noted in the South-Central, North-Central, and Southeast territories. Moving beyond mainland China, the species is also found throughout Eastern Asia, specifically in Korea. Additionally, its range extends into the Indo-China region, where it is documented in Vietnam. Collectively, these locations highlight the diverse environmental settings where the plant thrives.

Plant Parts

This plant, Salvia miltiorrhiza, possesses a complex underground structure consisting of a thick, fleshy rhizome that serves as a vital storage organ. From this central system, the plant develops extensive roots, which are primarily harvested for their medicinal properties. Above the ground, the plant is characterized by its lush, green leaves, which complete its morphological profile.
