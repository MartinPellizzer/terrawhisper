Red sage, scientifically known as Salvia miltiorrhiza, is a perennial herb native to East Asia, valued extensively in traditional medicine. Renowned for its bioactive compounds, it is primarily utilized to improve circulation, support cardiovascular health, and reduce inflammation, offering a natural approach to promoting overall vitality and wellness.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions in Asia. Within China, its presence is noted in the South-Central, North-Central, and Southeast territories. Moving beyond mainland China, the species is also found throughout Eastern Asia, specifically in Korea. Additionally, its range extends into the Indo-China region, where it is documented in Vietnam. Plant Parts

This plant, Salvia miltiorrhiza, possesses a complex underground structure consisting of a thick, fleshy rhizome that serves as a vital storage organ. From this central system, the plant develops extensive roots, which are primarily harvested for their medicinal properties. Among these, the hairy roots are characteristic of the species, adding to the intricate network of the subterranean anatomy. Above the ground, the plant is characterized by its lush, green leaves, which complete its morphological profile. Whether focusing on the primary root or the larger rhizome, each part plays a significant role in the plant's biological function and therapeutic value.
