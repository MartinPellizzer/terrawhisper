Pontederia crassipes

Pontederia crassipes, commonly known as water hyacinth, is a prolific aquatic plant found in tropical freshwater ecosystems. While often labeled an invasive weed, it possesses significant medicinal potential. Traditionally, various parts of the plant are utilized in folk medicine to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and digestive issues.

  • Scientific name: Pontederia crassipes
  • Family: Pontederiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Commelinales, specifically residing in the family Pontederiaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identification is completed by its placement in the genus Pontederia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ponderderia crassipes, is referred to by various common names depending on the language and region. In Catalan, it is called jacint d'aigua, while in Chinese, it is known as 凤眼蓝 (zh), 凤眼蓝 (zh-cn), or 鳯眼藍 (zh-tw). Additionally, in Polish, it is identified as Pontederia gruboogonkowa.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of locations across the European continent. In Middle Europe, its presence can be identified within the regions of Czechia and Slovakia. Moving toward the Mediterranean, it is found in several parts of Southwestern Europe. Specifically, its distribution includes the territories of France and Portugal. Furthermore, the species inhabits the islands of Corse and Sardegna within the Southwestern European zone.
