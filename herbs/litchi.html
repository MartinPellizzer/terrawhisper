Lychee, scientifically known as Litchi, is a tropical fruit celebrated for its juicy, aromatic flesh. Beyond its delicious taste, it offers significant medicinal benefits, including antioxidant properties that support immunity, improve digestion, and promote skin health. This versatile plant serves as both a nutritious snack and a natural remedy.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindales. Finally, it resides in the family Sapindaceae under the genus Litchi.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning across Asia and the Indian Ocean islands. In East Asia, it is found within the provinces of Hainan and China Southeast. Moving toward the Indian Subcontinent, the species is specifically documented in the region of Assam. Its range also extends into the Western Indian Ocean, where it grows in Mauritius. Additionally, the plant can be located on the island of Réunion within the same oceanic region.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as lychee, offers various components that can be utilized for different purposes. The most consumed part is the sweet, translucent pulp, which is widely enjoyed for its flavor and nutritional content. Surrounding this flesh is the pericarp, the leathery red skin that protects the inner fruit. Additionally, the litchi seed located at the center is also of interest, as it contains various bioactive compounds that are often studied for their specific medicinal properties.
