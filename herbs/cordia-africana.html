Large-leaved Cordia, scientifically known as Cordia africanica, is a versatile woody shrub native to tropical Africa. Widely utilized in traditional medicine, its leaves, bark, and roots possess significant therapeutic properties. It is frequently employed to treat inflammation, digestive ailments, and skin conditions, offering valuable pharmacological potential for modern healthcare.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Boraginales and the family Boraginaceae. It is specifically categorized under the genus Cordia as Cordia africana.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad presence across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its distribution includes the nations of Guinea, Mali, and Nigeria. Moving toward the center of the continent, it is also found within West-Central Tropical Africa. Specifically, its range extends into Burundi and the Central African Republic. This diverse geographical spread highlights the plant's ability to thrive in various tropical environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, Cordia africana, is a versatile medicinal species where various components are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The leaf and the broader collection of leaves are frequently harvested to prepare traditional remedies, often processed into a fine leaf powder for topical or internal applications.
