Cordia africana

Cordia africana, commonly known as the African Cordia, is a versatile evergreen tree native to tropical Africa. Highly valued in traditional medicine, its bark, leaves, and roots are utilized to treat ailments ranging from digestive issues to inflammation. This resilient species also holds significant ecological and cultural importance.

  • Scientific name: Cordia africana
  • Family: Boraginaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Boraginales and the family Boraginaceae. It is specifically categorized under the genus Cordia as Cordia africana.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Cordia africana, is referred to by various common names across different languages and regions. In Amharic, it is called ዋንዛ (am), while in Azerbaijani, it is known as Afrika kordiyası (az). In Kinyarwanda, the plant is called Umuvugangoma (rw), and in Arabic, it is identified as سبستان أفريقي (ar). Additionally, in Afrikaans, it is referred to as Grootblaarpieringbessie (af).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad presence across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, its distribution includes the nations of Guinea, Mali, and Nigeria. Moving toward the center of the continent, it is also found within West-Central Tropical Africa. Specifically, its range extends into Burundi and the Central African Republic. This diverse geographical spread highlights the plant's ability to thrive in various tropical environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, Cordia africana, is a versatile medicinal species where various components are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The leaf and the broader collection of leaves are frequently harvested to prepare traditional remedies, often processed into a fine leaf powder for topical or internal applications. Additionally, the roots are highly valued in ethnobotanical practices, contributing to the plant's significant medicinal profile.
