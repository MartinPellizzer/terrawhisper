Davallia formosana

Davalliia formosana, commonly known as the Formosan Rabbit's Foot Fern, is a resilient epiphytic plant prized for its soft, fuzzy rhizomes. While primarily enjoyed as an ornamental species, its unique biological properties offer intriguing potential in traditional ethnobotany, inviting further scientific exploration into its diverse medicinal applications.

  • Scientific name: Davallia formosana
  • Family: Polypodiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Davalliia formosana, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Polypodiinae, it is classified under the order Polypodiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Polypodiaceae and the genus Davallia.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Formosan rabbit's foot fern, features specialized structures that allow it to thrive in its natural habitat. The roots are particularly distinctive, forming thick, fleshy, and hairy rhizomes that resemble animal feet, which help the plant anchor itself to substrates and absorb moisture. These rhizomes extend from the stems, which are relatively short and integrated into the creeping rhizomatous structure, supporting the delicate, feathery fronds that characterize this medicinal fern.
