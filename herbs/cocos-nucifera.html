Coconut palm, scientifically known as Cocos nucifera, is a multipurpose tropical treasure revered for its medicinal properties. Often called the "Tree of Life," every part offers healing benefits. From its hydrating coconut water to its nourishing oils, this versatile plant provides essential nutrients and therapeutic applications in traditional wellness.

Distribution

This plant, Cocos nucifera, exhibits a specific geographical presence across several regions of West Tropical Africa. Within this part of the continent, it can be found growing in the coastal and tropical landscapes of Benin. Its distribution also extends to the nation of Gambia, where environmental conditions support its growth. Furthermore, the plant is documented throughout the territories of Ghana and Guinea-Bissau. Finally, its range reaches into Guinea, rounding out its presence in this West African stretch.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Cocos nucifera is characterized by an abiotic pollination syndrome, primarily driven by wind. The flowering process involves inflorescences that can vary significantly in size, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 0.15 m to a maximum of 18 m, featuring yellow-colored flowers. Self-fertilization is present within the species, facilitating the development of its fruit. The resulting fruits, which exhibit a brown coloration, vary in dimensions with a minimum length of 10 cm and a maximum of 35 cm (averaging 21.25 cm), and a minimum width of 8 cm and a maximum of 20 cm (averaging 14 cm). Each fruit typically contains between 1 and 10 seeds. These seeds are substantial in size, with a minimum length of 100 mm and a mass ranging from a minimum of 167 g to a maximum of 1276 g, with a mean seed mass of 576.8 g. Dispersal is achieved through a hydrochorous syndrome, utilizing water to transport the seeds to new locations.
