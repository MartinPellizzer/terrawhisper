Carica papaya

Papaya

Carica papaya, commonly known as papaya, is a tropical fruit-bearing plant celebrated for its nutritional and medicinal properties. Rich in vitamins A and C, it contains the powerful enzyme papain, which aids digestion and reduces inflammation. This versatile plant serves as a natural remedy for skin health and healing.

  • Scientific name: Carica papaya
  • Family: Caricaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Brassicaleales and the family Caricaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Carica.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Carica papaya, is recognized by a variety of common names across different languages and cultures. In Chinese, it is referred to as 番木瓜 (zh), while in Tibetan, it is called སེ་ཡབ། (bo). In Javanese, the plant is known as katès (jv), and in Polish, it is called melonowiec właściwy (pl). Furthermore, in the Qiang language, it is identified as Papaaya (qu).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution across several key regions. In Macaronesia, it can be found growing in Cape Verde. Its presence extends into West Tropical Africa, where it is documented in Benin. Additionally, the species inhabits the territories of Gambia and Guinea-Bissau. Finally, its range continues through the country of Guinea within the same West Tropical African zone.

Plant Parts

This plant, Carica papaya, is a versatile medicinal resource where almost every component serves a therapeutic purpose. The large, lobed leaves are frequently used in traditional medicine to treat various ailments, often prepared as a decoction or topical application. Within each leaf, bioactive enzymes and compounds are concentrated, making the individual leaf a vital part of many herbal remedies. The fruit itself is highly nutritious and serves as a primary source of vitamins, while the soft, orange pulp is easily digestible and rich in antioxidants. Even the small, black seeds are utilized for their potent antimicrobial and antiparasitic properties, ensuring that no part of the plant goes to waste in holistic healing.
