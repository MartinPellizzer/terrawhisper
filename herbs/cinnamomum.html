Cinnamomum

Cinnamomum, commonly known as cinnamon, is a versatile medicinal plant prized for its aromatic bark and leaves. Renowned for its potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties, it has been used for centuries in traditional medicine to support digestive health, regulate blood sugar, and enhance overall immune system function.

  • Scientific name: Cinnamomum
  • Family: Lauraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. It resides in the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Laurales, falling specifically under the family Lauraceae. Ultimately, the medicinal specimen is identified by its genus, Cinnamomum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several key tropical regions. In West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence is noted around the Gulf of Guinea Islands. Moving to East Tropical Africa, it can be found within the borders of Tanzania. The species also extends into South Tropical Africa, specifically documented in Angola. Finally, its reach includes the Western Indian Ocean, where it inhabits both the Comoros and Mauritius.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as cinnamon, is primarily valued for its medicinal and culinary properties derived from the cortex. The bark, or cortex, is harvested from the inner layer of the stems and branches, where it is carefully peeled and dried to form the characteristic rolled quills used in various treatments. This specific part of the plant contains high concentrations of essential oils and bioactive compounds that contribute to its warming and antimicrobial effects.
