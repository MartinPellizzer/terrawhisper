Bolivian coriander

Porophyllum ruderale · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Moderate (119 studies) · ★★★☆☆

Bolivian coriander, scientifically known as Porophyllum rudeale, is a versatile aromatic herb native to South America. Renowned for its pungent, citrus-like fragrance, it is widely utilized in traditional medicine to treat digestive issues and inflammation. This resilient plant serves as both a flavorful culinary staple and a potent medicinal resource.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread geographical distribution across several distinct tropical and subtropical regions. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is specifically noted within the Ivory Coast. Its range extends to the Western Indian Ocean, where it can be found on the island of Réunion. Within the Malesia region, the species inhabits Jawa as well as the Lesser Sunda Islands. Finally, its distribution reaches East Asia, specifically documented in Southeast China.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as Porophyllum rudeale, is primarily utilized in traditional medicine for the therapeutic properties found within its leaves. These leaves are characterized by their aromatic quality and are often harvested to prepare decoctions or topical applications. Because the medicinal compounds are concentrated in the foliage, the leaves serve as the most essential part of the plant for those seeking its healing benefits.
