Porophyllum ruderale

Bolivian coriander

Porophyllum rudeale, commonly known as Rue, is a versatile medicinal herb native to tropical regions. Renowned for its potent aromatic properties, it is traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat digestive issues, skin ailments, and respiratory discomfort. This resilient plant offers significant bioactive potential for modern pharmacological research and applications.

  • Scientific name: Porophyllum ruderale
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Porophyllum rudeale, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Asterales and the family Asteraceae. It is further identified by its genus, Porophyllum.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Porophyllum ruderale, is referred to by various common names depending on the language and region. In Russian, it is called Боливийский кориандр, while in the Quechua language, it is known as Killkiña. It is also referred to as Porophyllum in Portuguese, Bolivianischer Koriander in German, and Bolivian Coriander in Pamang.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread geographical distribution across several distinct tropical and subtropical regions. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is specifically noted within the Ivory Coast. Its range extends to the Western Indian Ocean, where it can be found on the island of Réunion. Within the Malesia region, the species inhabits Jawa as well as the Lesser Sunda Islands. Finally, its distribution reaches East Asia, specifically documented in Southeast China.
