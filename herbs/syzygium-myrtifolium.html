Often used to treat skin ailments and digestive issues, this botanical treasure offers significant therapeutic potential.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range spanning several distinct ecological regions. Within the Indian Subcontinent, it is specifically documented to grow in Bangladesh. Its distribution extends into the Indo-China region, where it can be found in both Myanmar and Thailand. Furthermore, the species is widely spread across the Malesia zone. In this area, its presence is noted in the islands of Borneo and Jawa.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the water guava, utilizes its aromatic leaves as a primary source of medicinal properties. These leaves are rich in essential oils, tannins, and flavonoids, which contribute to their ability to act as antibacterial, antifungal, and anti-inflammatory agents. In various traditional medicinal practices, the leaves are often crushed or brewed into decoctions to treat ailments such as diarrhea, skin infections, and even to help regulate blood sugar levels.
