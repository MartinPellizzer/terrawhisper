Dalbergia cochinchinensis

Dalbergia cochinchinensis, commonly known as Cochin rosewood, is a significant medicinal plant valued for its therapeutic properties. Historically prized for its rich red heartwood used in traditional dyes, it also contains bioactive compounds with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory potential, making it a subject of interest in ethnopharmacological research and natural medicine.

  • Scientific name: Dalbergia cochinchinensis
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Fabales, it is situated within the family Fabaceae. Specifically, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Dalbergia.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found within the diverse landscapes of the Indo-China region. Its natural habitat extends across several distinct countries, including Cambodia and Laos. Additionally, the species can be identified growing throughout the territories of Thailand. A significant portion of its geographical range is also located within Vietnam. Consequently, these four nations represent the core areas where this medicinal plant is distributed.
