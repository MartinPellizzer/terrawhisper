What's the taxonomical classification of Rhododendron sutchuenense?

Rhododendron sutchuenense belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the taxonomic hierarchy further, it falls under the order Ericales and is a member of the family Ericaceae. Ultimately, this plant is defined by its genus, Rhododendron, and its specific species designation, sutchuenense.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Rhododendron sutchuenense has evergreen leaves that are typically elliptical or oblong in shape, measuring between 5 and 15 centimeters in length, with a leathery texture and a dark green upper surface. The leaf margins are generally entire, though they may occasionally appear slightly revolute. Its flowers are funnel-shaped and typically range in color from pale pink to deep rose, often featuring distinct spotting on the interior of the petals. The plant develops woody, branching stems that form a dense, spreading shrub reaching heights of up to 3 meters. Small, scale-like hairs often cover the young stems and the undersides of the leaves, a characteristic common to many members of this genus.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-draining, acidic soil with a pH between 4.5 and 5.5 and a consistent supply of moisture without waterlogging.

It thrives in partial shade or dappled sunlight to prevent the delicate leaves from scorching under intense heat. Regular mulching is essential to maintain cool root temperatures and retain necessary humidity in the growing medium. For optimal growth, provide consistent organic matter and avoid heavy nitrogen fertilizers that can lead to excessive foliage at the expense of blooms.

During the winter, ensure the plant is protected from harsh, drying winds and extreme frost.
