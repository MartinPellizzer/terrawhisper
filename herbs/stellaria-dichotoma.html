What's the taxonomical classification of Stellaria dichotoma?

Stellaria dichotoma belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Its taxonomic progression continues through the order Caryophyllales and into the family Caryophyllaceae. Ultimately, this specific plant is categorized under the genus Stellaria, with its unique identity defined by the species name dichotoma.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Stellaria dichotoma has an annual growth habit characterized by slender, branched stems that often exhibit a reddish tint and reach heights of up to 15 centimeters. The leaves are small, lanceolate to linear in shape, and arranged oppositely along the stem. Its flowers are white with five distinct petals that are typically deeply notched at the tips. The reproductive structures include central stamens and a superior ovary that eventually develops into small, rounded capsules. These morphological traits allow the plant to thrive in moist, disturbed soil environments.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

Practitioners frequently utilize the root extracts to address digestive issues and inflammatory conditions. By focusing on the root system, healers can access potent bioactive elements not found in the foliage.

According to a study published by "Journal of natural products", two new cyclic peptides named dichotomins J (1) and K (2) have been isolated from the roots of Stellaria dichotoma. These specific compounds were identified through chemical degradation and extensive 2D NMR methods. Both dichotomins J (1) and K (2) demonstrated a moderate vasorelaxant effect on rat aorta. What are the pharmacological activities of Stellaria dichotoma?

This plant has antiallergic and antifungal pharmacological activities that make it a subject of medicinal interest. Research indicates that extracts from Stellaria dichotoma can effectively suppress allergic reactions by modulating immune responses. Additionally, the plant exhibits significant antifungal properties that help inhibit the growth of various pathogenic microorganisms. These biological effects are attributed to the presence of specific bioactive compounds found within its tissues. Such diverse pharmacological capabilities suggest potential applications in developing new therapeutic agents for skin or systemic ailments.

According to a study published by "Journal of natural products", the aqueous ethanolic extract from the roots of Stellaria dichotoma showed an antiallergic effect on ear passive cutaneous anaphylaxis (PCA) reaction in mice and inhibitory activity on the release of beta-hexosaminidase in RBL-2H3 cells. From this extract, new beta-carboline-type alkaloids including dichotomines A (1), B (2), C (3), and D (4) and dichotomides I (5) and II (6) were isolated. Among these, constituent 3 demonstrated inhibitory activity with an IC50 of 62 microM on the release of beta-hexosaminidase in RBL-2H3 cells. Additionally, constituent 3 inhibited the antigen-IgE-mediated releases of TNF-alpha and IL-4 at 19 microM and 15 microM, respectively, in RBL-2H3 cells.

Additionally, the plant possesses soothing properties that mitigate the inflammation caused by allergic reactions. According to a study published by "Natural product research", Stellaia dichotoma L. was investigated for its in vitro antifungal activity against fungi that cause cutaneous diseases. The species demonstrated antifungal activity against all studied fungi, which included Candida species, dermatophytes, and Malassezia furfur. The plant contains rutin in its extracts, which could be responsible for the observed antifungal activity. This study was part of an investigation into the minimal inhibitory concentration of extracts from 14 medicinal plants.
