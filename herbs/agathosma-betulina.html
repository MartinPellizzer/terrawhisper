Agathosma betulina

Agathosma betulina, commonly known as the gumwood, is a resilient shrub native to the Fynbos biome of South Africa. Renowned for its aromatic foliage and resinous bark, this medicinal plant has been traditionally utilized for its antimicrobial properties and its ability to soothe various inflammatory ailments and skin conditions.

  • Scientific name: Agathosma betulina
  • Family: Rutaceae
  • Native range: Africa

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized under the order Sapindales and the family Rutaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Agathosma.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the agarwood or agar tree, derives its most significant medicinal and aromatic value from its essential oils. These precious oils are primarily extracted from the heartwood, particularly when the tree has undergone a natural infection process that triggers the formation of dark, resinous structures. The complex chemical profile of these essential oils provides the plant with its characteristic intense fragrance and is the component responsible for its various therapeutic applications in traditional medicine.
