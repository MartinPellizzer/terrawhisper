Yellow guava, scientifically known as Psidium guajava, is a versatile tropical fruit renowned for its exceptional nutritional profile. Rich in vitamins, minerals, and potent antioxidants, this plant offers significant therapeutic benefits. It is widely utilized in traditional medicine to support digestive health, boost immunity, and combat oxidative stress.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several distinct regions. Within the Macaronesian archipelago, it can be found growing in the Canary Islands, Cape Verde, and Madeira. Additionally, its range extends into West Tropical Africa. Specifically, it is documented in the nations of Burkina and Gambia. Consequently, this species occupies both Atlantic islands and mainland African territories.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Psidium guajava is characterized by a biotic pollination syndrome, primarily driven by insects such as bees. The plant produces white flowers that typically range in length from 1 cm to 1.5 cm. Self-fertilization is present, facilitating the development of its fruit. The resulting fruit is a fleshy, indehiscent berry that serves as the primary vessel for seed dispersal. These berries vary in size, with a mean length of 5.5 cm (ranging from 3 cm to a maximum of 8 cm) and a mean width of 3.5 cm (ranging from 3 cm to a maximum of 5 cm). The dispersal syndrome is predominantly zoochorous, though it also exhibits anthropochorous patterns. Within the fruit, the seeds are numerous and vary in dimensions; seed length ranges from 1 mm to 6 mm, and seed volume ranges from a minimum of 16 mm³ to a maximum of 91 mm³, with a mean volume of 37.4 mm³. The individual seeds are quite small, with a mean mass of approximately 0.0109 g, a minimum mass of 0.00735 g, and a maximum mass of 0.01262 g.
