Reproduction

Reproduction in Ageratina adenophora is primarily achieved through sexual means, as asexual reproduction is absent. The plant features bisexual flowers that are white in color and arranged in a raceme inflorescence, with flower widths reaching a maximum of 0.6 cm. The flowering period typically begins in February and concludes in May, though the timing can be variable. Pollination is a biotic process driven by an insect pollination syndrome. While the plant does not exhibit self-fertilization, it successfully produces fruit in the form of indehiscent black achenes. These fruits are quite small, with a mean length and width of 0.1 cm, a minimum length of 0.15 cm, and a maximum length of 0.18 cm. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing between 101 and 1000 seeds. The seeds themselves are lightweight, with a mean mass of 5.76e-05 g, ranging from a minimum of 3e-05 g to a maximum of 7.4e-05 g. For dispersal, the plant utilizes an anemochorous syndrome, relying on the wind to spread its numerous seeds.
