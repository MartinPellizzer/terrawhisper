Cardamine diphylla

Crinkleroot

Cardamine diphylla, commonly known as two-leaved toothwort, is a charming perennial wildflower native to North American woodlands. Characterized by its distinct paired leaves and delicate white blooms, this plant plays a vital role in forest ecosystems, offering early-season nectar for pollinators and serving as a resilient woodland staple.

  • Scientific name: Cardamine diphylla
  • Family: Brassicaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Brassicales. Finally, it is a member of the family Brassicaceae and falls under the genus Cardamine.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Cardamine diphyllia, is referred to by various names depending on the language and region. In Arabic, it is commonly called حرف ثنائي الورق or حرف عريض الأوراق, while in Egyptian Arabic (arz), it is known as حرف ثنائى الورق. In French, it is identified as Cardamine carcajou, and in Spanish, its common name is Dentaria diphylla.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across parts of North America. In Eastern Canada, it can be found growing in the provinces of New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Ontario, and Québec. Beyond the Canadian border, its range extends into the North-Central United States. Specifically, it is documented as occurring within the state of Illinois. These locations highlight the plant's ability to inhabit diverse environments within these specific regions.
