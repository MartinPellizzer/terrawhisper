Areca catechu

Areca palm

Areca catechu, commonly known as the betel nut palm, is a significant tropical plant valued for its seeds. Traditionally used in many cultures as a mild stimulant, it holds a prominent place in Ayurvedic and traditional medicine. This species is widely cultivated for its diverse pharmacological and socio-economic applications.

  • Scientific name: Areca catechu
  • Family: Arecaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Areca catechu, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Arecales and the family Arecaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by its placement within the genus Areca.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several distinct tropical and subtropical regions. In West Tropical Africa, its presence is noted specifically in Guinea. Moving toward the Western Indian Ocean, it can be found growing in the Comoros. The species also occupies significant portions of East Asia, including Taiwan and the Hainan province of China. Furthermore, its range extends into the South-Central regions of mainland China.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Areca catechu, is primarily valued for its specific reproductive structures. The most significant medicinal and cultural component is the nut, which is actually the hard, stony endosperm found within the seeds. Each individual seed contains a highly concentrated amount of alkaloids, which are derived from the development of the seed embryos during the maturation process. These seeds are contained within the fleshy fruits, which are typically oval-shaped and serve as the protective vessel for the valuable internal components used in traditional medicine.
