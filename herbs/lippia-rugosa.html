Lippia rugosa

Lippia rugosa, commonly known as yerba buena, is a fragrant perennial shrub native to South America. Renowned for its aromatic, mint-like leaves, this medicinal plant is widely utilized in traditional herbalism. It serves as a potent remedy for digestive ailments, respiratory issues, and inflammation, offering diverse therapeutic benefits.

  • Scientific name: Lippia rugosa
  • Family: Verbenaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Lippia rugosa, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Lamiales and the family Verbenaceae. Ultimately, it is categorized under the genus Lippia.

This plant is primarily found across the diverse landscapes of West Tropical Africa. Specifically, its presence is documented within the borders of Benin. It also grows throughout the coastal and inland regions of Gambia. Furthermore, the species can be located in the territories of Guinea-Bissau and Guinea.
