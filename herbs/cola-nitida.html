Cola nitida

Cola nitida, commonly known as the kola nut, is a perennial evergreen tree native to the tropical rainforests of West Africa. Renowned for its caffeine-rich seeds, it holds profound cultural and medicinal significance, used traditionally to boost energy, improve mental alertness, and serve as a vital social stimulant.

  • Scientific name: Cola nitida
  • Family: Malvaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Cola nitida, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Malvales and the family Malvaceae. Finally, it is placed within the genus Cola.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across the diverse landscapes of West Tropical Africa. Its natural range extends significantly through the coastal and inland regions of Guinea and Guinea-Bissau. Furthermore, the species can be found growing within the borders of The Gambia. The distribution continues eastward to include the tropical territories of Ghana. Finally, the plant is also documented as being present in Benin.
