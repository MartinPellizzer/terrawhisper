Cola nitida

What's the taxonomical classification of Cola nitida?

Cola nitida belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows a specific evolutionary lineage that places it under the order Malvales. It is further categorized into the family Malvaceae, which defines its broader botanical relationships. Ultimately, its precise biological identity is established within the genus Cola, with its specific designation being the species nitida.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Cola nitida has a large, perennial woody structure characterized by a dense canopy of dark green, elliptical leaves with prominent pinnate venation. The plant typically grows as a small to medium-sized tree with a straight trunk and smooth to slightly textured bark. Its reproductive structures consist of axillary inflorescences that eventually develop into fleshy, globose fruits. These fruits are often bright red or orange when mature and contain several seeds encased in a pulp. The overall morphology reflects its adaptation to tropical forest environments where dense foliage is essential for light capture.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical rainforest regions of West Africa, specifically spanning across countries such as Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, and Gabon. It thrives primarily within the humid lowland forest zones where high rainfall and consistent temperatures are prevalent. The species is often found in the understory of dense equatorial vegetation where sunlight is filtered. Local populations are frequently distributed along river basins and moisture-rich coastal corridors. These specific environmental conditions dictate its restricted range within the Guineo-Congolian phytochorion.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, Cola nitida, requires a tropical environment with high humidity, consistent rainfall, and well-drained, fertile soil to thrive.

Cultivators typically plant the seeds in shaded nursery beds before transplanting the saplings into permanent field locations. Maintaining a temperature between 20 and 30 degrees Celsius is essential for the healthy development of the tree. Regular pruning and careful moisture management help ensure a productive yield of the kola nuts.

Farmers must also protect the young trees from intense direct sunlight during their initial growth stages.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Cola nitida is utilized medicinally through its caffeine-rich seeds and its stimulant-containing stem bark. The seeds are most commonly harvested to create kola nut products that act as powerful central nervous system stimulants. These seeds are often consumed to increase alertness, reduce fatigue, and suppress appetite. Additionally, the stem bark is employed in various traditional remedies for its therapeutic properties. Practitioners may use the bark to address specific ailments or to provide additional physiological stimulation.

According to a study published by "Pharmacology, biochemistry, and behavior", the fresh cola seed is the part of the plant used for its medicinal properties. The research focused on the effects of sub-chronic treatment with fresh stabilized cola seed extract in male rats. Tests such as the open-field, tail-tap, and resistance to capture showed that cola has behavioral effects similar to caffeine, though they are more gradual. Additionally, cola administration leads to an increase in the fall latency observed during the grasping test. These results suggest that the fresh cola seed possesses psychostimulating properties and an original effect on muscular tonus.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant Cola nitida is a central component of the Côte d'Ivoire pharmacopea where it is traditionally utilized to treat various ailments such as fatigue, digestive issues, and sexual dysfunction. Indigenous healers in West Africa have long employed the kola nut to stimulate the central nervous system through its natural caffeine content. Beyond medicinal purposes, the seeds are frequently used in ritualistic ceremonies to foster social cohesion and honor ancestral spirits. The plant also serves a significant economic role as a staple commodity in local markets across the region. This historical integration into daily life ensures its status as both a cultural symbol and a therapeutic resource.

According to a study published by "Phytotherapy research : PTR", Cola nitida is a member of the Sterculiacea family used within the Côte d'Ivoire Pharmacopea. This plant is utilized as a traditional extract derived from its stem bark. Data from the study showed that treatment with this extract induced ovulation and oestrous cycle blockade at the dioestrous II stage. The extract inhibited the release of gonadotropins, specifically both LH and FSH, acting as an antiestrogen-like substance.

What are the pharmacological activities of Cola nitida?

This plant has psychostimulating, antiestrogen-like, antigonadotropic, and gonadotropin release regulatory pharmacological activities, as well as a notable effect on muscular tonus. Its stimulant properties act on the central nervous system to increase alertness and mental activity. The plant exerts hormonal influences by mimicking or inhibiting estrogenic actions and modulating the endocrine system. Specifically, it can interfere with the natural release of gonadotropins, thereby altering reproductive signaling pathways. Additionally, the chemical constituents of Cola nitida contribute to physiological changes in muscular tonus throughout the body.

According to a study published by "Pharmacology, biochemistry, and behavior", sub-chronic treatment of male rats with extract of fresh stabilized cola seeds shows behavioral effects. Activity tests including the open-field, tail-tap, and resistance to capture demonstrate that fresh cola has an effect on behavior similar to that of caffeine. The effects of cola are more gradual than those observed with caffeine. Furthermore, cola administration leads to an increase in the fall latency observed during the grasping test. These results suggest that the fresh cola seed has psychostimulating properties and an original effect on muscular tonus.

According to a study published by "BioMed research international", the ethanolic extract of Cola nitiida seeds was formulated into solid and liquid oral dosage forms for the treatment of diarrhea. Similarly, the formulated suspension also showed an in vitro release profile of more than 90% after 45 minutes. Both the capsules and the suspension of the Cola nitiida seed extract were successfully formulated as standard dosage forms.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause significant reproductive health issues by blocking the oestrus cycle and decreasing follicle stimulating hormone release. This hormonal disruption often leads to infertility in certain species. While its chemical makeup is sometimes studied for ovulation induction, these effects must be carefully managed to avoid unintended consequences. Additionally, the plant possesses potent psychostimulating properties that can impact the central nervous system. These combined physiological effects make its consumption a complex matter for reproductive and mental health.

According to a study published by "Phytotherapy research : PTR", stem bark extracts of Cola nitida have been shown to block the oestrus cycle of female rats through antigoadotropic activity. The plant contains phyto-anti-oestrogens that exert antifertility actions by interacting with hormone receptors. This crude extract exerted inhibition of ((3)H)-oestradiol or ((3)H)-Organon binding to their respective receptors, though its relative affinities were much lower than those of oestradiol or progesterone. In terms of efficiency, Cola nitida ranked lower than other extracts when competing for the oestrogen receptor. Regarding the progestiin receptor, Cola nitida was identified as the third most potent competitor. These results suggest that the plant extract may interfere with natural oestrogen and/or progestaogen in vivo by binding to steroid receptors.
