Alchornea trewioides

What's the taxonomical classification of Alchornea trewioides?

Alchornea trewioides belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it follows the biological lineage of the order Malpighiales. The plant is further categorized under the family Euphorbiaceae, specifically within the genus Alchornea, and is identified by its unique species name, trewioides.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Alchornea trewioides provides medicinal value through its roots, leaves, and bark. The root is often processed into decoctions to treat various internal ailments and inflammatory conditions. Extracts from the leaves are frequently applied topically or ingested to manage specific symptoms. Additionally, the bark contains bioactive compounds that contribute to its overall therapeutic properties. These different components allow practitioners to utilize the entire structure of the plant for healing.

According to a study published by "Yao xue xue bao = Acta pharmaceutica Sinica", a 95% EtOH extract of the root of Alchornea trewioides was used to study its chemical constituents. Through various chromatography methods, 8 phenolic acids were obtained and identified from the plant. These constituents include 1-O-galloyl-6-O-vanilloyl-beta-glucose (1), gallic acid (2), ethyl gallate (3), syringic acid (4), glucosyringic acid (5), erigeside C (6), 3, 4-dimethoxyphenyl-(6'-O-alpha-L-rhamnosyl)-beta-D-glucopyranoside (7) and 3, 4, 5-trimethoxyphenyl-(6'-O-galloyl)-O-beta-D-glucopyranoside (8). Among these, compound 1 is a new compound, while compounds 4-8 were isolated from the genus Alchornea for the first time. The study also notes that the other compounds were isolated from the plant for the first time.
