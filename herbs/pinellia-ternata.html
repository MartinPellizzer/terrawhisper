Pinellia ternata

Pinellia ternata, commonly known as Ternate Pine, is a distinctive medicinal plant revered in traditional healing practices. Renowned for its unique bioactive compounds, this species offers significant therapeutic potential. Exploring its botanical properties provides vital insights into its role in treating various ailments and its importance in herbal pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Pinellia ternata
  • Family: Araceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further organized into the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Alismatales, falling under the family Araceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Pinelliia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Pinellia ternata, is recognized by various common names across different languages and regions. In Chinese, it is referred to as 半夏 (Bànxià), while in Vietnamese, it is called Bán hạ bắc. Its botanical identity is also expressed as pinelia trójlistkową in Polish and Dreizählige Pinellie in German. Additionally, in Korean, the plant is known as 반하 (Ban-ha).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution that spans across both Europe and Asia. In Middle Europe, its presence is specifically noted within the territories of Austria and Germany. Moving towards the eastern part of the continent, the species is also found in China. Within China, it inhabits several distinct regions including South-Central China and the island of Hainan. Additionally, its range extends further north to include the Inner Mongolia region.

Plant Parts

This plant, Pinellia ternata, possesses a complex underground structure that is essential to its medicinal properties. The primary storage organ is a fleshy, thickened tuber, which is actually a modified underground stem known as a rhizome. This central tuber serves as the main source of active compounds used in traditional remedies. From this base, the plant develops its functional aerial parts, most notably the broad, green leaves that facilitate photosynthesis. While the plant is characterized by this singular, prominent tuber, the overall anatomy relies on the connection between the nutrient-storing rhizome and the leafy canopy above.
