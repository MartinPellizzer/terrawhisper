Digitalis purpurea

Foxglove

Digitalis purpurea, commonly known as foxglove, is a striking biennial herb renowned for its bell-shaped, purple flowers. While aesthetically beautiful, it contains potent cardiac glycosides like digoxin. These compounds are vital in treating heart failure but are highly toxic, requiring precise medicinal application to avoid life-threatening effects.

  • Scientific name: Digitalis purpurea
  • Family: Plantaginaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Digitalis purpurea, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Lamiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Plantaginae within the genus Digitalis.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide presence across several key regions in Northern Europe. Its range specifically includes the coastal and inland areas of Denmark. Furthermore, it is commonly found throughout the landscapes of Great Britain and Ireland. In the northernmost stretches, the species is also well-established within Sweden. Finally, its distribution extends into Middle Europe, where it can be located in Austria.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as foxglove, utilizes its various components for both reproduction and the production of potent cardiac glycosides. The most significant medicinal part is the leaves, which contain the active compounds used in treating heart conditions, though they must be handled with extreme caution due to their high toxicity. For the continuation of its life cycle, the plant produces numerous tiny seeds within elongated capsules, which are dispersed to ensure the growth of new generations in suitable habitats.
