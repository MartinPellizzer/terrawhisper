Papaver rhoeas

Common poppy

Papaver rhoeas, commonly known as the corn poppy, is a striking annual wildflower celebrated for its vibrant scarlet petals. Historically significant in traditional medicine, this resilient plant is recognized for its soothing properties. It grows wild in agricultural fields, adding a splash of brilliant color to many natural landscapes.

  • Scientific name: Papaver rhoeas
  • Family: Papaveraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and order Ranunculales, it is a member of the Papaveraceae family. Specifically, it is categorized under the genus Papaver.

Distribution

This plant is widely recognized for its presence across various parts of Northern Europe. It can be found growing naturally in the coastal and inland regions of Denmark. Its distribution extends further north into the landscapes of Norway and Finland. Additionally, the species is well-established within the islands of Great Britain and Ireland. Together, these locations highlight the specific northern range of Papaver rhoeas.
