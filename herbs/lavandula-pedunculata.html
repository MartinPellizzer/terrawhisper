Lavandula pedunculata

Lavandula pedunculata, commonly known as Pedunculate Lavender, is a distinctive perennial shrub native to the Mediterranean. Characterized by its fragrant, upright lavender spikes and silvery foliage, this aromatic plant is cherished in herbalism for its calming properties, antiseptic qualities, and its ability to soothe skin and ease anxiety.

  • Scientific name: Lavandula pedunculata
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae, the order Lamiales, and the family Lamiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Lavandula.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution spanning several distinct regions. In Southwestern Europe, it can be found growing throughout both Portugal and Spain. Its range extends into Southeastern Europe, specifically within the European portion of Türkiye. Additionally, the species is present in Northern Africa, particularly in Morocco. Finally, its habitat includes the Macaronesian islands, most notably the Canary Islands.

Plant Parts

This plant, Lavandula pedunculata, is primarily valued for its aromatic properties, which are concentrated within its essential oil. This potent oil is extracted from the plant's flowering tops and leaves, capturing the complex volatile compounds that provide its characteristic scent. Beyond the oil itself, the plant's structure consists of slender, woody stems and narrow, silvery-green foliage that supports the fragrant spikes, all of which contribute to the overall therapeutic profile used in various medicinal applications.
