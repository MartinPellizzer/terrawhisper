Crepidiastrum denticulatum subsp. denticulatum

Crepidiascrum denticulatum subsp. denticulatum, commonly known as the "Siam Tulip" or "Curcuma Siamensis," is a versatile medicinal plant native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its striking bracts, this perennial herb is traditionally utilized in herbal medicine to treat inflammatory conditions, digestive ailments, and skin disorders through its bioactive compounds.

  • Scientific name: Crepidiastrum denticulatum subsp. denticulatum
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Asterales, it is classified under the family Asteraceae and the genus Crepidiastrum. Specifically, the medicinal taxon is identified as Crepidiastrum denticulatum subsp. denticulatum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical range across several distinct regions in East Asia. In the Russian Far East, its distribution includes the Amur, Khabarovsk, and Primorye regions. Its presence extends significantly into China, where it can be found throughout the South-Central areas. Additionally, the species is documented to inhabit the Inner Mongolia region of China. These diverse locations highlight the adaptable nature of its natural habitat.
