Korean Pine, scientifically known as Pinus koraiensis, is a resilient coniferous species valued for its diverse therapeutic potential. Renowned for its nutrient-rich seeds and aromatic resins, this plant offers significant medicinal benefits, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties, making it a vital resource in traditional and modern herbal medicine.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several key regions of East Asia. In the Russian Far East, it can be found spanning the Amur, Khabarovsk, and Primorye regions. Its range extends southward into the Manchuria region of China. Additionally, the species is established within the island nation of Japan. Together, these locations define its primary geographical presence in the temperate forests of the East.

The ecology of Pinus koraiensis is primarily defined by its existence in high-altitude mountainous regions, where it serves as a foundational species within temperate coniferous forests. It thrives in well-drained, often acidic soils characteristic of montane terrains, occupying specific elevational belts where moisture availability and temperature regimes are optimal for its growth. These mountainous habitats provide the necessary cool, moist climate and seasonal snow cover required to protect its root systems and support its life cycle.
