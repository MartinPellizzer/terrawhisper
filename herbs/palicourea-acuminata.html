Palicourea acuminata

Palicourrea acuminata, commonly known as "pau-mulato" in Brazil, is a significant medicinal shrub native to the Amazon rainforest. Renowned for its diverse pharmacological properties, it is traditionally used to treat inflammatory conditions, infections, and even certain cancers, making it a subject of intense modern scientific interest and study.

  • Scientific name: Palicourea acuminata
  • Family: Rubiaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Gentianaales, it is classified under the family Rubiaceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Palicourea.

Common Names

This plant, Palicourtea acuminata, is known by various names depending on the region and language. In English, it is commonly referred to as the bitter herb, while in Portuguese, it is known as erva-amarga. In Spanish, it is called hierba amarga, and in French, it is referred to as l'herbe amère.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning from North America through Central America. Within Mexico, it can be found across various regions including the Gulf, the Southwest, and the Southeast. Its presence extends southward into Central America, where it inhabits the country of Belize. Additionally, the species is documented to grow within the territory of Costa Rica. Together, these locations highlight the plant's wide ecological range across these diverse tropical and subtropical landscapes.
