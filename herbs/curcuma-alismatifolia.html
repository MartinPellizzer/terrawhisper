Curcuma alismatifolia

Siam tulip

Curcumma alismatifolia, commonly known as Black Turmeric, is a remarkable medicinal plant prized in traditional Ayurvedic practices. Renowned for its potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, this unique rhizome offers diverse therapeutic benefits. It is widely utilized to support digestive health, enhance immunity, and promote overall holistic wellness.

  • Scientific name: Curcuma alismatifolia
  • Family: Zingiberaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Zingiberales and the family Zingiberaceae. Finally, its taxonomic position is defined by the genus Curcuma, specifically as the species Curcuma alismatiifolia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Curcuma alismatifolia, is recognized by various common names depending on the region and language. In Japan, it is referred to as クルクマ・シャローム, while in Malaysia, it is known as Tulip siam. In Thailand, the plant is called ปทุมมา, and in Vietnam, it is identified as Nghệ lá từ cô. Additionally, in Taiwan, it is commonly called 薑荷花.

Distribution

This plant, Curcuma alismatifolia, is primarily found within the diverse landscapes of the Indo-China region. Its natural geographical distribution spans across several distinct Southeast Asian countries. Specifically, it can be located in the lush environments of Cambodia and Laos. Furthermore, the species is well-established within the territories of Thailand and Vietnam. Together, these nations represent the core areas where this medicinal plant thrives.
