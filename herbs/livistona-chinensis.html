Livistona chinensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Livistona chinensis?

Livistona chinensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Its evolutionary lineage continues through the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic descent through the order Arecales, it is placed within the family Arecaceae. Ultimately, this specific plant is identified under the genus Livistona and is distinguished as the species chinensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Livistona chinensis has a solitary, stout trunk that can reach heights of up to 10 meters and is topped by a dense crown of large, arching, pinnate leaves measuring up to 3 meters in length. The individual leaflets are long, narrow, and lanceolate, characterized by a distinctively waxy, dark green surface. A defining feature of the foliage is the presence of fine, hair-like fibers that emerge from the margins of the leaflets, creating a fringed appearance. The plant produces large, pendulous inflorescences that bear clusters of small, yellowish-white flowers. These flowers eventually develop into fleshy, ovoid fruits that turn a deep blue or black when fully ripe.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the subtropical and tropical regions of southern China and northern Vietnam. It primarily thrives in the humid, evergreen broad-leaved forests found in provinces such as Guangdong, Guangxi, Hainan, and Yunnan. Within these specific habitats, the species favors moist environments and well-drained soils found in hilly or mountainous terrains. Its range is relatively restricted compared to other palm species, remaining concentrated in these specific Southeast Asian ecological zones. Local microclimates in these areas provide the consistent warmth and moisture necessary for its survival.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant thrives in well-draining, humus-rich soil with consistent moisture and bright, filtered sunlight to mimic its natural subtropical forest habitat.

It requires a warm environment with temperatures ideally staying above 50 degrees Fahrenheit to prevent damage. Regular watering is essential, though the substrate must never become waterlogged to avoid root rot. What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Liviston chinensis utilizes its roots and seeds for various medicinal purposes in traditional practices. Similarly, the seeds are processed to extract components used in specific therapeutic formulations. These different parts of the plant offer distinct chemical properties that contribute to its overall healing potential. Practitioners carefully select these specific elements to address diverse health concerns.

According to a study published by "Zhong yao cai = Zhongyaocai = Journal of Chinese medicinal materials", ethyl acetate of alcohol extracts from roots of Livistona chinensis were used to observe growth inhibition in 7 carcinoma cell lines. These cell lines included SGC7901, L1210, P388D1, Hela, hele 7404, B16, and NG108-15. The growth of all tumor cells was not affected by a low dose extract of 0.5 microg/ml. However, the growth of all tumor cells was obviously inhibited by a higher dose extract of 5.0 microg/ml. The results show that these extracts from the roots of Livistona chinensis possess an antitumor role in cell culture.

What are the pharmacological activities of Livistona chinensis?

This plant has demonstrated significant pharmacological activities including anti-angiogenesis, anti-tumor, and anticancer properties. These biological effects contribute to its potential as a therapeutic agent in managing various forms of malignancy. By inhibiting the formation of new blood vessels, the plant helps restrict the nutrient supply required for tumor growth. Its multifaceted anticancer mechanisms work to disrupt the progression of abnormal cell proliferation. Consequently, research into these antitumo-r capabilities continues to explore new ways to combat cancer through natural compounds.

According to a study published by "Zhong yao cai = Zhongyaocai = Journal of Chinese medicinal materials", the ethyl acetate part of alcohol extract from seeds of Livistona chinensis R. Br. can selectively inhibit the proliferation of HT-29 and T24 cell lines with maximum inhibitory rates of 74.66% and 86.52%. This same part can significantly inhibit the secretion of VEGF protein across four tumor cell lines, including K562, SKOV3, HT-29, and T24. The petroleum ether part of the seeds also shows inhibitory effects, though its potency is inferior with maximum inhibitory rates of 43.80% and 38.67%. While the water-soluble and n-butanol parts have no effect on different tumor cell lines, the ethyl acetate part reduces the expression of Flk-1 protein and mRNA of HUVEC induced by VEGF. Consequently, the ethyl acetate part is considered the active part of the alcohol extract from seeds of Livistona chinensis R. Br. due to its fine anti-tumor activity.
