Lycopus americanus subsp. americanus

Lycopus americanus subsp. americanus, commonly known as American Lobelia or Gypsywort, is a perennial herb native to North American wetlands. Traditionally utilized in herbal medicine, it is valued for its cooling properties and its ability to support respiratory health, particularly in managing congestion and soothing inflammatory conditions.

  • Scientific name: Lycopus americanus subsp. americanus
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae in the order Lamiales, falling within the family Lamiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Lycopus.

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several major regions of the country. In Western Canada, it can be found throughout the provinces of Alberta and British Columbia. Plant Parts

This plant, Lycopus americanus subsp. americanus, is characterized by its distinctive foliage, where the leaf serves as a primary morphological feature. The leaf is typically arranged in opposite pairs along the square stem, exhibiting an ovate to lanceolate shape with serrated margins and a pointed tip. Often possessing a slightly hairy surface and a prominent vein pattern, the leaf is not only vital for the plant's photosynthetic processes but also contains the concentrated phytochemicals that contribute to its traditional medicinal properties.
