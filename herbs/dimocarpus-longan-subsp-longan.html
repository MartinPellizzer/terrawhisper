Dimocarpus longan subsp. longan

Dimocarpus longan subsp. longan, commonly known as longan, is a tropical evergreen tree prized for its sweet, translucent fruit. Deeply rooted in traditional medicine, it is valued for its ability to nourish the blood, calm the spirit, and support overall vitality, making it a staple in holistic wellness practices.

  • Scientific name: Dimocarpus longan subsp. longan
  • Family: Sapindaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified within the order Sapindales and the family Sapindaceae. Finally, the specific subspecies longan is identified under the genus Dimocarpus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse range of habitats across several key regions of Asia and the Indian Ocean. In East Asia, its presence is notably established throughout Taiwan. Moving toward the mainland, it can be found extensively within China, specifically in the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan regions. Beyond the Asian continent, its distribution extends into the Western Indian Ocean. Within this oceanic area, the species is documented on the island of Réunion.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as longan (Dimocarpus longan subsp. longan), is primarily valued for its fleshy, translucent arillus, which is the edible portion surrounding the seed. While the entire fruit is consumed, it is specifically this sweet, succulent sac that contains the concentrated nutrients and medicinal properties sought in traditional practices. The surrounding pericarp serves as a protective layer, but it is the juicy, white arillus that defines the fruit's unique texture and provides its characteristic flavor.
