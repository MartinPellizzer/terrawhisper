Scrophularia kakudensis

What's the taxonomical classification of Scrophularia kakudensis?

Scrophularia kakudensis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is positioned within the order Lamiales. It further falls under the family Scrophulariaceae, which contains the genus Scrophularia, and is specifically identified by its species name, kakudensis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Scrophularia kakudensis has erect, unbranched stems that typically reach heights of 10 to 30 centimeters and bear opposite, ovate to lanceolate leaves with serrated margins. The leaves possess thin textures and distinct venation patterns that define their surface structure. Small, tubular flowers emerge from the leaf axils in characteristic clusters. These blossoms exhibit a subtle coloration often featuring yellowish or brownish hues common to the Scrophulariaceae family. The plant's overall architecture is characterized by a compact, herbaceous growth habit suitable for its specific habitat.

According to a study published by "International journal of molecular sciences", Scrophularia kakudensis is an important medicinal plant containing pharmaceutically valuable secondary metabolites. Friable calli were induced from leaf explants cultured on a Murashige and Skoog (MS) medium containing 3.0 mg·L(-1) 6-benzyladenine (BA) in combination with 2 mg·L(-1) 2,4-dichlorophenoxy acetic acid (2,4-D). The study established a cell suspension culture for the first time to produce these metabolites. Methyl jasmonate was shown to elicit the accumulation of total phenols, flavonoids, and acacetin, which is a flavonoid compound with multiple pharmaceutical values. Higher concentrations of methyl jasmonate significantly modulated antioxidant enzyme activities and enhanced free radical scavenging potentials.
