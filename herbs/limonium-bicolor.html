Limonium bicolor

What's the taxonomical classification of Limonium bicolor?

Limonium bicolor belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it is classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and falls into the order Caryophyllales. This plant is a member of the family Plumbaginaceae and is specifically identified within the genus Limonium, ultimately reaching its unique designation as the species bicolor.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the coastal regions of the Mediterranean Sea, specifically spanning across parts of Southern Europe and Northern Africa. It is frequently found growing in saline environments such as salt marshes and sandy seaside cliffs. Its distribution is closely tied to maritime climates where it can tolerate high salt concentrations in the soil. According to a study published by "Zhong yao cai = Zhongyaocai = Journal of Chinese medicinal materials", four compounds were isolated and identified from the hypogeal part of Limonium bicolor as beta-sitosterol (1), gallic acid (2), luteolin (3), and quercetin (4). Additionally, some white grease was obtained from the plant. Nine constituents were identified by GC-MS elementarily from this grease, which include 2,4-dimethyl-heptene (5), ethylbenzene (6), O-xylene (7), styrene (8), hexadecanoic acid etylester (9), 9,12-octodecadienoic acid etylester (10), diisooctyl phthalate (11), alpha-amyrin (12), and beta-amyrin (13). Compound 1 is specifically isolated from Limonium bicolor.

What health conditions is this plant used for?

This plant is used for studying mechanisms of salinity tolerance to mitigate the negative effects of salinity stress on agricultural crops. Researchers examine how Limonium bicolor manages Na+ damage within its cellular structures to maintain metabolic functions. By investigating this species, scientists can better understand the biological pathways that allow certain organisms to survive high levels of salt damage. These studies aim to identify specific genes or traits that contribute to overall salinity tolerance in various environments. Such knowledge is essential for developing resilient plant varieties capable of growing in increasingly saline soils.

According to a study published by "Plant cell reports", Limonium bicolor is used to address salt damage and improve salt tolerance. The study investigated treating seedlings with 300 mM NaCl and different concentrations of 6-BA, specifically 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 mg/L. Results showed that exogenous applications of 1.0 mg/L 6-BA significantly improved plant growth and photosynthesis. This treatment increased cytokinin and auxin contents, as well as K+ and organic soluble matter contents. Additionally, 1.0 mg/L 6-BA increased the activities of SOD, CAT, APX, and POD while decreasing Na+, H2O2, and O2- contents. These mechanisms help reduce the Na+ concentration in leaves to alleviate Na+ damage to the species.
