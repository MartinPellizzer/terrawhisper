What's the taxonomical classification of Carpesium longifolium?

Carpesium longifolium belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is organized under the order Asterales. It is further categorized into the family Asteraceae, which is a large group of flowering plants, and falls under the genus Carpesium. Ultimately, its specific biological identity is defined by its species designation, longifoliuum.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Carpesium longifolium is utilized medicinally through its aerial parts, which include the leaves, stems, and flowers. These above-ground components contain specific bioactive compounds that are harvested for therapeutic purposes. Practitioners often prepare decoctions or infusions from these structures to treat various ailments. The concentration of active constituents can vary depending on the specific stage of the plant's growth cycle. By targeting the aerial portions, herbalists can efficiently collect the necessary medicinal material without disturbing the root system.

According to a study published by "Journal of natural products", the aerial parts of Carpesium longifoliuom are used for medicinal purposes. Three new xanthanolides, labeled 1-3, were isolated from these parts alongside 9 known compounds. The structures of the 3 new compounds were identified as 1beta,4beta-epoxy-5beta-hydroxy-10alphaH-xantha-11(13)-en-12,8beta-olide (1), 1beta,4beta,4alpha,5beta-diepoxy-10alphaH,11alphaH-xantha-12,8beta-olide (2), and 4-acetoxy-1beta,5beta-epoxy-10alphaH-xantha-11(13)-en-12,8beta-olide (3). These constituents demonstrated significant cytotoxic activity against 2 specific cultured cell lines, namely SMMC-7721 (human hepatoma) and HO-8910 (human ovarian carcinoma).
