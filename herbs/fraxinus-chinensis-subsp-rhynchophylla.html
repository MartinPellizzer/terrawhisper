Fraxinus chinensis subsp. rhynchophylla

Fraxinus chinensis ssp. rhynchophylla

Fraxinus chinensis subsp. rhynchophylla, commonly known as Chinese Ash, is a significant medicinal plant in traditional herbal practices. Renowned for its therapeutic properties, various parts of this subspecies are utilized to treat inflammation, fever, and skin ailments. Its bioactive compounds offer valuable insights into modern pharmacological research and wellness.

  • Scientific name: Fraxinus chinensis subsp. rhynchophylla
  • Family: Oleaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales, it is classified under the family Oleaceae and the genus Fraxinus. Specifically, the medicinal species is identified as Fraxinus chinensis subsp. rhyncophylla.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range spanning across several distinct regions in East Asia. Within the Russian Far East, its presence is specifically noted in the Primorye region. In China, the species can be found throughout the territories of Inner Mongolia and Manchuria. Furthermore, its distribution extends into the China North-Central areas. Finally, the plant is also documented to grow within the China Southeast region.

Plant Parts

This plant, Fraxinus chinensis subsp. rhyncophylla, is valued in traditional medicine primarily for its therapeutic properties derived from its various bark components. The medicinal application typically focuses on the stem bark, which is harvested for its bioactive compounds. For pharmaceutical and herbal preparations, the dried stem barks are processed to ensure stability and potency during use.
