What's the taxonomical classification of Scorzonera divaricata?

Scorzonera divaricata belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this group, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following the hierarchical structure of botanical classification, it falls under the order Asterales and is a member of the family Asteraceae. Finally, the plant is identified by its specific genus, Scorzonera, and its unique species designation, divaricata.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Scorzonera divaricata has a perennial growth habit characterized by a woody caudex and stems that are typically erect or ascending, often reaching heights of 10 to 30 centimeters. The leaves are primarily basal and arranged in rosettes, possessing an oblong to lanceolate shape with entire or slightly dentate margins. Along the stems, the leaves become smaller and more sparse, exhibiting a somewhat succulent or leathery texture adapted to arid environments. The inflorescence consists of bright yellow ligulate flowers that emerge from terminal heads, each containing numerous individual florets. These flowering structures are supported by involucral bracts that are often multi-layered and slightly hairy.

This plant Scorzonera divaricata is utilized medicinally through the use of its aerial parts, which encompass the stems, leaves, and flowers. The chemical constituents found within these aerial parts contribute to the plant's traditional role in supporting digestive and respiratory health. Practitioners typically collect the vegetation during its flowering stage to ensure maximum potency of its active compounds. This specific focus on the aerial parts allows for efficient gathering and processing of the medicinal material.

According to a study published by "Journal of natural products", the aerial parts of the Mongolian medicinal plant Scorzonera divaricata were used to obtain a crude extract. Chromatographic separation of this extract yielded two new quinic acid derivatives named feruloylpodospermic acids A and B. For feruloylpodospermic acid A, the dihydrocaffeiic acid substituents were found esterified at positions 1 and 5 of the quinic acid moiety, while the feruloyl group was attached at position 3. In feruloylpodospermic acid B, the substituents were linked at positions 1, 3, and 4. These two new quinic acid derivatives exhibited strong antioxidative activity when analyzed in the DPPH (2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl) assay.
