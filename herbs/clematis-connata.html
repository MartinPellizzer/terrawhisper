Clematis connata

What's the taxonomical classification of Clematis connata?

Clematis connata belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic descent, it is placed in the order Ranunculales and falls within the family Ranunculaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by its specific genus, Clematis, and its unique species designation, connaata.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Clematis connata has woody, climbing stems that utilize petioles to scramble over surrounding vegetation. The leaves are typically trifoliate or pinnate with ovate to lanceolate leaflets that feature serrated margins. Its inflorescence consists of solitary or small clusters of star-shaped flowers characterized by distinct, spreading tepals. The floral coloration often displays subtle variations of cream or pale yellow hues depending on the specific specimen. After flowering, the plant produces characteristic achenes that are attached to feathery, persistent styles used for wind dispersal.
