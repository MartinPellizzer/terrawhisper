Mallotus japonicus

Mallotus japonicus, commonly known as Kamala, is a versatile medicinal shrub native to Southeast Asia. Renowned for its unique reddish-brown powder, it possesses significant antimicrobial, insecticidal, and anti-inflammatory properties. Traditionally used to treat skin infections and intestinal parasites, this plant offers promising therapeutic potential in modern pharmacology.

  • Scientific name: Mallotus japonicus
  • Family: Euphorbiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Mallotus japonicus, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Malpighiales and the family Euphorbiaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Mallotus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide distribution across several regions in Eastern Asia. In China, its presence is specifically noted within the Southeast portion of the country. Its range extends significantly through the island nation of Japan, including the Nansei-shotō archipelago. The species is also found throughout the Korean Peninsula. Finally, its geographical reach encompasses the island of Taiwan.
