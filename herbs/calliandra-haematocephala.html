What's the taxonomical classification of Calliandra haematocephala?

Calliandra haematocephala belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Its hierarchical progression continues through the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, eventually placing it in the order Fabales. As a member of the family Fabaceae, it is further categorized into the genus Calliandra, with its specific identification being the species haematocephala.

According to a study published by "Applied and environmental microbiology", Calliandra haematocephala is a tropical legume known to contain large amounts of rare nonprotein imino acids in its leaves and seeds. Four saprophytic and pathogenic fungi, including Aspergillus niger, Aspergillus sp., Curvularia sp., and Penicillium sp., were isolated from the leaf surface of the plant. These fungi were tested for susceptibility to leaf extracts of the host plant and to specific imino acids including proline, pipecolic acid, cis-5-hydroxypipecolic acid, and 2,4-trans-4,5-cis-4,5-dihydroxypipecolic acid. The study measured fungal spore germination and germ tube growth to determine the effects of these substances. Data indicate that imino acids may play a role in the specific resistance of Calliandra spp. to Aspergillus sp.
