Dill, scientifically known as Anethum graveolens, is a versatile aromatic herb celebrated for both its culinary and medicinal properties. Rich in essential oils, it is traditionally used to soothe digestive issues, alleviate bloating, and reduce inflammation. This fragrant plant serves as a potent natural remedy in holistic wellness practices.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as dill (Anethum graveolens), is characterized by several distinct morphological features used in both botanical study and herbal medicine. Its delicate, feathery green leaves are highly aromatic and frequently utilized for culinary and therapeutic purposes. The plant produces characteristic inflorescences in the form of flat-topped umbels, which consist of numerous small flowers arranged in a radiating pattern. Following flowering, the plant develops small, dry fruits that are technically schizocarps, which are harvested as seeds for their potent essential oils. While not as prominent as in other medicinal species, certain parts of the plant can also contain trace amounts of resins that contribute to its complex chemical profile.
