Anethum graveolens

Anethum graveolens, commonly known as dill, is a fragrant annual herb celebrated in both culinary and medicinal traditions. Renowned for its delicate, feathery leaves and aromatic seeds, it is widely used to soothe digestive issues, reduce bloating, and support overall gastrointestinal health through its potent, natural bioactive compounds.

  • Scientific name: Anethum graveolens
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it is organized into the order Apiales and the family Apiaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Anethum, which includes the species Anethum graveolens.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across several regions within Northern Europe. It can be found growing naturally throughout the coastal and inland areas of Denmark. Furthermore, its distribution extends northward into the landscapes of Finland and Norway. The species is also well-established within the British Isles, specifically in Great Britain and Ireland. Together, these locations highlight the plant's significant range across the northern reaches of the continent.
