What's the taxonomical classification of Iris lactea?

Iris lactea belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is further categorized under the order Asparagales. It is a member of the family Iridaeceae, falling into the genus Iris, and is specifically identified by its species name, lactea.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Iris lactea has lanceolate, glaucous leaves that grow in a basal rosette and slender, pale lavender to white flowers with distinct yellow markings on the falls. The foliage is characterized by a waxy, bluish-green coating that helps the plant retain moisture in arid environments. Each flower features three upright petals known as standards and three drooping petals called falls, which serve to guide pollinators toward the nectar. The plant's rhizomes are thick and creeping, allowing it to spread slowly across the soil surface. These sturdy underground structures also act as storage organs for nutrients during dormant periods.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires well-draining sandy or loamy soil, full sun to partial shade, and a period of summer dormancy to thrive.

During the growing season, provide consistent moisture but ensure the roots never sit in standing water to avoid rot. Once the plant enters its dormant phase in the summer, reduce watering significantly to mimic its natural habitat.
