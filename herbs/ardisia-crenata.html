Physiology

The physiology of Ardisia crenata is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, indicating a metabolic process optimized for environments where carbon dioxide fixation occurs via the Calvin cycle. The plant's foliage exhibits significant morphological variation, with leaf lengths ranging from a minimum of 4.5 cm to a mean of 8.625 cm, reaching a maximum of 15 cm. Correspondingly, leaf widths vary from 1.2 cm to a maximum of 4 cm, with an average width of 3 cm. The leaves are elliptic-lanceolate in form and feature crenate margins, lacking both thorns and spines. From a resource-use perspective, the plant maintains a consistent Specific Leaf Area (SLA) of 177.2229779 cm²/g across its minimum, mean, and maximum values, reflecting a stable leaf mass per unit area. Furthermore, Ardisia crenata does not function as a nitrogen fixer, relying instead on external soil nutrient availability for its physiological development.

The reproduction of Ardisia crenata is characterized by a versatile flowering period that can occur throughout the entire year, with a notable flowering start in December, though the timing remains variable across the months of January through November. The plant produces inflorescences that typically range in length from a minimum of 0.04 m to a maximum of 0.16 m. Its reproductive success is further supported by the production of fleshy fruits, which facilitates a zoochorous dispersal syndrome, relying on animals for the distribution of its seeds. The seeds themselves are uniform in their documented measurements, maintaining a consistent seed mass of 0.221 g (with minimum, maximum, and mean values all recorded at this level) and a seed volume of 99 mm³ across minimum, maximum, and mean parameters.
