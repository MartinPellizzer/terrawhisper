Oxytropis chiliophylla

Oxytropis chiliophylla, commonly known as Chilean Oxytropis, is a resilient perennial herb native to the rugged landscapes of Chile. Valued in traditional ethnobotany, this plant belongs to the Fabaceae family. It is studied for its unique phytochemical profile, offering potential medicinal properties within its specialized, high-altitude ecological niche.

  • Scientific name: Oxytropis chiliophylla
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fabales, it is classified under the family Fabaceae. It is further identified by the genus Oxytropis, specifically as the species Oxytropis chiliophylla.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Oxytropis chiliophylla, is referred to by several different common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In Chinese, it is commonly known as 臭棘豆, 达夏, or 轮叶棘豆. Specifically, within mainland China, the name 臭棘豆 is used (zh-cn), while the name 臭棘豆 is also the standard designation in Taiwan (zh-tw).

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several diverse regions of Asia. In Central Asia, it can be found growing within the borders of Tajikistan. Its range extends into Western Asia, where it is documented in Afghanistan. Moving toward the East, the species is also native to the vast landscapes of Mongolia. Finally, it populates parts of the Indian Subcontinent, specifically within India and Pakistan.
