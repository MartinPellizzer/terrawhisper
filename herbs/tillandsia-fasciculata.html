Tillandsia fasciculata

Tillandsia fasciata, commonly known as the banded air plant, is a captivating epiphytic species native to tropical regions. While primarily prized by horticulturists for its striking silver foliage, it holds traditional significance in ethnobotany. This resilient plant thrives on moisture and air, offering unique biological insights into medicinal ecosystems.

  • Scientific name: Tillandsia fasciculata
  • Family: Bromeliaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Poales, it is classified under the family Bromeliaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Tillandsia.

This plant is primarily distributed across the Southeastern United States, with a significant presence in Florida. Its range extends deep into Mexico, where it can be found throughout the central regions. Plant Parts

This plant, Tillandsia fasciiculata, is characterized by its unique morphology, where the leaves serve as the primary interface for nutrient and moisture absorption. As an atmospheric epiphyte, its leaves are typically stiff, succulent, and covered in a dense layer of specialized silvery scales called trichomes, which allow the plant to capture water and minerals directly from the air and rainfall. These leaves are often arranged in a tight, rosette-like formation, helping to funnel moisture toward the center of the plant to support its survival in various environments.
