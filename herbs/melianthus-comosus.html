Touch-me-not, scientifically known as Melianthus comoesus, is a striking perennial shrub native to South Africa. Characterized by its large, serrated leaves and unique, nectar-rich red flowers, it is highly ornamental. While celebrated for its beauty, it contains toxic compounds, requiring careful handling and consideration in medicinal applications.

Distribution

This plant is primarily found across various regions within Southern Africa. Its distribution includes the diverse landscapes of the Cape Provinces. Plant Parts

This plant, known as the Honey Bush, features distinctive leaves that are a primary component of its physical structure. The leaves are typically large, deeply lobed, and possess a somewhat leathery texture, often displaying a dark green color that contrasts with their intricate, serrated margins. In the context of its medicinal use, these leaves are often the focus of interest, as they contain the specific phytochemical compounds sought after for their various therapeutic properties.
