Quercus robur

English oak

Quercus robur, commonly known as the English oak, is a majestic deciduous tree revered for its longevity and strength. Beyond its ecological significance, it holds profound medicinal value. Its bark, rich in tannins, has traditionally been used for its potent astringent, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties in various herbal remedies.

  • Scientific name: Quercus robur
  • Family: Fagaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fagales, it is classified under the family Fagaceae and the genus Quercus.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across various regions of Northern Europe. In the western reaches of the continent, it can be found growing throughout Great Britain and Ireland. Moving toward the Scandinavian Peninsula, its presence is established in both Norway and Finland. Additionally, the species is commonly documented within the borders of Denmark. Collectively, these locations highlight the plant's strong affinity for northern temperate climates.

Plant Parts

This plant, Quercus robur, commonly known as the English oak, offers various components utilized in traditional medicine, most notably its cortex. The bark, or cortex, is highly valued for its rich tannin content, which provides astringent properties used to treat inflammatory conditions. In addition to the bark, specific growths known as Knopper galls can be harvested from the tree. These Knopper Gall structures, which are abnormal growths induced by certain insects, are also exceptionally high in tannins and are frequently employed in herbal preparations to support digestive and skin health.
