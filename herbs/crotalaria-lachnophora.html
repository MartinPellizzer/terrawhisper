Crotalaria lachnophora

Crotalaria lachnophora, commonly known as rattlepod, is a versatile medicinal herb widely used in traditional Ayurvedic and folk medicine. Renowned for its potent pharmacological properties, it effectively treats skin diseases, inflammation, and respiratory ailments. This plant serves as a vital resource in herbal remedies across various tropical regions.

  • Scientific name: Crotalaria lachnophora
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Fabales and the family Fabaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Crotalaria, specifically as the species Crotalaria lachnophora.

Common Names

This plant, Crotalaria lachnophora, is known by various common names depending on the region and language. In English, it is frequently referred to as rattlepod or cobbler's pegs. Within the Indian subcontinent, it holds significant cultural and medicinal importance, being known as Rattlepod in English, Gunda in Hindi, and Gunda in Telugu. Additionally, it is identified as Gunda in Kannada and Gunda in Malayalam, reflecting its widespread presence and traditional use across different linguistic landscapes.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution concentrated within the West Tropical Africa region. It can be found growing across several distinct countries in this part of the continent. Specifically, its presence is documented in Benin and Burkina Faso. The species also extends its range into Ghana and the Ivory Coast. Finally, its distribution includes the territory of Nigeria.
