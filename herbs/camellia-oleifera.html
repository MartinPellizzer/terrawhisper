Camellia oleifera

Camellia oleifera, commonly known as the tea oil plant, is a versatile evergreen shrub native to East Asia. Renowned for its nutrient-rich seeds, it produces premium tea seed oil prized in culinary and medicinal applications. This botanical powerhouse offers significant antioxidant properties, supporting skin health and overall wellness naturally.

  • Scientific name: Camellia oleifera
  • Family: Theaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Ericales, it is classified under the family Theaceae and the genus Camellia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Camellia oleifera, is recognized by various common names across different languages and regions. In Chinese, it is referred to as 油茶 in Simplified Chinese (zh-hans), Traditional Chinese (zh-hant), and in the Hong Kong dialect (zh-hk). In Poland, it is called Kamelia olejodajna (pl), while in Russia, it is known as Камелiя масличная (ru).

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across several distinct regions within China. It can be found throughout China South-Central and the coastal areas of China Southeast. Additionally, its natural range extends to China North-Central and the tropical island of Hainan. Beyond the Chinese borders, the species also inhabits parts of the Indian Subcontinent, specifically in Assam. These diverse locations highlight the broad geographical footprint of this medicinal species.

Plant Parts

This plant, Camellia oleifera, is highly valued for its diverse botanical components that contribute to its medicinal and economic utility. The stem serves as the structural foundation of the plant, supporting the growth of tender branches that are often studied for their bioactive properties. The most significant part of the plant is the fruit, which yields high-quality seed oils that are rich in essential fatty acids. Through specialized processing, these are refined into pure tea oil, a substance prized for its nutritional and therapeutic benefits. Additionally, the production process yields defatted seeds, which remain a valuable byproduct that can be utilized in various medicinal or industrial applications.
