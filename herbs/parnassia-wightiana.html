Parnassia wightiana

What's the taxonomical classification of Parnassia wightiana?

Parnassia wightiana belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, the plant is organized under the order Celastrales and the family Celastraceae. Finally, it is identified within the genus Parnassia, specifically as the species wightiana.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Parnassia wightiana has a basal rosette of ovate to lanceolate leaves that are relatively thin and lack prominent venation. The inflorescence consists of a single, terminal flower held atop a slender, erect scape. This flower features five white petals characterized by prominent, dark, nectar-secreting glands located near the center. The reproductive structures include a central gynoecium surrounded by several stamens that facilitate pollination. As the plant matures, the flower eventually gives way to a small, capsule-like fruit containing numerous seeds.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Parnassia wightiana is used medicinally through the application of its root. The root is typically harvested and processed to extract specific bioactive compounds. These components are often utilized in traditional remedies to treat various ailments. Practitioners may prepare the root in different forms depending on the intended therapeutic effect. This specific part of the plant serves as the primary source for its healing properties.

According to a study published by "BMC plant biology", Parnassia wightiana Wall. is a traditional medicinal herb with potential for developing eco-friendly botanical insecticides. Different plant tissues were evaluated, and root extracts exhibited the highest insecticidal efficacy against third-instar Mythimna separata larvae. These root extracts showed a median lethal concentration (LC50) of 19.659 mg/mL. Through bioactivity-guided isolation, Celahin B was characterized as a potent insecticidal sesquiterpenoid. This specific compound showed remarkable toxicity against M. separata larvae with an LC50 of 116.642 µg/mL.
