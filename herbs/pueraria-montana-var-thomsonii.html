Pueraria montana var. thomsonii

Pueraria montana var. thomsonii, commonly known as Bengal Kudzu, is a versatile climbing legume celebrated in traditional medicine. Rich in isoflavones and bioactive compounds, this plant is widely utilized for its anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and sedative properties, offering significant therapeutic potential for managing various neurological and metabolic health conditions.

  • Scientific name: Pueraria montana var. thomsonii
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified within the order Fabales and the family Fabaceae. Finally, it is identified under the genus Pueraria as the variety thomsonii.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range across several key regions in East Asia. Within China, its distribution is quite extensive, spanning from the South-Central provinces to the southeastern territories. It can also be found in the tropical landscapes of Hainan and the high-altitude regions of Tibet. Beyond mainland China, the species extends its reach to the Nansei-shoto islands in Eastern Asia. Together, these locations highlight the varied ecological niches occupied by this medicinal variety.
