Boneset, scientifically known as Eupatorium perfoliatum, is a perennial herb native to North America, renowned for its historical medicinal uses. Traditionally employed to treat fevers, influenza, and various inflammatory conditions, this plant is easily identified by its distinctive leaves that appear to clasp directly onto the stem.

Physiology

The physiology of Eupatorium perfoliatum is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, which governs its carbon fixation processes and metabolic efficiency. In this C3 cycle, the initial fixation of atmospheric carbon dioxide occurs through the enzyme Rubisco, resulting in the direct formation of a three-carbon compound, 3-phosphoglycerate. This metabolic strategy dictates the plant's water-use efficiency and its physiological response to varying light intensities and temperature gradients within its natural habitat. Because it lacks the specialized Kranz anatomy associated with C4 plants or the nocturnal acid accumulation of CAM metabolism, its gas exchange and transpiration rates are closely synchronized with daytime stomatal activity to maintain optimal photosynthetic output.
