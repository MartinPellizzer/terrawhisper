Gouania microcarpa var. microcarpa

Gouania microcarpa var. microcarpica, commonly known as the "sweet gum" or "sweet bark," is a medicinal shrub native to West Africa. Renowned for its high saponin content, this plant is traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat various ailments, ranging from skin conditions to respiratory and inflammatory issues.

  • Scientific name: Gouania microcarpa var. microcarpa
  • Family: Rhamnaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Tropical

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Following its placement in the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Rosales, it is further categorized under the family Rhamnaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Gouania as the specific variety Gouania microcarpa var. microcarpa.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical distribution across several regions within the Indian Subcontinent. It is documented to grow within the country of India and is also found in the state of Assam. Furthermore, its presence extends to the island nation of Sri Lanka. The species can also be located in the mountainous terrain of the West Himalaya. Beyond the Indian Subcontinent, its range reaches into the Indo-China region, specifically in Myanmar.
