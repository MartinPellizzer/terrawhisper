Cymbopogon schoenanthus subsp. proximus

Cymbopogon schoenanthus subsp. proximus, commonly known as lemongrass or lemon grass, is a fragrant perennial herb valued for its medicinal properties. Rich in essential oils like citral, it is widely utilized in traditional medicine to treat digestive issues, reduce inflammation, and provide soothing aromatherapy for stress relief and relaxation.

  • Scientific name: Cymbopogon schoenanthus subsp. proximus
  • Family: Poaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Poales and family Poaceae, it is classified under the genus Cymbopogon. Specifically, it is identified as the subspecies proximus of the species Cymbopogon schoenanthus.

Plant Parts

This plant, Cymbopogon schoenanthus subsp. proximus, is characterized by its robust growth pattern, beginning with vigorous sprouts that emerge from the base to establish new clusters. As the plant matures, it develops elongated, grass-like shoots that extend upward, forming dense tufts. The primary components utilized for medicinal purposes are the green aerial parts, which encompass the leaves and stems above the ground. Within these aerial parts, the plant synthesizes high concentrations of essential oils, which provide its distinct aromatic profile and contain the bioactive compounds necessary for its therapeutic properties.
