Chinese Ephedra

Ephedra sinica · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (874 studies) · ★★★★★

Chinese Ephedra, scientifically known as Ephedra sinica, is a perennial shrub native to the arid regions of China. Renowned in traditional medicine, it contains bioactive alkaloids like ephedrine. These compounds are widely studied for their potent stimulant effects on the central nervous and respiratory systems, impacting various physiological functions.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Gnetidae, it is placed in the order Ephedrales and the family Ephedraceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Ephedra, specifically the species Ephedra sinica.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across specific regions of Northern Asia. In the territory of Siberia, it can be found in both Buryatiya and Chita. Its range extends into the Russian Far East, specifically within the Primorye region. Within China, the species is located throughout Inner Mongolia. Finally, it is also widely distributed across the Manchuria region of China.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Ephedra sinica, is a perennial herb that is primarily valued for its medicinal properties. The structure of the plant is characterized by its unique stems, which are slender, green, and photosynthetic in nature. While the stem serves as the main axis for growth, the plant also produces distinct yellow stalks that can be observed during certain stages of its development. Although much of the medicinal value is concentrated in the aerial parts, the roots also contribute to the overall biology of this desert-dwelling species.
