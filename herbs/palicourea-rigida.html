Palicourea rigida

Palicourtea rigida, commonly known as "erva-de-rato," is a significant medicinal shrub native to South American rainforests. Renowned for its potent bioactive compounds, particularly indole diterpenes, it is traditionally used to treat malaria and various infections. Modern pharmacological research continues to explore its diverse therapeutic potential and biological activity.

  • Scientific name: Palicourea rigida
  • Family: Rubiaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Gentianaales, it is classified under the family Rubiaceae. It is further identified by the genus Palicourea, which distinguishes this specific medicinal species.

Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across several regions in Northern South America. Specifically, it can be found growing within the territories of French Guiana, Guyana, and Suriname. Its presence also extends into the country of Venezuela. Beyond the northern reaches, the species is located in Western South America.
