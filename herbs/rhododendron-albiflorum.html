Rhododendron albiflorum

Rhododendron albiflorum, commonly known as the white-flowered rhododendron, is a captivating shrub cherished for its striking, snowy blooms. Native to specific high-altitude regions, this medicinal plant is valued in traditional practices for its unique bioactive properties, offering potential therapeutic benefits in treating various ailments through its potent natural compounds.

  • Scientific name: Rhododendron albiflorum
  • Family: Ericaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Ericales, falling into the family Ericaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Rhododendron as the species Rhododendron albiflorum.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Rhododendron albiflorum, is referred to by various common names depending on the language. In Bulgarian, it is called белоцветен рододендрон, while in Russian, it is known as Рододендрон белоцветковый. In Arabic, the species is called جلسان أبيض, and in French, it is commonly referred to as Azalée blanche. Finally, in German, it is identified as Azaleastrum albiflorum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread distribution across several regions of Western North America. In Western Canada, it can be found growing within the provinces of Alberta and British Columbia. Its range extends southward into the Northwestern United States as well. Specifically, it inhabits the mountainous terrains of Idaho and Montana. Additionally, this medicinal species is located as far south as Colorado.
