Peruvian pepper

Schinus molle · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (397 studies) · ★★★★★

Peruvian pepper, scientifically known as Schinus molle, is a versatile evergreen tree native to the Andes. Renowned for its aromatic foliage and pink peppercorns, it offers significant medicinal value. Traditionally used to treat respiratory ailments, inflammation, and digestive issues, this resilient plant remains a vital resource in ethnobotany.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across several distinct regions of the Mediterranean. In Southwestern Europe, it can be found growing in Portugal and Spain. Its distribution also extends to the island of Sardegna within the same part of the continent. Moving toward Southeastern Europe, the species is established in Greece. Finally, its geographical reach includes the territory of Italy.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the Peruvian peppertree, offers various components that are utilized for their medicinal properties. The whole plant is often studied for its diverse therapeutic potential, with specific attention given to the aromatic leaves. Each individual leaf is pinnate and contributes to the plant's characteristic scent, which is also found in the essential oil extracted from the specimen. Furthermore, the trunk bark resin is a notable feature used in traditional applications, while the small, pungent seeds are frequently harvested for their distinct chemical properties.
