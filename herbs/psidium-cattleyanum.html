Psidium cattleianum, scientifically known as Psidium cattleyanum, is a versatile evergreen shrub valued for its medicinal properties. Native to Brazil, its fruits and leaves are rich in antioxidants and bioactive compounds. Traditionally, it is used to support digestive health, reduce inflammation, and provide essential vitamins for holistic wellness.

Reproduction

Reproduction in Psidium cattleianum is driven by bisexual flowers that exhibit a white coloration. These flowers vary in length, ranging from a minimum of 0.3 cm to a maximum of 0.8 cm. The pollination process is characterized by a biotic syndrome, specifically utilizing insects to facilitate the transfer of pollen; notably, self-fertilization is absent, ensuring genetic diversity through cross-pollination. Following successful fertilization, the plant produces fleshy, indehiscent fruit classified as a berry. These fruits have an average width of 0.3 cm and serve as the vessel for a high volume of seeds, with each fruit containing more than 1000 seeds. The seeds themselves are quite small, with a mean mass of 0.012 g and a mean length of 3.431 mm (ranging from 3.431 mm to 5 mm). In terms of volume, the seeds vary from a minimum of 0.42 mm³ to a maximum of 3.7 mm³, with a mean volume of 2.06 mm³. The dispersal of these seeds is zoochorous, specifically employing an endozoochorous strategy, where animals consume the fleshy fruit and disperse the seeds through their digestive tracts.
