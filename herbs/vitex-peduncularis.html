Vitex peduncularis

What's the taxonomical classification of Vitex peduncularis?

Vitex peduncularis belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it falls under the order Lamiales. Its taxonomic lineage continues through the family Lamiaceae, where it is placed within the genus Vitex, ultimately reaching its specific designation as the species peduncularis.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Vitex peduncularis has opposite, decussate leaves that are typically lanceolate to oblong in shape with entire or slightly serrated margins and a pubescent underside. The plant exhibits a woody structure characterized by slender stems and a branching habit common to many shrubs in the Lamiaceae family. Its reproductive morphology features small, tubular flowers that are borne on long, slender pedicels, which gives the species its specific epithet. These inflorescences are often arranged in terminal panicles or clusters that emerge from the axils of the foliage. The resulting fruit is a small, fleshy drupe that serves as a primary component of its morphological profile.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical regions of northern and central South America, specifically occurring within the moist forests of Brazil, Guyana, Suriname, and French Guiana. Its range extends through parts of the Amazon basin where specific soil compositions support its growth. Localized populations also exist in fragmented patches throughout the Guiana Shield. What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant uses its leaves for medicinal purposes to treat various ailments. These leaves are often prepared as decoctions or infusions to harness their bioactive properties. They are frequently applied in traditional remedies to address inflammatory conditions and skin issues. Some practitioners also utilize the leaf extracts to help manage digestive disturbances. Through these applications, the foliage serves as a primary source of healing within its natural habitat.

According to a study published by "Asian Pacific journal of tropical medicine", the leaf methanol extracts of Vitex peduncularis were used to study antibacterial activity. This plant possessed the highest activity against all the microorganisms screened, including 5 strains of Gram-positive and 7 strains of Gram-negative human pathogenic bacterial strains. It produced a zone of inhibition ranging between (11.000 ± 0.577) and (22.670 ± 0.667) mm. The MIC values for Vitex peduncularis were from 62.5 to 1 000.0 μg/mL. Additionally, the MBC values ranged from 125.0 to 2 000.0 μg/mL.

What are the pharmacological activities of Vitex peduncularis?

This plant has significant antibacterial and antimalarial pharmacological activities that demonstrate its therapeutic potential. Research indicates that its chemical constituents can inhibit the growth of various pathogenic bacteria. Furthermore, the plant exhibits potent effects against malaria-causing parasites, making it a subject of interest for drug development. These biological properties are attributed to the specific secondary metabolites found within its tissues. Such medicinal qualities highlight the importance of investigating this species for future pharmaceutical applications.

According to a study published by "Asian Pacific journal of tropical medicine", Vitex peduncularis possesses a broad spectrum of antibacterial activity and showed the highest activity against all the screened microorganisms.
