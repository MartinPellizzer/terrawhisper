Cuscuta monogyna

Cuscuta monogyna, commonly known as dodder, is a distinctive parasitic plant characterized by its leafless, thread-like stems. Lacking chlorophyll, it survives by extracting nutrients from host plants via specialized structures called haustoria. While often viewed as a weed, it holds significant interest in ecological studies and traditional medicinal research.

  • Scientific name: Cuscuta monogyna
  • Family: Convolvulaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, situated within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. It is classified under the order Solanales and the family Convolvulaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Cuscuta, specifically as the species Cuscuta monogyna.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad presence across several regions within Southern and Southeastern Europe. In Southwestern Europe, it can be found growing throughout France, Portugal, and Spain. Its range also extends into Southeastern Europe, where it is established in Bulgaria. Additionally, the species is widely distributed across the landscapes of Greece. These diverse locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in various Mediterranean climates.
