Physiology

The physiology of Mitragyna parvifolia is characterized by a metabolic framework consistent with many tropical woody species, specifically utilizing a C3 photosynthetic pathway for carbon fixation. This pathway involves the direct fixation of atmospheric carbon dioxide through the enzyme RuBisCO during the Calvin cycle, a mechanism that is highly effective under the shaded or semi-shaded canopy conditions often inhabited by this species. Furthermore, the plant does not function as a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria, such as those found in root nodules of leguminous plants, and must therefore rely on the uptake of available nitrates and ammonium ions from the soil through its root system to satisfy its nutritional requirements for protein synthesis and growth.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Mitragyna parvifolia is characterized by the production of specialized fruit and seeds adapted for efficient distribution. The plant produces fruit classified as a capsule, which is a dehiscent type, meaning it splits open at maturity to release its contents. The seeds contained within are notably small and lightweight, with a mean seed mass of approximately 9.83e-05 g, ranging from a minimum of 9.5e-05 g to a maximum of 1e-04 g. Due to their minute size and low mass, the plant employs an anemochorous dispersal syndrome, relying on wind currents to transport the seeds away from the parent plant to colonize new areas.
