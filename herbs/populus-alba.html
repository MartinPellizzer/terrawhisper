Silver poplar, scientifically known as Populus alba, is a deciduous tree valued for its distinctive shimmering foliage. Beyond its aesthetic appeal, this species holds significance in traditional medicine. Its bark and leaves are frequently utilized for their anti-inflammatory and analgesic properties, offering natural remedies for various common ailments.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Populus alba is characterized by a dioecious sexual reproductive system, meaning that self-fertilization is absent. The flowering process occurs primarily during the spring, with flowering starting in March and concluding in April. The inflorescence is structured as a catkin, with lengths ranging from a minimum of 0.03 m to a maximum of 0.07 m. Pollination is an abiotic process driven by a wind pollination syndrome. Following successful pollination, the plant produces fruit in the form of a capsule. These capsules are dehiscence-type fruits, maintaining a consistent fruit length of approximately 0.5 cm. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing more than 1000 seeds. The seeds are extremely minute, with a mean mass of 0.00012 g (with minimum and maximum values also recorded at 0.00012 g) and dimensions for length and width that are effectively 0 mm. Dispersal of these seeds is achieved through an anemochorous syndrome, utilizing the wind to spread the progeny. Asexual reproduction is noted as being absent in this species.
