Malva sylvestris

Mallow

Malva sylvestris, commonly known as common mallow, is a versatile medicinal herb cherished for centuries. Characterized by its delicate purple flowers, this plant offers soothing mucilage properties. It is widely used to alleviate respiratory issues, soothe digestive discomfort, and reduce inflammation, making it a staple in traditional herbalism.

  • Scientific name: Malva sylvestris
  • Family: Malvaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malvales. Finally, it is a member of the family Malvacaceae and the genus Malva.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Malva sylvestris, is recognized by various regional names across the globe. In Poland, it is called ślaz dziki, while in France, it is referred to as grande mauve. Hungarian speakers call it erdei mályva, and in Germany, it is commonly known as Wilde Malve. Furthermore, in Japan, the species is identified by the name ウスベニアオイ.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a widespread presence across several regions of Northern Europe. In the Scandinavian peninsula, it can be found growing in both Denmark and Norway. Its distribution also extends northward into the territory of Finland. Moving toward the Atlantic, the species is well-established throughout Great Britain. Finally, the plant is commonly documented across the landscape of Ireland.

Plant Parts

This plant, Malva sylvestris, offers various components that are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The soft, velvety leaves are often used in infusions to soothe inflammations, while the delicate purple flowers serve as a gentle mucilage source for respiratory relief. The small seeds can be harvested for their nutritional and emollient qualities. Beneath the surface, the plant develops sturdy roots that contain concentrated bioactive compounds; specifically, the specialized mallow roots are highly valued in traditional herbalism for their ability to calm irritated mucous membranes.
