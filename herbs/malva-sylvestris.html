Mallow, scientifically known as Malva sylvestris, is a versatile perennial herb cherished in traditional herbalism. Renowned for its soothing mucilage, this plant offers significant therapeutic benefits for respiratory and digestive health. Its delicate, violet flowers are not only edible but also serve as a symbol of healing and nourishment.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Malva sylvestris is primarily sexual, characterized by bisexual flowers that facilitate the plant's life cycle. The flowering period typically begins in June and extends through October. This reproductive process relies on biotic pollination syndromes, specifically involving insects, with bees being a prominent pollinator. While the plant is capable of self-fertilization, its reliance on insect visitors ensures genetic diversity. Following successful pollination, the plant develops dry fruit in the form of a capsule. The resulting seeds are quite small, with a mean mass of approximately 0.004258 g (ranging from a minimum of 0.00372 g to a maximum of 0.00732 g) and a consistent seed volume of 5.5 mm³. These seeds are distributed via an unspecialized dispersal syndrome, allowing the species to spread effectively through various non-specific environmental mechanisms. Notably, asexual reproduction is absent in this species.
