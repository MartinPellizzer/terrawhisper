Chaya

Cnidoscolus aconitifolius subsp. aconitifolius · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (299 studies) · ★★★★★

Chaya, scientifically known as Cnidoscolus aconitifolius subsp. aconitifolius, is a nutrient-dense leafy green native to Mexico. Often called "spinach of the gods," this perennial shrub offers exceptional levels of protein, vitamins, and minerals. When properly cooked to remove cyanogenic glycosides, it serves as a potent medicinal and nutritional powerhouse.

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is classified under the subclass Magnoliidae in the order Malpighiales, specifically falling within the family Euphorbiaceae. Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning from the Americas to the Pacific Islands. Within Mexico, its presence is noted across several distinct regions, including Mexico Central, Mexico Northeast, and the Mexico Gulf area. The distribution extends far into the Malesia region, where it can be found in the Philippines. Additionally, the species reaches the South-Central Pacific, specifically inhabiting the Line Islands. Consequently, this subspecies occupies a vast and varied ecological footprint across different continents.
