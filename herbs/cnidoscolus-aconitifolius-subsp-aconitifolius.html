Chaya

Cnidoscolus aconitifolius subsp. aconitifolius · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (299 studies) · ★★★★★

Chaya, scientifically known as Cnidoscolus aconitifolius subsp. aconitifolius, is a nutrient-dense leafy green native to Mexico. Often called "spinach of the gods," this perennial shrub offers exceptional levels of protein, vitamins, and minerals. When properly cooked to remove cyanogenic glycosides, it serves as a potent medicinal and nutritional powerhouse.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as chaya, possesses various parts that are utilized for their nutritional and medicinal properties. The stem provides the structural framework for the shrub, supporting the vigorous growth of its foliage. The most significant part used in consumption and traditional medicine is the leaves, which are rich in proteins and vitamins but must be cooked thoroughly to neutralize cyanogenic glycosides. Additionally, dried leaves can be prepared or processed for specific therapeutic uses, offering a concentrated way to incorporate the plant's bioactive compounds into herbal preparations.
