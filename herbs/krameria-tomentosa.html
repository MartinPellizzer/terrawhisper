Ecology

The ecology of Krameria tomentoza is characterized by an extraordinary degree of environmental plasticity, allowing it to inhabit a vast array of distinct Brazilian biomes. It demonstrates a broad ecological amplitude, thriving in the semi-arid conditions of the Caatinga (stricto sensu) as well as the diverse landscapes of the Cerrado (lato sensu), including specific formations such as the Campo Limpo, Campo Rupestre, and the Savannah of the Amazon (Savaana Amazônica). This species is also capable of colonizing more humid and specialized environments, ranging from the coastal Restingas to the moisture-rich settings of the Floresta Ciliar or Galeria, the seasonal dynamics of the Floresta Estacional Decidual, and the high-precipitation zones of the Floresta Ombrófila (Floresta Pluvial). Additionally, its presence in the Carrasco formation further underscores its ability to adapt to varied soil types and hydrological regimes across the Neotropical landscape.
