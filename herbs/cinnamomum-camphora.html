Cinnamomum camphora

What's the taxonomical classification of Cinnamomum camphora?

Cinnamomum camphorum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida, it falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Laurales. This plant is a member of the family Lauraceae, situated within the genus Cinnamomum, and is specifically identified by its species name, camphorum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Cinnamomum camphora has evergreen leaves that are opposite, elliptical to lanceolate in shape, and possess a distinctively glossy, dark green upper surface with a paler underside. The leaf margins are typically entire or slightly undulate, and the texture feels somewhat leathery to the touch. Its bark is characteristically smooth and greyish when young, but it becomes deeply fissured and scaly as the tree matures. The plant produces small, inconspicuous yellowish-green flowers arranged in axillary panicles. Following pollination, it develops small, globose drupes that transition from green to a bright red color when ripe.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical and subtropical regions of Southeast Asia, specifically spanning from southern China and Taiwan through Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, and Indonesia. It flourishes in the humid climates of the Indochina peninsula and the various archipelagos of the Malay Archipelago. Its natural range is primarily concentrated within low-altitude rainforests and moist evergreen forests. Due to its historical use in traditional medicine and fragrance, it has been introduced to other tropical territories around the world. This widespread distribution is supported by the species' ability to thrive in high humidity and warm temperatures.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, Cinnamomum camphora, requires well-drained, fertile soil and a subtropical or temperate climate with consistent moisture to thrive.

It grows best when planted in areas that receive full sun to partial shade and are protected from extreme frost. Regular watering is essential during the establishment phase to ensure the root system develops deeply in the ground. As the tree matures, it can tolerate various soil types as long as they do not become waterlogged.

Proper spacing is necessary during cultivation to allow for its expansive canopy and large size.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant provides medicinal value through its leaves, buds, wood, roots, and fruit. The aromatic leaves and buds are frequently harvested to extract essential oils used for their antiseptic and calming properties. The wood contains high concentrations of camphor, which is utilized in various topical treatments and traditional remedies. Additionally, the roots and the fruit offer distinct chemical compounds that contribute to the plant's overall therapeutic profile. Together, these diverse components make the species a versatile resource in herbal medicine.

According to a study published by "Zhongguo Zhong yao za zhi = Zhongguo zhongyao zazhi = China journal of Chinese materia medica", the young leaf of C. camphora is used to induce callus that contains borneol. In contrast, the callus induced by the young stem does not contain borneol. To optimize the growth of the callus, the study utilized an MS medium containing 4 mg x L(-1) 2,4-D and 0.2 mg x L(-1) 6-BA. This specific ratio of 4 mg x L(-1) 2,4-D and 0.2 mg x L(-1) 6-BA resulted in the highest ratio of callus induced and the most prosperous growth.

This plant has antibacterial, antifungal, antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant pharmacological activities. According to a study published by "Molecules (Basel, Switzerland)", the camphor tree Cinnamomum camphora (L.) J. Presl has been valued for centuries for its various applications. Camphor extracted from C. camphora exhibits a broad spectrum of biological activities, including insecticidal, antimicrobial, antiviral, anticoccidial, antinociceptive, anticancer, and antitussive effects. What are the herbal preparations of this plant?

This plant is used to produce medicinal preparations including cinnamon camphor seed kernel extracts, camphor oil, essential oils, ethanolic extracts, and steam distillates. These various forms allow for diverse therapeutic applications ranging from topical ointments to aromatic treatments. The steam distillate and essential oils provide highly concentrated aromatic compounds for inhalation or topical use. For more systemic or liquid-based applications, pharmacists often utilize the ethanolic extract to ensure solubility of active constituents. Additionally, specific seed kernel extracts offer targeted medicinal properties derived directly from the plant's reproductive components.

According to a study published by "Foods (Basel, Switzerland)", Cinnamomum camphora seed kernel extracts (CKEs) are rich in phytochemicals that provide strong antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities. These CKE samples contain phenolic compounds ranging from 15.28% to 20.29% and alkaloids ranging from 24.44% to 27.41%. The sub-critical fluid extraction method (CKE-SCFE) resulted in better extraction yields, bioactive substances content, and in vitro antioxidant capacity compared to other methods. Additionally, the CKE pretreated by the solvent extraction method (CKE-SE) demonstrated the best lipid emulsion protective capacity. The volatile substances composition of the CKE samples is also greatly influenced by the specific pretreatment method used.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause a wide range of adverse reactions including skin irritation, nausea, vomiting, allergic reaction, dizziness, confusion, seizures, stomach pain, and headache. Topical application of its oils may lead to significant skin irritation or an intense allergic reaction upon contact. Ingesting components of the plant often triggers gastrointestinal distress such as nausea, vomiting, and severe stomach pain. Neurological complications can also arise, manifesting as a headache, dizziness, or mental confusion. In extreme cases of toxicity, the central nervous system may be affected enough to cause seizures.
