Lilium davidii

What's the taxonomical classification of Lilium davidii?

Lilium davidii belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. Moving through its hierarchical structure, it is placed in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Its taxonomic lineage continues through the order Liliales and the family Liliaceae, eventually reaching its specific designation within the genus Lilium. Ultimately, the plant is identified by its unique species name, davidii.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Lilium davidii has upright, lanceolate leaves that grow in whorls or alternate patterns along a stem reaching up to one meter in height. The plant produces large, trumpet-shaped flowers that typically exhibit a deep reddish-orange color with prominent dark spots. These blooms feature long, spreading tepals and prominent stamens that contribute to its striking visual appearance. The base of the plant consists of fleshy bulbs which serve as the primary energy storage organ. Small, oval-shaped capsules form from the flowers after successful pollination to protect the developing seeds.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

Within these habitats, the species is typically found growing in forest edges, meadowlands, and along rocky slopes. Its range is largely restricted to these specific elevations where consistent moisture and temperate conditions are available.
