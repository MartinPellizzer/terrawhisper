Mangostan, scientifically known as Garcinia mangostana, is a tropical fruit renowned for its exquisite flavor and therapeutic properties. Often called the "Queen of Fruits," it contains potent xanthones. These bioactive compounds provide powerful antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial benefits, making it a highly valued plant in traditional and modern medicine.

This plant, Garcinia mangostana, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Malpighiales and the family Clusiaceae. It is further categorized under the genus Garcinia.

Distribution

This plant, Garcinia mangostana, exhibits a specific geographical distribution across parts of Asia. In China, it is found within the South-Central, Southeast, and Hainan regions. Its range extends into the Indian Subcontinent, where it grows in Assam. Additionally, the species is documented to be present in Bangladesh. Together, these locations define the primary natural habitats for this medicinal plant.

Plant Parts

This plant, Garcinia mangostana, is highly valued in traditional medicine, where various components of its reproductive structures are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The most significant medicinal parts are the fruits, which are encased in a thick, protective layer. Specifically, the pericarp serves as a dense, antioxidant-rich barrier, and the medicinal compounds are often concentrated within these fleshy pericarps. When processing the plant for extracts, the fruit hull is frequently harvested due to its high concentration of xanthones, and the dried fruit hulls are commonly used in decoctions to treat inflammation and various ailments.
