Physiology

The physiology of Celtis iguanaea is characterized by its reliance on the C3 photosynthetic pathway, a mechanism where carbon dioxide is fixed directly through the Calvin cycle without the specialized anatomical adaptations seen in C4 or CAM plants. This pathway dictates its metabolic efficiency and water-use strategies in its native environment. Furthermore, the species does not function as a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationship with diazotrophic bacteria, such as Rhizobium, required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable forms, meaning it relies on the uptake of inorganic nitrogen from the soil to support its growth and physiological processes.
