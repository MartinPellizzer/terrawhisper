Celtis iguanaea

Celtis iguanaea, commonly known as the Sea Celtis or Iguana Celtis, is a versatile tropical evergreen shrub native to coastal regions. Renowned for its glossy foliage and small, fleshy fruits, this resilient plant serves important ecological roles, offering nourishment to local wildlife while providing ornamental beauty in subtropical landscapes.

  • Scientific name: Celtis iguanaea
  • Family: Cannabaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Celtis iguanaea, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae within the order Rosalales. Finally, it is a member of the family Cannabaeae and the genus Celtis.

Common Names

This plant, Celtis iguanaea, is known by a variety of traditional names depending on the linguistic region. In Quechua, it is referred to by several terms, including Kalikali waska, Yanakaspi, and Yana kaspi. In Arabic, it is called ميس إغواني, while in Papuan contexts, it is identified as Beishi di yuana.

Distribution

This plant is widely distributed across several key regions within North America. In the United States, its presence is specifically noted in the state of Florida. Its range extends significantly into Mexico, where it can be found throughout the central territories. Additionally, the species inhabits the northeastern and gulf coastal regions of Mexico. Finally, the plant is also established in the northwestern parts of the Mexican landscape.
