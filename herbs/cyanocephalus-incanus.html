Cyanocephalus incanus

Cyanocephalus incanus, commonly known as the Silver-headed Bluebell, is a rare medicinal herb valued for its unique phytochemical properties. Found in specific high-altitude ecosystems, this botanical treasure is traditionally utilized to treat inflammatory ailments and support respiratory health, offering significant potential for modern pharmacological research and holistic wellness.

  • Scientific name: Cyanocephalus incanus
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It falls under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Lamiales, specifically within the family Lamiaceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Cyanocephalus.

Common Names

This plant, Cyanocephalus incanus, is known by several different common names depending on the region and language. In English, it is frequently referred to as the blue-headed plant, while in Spanish, it is commonly called la planta de cabeza azul. In Portuguese, the species is known as planta de cabeça azul, and in French, it is identified as la plante à tête bleue.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern within the South American continent. It is found natively in the West-Central region of Brazil. Additionally, its range extends further south into the territory of Paraguay. These locations represent the primary geographical areas where the species can be located in the wild. Consequently, its habitat is concentrated within these specific parts of Southern South America.
