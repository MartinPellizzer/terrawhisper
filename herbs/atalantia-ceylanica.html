Morphology

The morphology of Atalantia ceylanica is characterized by its woody growth habit, forming perennial shrubs or small trees. The stems are typically woody, often featuring slightly zig-zagging patterns and may possess small spines or prickles depending on the specific specimen's maturity. The leaves are arranged in an opposite or sub-opposite pattern, appearing as simple, evergreen, and coriaceous (leathery) in texture, with an elliptical to oblong shape and entire margins. The venation is prominent, contributing to the structural rigidity of the foliage. The inflorescence consists of small, fragrant, white flowers that are typically solitary or borne in small clusters in the leaf axils. The fruit is a fleshy, globose to subglobose berry, which transitions in color as it matures, eventually housing numerous small seeds within its pulp.
