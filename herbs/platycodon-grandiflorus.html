Platycodon grandiflorus

Balloon flower

Platycodon grandiflorus, commonly known as Balloon Flower, is a cherished medicinal herb in traditional East Asian medicine. Renowned for its bell-shaped blooms, its roots are prized for treating respiratory ailments, soothing sore throats, and reducing inflammation. This versatile perennial offers significant therapeutic potential for managing coughs and bronchial issues.

  • Scientific name: Platycodon grandiflorus
  • Family: Campanulaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered as Asterales and placed in the family Campanulaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Platycodon.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Platycodon grandiflorus, is recognized by various names across different cultures and languages. In Chinese, it is referred to as 桔梗 (jiégěng), while in Vietnamese, it is called cát cánh. In Korean, the plant is known as 도라지 (doraji), and in German, it is commonly called Ballonblume due to the balloon-like shape of its flower buds. Additionally, in Portuguese, it is often referred to by its generic name, Platycodon.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical range that spans across several distinct regions of Eurasia. In Middle Europe, its presence can be documented in both Austria and Germany. Moving eastward, the species is found in the Siberian region, specifically within the Chita area. Its distribution further extends into the Russian Far East, where it inhabits the Amur region. Finally, the plant is also recorded growing in the Khabarovsk territory of the Russian Far East.
