Abies sibirica

Siberian fir

Abiés sibirica, commonly known as the Siberian fir, is a majestic coniferous tree native to the northern forests of Eurasia. Renowned for its aromatic needles and resin, it holds significant medicinal value. Its essential oils are widely utilized in aromatherapy and traditional remedies to support respiratory health and relaxation.

  • Scientific name: Abies sibirica
  • Family: Pinaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, specifically classified within the class Equisetopsida. It is further organized under the subclass Pinidae and order Pinales, falling into the family Pinaceae. Finally, it is categorized under the genus Abies.

Plant Parts

This plant, Abies sibirica, commonly known as the Siberian fir, is highly valued for its complex chemical composition, particularly the presence of various bioactive compounds found throughout its tissues. While the needles and resin are the most prominent sources of medicinal properties, the plant's therapeutic value is largely defined by its rich concentration of terpenes. These organic compounds, including monoterpenes like alpha-pinene and limonene, are found in high amounts within the essential oils of the needles and the sticky resin, providing the plant with its distinct aromatic scent and serving as potent antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory agents in herbal medicine.
