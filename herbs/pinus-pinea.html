Beyond its culinary value, this species offers significant medicinal potential, containing bioactive compounds with antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties. It remains a vital resource in traditional herbalism and modern pharmacological research.

This plant, known as Pinus pinea, exhibits a specific geographical distribution across the Mediterranean region. Within this region, its presence is well-documented in the Balearic Islands and the island of Corsica. The species also thrives extensively across the mainland territories of France and Portugal. Plant Parts

This plant, known commonly as the stone pine, is highly valued for various components, though its wood is particularly significant in traditional and practical applications. The wood of Pinus pinea is characterized by its straight grain and relatively light weight, making it a useful material for construction and carpentry. Beyond its structural utility, the wood contains resinous compounds that have historically been studied for their chemical properties. While the edible seeds are often the most famous medicinal and culinary product, the woody parts of the tree contribute to the overall ecosystem and provide the essential framework for the production of its many beneficial derivatives.
