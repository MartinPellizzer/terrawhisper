Eucalyptus robusta

Eucalyptus robusta, commonly known as the Swamp Mahogany, is a resilient evergreen tree native to Australia. Renowned for its hard timber and aromatic foliage, it holds significant medicinal potential. Its leaves contain essential oils with potent antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties, traditionally used to treat respiratory ailments and skin infections.

  • Scientific name: Eucalyptus robusta
  • Family: Myrtaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. Following the subclass Magnoliidae, it is situated in the order Myrtales and the family Myrtaceae. Finally, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Eucalyptus.

Plant Parts

This plant, Eucalyptus robusta, possesses various components that are utilized for their therapeutic properties, most notably its leaves. The leaves are highly valued in traditional and modern medicine for their essential oils, which exhibit strong antiseptic, decongestant, and anti-inflammatory effects when inhaled or applied topically. In addition to the foliage, the fruits, often referred to as woody capsules or gumnuts, represent a distinct morphological part of the plant's reproductive structure. While the leaves are the primary source of medicinal compounds, the overall structure of the plant, including its robust canopy and fruit, contributes to its ecological and pharmacological significance.
