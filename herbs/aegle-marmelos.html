Bengal quince

Aegle marmelos · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (901 studies) · ★★★★★

Bengal quince, scientifically known as Aegle marmelos, is a versatile medicinal tree native to the Indian subcontinent. Revered in Ayurveda, its fruits, leaves, and bark possess potent antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and digestive properties. This sacred plant offers significant therapeutic potential in treating gastrointestinal disorders and managing various metabolic conditions naturally.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, known scientifically as Aegle marmelos, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is further classified under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae within the order Sapindales. Finally, it is a member of the family Rutaceae and falls under the genus Aegle.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical presence across several key regions in Asia. Within the Indian Subcontinent, it can be found growing in India and throughout Bangladesh. Its distribution also extends to the specific areas of Assam and the East Himalaya. Additionally, the species is located within the South-Central part of China. These diverse locations highlight the wide natural range of Aegle marmelos.

Plant Parts

This plant, scientifically known as Aegle marmelos, is a versatile medicinal species where various components are utilized for therapeutic purposes. The thick, woody bark is often prepared into decoctions to treat digestive ailments, while each individual leaf contains bioactive compounds used in traditional healing. Whether used as a single leaf in topical applications or as part of a larger herbal mixture, the foliage holds significant value. Furthermore, the fruit is highly regarded for its nutritional and medicinal properties, and even when the fruits are dried or processed, they continue to serve as a vital source of healing agents in various folk medicines.
