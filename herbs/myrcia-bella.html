Myrcia bella

Myrcia bella, commonly known as the "Guavinha," is a versatile medicinal shrub native to tropical regions. Renowned for its antioxidant properties, this plant offers significant therapeutic potential. Traditionally used to support digestive health and manage inflammation, it serves as a valuable botanical resource in various holistic healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Myrcia bella
  • Family: Myrtaceae
  • Native range: Southern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Myrcia bella, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Myrtales and the family Myrtaeaceae. Finally, it is positioned within the genus Myrcia.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Myrcia bella, is recognized by various regional common names depending on the linguistic context. In English, it is frequently referred to as the bayberry, while in Spanish, it is commonly called uva de playa. In Portuguese, the plant is known as uva-da-praia, and in the local vernacular of some regions, it is also referred to as uva-de-beira.
