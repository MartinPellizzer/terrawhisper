Ecology

The ecology of Solanum jabrense is characterized by its adaptability to diverse and distinct Brazilian biomes, allowing it to occupy a broad range of environmental niches. It is found across the semi-arid landscapes of the Caatinga (stricto sensu), where it must contend with seasonal drought and high solar radiation, as well as within the seasonal dynamics of the Floresta Estacional Decidual. Furthermore, its distribution extends into the more humid environments of the Floresta Ombrófila (also known as Floresta Pluvial), indicating a capacity to thrive in both moisture-stressed and high-precipitation ecosystems. This wide ecological amplitude suggests that the species can tolerate varying degrees of water availability and seasonal vegetation changes across these heterogeneous forest and scrubland habitats.
