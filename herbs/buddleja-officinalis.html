Buddleja officinalis

Buddleja officinalis, commonly known as the official butterfly bush, is a versatile medicinal shrub valued in traditional practices. Renowned for its bioactive compounds, it exhibits significant antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties. This plant serves as a vital resource in ethnobotany, offering therapeutic potential for treating various systemic ailments.

  • Scientific name: Buddleja officinalis
  • Family: Scrophulariaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Buddleja officinalis, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae within the order Lamiales. Finally, it is a member of the family Scrophulariaceae and the genus Buddleja.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Buddleja officinalis, is recognized by several different common names depending on the linguistic and regional context. In Chinese, it is most frequently referred to as 密蒙花 (mìménghuā), a name used consistently across the zh, zh-cn, zh-tw, and zh-hans language codes. Additionally, it is sometimes referred to by the name 羊耳朵 (yáng ěrduǒ) in certain Chinese contexts.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad distribution across several key regions of Asia. Within China, it can be found spanning from the South-Central provinces to the North-Central territories. Its presence is also noted throughout the Southeast regions of China and the high-altitude areas of Tibet. Beyond the Chinese borders, the species extends into the Indo-China region. Specifically, its geographical range includes the country of Myanmar.

Plant Parts

This plant, Buddleja officinalis, is characterized by its distinctive morphological structures that contribute to its medicinal profile. The development begins with the tightly packed flower buds, which eventually give rise to dense, elongated inflorescences that serve as the primary reproductive clusters. Within these clusters, each individual flower displays intricate floral parts, including petals and reproductive organs, that are essential for pollination. Complementing these reproductive structures are the large, textured leaves, which provide the necessary surface area for photosynthesis and often contain the bioactive compounds sought for therapeutic use.
