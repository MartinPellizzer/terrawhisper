What's the taxonomical classification of Trapa incisa?

Trapa incisa belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta. As a member of the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, this plant is further categorized under the order Myrtales. It falls within the family Lythraceae, which characterizes its botanical lineage. Finally, the specific classification is defined by its placement in the genus Trapa, with the specific epithet incisa, forming its complete scientific name.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Trapa incisa has floating, rosette-forming leaves that are deeply serrated or incised along their margins and possess a wedge-shaped base. These leaves are typically dark green and can reach significant sizes to maximize surface area for photosynthesis on the water's surface. The plant also produces small, inconspicuous flowers that emerge from the center of the leaf rosette. Its most distinctive morphological feature is the fruit, which is a nut encased in a woody, prickly husk designed for protection and dispersal. The entire structure is adapted for an aquatic lifestyle, allowing the plant to dominate the surface layer of its habitat.
