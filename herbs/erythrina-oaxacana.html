Erythrina oaxacana

Erythrina oaxaana, commonly known as the coral tree, is a striking medicinal plant native to tropical regions. Renowned for its vibrant red blooms, it possesses significant ethnobotanical value. Various parts are traditionally utilized in folk medicine to treat inflammation, skin ailments, and digestive issues, offering promising pharmacological potential.

  • Scientific name: Erythrina oaxacana
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Northern America

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. It is further classified within the order Fabales and the family Fabaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Erythrina and the specific species name Erythrina obovata.

Common Names

This plant, Erythrina obovata (often referred to in botanical literature as Erythrina oxacea or Erythrina obovata depending on the taxonomic classification), is known by various regional names. In English, it is frequently referred to as the Coral Tree. In Spanish, it is commonly called Árbol coral or Árbol de coral, and in Portuguese, it is known as Coral.
