Scientific literature: Very Extensive (462 studies) · ★★★★★

Achiote, scientifically known as Bixa orellana, is a versatile tropical shrub celebrated for its vibrant, pigment-rich seeds. Beyond its use as a natural food coloring, it holds significant medicinal value in traditional practices, offering antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties that support skin health and overall wellness.

This plant, Bixa orellana, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae, it is classified under the order Malvales. Finally, it is a member of the family Bixaceae and the genus Bixa.

Distribution

This plant, known as Bixa orellana, exhibits a specific geographical distribution throughout the West Tropical Africa region. Within this part of the continent, it can be found growing in the country of Benin. Its presence extends further across the landscape to include the nation of Burkina. Additionally, the plant is distributed through Ghana and the coastal territory of Guinea-Bissau. Finally, its range encompasses the territory of Guinea, marking its spread across these diverse West African nations.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as achiote, utilizes various components for medicinal and culinary purposes. The vibrant red color prized in many cultures is derived directly from the seeds, which are housed within the protective inside of the pods. Beyond the fruit, the leaves serve as important medicinal resources, with the individual leaf often prepared in infusions to treat ailments. Even the smaller structures, such as the twigs and the broader foliage of the leaves, can be integrated into traditional healing practices to harness the full botanical potential of the species.
