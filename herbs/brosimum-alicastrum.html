Reproduction

The reproduction of Brosimum alicastrum is characterized by a complex interplay of abiotic pollination and biotic dispersal mechanisms. The pollination process is classified as abiotic, specifically utilizing wind as the primary pollination syndrome. Following successful fertilization, the plant produces fleshy fruits categorized as berries. These fruits are indehiscent, meaning they do not split open at maturity to release their contents, which facilitates a zoochorous dispersal syndrome, where animals aid in the movement of the seeds. The seeds themselves exhibit significant morphological variation; they possess a mean seed mass of approximately 1.3748 g, with individual seeds ranging from a minimum of 0.3605 g to a maximum of 2.721 g. In terms of dimensions, the seed length varies from a minimum of 6 mm to a maximum of 15 mm, with a mean length of 11.3 mm, while the maximum seed width reaches 10.2 mm and the maximum seed height is 7 mm. The seed volume is equally diverse, ranging from a minimum of 500 mm³ to a maximum of 2200 mm³, with a calculated mean volume of 1244 mm³.
