Ocimum obovatum

Cat's whiskers

Ocimum obovatum, commonly known as clove basil, is a versatile medicinal shrub native to tropical regions. Renowned for its aromatic leaves, this plant offers potent antibacterial and anti-inflammatory properties. Traditionally used in herbal remedies, it serves as a natural remedy for digestive issues, skin infections, and respiratory ailments worldwide.

  • Scientific name: Ocimum obovatum
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales and family Lamiaceae, it is classified under the genus Ocimum. Specifically, this species is known as Ocimum obovatum.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Ocimum obovatum, is referred to by various common names depending on the language and region. In English, it is frequently called Cat's whiskers, while in Arabic, it is known as حبق بيضاوي. Within the Zulu language, it is recognized by several names, including iDada, iZiba, and uFukuzela.

Distribution

This plant, Ocimum obovatum, exhibits a specific distribution across the West Tropical Africa region. It can be found growing naturally within the borders of Benin. The species also inhabits various territories in Burkina. Furthermore, its presence is documented throughout Ghana and Guinea. Finally, this medicinal plant is distributed within the Ivory Coast.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as Holy Basil or Tulsi, utilizes various components for its medicinal properties, specifically the leaves and the root bark. The aromatic leaves are the most frequently used part, often prepared as a tea or infusion to treat respiratory ailments and reduce stress. Additionally, the root bark can be processed for its specific therapeutic compounds, contributing to the plant's overall potency in traditional healing practices.
