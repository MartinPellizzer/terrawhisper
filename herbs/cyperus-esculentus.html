Juncia de chufa, scientifically known as Cyperus esculentus, is a perennial sedge celebrated for producing tiger nuts. Beyond its culinary appeal, this versatile plant offers significant medicinal properties, including antioxidant and anti-inflammatory benefits. It has been traditionally used to support digestive health and provide essential nutrients for overall wellness.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Cyperus esculentus involves both sexual and asexual mechanisms, with asexual reproduction being notably present. Sexually, the plant features bisexual flowers that are brown in color, measuring between 0.22 cm and 0.39 cm in length and 0.1 cm to 0.2 cm in width. The flowering period typically commences in July and extends through October. Pollination is an abiotic process, primarily driven by the wind. Following successful pollination, the plant produces indehiscent fruits classified as achenes. These fruits are relatively small, with dimensions ranging from 0.12 cm to 0.18 cm in length and 0.06 cm to 0.12 cm in width. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing between 101 and 1000 seeds. The individual seeds are minute, with a mean mass of 0.00015 g (ranging from a minimum of 0.00013 g to a maximum of 0.00019 g). Self-fertilization is absent in this species. Dispersal is characterized by an unspecialized syndrome, though it also exhibits anthropochorous tendencies, meaning it can be spread by human activity.
