Allium chinense

Chinese onion

Allium chinense, commonly known as Chinese garlic, is a perennial plant prized in traditional medicine for its potent bioactive properties. Characterized by its distinct pungent aroma, this species offers significant therapeutic benefits, including antioxidant, antimicrobial, and anti-inflammatory effects, making it a vital component in various herbal healing practices.

  • Scientific name: Allium chinense
  • Family: Amaryllidaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Asparagales, falling into the family Amaryllidaceae. Finally, it is identified by its genus, Allium.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning several key regions across Asia. In Central Asia, its presence is specifically noted within Kyrgyzstan. The species is widely found throughout China, including the South-Central regions, Southeast areas, and the island of Hainan. Additionally, its range extends into Eastern Asia, where it can be found in Japan. This diverse spread highlights the plant's ability to inhabit various ecological zones across the continent.
