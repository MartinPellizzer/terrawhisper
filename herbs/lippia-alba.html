Bush Tea

Lippia alba · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Very Extensive (343 studies) · ★★★★★

Bush Tea, scientifically known as Lippia alba, is a versatile perennial shrub native to tropical regions of South America. Widely utilized in traditional medicine, it possesses significant antimicrobial, sedative, and antispasmodic properties. Often brewed as a fragrant herbal infusion, this plant offers a natural approach to promoting relaxation and wellness.

Physiology

The physiology of Lippia alba is characterized by a metabolic framework that utilizes the C3 photosynthetic pathway, meaning it fixes carbon dioxide through the Calvin cycle without the specialized carbon-concentrating mechanisms found in C4 or CAM plants. This pathway makes the plant particularly sensitive to environmental conditions such as water availability and light intensity, which directly influence its rate of carbon assimilation and biomass production. Furthermore, Lippia alba is not a nitrogen fixer; it lacks the symbiotic relationship with nitrogen-fixing bacteria, such as Rhizobium, required to convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms. Consequently, the plant relies entirely on the uptake of inorganic nitrogen, such as nitrates and ammonium, from the soil to support its protein synthesis and overall metabolic functions.
