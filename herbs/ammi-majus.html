Bishop's weed, scientifically known as Ammi majus, is a versatile medicinal herb belonging to the Apiaceae family. Widely recognized in traditional medicine, it possesses potent anti-inflammatory, diuretic, and antimicrobial properties. Often used to treat digestive issues and skin ailments, this aromatic plant remains a significant subject in ethnopharmacology.

Plant Parts

This plant, Ammi majus, possesses various morphological features that contribute to its medicinal profile. The plant produces delicate, white flowers that bloom in umbel clusters, eventually giving way to its characteristic schizocarp fruit. These fruits are distinctive for their hooked bristles, which aid in dispersal, and they contain the potent seeds often utilized in traditional remedies. The lush, green leaves are finely divided and provide a significant surface area for photosynthesis, while the underground roots serve as the foundation, anchoring the plant and storing essential nutrients.
