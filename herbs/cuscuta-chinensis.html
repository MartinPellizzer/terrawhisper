Distribution

This plant, Cuscuta chinensis, exhibits a specific distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West Tropical Africa, it can be found within the borders of Ghana. Moving toward the northeastern part of the continent, the species is present in Eritrea and Ethiopia. Its range also extends to the island of Socotra, which is located in Northeast Tropical Africa. Finally, the plant is distributed throughout the areas of Sudan and South Sudan.

Plant Parts

This plant, Cuscuta chinensis, is a parasitic climber that lacks functional leaves and chlorophyll, relying entirely on its host for survival. Its life cycle begins with its seeds, which lie dormant in the soil until they detect the chemical signals of a nearby host plant. Once germination occurs, the seedling rapidly develops a specialized organ called a haustorium, a penetrating structure that pierces the host's tissues to establish a direct connection to its vascular system, allowing the parasite to siphon essential nutrients and water.
