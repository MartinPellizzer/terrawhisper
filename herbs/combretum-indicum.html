Combretum indicum

What's the taxonomical classification of Combretum indicum?

Combretum indicum belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is situated in the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Following its taxonomic descent, it falls under the order Myrtales and is a member of the family Combretaceae. Ultimately, the plant is defined by its specific classification within the genus Combretum, with its unique identity being designated as the species indicum.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Combretum indicum has a woody climbing shrub structure characterized by long, flexible stems that utilize tendrils to ascend supporting vegetation. The leaves are typically arranged in an opposite or whorled pattern and feature an elliptic to lanceolate shape with entire margins. Small, inconspicuous flowers are produced in dense clusters or spikes, often exhibiting a pale color. The plant also develops specialized bark textures that vary as the stems mature in diameter. These morphological adaptations allow the species to compete effectively for sunlight within dense forest canopies.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical and subtropical regions of Madagascar, where it grows primarily in dry deciduous forests and scrublands. Its distribution is highly localized to the island's unique ecosystems rather than being spread across the African mainland. Within these specific habitats, it thrives in well-drained soils and areas that experience distinct seasonal variations in rainfall. The species remains endemic to this single geographic location, making it a specialized part of the Malagasy flora. Local environmental conditions dictate its specific niche within the island's diverse landscape.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant, commonly known as Ketumpang or Indian Almond, thrives in tropical climates with well-drained soil and requires full sunlight to reach its maximum growth potential.

It is typically propagated through seeds or cuttings and benefits from regular watering during its early establishment phase. Once mature, the tree can tolerate varying soil types but performs best in nutrient-rich, moist environments. Growers must monitor for pests and ensure the tree has sufficient space due to its large canopy and spreading root system.

Proper pruning helps maintain its shape and encourages healthy foliage development.

What traditional systems uses this plant?

This plant, known as Bai Hua Feng in traditional Chinese medicine, has been historically utilized to treat various skin conditions including fungal infections, eczema, and sores.

Practitioners frequently employ the dried leaves to prepare decoctions or topical washes meant to clear heat and detoxify the body. In many Southeast Asian cultures, it is valued for its ability to alleviate inflammation and soothe itchy skin ailments. The plant is also traditionally applied in poultices to aid the healing of wounds and external irritations.

These ancient applications rely on the plant's perceived ability to regulate internal heat and restore skin health.

What are the pharmacological activities of Combretum indicum?

This plant has anthelmintic and anti-infectious pharmacological activities that allow it to combat various parasitic and microbial threats. The extract demonstrates a significant ability to expel intestinal worms, making it a valuable resource for managing helminth infections. Its anti-infectious properties further extend its utility by inhibiting the growth of harmful bacteria and other pathogens. Researchers have identified specific bioactive compounds within the leaves and bark that drive these therapeutic effects. These diverse medicinal capabilities highlight the importance of the species in traditional and modern pharmacology.

According to a study published by "Antibiotics (Basel, Switzerland)", Combretum indicum is one of the most commonly used species in treatments of infectious diseases. This plant was identified through a meta-analysis of data from 16 ethnobotanical studies of 25 Karen villages. Among the 127 plant species from 59 plant families used by the Karen, Combretum indicum stands out as a priority for pharmacological testing. The researchers suggest that this specific species should be among the first to undergo testing for the possible development of medicines.

  • anthelmintic

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause hypoglycemia, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, dizziness, and headache. These side effects often manifest as gastrointestinal distress when the plant is consumed in certain quantities. Some individuals may experience a significant drop in blood sugar levels, which can lead to lightheadedness or fainting. Neurological symptoms like a persistent headache or a sense of dizziness may also occur during use. Monitoring these physical reactions is important for anyone considering the plant's use.
