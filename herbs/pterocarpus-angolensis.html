Pterocarpus angolensis

Bleedwood tree

Pterocarpus angolensis, commonly known as the Angolan Rosewood, is a significant medicinal plant native to African savannas. Renowned for its diverse therapeutic properties, various parts of the tree are utilized in traditional medicine to treat ailments ranging from inflammation to digestive issues, offering valuable insights for modern pharmacological research.

  • Scientific name: Pterocarpus angolensis
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Fabales, it is classified under the family Fabaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic identity is established within the genus Pterocarpus.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Pterocarpus angolensis, is recognized by various regional and international names depending on the language. In Estonia, it is referred to as Angoola tiibviljak, while in Korea, it is called 키아트. Within certain African linguistic traditions, it is known as Muvhangazi in Tsonga and iorócó in Ga. Distribution

This plant is primarily distributed across several key regions within the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, its presence is noted in both Congo and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Moving toward East Tropical Africa, the species can be found in Tanzania. Its range extends into South Tropical Africa, where it is located in Angola. Finally, the plant is also documented as growing in Malawi.

Plant Parts

This plant, Pterocarpus angolensis, is a multipurpose medicinal resource where various components are utilized for their therapeutic properties. The leaves are frequently employed in traditional remedies to treat ailments such as fever or skin irritations, while the stem-bark and the general bark of the tree are often decocted to address digestive issues or inflammatory conditions. Furthermore, the roots are known to possess significant medicinal value in various ethnobotanical practices, and even the fruits can be integrated into traditional wellness regimens.
