Elephant apple, scientifically known as Dillenia indica, is a distinctive tropical tree native to South and Southeast Asia. Renowned for its large, edible, acidic fruits, it holds significant ethnobotanical value. Beyond its culinary uses, this versatile plant offers numerous medicinal properties, widely utilized in traditional healing practices across various cultures.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution spanning across several tropical regions. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found specifically around the Gulf of Guinea Islands. Its presence extends significantly into the Western Indian Ocean, where it inhabits Comoros, Mauritius, and Réunion. Additionally, the species is located within the South-Central regions of China. Together, these locations highlight the diverse environments where the plant thrives.

The broad, green leaf is another significant part of the plant's anatomy, often studied for its biological properties.
