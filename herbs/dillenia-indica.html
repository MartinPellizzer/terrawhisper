Dillenia indica

What's the taxonomical classification of Dillenia indica?

Dillenia indica belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is categorized under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. The plant follows the order Dilleniales and is a member of the family Dilleniaceae. Finally, its specific taxonomic position is defined by the genus Dillenia and the species indica.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Dillenia indica has large, leathery, obovate leaves that measure up to 30 centimeters in length and are characterized by prominent, parallel secondary veins. The plant grows as a medium to large tree with spreading branches and a thick trunk. Its flowers are showy and consist of numerous white petals arranged around a central cluster of numerous stamens. Following pollination, the plant develops a distinctive large, fleshy, and many-seeded fruit that is often referred to as the elephant apple. The bark is typically greyish and can become deeply fissured as the tree matures.

What is the geographical distribution of this plant?

This plant is native to the tropical regions of South and Southeast Asia, specifically occurring in countries such as India, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, and parts of Indonesia. It typically thrives in moist, lowland tropical forests and along the banks of streams or rivers where humidity is high. Within its natural range, it is most frequently found in the evergreen and semi-evergreen forest ecosystems of the Indian subcontinent. The species prefers well-drained, nutrient-rich soils that are characteristic of these warm, wet environments. Because of these specific climatic requirements, its distribution is strictly limited to the equatorial and subtropical zones of the region.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant grows best in tropical climates with well-drained, acidic soils and high humidity levels.

It requires consistent moisture and partial shade to thrive during its early developmental stages. Once established, the plant can tolerate full sun if the soil remains sufficiently damp to prevent root stress. Regular organic fertilization helps support its large, leathery leaves and the production of its distinctive large white flowers.

Proper drainage is essential to prevent root rot in the heavy soils common to its native habitats.

According to a study published by "Microbial pathogenesis", Dillenia indica is a medicinal tree of the Dilleniaceae. The flower extract of this plant was used for the synthesis of silver nanoparticles under optimal conditions of 2 mM AgNO3, pH 4.5, and a 48-h reaction time. These nanoparticles exhibited an average size of 50.17 nm with a polydispersity index value of 0.298 and a zeta potential of -24.9 mV. The characteristic band was observed at a wavelength of 435 nm, while Energy Dispersive X-Ray showed an absorption peak approximately at 3 keV. When combined with antibiotics, the nanoparticles were maximum effective against Shigella boydii (16.07 ± 0.35) and Klebsiella pneumoniae (15.03 ± 0.20). Additionally, AgNPs alone showed maximum inhibition for methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (19.97 ± 0.20 mm) and Enterococcus faecium (19.80 ± 0.15 mm).

What are the pharmacological activities of Dillenia indica?

This plant has demonstrated significant pharmacological activities including anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidant, antibacterial, antimicrobial, and hepatoprotective properties. Its chemical constituents work effectively to neutralize free radicals and reduce oxidative stress within biological systems. The plant also exhibits potent antimicrobial and antibacterial actions that help inhibit the growth of various harmful pathogens. Furthermore, its hepatoprotective qualities assist in shielding liver cells from toxic damage and chemical injury. These diverse therapeutic effects suggest that the species holds substantial potential for pharmaceutical development.

According to a study published by "Chinese herbal medicines", Dillenia indica possesses diverse biological activities including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, anticancer, antidiabetic, and hepatoprotective properties. The plant contains bioactive compounds such as flavonoids, tannins, saponins, and triterpenoids that underpin these functions. Its anti-inflammatory effects involve the inhibition of pro-inflammatory cytokines like tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6). Preclinical studies show that extracts can inhibit cancer cell proliferation and induce apoptosis in breast, colorectal, and liver cancers. While generally safe at recommended doses, excessive intake may lead to gastrointestinal discomfort or rare cases of hepatotoxicity.

According to a study published by "3 Biotech", Dillenia indica L. has been traditionally utilized for the management and treatment of metabolic diseases. In the study, D. indica fruit extract was administered orally for 30 days at doses of 200 and 400 mg/kg to rats with diabetes induced by a 60 mg/kg injection of STZ. A dose of 400 mg/kg resulted in a significant 38.78% decrease in blood glucose levels (P < 0.01). The extract also attenuated hepatic dysfunction by reducing ALA T and ASAT levels by 57.95% and 52.71%, respectively. Additionally, the D. indica fruit extract showed a significant reduction in triglyceride and total cholesterol levels while increasing SOD, CAT, and GPx activities.

What side effects this plant can have?

This plant can cause localized skin irritation, allergic reactions, or digestive distress if consumed in quantities that exceed safe medicinal limits. While many parts of the plant are used in traditional medicine, direct contact with the sap or foliage may trigger contact dermatitis in sensitive individuals. Ingestion of large amounts could potentially lead to stomach upset or nausea due to the concentration of certain phytochemicals. Because scientific research on human toxicity is limited, individuals should exercise caution when applying or consuming any part of the species. Consulting a healthcare professional is advised before using this plant for therapeutic purposes to avoid unforeseen adverse reactions.
