Urtica urens · Accepted scientific name

Small Nettle, scientifically known as Urtica urens, is a stinging herbaceous plant native to Europe and Asia. Characterized by its intense prickles, it contains bioactive compounds used in traditional medicine. Often utilized for its anti-inflammatory and diuretic properties, this potent species plays a significant role in herbal pharmacology.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution across various regions of Northern Europe. Its presence is documented in the coastal areas of Denmark and throughout the landscape of Finland. It can also be found growing within the Faroe Islands and across much of Great Britain. Furthermore, its geographical range extends to the unique environment of Iceland. These locations highlight the plant's affinity for the northernmost territories of the continent.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as the stinging nettle, utilizes its leaves as its most prominent and pharmacologically active component. These leaves are characterized by a dense covering of fine, hollow hairs called trichomes, which contain irritating chemicals that cause a stinging sensation upon contact with the skin. Beyond this defensive mechanism, the leaves serve as a vital part of the plant's medicinal profile, as they are rich in vitamins, minerals, and bioactive compounds used in various traditional remedies.
