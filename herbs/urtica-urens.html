Small Nettle, scientifically known as Urtica urens, is a stinging herbaceous plant native to Europe and Asia. Characterized by its intense prickles, it contains bioactive compounds used in traditional medicine. Often utilized for its anti-inflammatory and diuretic properties, this potent species plays a significant role in herbal pharmacology.

Reproduction

The reproduction of Urtica urens is characterized by a monoecious sexual reproduction strategy, with asexual reproduction being absent. The flowering period typically begins in May and extends through October. The plant relies on abiotic pollination, specifically utilizing a wind-based pollination syndrome to facilitate fertilization, as self-fertilization is noted to be absent. Following pollination, the plant develops dry, green fruits classified as achenes. These fruits are indehiscent and exhibit dimensions with a mean length of 0.14 cm (ranging from 0.08 to 0.175 cm) and a maximum width of 0.1 cm. Each fruit is highly prolific, containing more than 1,000 seeds. The seeds themselves are minute, with a consistent length of 2.1 mm and a volume of 0.45 mm³. The seed mass is extremely low, averaging 0.0005175 g, with a range between 0.00048 g and 0.00055 g. For dispersal, the plant employs an anemochorous syndrome, utilizing the wind to spread its abundant seeds.
