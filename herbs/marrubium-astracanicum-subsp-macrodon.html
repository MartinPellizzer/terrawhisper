Marrubium astracanicum subsp. macrodon

Marrubium astracanicum subsp. macrodon, commonly known as macrodon horehound, is a distinctive medicinal herb belonging to the Lamiaceae family. Renowned for its aromatic foliage, this subspecies is valued in traditional pharmacopeia for its antitussive and expectorant properties, making it a vital botanical resource for respiratory health and wellness.

  • Scientific name: Marrubium astracanicum subsp. macrodon
  • Family: Lamiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, falling under the class Equisetopsida and subclass Magnoliidae. Within the order Lamiales and the family Lamiaceae, it is classified under the genus Marrubium. Specifically, it is identified as the subspecies macrodon of the species Marrubium astracanicum.

Plant Parts

This plant, Marrubium astracanicum subsp. macrodon, is characterized by its distinct foliage, which serves as the primary source of its medicinal properties. The leaves are typically arranged in opposite pairs along the stem, exhibiting a rugose or wrinkled texture on the upper surface. They are often covered in fine, grayish-white hairs that give the plant a soft, pubescent appearance, and they possess a characteristic aromatic scent when crushed. In the context of herbal medicine, these leaves are the most vital part of the plant, containing the essential compounds used to treat various respiratory ailments.
