Equisetum palustre

Marsh horsetail

Equisetum palustre, commonly known as marsh horsetail, is a perennial aquatic plant found in wetlands globally. Characterized by its segmented, non-flowering stems, it holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditionally used in various cultures for its diuretic and astringent properties, modern research explores its complex bioactive compounds for therapeutic potential.

  • Scientific name: Equisetum palustre
  • Family: Equisetaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and is classified under the class Equisetopsida. Following its placement in the subclass Equisetidae and order Equisetales, it is a member of the family Equisetaceae. Finally, it is identified by the genus Equisetum.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as marsh horsetail, is categorized under the group of herbs, characterized by its non-woody and relatively soft stems. The herb consists of specialized aerial stems that are segmented and jointed, often displaying a hollow structure typical of the Equisetum genus. These herbaceous stems function as the primary photosynthetic organs and are responsible for the plant's distinctive, ribbed appearance as they grow through wetland environments.
