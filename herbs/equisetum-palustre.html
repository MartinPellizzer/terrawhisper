Marsh horsetail, scientifically known as Equisetum palustre, is a perennial herbaceous plant found in wetlands and damp habitats. Renowned in traditional medicine, it is valued for its high silica content and diuretic properties. This resilient species plays a vital role in aquatic ecosystems while offering unique ethnobotanical applications.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as marsh horsetail, is categorized under the group of herbs, characterized by its non-woody and relatively soft stems. The herb consists of specialized aerial stems that are segmented and jointed, often displaying a hollow structure typical of the Equisetum genus. These herbaceous stems function as the primary photosynthetic organs and are responsible for the plant's distinctive, ribbed appearance as they grow through wetland environments.
