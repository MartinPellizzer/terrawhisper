Zanthoxylum austrosinense

What's the taxonomical classification of Zanthoxylum austrosinense?

Zanthoxylum austrosinense belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving down the taxonomic hierarchy, it falls under the order Sapindales and the family Rutaceae. Finally, the plant is identified by its specific genus, Zanthoxylum, and its unique species designation, austrosinense.

What parts of this plant are used medicinally?

This plant Zanthoxylum austrosinense utilizes its roots for medicinal purposes to treat various ailments. These underground structures contain specific bioactive compounds that contribute to the species' therapeutic profile. Traditional practitioners often process the roots to extract essence for medicinal applications. The chemical composition found within the root system is essential for its pharmacological utility. This particular part of the plant serves as a primary source for developing herbal remedies.

According to a study published by "Bioorganic chemistry", the roots of Zanthoxylum austrosinense contain three new carbazole alkaloids, zanthoaustrones A-C (1-3), and nine known compounds 4-12. These 12 isolated alkaloids display notable antiproliferative activities against diverse human cancer cell lines with IC50 values in the range of 0.85 ± 0.06 to 29.56 ± 0.17 µM. Additionally, these compounds 1-12 exhibit pronounced inhibitory activities on nitric oxide production with IC50 values ranging from 0.89 ± 0.05 to 9.62 ± 0.15 µM. These medicinal effects are comparable to positive controls that show IC50 values of 1.58 ± 0.09 to 28.69 ± 0.21 µM for cisplatin and 4.06 ± 0.11 µM for hydrocortisone. The findings suggest that these alkaloids from the roots could be meaningful for the development of new anti-cancer drugs and anti-inflammatory agents.
