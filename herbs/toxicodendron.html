Poison ivy, scientifically known as Toxicodendron, is a notorious plant renowned for its allergenic properties. Containing the potent oil urushiol, contact with its foliage triggers severe skin irritation and rashes. While widely feared by hikers, understanding its botanical characteristics and growth patterns is essential for safe outdoor navigation.

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Sapindales, it is classified under the family Anacardiaceae. Specifically, it is identified by the genus Toxicodendron.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution spanning across multiple continents and diverse climates. In Middle Europe, its presence is documented within the territories of Germany as well as the region encompassing Czechia and Slovakia. Moving toward Southeastern Europe, the species can be found growing in Italy. Its range further extends into the Russian Far East, specifically across the Kuril Islands. Finally, the plant is also established within the natural landscapes of Sakhalin.
