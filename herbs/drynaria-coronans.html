Drynaria coronans, scientifically known as Drynaria coronans, is a fascinating epiphytic fern prized for its unique shield-like fronds. Often found in tropical forests, this resilient plant holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditional medicinal practices frequently utilize its rhizomes and leaves to treat various ailments, highlighting its profound therapeutic potential.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a broad geographical distribution across several key regions of East Asia. Within China, its presence is noted in the South-Central provinces as well as the southeastern coastal areas. It can also be found in the unique tropical environment of Hainan and the high-altitude landscapes of Tibet. Beyond mainland China, the species extends its range into the Nansei-shoto islands. This diverse spread demonstrates the plant's ability to inhabit a variety of distinct ecological zones.

Plant Parts

This plant, Drynaria coronans, commonly known as the basket fern, possesses a distinct morphological structure characterized by its specialized foliage. While much of its visual identity comes from its dimorphic fronds—which include sterile, shield-shaped leaves that sit on the ground—the plant's underground architecture is equally important. The rhizome serves as the central creeping stem, acting as the primary organ for nutrient storage and vegetative propagation, allowing the fern to spread across its substrate and anchor itself firmly in its natural habitat.
