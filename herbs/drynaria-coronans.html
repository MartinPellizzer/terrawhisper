Morphology

The morphology of Drynaria coronans is characterized by its specialized growth habit as an obligatory epiphyte, meaning it relies on growing upon other plants for physical support to reach higher light intensities in forest canopies. The plant features a distinctive dimorphic leaf structure; it produces two types of fronds: fertile fronds, which are typically smaller and more upright to facilitate spore dispersal, and sterile, highly modified vegetative fronds known as nest leaves. These nest leaves are large, brown, papery, and recurved, forming a funnel-like structure designed to trap falling organic debris and moisture, which creates a self-contained substrate for nutrient uptake. The rhizome is creeping and scales-covered, providing a sturdy anchor to the host branch. The fertile fronds are pinnatifid to deeply pinnate, displaying a lush green coloration, while the overall architecture of the plant is defined by this complex arrangement of functional foliage that supports its specialized life cycle within the arboreal ecosystem.
