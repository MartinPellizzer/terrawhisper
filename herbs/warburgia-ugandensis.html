East African greenbark

Warburgia ugandensis · Accepted scientific name

Scientific literature: Moderate (106 studies) · ★★★☆☆

East African greenbark, scientifically known as Warburgia ugandensis, is a vital medicinal shrub native to African montane forests. Renowned for its potent antimicrobial and antifungal properties, this plant is deeply embedded in traditional medicine, frequently used to treat respiratory infections, digestive issues, and various skin ailments throughout the region.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide geographical distribution across several regions of the African continent. In West-Central Tropical Africa, it can be found growing within the borders of Rwanda and the DR Congo. Moving toward the Northeast, the species is also documented in Ethiopia. Its range extends into East Tropical Africa, where it is present in Kenya. Finally, the distribution continues southward to include Tanzania.

Plant Parts

This plant, known scientifically as Warburgia ugandenensis, is highly valued in traditional medicine for its potent bioactive properties found within its various anatomical structures. The stem bark and the general bark of the tree are frequently harvested and utilized due to their concentrated medicinal potency. These components are particularly rich in specific chemical compounds, most notably various aldehydes, such as polygodial, which are responsible for the plant's intense pungent flavor and its significant antimicrobial and antifungal activities. Furthermore, the leaves are also employed in medicinal preparations, contributing to the overall therapeutic profile of the species.
