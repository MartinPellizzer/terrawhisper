Heracleum rapula

Heracleum rapula, commonly known as hogweed, is a perennial herb belonging to the Apiaceae family. While often regarded as a weed, it holds historical significance in traditional medicine. It is characterized by large, umbelliferous flower clusters and deeply lobed leaves, requiring careful identification to avoid toxic look-alikes.

  • Scientific name: Heracleum rapula
  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Native range: Asia-Temperate

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Apiales, it is classified under the family Apiaceae. It is specifically identified by the genus Heracleum, where it is known as Heracleum rapula.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Heracleum rapula, is referred to by several different common names in Chinese depending on the specific part or variety being discussed. It is commonly called 鹤庆独活 (Hèqìng dúhuó) and is also known as 白云花 (Báiyúnhuā). Furthermore, it can be identified as 鹤庆独活（白云花） (Hèqìng dúhuó (Báiyúnhuā)) to combine these identifiers, while the specific root component is referred to as 白云花根 (Báiyúnhuā gēn).
