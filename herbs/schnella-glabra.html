Physiology

The physiology of Schnella glabra is characterized by a C3 photosynthetic pathway, a metabolic strategy common among many temperate and subtropical herbaceous species. In this pathway, the initial fixation of atmospheric carbon dioxide occurs through the enzyme Rubisco, which facilitates the carboxylation of ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate to produce two molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate. This process occurs within the mesophyll cells, where the absence of specialized Kranz anatomy or nocturnal acid accumulation—typical of C4 or CAM plants—means that the plant relies on a direct carbon fixation cycle that is highly sensitive to photorespiration under high-temperature or low-CO2 conditions. Consequently, its physiological efficiency is optimized for environments where water availability and moderate light intensities allow for steady gas exchange and efficient glucose production through the Calvin cycle.
