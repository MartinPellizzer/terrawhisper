Brazilian calla-lily, scientifically known as Richardia brasiliensis, is a versatile herbaceous plant native to the Americas. Often found in disturbed soils, it holds significant ethnobotanical value. Traditionally used in various cultures, its leaves and roots possess medicinal properties frequently utilized to treat skin conditions, inflammation, and gastrointestinal ailments.

Richardia brasiliensis exhibits a broad ecological plasticity, thriving across a diverse array of environmental landscapes ranging from disturbed anthropic areas (Área Antrópica) to specialized natural formations. Its habitat distribution is extensive, encompassing the semi-arid Caatinga (strictu sensu), various grassland types such as Campo de Altitude, Campo Limpo, and Campo Rupestre, as well as the Cerrado (lato sensu), Carrasco, and coastal Restinga environments. Additionally, the species is found colonizing vegetation over rocky outcrops (Vegetação Sobre Afloramentos Rochosos). This adaptability allows it to occupy a wide altitudinal gradient, spanning from sea level (0 m AMSL) up to a maximum elevation of 1550 m AMSL.
