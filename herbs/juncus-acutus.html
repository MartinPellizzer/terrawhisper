Juncus acutus

Spiny rush

Juncus acutus, commonly known as sharpneck rush, is a resilient perennial herb found in coastal and wetland ecosystems. Renowned for its tough, fibrous stems, this plant plays a vital role in soil stabilization. In traditional medicine, its various parts are utilized for their unique bioactive properties and healing potential.

  • Scientific name: Juncus acutus
  • Family: Juncaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta, specifically within the class Equisetopsida. It is further classified under the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Poales, falling into the family Juncaceae. Ultimately, it is identified by its genus name, Juncus.

Moving toward the Mediterranean, it can be found throughout various parts of Southwestern Europe. Specifically, its range includes the islands of Baleares and Corse. Additionally, the species is widely distributed across the mainland of France.

Plant Parts

This plant, Juncus acutus, commonly known as hard rush, is characterized by its distinctive morphology, where the most significant medicinal and structural component consists of its tough, slender stems. These stems are cylindrical, pithy, and often feature longitudinal ridges, serving as the primary vegetative structure that supports the plant in its wetland habitats. In various traditional practices, these stems are the parts most frequently identified and utilized due to their resilient fiber and specific chemical properties.
