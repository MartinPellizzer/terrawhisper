Dendrobium longicornu

What's the taxonomical classification of Dendrobium longicornu?

Dendrobium longicornu belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae, it is categorized under the order Asparagales. This plant is a member of the Orchidaceae family, placing it within the diverse and complex genus Dendrobium, and is specifically identified by the species epithet longicornu.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Dendrobium longicornu has elongated, cane-like pseudobulbs that are slender, cylindrical, and typically reach lengths of several dozen centimeters. These stems are characterized by closely spaced, lanceolate leaves that emerge from the nodes along the upper portions of the plant. The flowers are relatively small and feature distinctively long, tapering sepals and petals that give the species its name. Each bloom exhibits a complex structure with a prominent labellum that often displays intricate patterns. The overall growth habit is epiphytic, allowing the plant to cling to tree bark in its natural habitat.

How is this plant cultivated?

This plant requires bright, filtered light and high humidity levels between 60 and 80 percent to thrive in a controlled environment.

Growers should use a well-draining medium such as bark or sphagnum moss to prevent root rot during the watering cycle. Regular misting and a consistent watering schedule are essential, especially during the active growing season when temperatures are warmer. During the winter months, reducing water frequency and allowing a slight drop in temperature can help stimulate new growth.

Proper air circulation around the foliage is also necessary to prevent fungal diseases from developing on the leaves.
