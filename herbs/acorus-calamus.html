Acorus calamus

Acorus roseau

Acorus calamus, commonly known as sweet flag, is a perennial herb cherished in traditional medicine for centuries. Renowned for its aromatic rhizomes, it possesses potent antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties. Used extensively in Ayurvedic and Chinese practices, this versatile plant offers therapeutic benefits for digestive health and neurological support.

  • Scientific name: Acorus calamus
  • Family: Acoraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta within the class Equisetopsida. It is further organized into the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Acorales, falling under the family Acoraceae. Ultimately, its taxonomic identity is defined by the genus Acorus.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a wide presence across various regions of Northern Europe. Specifically, it can be found growing in the natural landscapes of Denmark and Finland. Its distribution also extends to the island of Iceland and the Faroe Islands. Additionally, the species is well-documented throughout Great Britain. These various locations highlight the plant's ability to thrive in different northern environments.

Plant Parts

This plant, Acorus calamus, is a perennial herb where various components offer distinct medicinal properties. The most therapeutically significant part is the rhizome, which is often collected and processed for its aromatic and healing qualities. While the rhizo-mes are the primary source of essential oils, the roots and the underground stems are frequently used interchangeably in traditional herbal preparations. Above the ground, the plant features long, sword-shaped leaves that are characteristic of its growth habit. In some traditional practices, the whole plant may be utilized to ensure a full spectrum of its bioactive compounds.
