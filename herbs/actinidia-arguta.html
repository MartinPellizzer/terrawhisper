Actinidia arguta

Hardy kiwi

Actinidia arguta, commonly known as hardy kiwi or kiwi berry, is a vigorous climbing vine native to East Asia. Unlike its larger relatives, it produces small, grape-sized fruits with edible skins. Rich in vitamin C and antioxidants, this resilient plant offers significant nutritional benefits and versatile culinary applications.

  • Scientific name: Actinidia arguta
  • Family: Actinidiaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant, Actinidia arguta, belongs to the kingdom Plantae and the phylum Streptophyta. It is classified under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae within the order Ericales. Finally, it is a member of the family Actinidiaceae and the genus Actinidia.

Plant Parts

This plant, known as hardy kiwi, is a perennial woody climber characterized by several distinct morphological features. Its climbing stems are flexible and vigorous, often utilizing tendrils to ascend through surrounding vegetation. Beneath the soil, a robust system of roots provides necessary anchorage and nutrient absorption to support its sprawling growth. The plant features broad, simple, and heart-shaped leaves that are densely arranged along the vines, playing a crucial role in photosynthesis. Finally, it produces edible, small, oval-shaped fruits that are covered in a fine fuzz, containing the concentrated medicinal and nutritional properties for which the species is valued.
