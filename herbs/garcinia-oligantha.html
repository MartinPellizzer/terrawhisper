Garcinia oligantha

What's the taxonomical classification of Garcinia oligantha?

Garcinia oligantha belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta. Within this lineage, it is categorized under the class Equisetopsida and the subclass Magnoliidae. Moving through its higher taxonomic ranks, the plant is placed in the order Malpighiales and falls under the family Clusiaceae. Finally, it is identified by its specific genus, Garcinia, and its unique species designation, oligantha.

What are the morphological characteristics of this plant?

Garcinia oligantha has small, elliptical to oblong leaves that are oppositely arranged on woody stems, characterized by a coriaceous texture and entire margins. The foliage typically exhibits a deep green color on the upper surface with a paler underside. Its inflorescence consists of small, relatively infrequent flowers that emerge from the leaf axils. The reproductive structures produce fleshy fruits that are characteristic of the Clusiaceae family. These morphological traits help distinguish the species within its tropical forest habitat.

What medicinal compounds this plant contains?

This plant contains caged xanthones (1-6), a rearranged xanthone derivative (7), xanthones, a benzofurane derivative, and biphenyl compounds. These diverse phytochemicals represent the primary secondary metabolites isolated from the species. The presence of caged xanthones and rearranged structures highlights the chemical complexity found within the plant's tissues. Such a variety of bioactive molecules underscores the medicinal potential of Garcinia oligantha.

According to a study published by "Bioorganic chemistry", a total of 26 compounds were purified from Garcinia oligantha, which includes six caged xanthones (1-6) and one rearranged xanthone derivative (7). An affinity ultrafiltration-ultra performance liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry screen revealed that ten of these isolated compounds exhibited significant binding affinity to VEGFR-2. Molecular docking studies showed that these active compounds interacted with the target protein with predicted binding strengths varying between -7.1 to -9.3 kcal/mol. Furthermore, compound 1 demonstrated significant inhibitory activity against vessel formation in a transgenic zebrafish model. These findings underscore the pharmacological value of Garcinia oligantha and its potential as a source of novel antitumo r candidates.
