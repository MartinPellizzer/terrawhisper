Sideroxylon spinosum

Argan

Sideroxylon spinosum, commonly known as the "palo de sangre" or bloodwood, is a resilient shrub native to arid regions. Renowned in traditional medicine, its bark and resins are utilized to treat wounds, inflammation, and digestive ailments. This hardy species offers significant ethnobotanical value through its diverse therapeutic properties.

  • Scientific name: Sideroxylon spinosum
  • Family: Sapotaceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Ericales, it is a member of the family Sapotaceae. It is further categorized under the genus Sideroxylon, which includes the species Sideroxylon spinosum.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific geographical distribution primarily concentrated around the Mediterranean basin. In Southwestern Europe, its presence is notably documented within the country of Spain. Moving across the sea, it is widely distributed throughout various regions of Northern Africa. Specifically, it can be found inhabiting the landscapes of Algeria, Morocco, and Tunisia. Additionally, the species extends its range into the territory of Western Sahara.

Plant Parts

This plant, commonly known as the argan tree, is a vital source of valuable lipids derived from its fruit. The process begins with the extraction of the kernel from inside the nut, which is then processed to produce high-quality argan oil. During the refining stage, the extraction of the primary oil often leaves behind a nutrient-rich press-cake. This byproduct can be further utilized to create specialized seed oil, ensuring that every part of the harvest contributes to its medicinal and cosmetic value.
