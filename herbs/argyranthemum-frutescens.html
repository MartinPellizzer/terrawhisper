Argyranthemum frutescens

Marguerite daisy

Argyranthemum frutescens, commonly known as the marguerite daisy, is a versatile evergreen shrub valued for its vibrant, daisy-like blooms. Beyond its ornamental beauty, this plant holds significant medicinal potential in traditional practices. It is frequently studied for its bioactive compounds, offering promising applications in various natural therapeutic treatments.

  • Scientific name: Argyranthemum frutescens
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Native range: Europe

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified under the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. Within the subclass Magnoliidae, it is ordered under Asterales and placed in the family Asteraceae. Finally, it is specifically categorized under the genus Argyranthemum.

Common Names

This plant, known scientifically as Argyranthemum frutescens, is referred to by various common names depending on the language and region. In German, it is called the Strauchmargerite, while in Japanese, it is known as マーガレット. In Catalan, it retains a similar form as Argyrathemum frutescens, and in Finnish, it is called kanarianmarketta. Additionally, in Danish, the plant is identified as the almindelig buskmargerit.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a specific distribution pattern across several regions of the European continent. In Middle Europe, its presence can be documented within the borders of Germany. Moving toward the Mediterranean, it is found throughout Southwestern Europe, specifically on the islands of Corse and Sardegna, as well as on the mainland of Spain. Furthermore, the species extends into Southeastern Europe, where it inhabits various areas of Italy. Together, these locations define the diverse geographical range of Argyranthemum frutescens.
