Abutilon pannosum var. pannosum

Abutilon pannosum var. pannosum, commonly known as the velvetleaf abutilon, is a significant medicinal shrub native to tropical regions. Renowned for its distinctive textured foliage, this plant holds immense ethnobotanical value. It is frequently utilized in traditional medicine to treat various ailments, offering unique therapeutic properties for holistic healing.

  • Scientific name: Abutilon pannosum var. pannosum
  • Family: Malvaceae
  • Native range: Africa

Taxonomical Classification

This plant belongs to the kingdom Plantae and is classified within the phylum Streptophyta and the class Equisetopsida. As a member of the subclass Magnoliidae and the order Malvales, it falls under the family Malvaceae. It is specifically categorized within the genus Abutilon as the variety pannosum.

Common Names

This plant, Abutilon pannosum var. pannosum, is known by various local names depending on the region and language. In English, it is commonly referred to as the Velvetleaf or Velvetleaf Mallow. Within specific linguistic contexts, it is identified as Kapul (in Tagalog), Siring (in Cebuano), and it is also known as many other names across different local dialects.

Distribution

This plant exhibits a diverse geographical range spanning several distinct regions across the African continent and its surrounding islands. In Northern Africa, its presence is documented in both Algeria and Egypt. Its distribution extends into the Macaronesian islands, specifically within Cape Verde. Furthermore, the species is found in West Tropical Africa, inhabiting the countries of Burkina and Mali. These varied locations highlight the wide ecological adaptability of this particular medicinal variety.
